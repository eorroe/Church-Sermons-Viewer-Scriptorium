# Winning the War Within

## Overview

Pastor Philip Anthony Mitchell teaches that every believer is engaged in an internal spiritual battle, but victory does not come from personal effort alone. Through honest vulnerability and unwavering trust in Christ, this conversation reveals how to stay on fire for God, endure suffering without condemnation, resist pride, and remain faithful to the gospel regardless of opposition or personal pain.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel spiritually dry or distant from God
- When suffering makes you question your faith
- When facing criticism, persecution, or opposition for your beliefs
- When you struggle with pride or self-exaltation
- When you need to recommit to faithful ministry regardless of results
- When discerning between righteous correction and slanderous attacks

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize the Urgency of Your Spiritual Warfare

Understand that you are likely living in the last seconds of the church age. Every opportunity to minister or share the gospel could be someone's last chance to hear it. Minister with the urgency of someone on the front line of battle, not as a casual performance. Take your call to spread the gospel and multiply disciples seriously.

### Step 2: Return to the Source When Your Fire Dims

When you feel spiritually cold or distant, go back to the secret place: pray honestly, read scripture, fast, and surround yourself with community that is on fire for God. Being "on fire" is about intimacy with Christ, not platform size or public volume. Throw wood on the fire through scripture, prayer, fasting, purity, and community. The source is Jesus himself.

### Step 3: Normalize Suffering as Part of the Christian Experience

Suffering is not evidence of failed faith. When pain, depression, or distance from God enter your life, give yourself grace. Read the Psalms for permission to feel. Understand that suffering for righteousness draws you closer to Christ and strengthens your character. There is beauty in suffering when it is endured in the will of God.

### Step 4: Separate Critique into Categories

Not all criticism is equal. Distinguish between: (1) righteous critique that should humble and refine you, (2) critique born from jealousy and slander that you should ignore, and (3) satanic attacks aimed at discrediting you and stopping your ministry. Have the humility to accept valid correction without being destroyed by false accusations.

### Step 5: Guard Your Heart Against Pride

The greatest threat to your ministry is not external opposition but internal pride. View your platform, influence, and blessings as stewardship, not personal achievement. Regularly examine your heart and submit to accountability from trusted people who can tell you no. Remember that all favor belongs to God, and you are merely a steward.

### Step 6: Keep Your Promises to God

Make specific, intentional commitments to God about how you will serve Him and then guard them with your life. Be faithful to your word even when no one is watching, even when the room is empty, and even when it costs you everything. Write down your promises and return to them regularly.

### Step 7: Reject the Christian Mask

Stop performing perfection for others. Authentic faith means being honest about your brokenness, your pain, and your ongoing struggles. Vulnerability about your humanity is what allows others to relate to the Christ you proclaim. Do not use Christian language as a cover for what is really going on underneath.

### Step 8: Be a Man of Character and Accountability

For men specifically: resist the cultural attack on biblical masculinity by taking accountability for your actions, owning your mistakes, and surrounding yourself with men who will speak truth into your life. Real strength is found in humility, not dominance. Teach the next generation that real men take accountability, own their crap, and pursue holiness.

## Examples

### Example 1: Preaching While Sick

Pastor Mitchell shares how he preached an altar call for healing while battling a mysterious medical condition, with full faith that God was healing others even as he faced his own suffering. Your circumstances do not negate God's character or your calling to minister. You can still preach healing because Christ is a healer, regardless of your own condition.

### Example 2: The Donkey Analogy

View yourself as the donkey Jesus rides on. The praise and platform you receive belong to Christ, not you. Stay humble by remembering that God could strip away your platform at any moment, and you are merely a steward of what He has given. Do not let the crowds make you think the procession is about you.

### Example 3: The Apostle Paul's Vulnerability

Paul wrote two-thirds of the New Testament, had divine revelation, and yet he boasted in his weaknesses so the power of Christ could be displayed. He confessed his wrestle with sin, his persecutions, and his sufferings. If Paul could be honest about his brokenness, so can you.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Return to prayer and scripture when your spiritual fire feels cold
- ✅ Normalize seasons of suffering and distance from God as part of faith
- ✅ Accept valid, righteous critique and use it for growth
- ✅ Keep specific promises you have made to God
- ✅ Be honest about your brokenness rather than performing perfection
- ✅ Surround yourself with people who can tell you no and rebuke you
- ✅ Exalt Christ in all proclamation rather than yourself
- ✅ Own your mistakes without making excuses
- ✅ Create safe spaces for other believers to be vulnerable
- ✅ Distinguish between types of critique before responding

## Keep In Mind

- Suffering for righteousness is not evidence of God's absence but an invitation to deeper intimacy with Him
- The enemy uses both slander and pride to derail your ministry; guard your heart against both
- No one is above critique, but not all critique is from God
- Your fire for God is measured by intimacy, not by platform size or public volume
- God never creates a life for you that makes Him unnecessary
- Even your most favorite preacher or the greatest platform cannot make you suffer-free

## Security & Safety Notes

- When facing online criticism or persecution, do not respond in anger; instead, pray for discernment to identify the source and for the people being deceived
- Protect your heart by maintaining strict accountability relationships that can call out pride before it grows
- Keep your promises to God private and sacred; do not boast about your spiritual commitments
- Do not rely solely on your own understanding of critique; seek the Holy Spirit's discernment before responding to opposition

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Feeling condemned when you experience seasons of spiritual dryness or suffering
  **Solution:** Remember that even the Apostle Paul wrestled with these feelings. Give yourself grace and return to the secret place honestly.
- **Problem:** Taking valid critique personally and becoming defensive
  **Solution:** Separate the critique from your identity. If it's righteous, receive it humbly. If it's slanderous, release it and pray for the accuser.
- **Problem:** Letting pride creep in after God blesses your ministry
  **Solution:** Remind yourself daily that all favor belongs to God. Stay accountable to people who know the real you and aren't impressed by your platform.
- **Problem:** Performing Christianity for others while hiding your true pain
  **Solution:** Vulnerability is strength. Share your real struggles so others can see that faith is authentic even in the valley.
- **Problem:** Responding to persecution or slander with retaliation
  **Solution:** The enemy wants to provoke you into reacting in the flesh. Stay silent, pray for discernment, and trust God to defend your character.
- **Problem:** Treating suffering as a sign of failed faith or God's displeasure
  **Solution:** Suffering is part of the Christian experience. God uses suffering to shape character, strengthen faith, and draw you closer to Christ.
