# "Elder Judith | Mother's Day | May 10, 2026"

## Overview

This sermon is a Mother's Day message delivered by Elder Judith at Kingdom Culture Ministries as part of the "Watch Your Mouth" series. It honors the role of mothers and nurturers while teaching them to trust God with their children, intercede in prayer rather than try to control outcomes, and speak life through intentional declarations. The message reminds mothers that they are called to plant seeds and trust God to give the increase, even when the path is difficult or lonely.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are a mother, nurturer, or caregiver feeling isolated in your journey and need encouragement that God is with you
- When you struggle with the tension of wanting to protect your children from pain while trusting God to work in their lives
- When you need to be reminded of the power of your words, prayers, and declarations over your family

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize and honor mothers authentically

Reflect on the original intent of Mother's Day — intimate, personal recognition of mothers — rather than letting the day become purely commercialized. Acknowledge that every mother carries multiple roles: nanny, cook, doctor, counselor, teacher, drill sergeant, and more. Celebrate the mother figures in your life by honoring them with presence and gratitude, not just gifts.

### Step 2: Be strong and courageous in motherhood

When you feel alone, afraid, or discouraged, remind yourself of Deuteronomy 31:6 and 31:8: "Be strong and courageous. Do not be afraid or discouraged, for the Lord your God goes with you; he will never leave you nor forsake you." Motherhood has lonely seasons, especially when your children are at different stages than your friends' children. God is present in those isolated moments.

### Step 3: Release your children to God

Understand that you are tasked to plant seeds, but God gives the increase. Train your child according to Proverbs 22:6, but trust Isaiah 54:13 — "All your children will be taught by the Lord, and great will be their peace." You cannot force their relationship with God; they must come to know Him for themselves.

### Step 4: Intercede, don't intercept

Stop trying to intercept your children's decisions or control their outcomes. Instead, hit your knees and intercede in prayer. You can pray for their protection, wisdom, and guidance, but you must release them to make their own choices and learn from their own experiences.

### Step 5: Make declarations over your household

Follow the example of Joshua: "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord." Speak this and other Scriptural declarations over your family. You are charging the atmosphere with your words, and you do not know what obstacles you are pushing back through your prayers until you reach heaven.

### Step 6: Watch your mouth

Practice the central theme of this series — watch your mouth. Do not speak idle words. Do not tell someone "I know how you feel" unless you have truly walked through their experience. Every idle word will be accounted for, and the words you speak into your children's lives will stay with them long after they are grown.

### Step 7: Trust God through the pain

You cannot pray away pain, discouragement, or the consequences of a fallen world. Trust that Jesus will fix it in His time, not yours. There is purpose in the crooked path, the unlevel ground, and the climb up the mountain. Character and strength are forged in those difficult places.

## Examples

### Example 1: Anne Jarvis and the commercialization of Mother's Day

Anne Jarvis petitioned for official recognition of Mother's Day, which became law in 1914 under President Woodrow Wilson. However, she became disillusioned when the holiday was commercialized with flower sales and cards. In 1925, she was even arrested for disrupting a fundraising event by the American War Mothers. By the 1940s, she disowned the holiday entirely and died in a sanitarium in 1948, never having experienced motherhood herself. Her story warns against letting the genuine call to honor mothers be overshadowed by consumerism.

### Example 2: Jochebed giving up Moses

Jochebed, the mother of Moses, faced an impossible situation when Pharaoh ordered Hebrew baby boys killed. She placed Moses in a basket on the Nile, trusting God with his life. She did what she had to do — even let him go — to save him. Like Jochebed, mothers today must sometimes let go and trust God with their children's futures, even when it is painful or uncertain.

### Example 3: The "Ruth child" versus the "Samson child"

Some children, like Ruth, follow their mother's faith without question: "Where you go, I will go." Others, like Samson, are prayed for and promised by God but still make choices that break their mother's heart. Samson wanted a Philistine bride against his parents' wishes. God's plan was still at work even in Samson's rebellion. This teaches mothers that their children's missteps do not disqualify God's purpose — and that intercession continues even when things go off course.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Speak intentional, life-giving declarations over your children and household, charging the atmosphere with God's Word
- ✅ Intercede in prayer for your children rather than trying to control or intercept their choices
- ❌ Don't speak idle words or make presumptuous statements about someone else's pain unless you have truly walked through their experience
- ❌ Don't try to force your children to avoid all hardship or mistakes — trust that God is their teacher and that there is purpose in their struggles

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You try to control your children's decisions and shield them from every mistake, thinking that protecting them is your highest duty.
  **Solution:** Release them to God. You plant the seed and intercede in prayer, but God is their teacher. Trust that the lessons they learn through difficulty will be used for their growth and His glory.

- **Problem:** You speak carelessly, offering clichés or claiming to understand someone's grief without having experienced it yourself.
  **Solution:** Watch your mouth. If you have not walked through what someone else is walking through, keep your mouth shut. Let your words be inspired by the Holy Spirit rather than spoken from a place of presumption.
