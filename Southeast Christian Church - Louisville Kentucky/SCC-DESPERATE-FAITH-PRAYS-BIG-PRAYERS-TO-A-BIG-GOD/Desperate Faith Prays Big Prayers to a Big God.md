# Desperate Faith Prays Big Prayers to a Big God – Brock, Student Pastor, Southeast Christian Church

## Overview

This sermon shows followers of Jesus how to move from vague, small prayers and from big prayers backed by a small view of God, into desperate faith that brings specific, impossible requests to a big God. Working from Matthew 9:18-38 (Jairus, the bleeding woman, Jesus' compassion for the crowds, and the Lord of the harvest), it teaches that faith is a decision expressed in action, that God always answers (yes, no, or not yet), and that prayer itself is the strategy for reaching the next two generations. It ends with a concrete practice: write one prayer so big only God can answer, and pray it desperately, persistently, and specifically.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you cannot remember the last time God answered one of your prayers
- When your prayers feel small, vague, or unspecific and you would not recognize an answer
- When you pray big prayers — the sermon's examples are "God, bring revival into this nation. God, transform this city" — but live without holy anticipation that God wants to answer
- When you are in a season of suffering — physical, mental, or relational, lasting twelve days, twelve months, or twelve years — and need to bring it to Jesus
- When God has not answered your prayer the way you wanted or expected
- When you want to decide for yourself what you actually believe about Jesus
- When you want to pray for students, the next two generations, and workers for the harvest

## Steps

### Step 1: Audit Your Prayers Against Two Failure Modes

Ask honestly which failure describes you. The first is praying small prayers to a big God ("God, make today a good day. Lord, bless this food") — good prayers, but so vague that you could not tell whether God answered or whether the day simply turned out well on its own. The second is praying big prayers to a small God ("God, bring revival to our nation") without living in holy anticipation that God wants to answer. Name which one is yours before going further.

### Step 2: Enlarge Your View of God Before Enlarging Your Requests

Confess where your view of God has been too small. The sermon's warning is blunt: "The size of your suffering never determines the size of your God." Begin every big prayer by repeating two truths from this sermon: that the God of the universe is not overwhelmed by what looks impossible to you, and that with him anything is possible.

### Step 3: Bring Your Specific, Impossible Request to Jesus

Follow Jairus (Matthew 9:18). Surrounded by a crowd, he knelt before Jesus and said, "My daughter has just died. But you can bring her back to life again if you just come and lay your hand on her." Do not soften your request into a general one. State the impossible thing, and state it specifically. As pastor and author Mark Batterson of Washington, D.C., writes in The Circle Maker, "The more specific your prayers are, the more glory God receives" — because when only God can do it, only God gets the credit.

### Step 4: Treat Faith as a Decision, Not Just a Belief

Follow the bleeding woman (Matthew 9:20-21). She had suffered for twelve years, spent everything on doctors and cures, and decided: "If I can just touch his robe, I will be healed." Her inward belief produced outward action. Jesus said, "Your faith has made you well." Faith is not something you possess; it is something you decide — and each person must decide it for themselves.

### Step 5: Name Your Suffering Honestly and Magnify Jesus' Authority

Do not pretend your suffering is small. Whether it has lasted twelve years, twelve months, or twelve days — physical decline, anxiety, depression, hopelessness, a broken family, a friend group that dismantled, an ended relationship — bring it to Jesus as it is. Desperate faith does not minimize the pain; it believes Jesus is bigger than the pain. Jesus meets suffering with compassion.

### Step 6: Expect Answers You Did Not Script — Yes, No, or Not Yet

Learn from the Pharisees' error (Matthew 9:33-34): the most religious people of the day prayed for a Messiah-king, and when God sent the King they rejected him because he did not look like the king they expected. God always answers prayer: sometimes yes, sometimes no, sometimes not yet. Faith is not believing that God must follow your plan; it is deciding to trust that God is good and the Bible is true even when you cannot currently fully understand his plan.

### Step 7: When Fear Rises, Take Jesus' Own Words: "Don't Be Afraid. Just Have Faith."

The same words Jesus spoke to Jairus are for you when God has not yet answered your prayer. Decide to trust that God has better plans for you than you have for you. Do not settle for small prayers or a small view of God.

### Step 8: Rest in What Jesus Has Already Done About Your Suffering

Remember Romans 5:8: "God showed his great love for us by sending Christ to die for us while we were still sinners." Jesus stamped an expiration date on suffering itself — if you follow Jesus, your suffering will end, but your life will not. He is not angry at you; He is angry for you, moved with gut-level compassion (splanchna).

### Step 9: Make the Faith Decision for Yourself If You Never Have

Faith is an internal belief that Jesus is Lord expressed in outward obedience: turning from sin, submitting to Jesus in baptism, and walking under the Holy Spirit's guidance daily. No one can decide this for you. If you have not yet decided to follow Jesus, today is the day to talk with someone at Southeast Christian Church about making Jesus your Lord — the sermon's invitation is, "We would love to talk to you about making Jesus your Lord."

### Step 10: Join Southeast Christian Church's Big Prayers for the Harvest

Jesus said, "The harvest is great, but the workers are few. So pray to the Lord who is in charge of the harvest. Ask him to send more workers into his fields." In student ministry the sermon declares: "prayer is the strategy, and there is no other strategy." Join in three specific prayers: (1) that students already engaged in the church would be missionaries in their schools, reaching and baptizing friends who do not know Jesus; (2) that over 500 more adults would step into student ministry with the mission of making disciples; (3) that by the end of December 2029 the majority of Southeast Christian Church's growth would come from reaching the next two generations — the young people the sermon describes as so lost and confused that they are ready for something real and lasting.

### Step 11: Write Down One Prayer So Big Only God Could Answer

Find an index card. Write one specific prayer that is so big that only God could do it — for example, names of people you want to see repent, believe, and be baptized. This is the prayer card practice from the sermon, done by a college-age student named Nick (Nick Young), who was stepping into high school ministry to lead a group of freshman boys.

### Step 12: Carry the Card Every Day and Pray Desperately, Persistently, Specifically

Keep the card on your person every day and keep praying it through the months when nothing seems to happen — Nick kept praying through six silent months. Anyone who saw Nick out in public had the right to say, "Hey, show me your prayer card," and if he could not show it, they got to make him do something embarrassing. Nick prayed for three disengaged freshmen boys with no visible change, wondering Wednesday after Wednesday if he was wasting his time — and at a high school camp all three repented, trusted Jesus, and were baptized. It happened not because Nick is awesome, but because he prayed big prayers to a big God.

## Examples

### Example 1: Jairus Prays an Impossible Prayer (Matthew 9:18)

A synagogue leader pushes through a crowd surrounding Jesus and kneels: "My daughter has just died. But you can bring her back to life again if you just come and lay your hand on her." He states his worst news and his biggest request together. Jesus gets up and goes with him, raises the girl, and the report spreads through the countryside.

### Example 2: The Bleeding Woman Touches the Robe (Matthew 9:20-22)

The woman had suffered twelve years with constant bleeding; she had seen every doctor and every specialist and had spent all her money on options and clinical trials, but nothing had worked. She decides internally that Jesus is stronger than her suffering and acts externally, pressing through the crowd to touch the fringe of his robe. Jesus turns, sees her, and says, "Daughter, be encouraged. Your faith has made you well" — and she is healed at that moment.

### Example 3: Nick's Prayer Card for Three Freshmen Boys

Nick Young, a college-age student volunteering in high school ministry, joined his co-leaders in the sermon's prayer-card practice: after a co-leader prompted them to write down a prayer so big that only God could do it, they each wrote one on a plain index card. He wrote the names of three boys from the freshman group he leads whom he wanted to see baptized, and he committed to carrying that prayer card on his person every day. Months one through six: disengaged boys, silence, doubt ("Am I just wasting my time, Lord?"). At high school camp, all three boys repented, put their faith in Jesus, and were baptized — with Nick there.

### Example 4: The Pharisees Miss the Answer They Prayed For (Matthew 9:33-34)

The Pharisees had been begging God to send the Messiah-king to save the nation. God answered by sending Jesus — a king who came to wear a crown of thorns, not a royal crown. Standing in front of healings and exorcisms, the most religious people of the day concluded Jesus was empowered by demons. Their prayers were answered; their expectations were not.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Pray specific requests — write down names, dates, outcomes, and numbers so you could recognize an answer
- ✅ Pray big prayers to a big God — ask God to do something so big and so specific that if he actually did it, the only logical explanation would be that God showed up and showed off (the sermon's words)
- ✅ Keep asking desperately, persistently, and specifically — God is not annoyed at your asking
- ✅ Let your inward belief change your outward behavior — the bleeding woman pressed through the crowd to touch the fringe of Jesus' robe
- ✅ Bring your suffering to Jesus honestly instead of minimizing it
- ✅ Receive a "no" or a "not yet" as an answer from a good God, not as evidence of a small God
- ✅ Make prayer your strategy before programs and campaigns
- ❌ Don't settle for prayers so vague you could not tell whether God answered
- ❌ Don't throw out a big prayer while living as though God will not or cannot answer it
- ❌ Don't measure God by your timeline or by whether the answer matches your expectations
- ❌ Don't define faith as inner belief alone with no obedient action
- ❌ Don't conclude from a season of silence that God did not hear or does not care

## Keep In Mind

- Today's sermon in a sentence: desperate faith prays big prayers to a big God.
- Faith is not something you possess; faith is something you decide.
- Desperate faith does not pretend your suffering is small; it believes Jesus is bigger.
- God always answers — sometimes yes, sometimes no, sometimes not yet.
- As the sermon puts it, you are like a single thread in a master tapestry — God has the bigger perspective and sees the whole picture.
- Jesus looks at your suffering and is not angry at you — He is angry for you.
- If Jesus is your Lord, this earth is not your eternal home; suffering will end, but your life with Jesus will not end.
- The harvest is ready: people around you are so lost they are ready for something real and lasting.
- It is wrong to wait for a serving campaign or a good video before praying for workers — Jesus said pray.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Anchor your safety in the size of God, not the size of your circumstances: "With him, anything is possible."
- Students need an older, wiser Christian to follow and to learn from — shepherds and disciple makers who give them a journey of discipleship, not just another program or church service.
- If you do not yet have an older, wiser Christian to follow and learn from, ask the leaders of your local church to connect you with one.
- Never stake your belief in God on a single answer — yes, no, or not yet; the Pharisees had the most scripture knowledge and still missed God standing in front of them.
- The faith decision cannot be delegated — make sure the decision to follow Jesus is your own: an internal belief that Jesus is Lord expressed in outward obedience (repentance, baptism, daily Holy Spirit guidance).
- Beware spiritual routine: knowing the most about God can coexist with being blind to what God is doing.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Your prayers are so general ("bless this food," "make today good") that answered prayer blends into coincidence.
  **Solution:** Rewrite the request specifically — name the person, the need, the outcome — so an answer is unmistakable.
- **Problem:** You pray the big prayer but live without eager expectation that God will answer.
  **Solution:** Confess the small view of God and pray with holy, eager expectation that God wants to answer.
- **Problem:** Months pass after committing to a big prayer and nothing is visible, so you assume God is absent or you are wasting time.
  **Solution:** Keep carrying and praying the prayer card — Nick saw all three boys come to faith after six silent months.
- **Problem:** God does not answer in the way or timing you expected, and you quietly lose faith.
  **Solution:** Take Jesus' words to Jairus — "Don't be afraid. Just have faith" — and trust his goodness and his bigger perspective.
- **Problem:** You believe in Jesus inwardly but produce no obedient action.
  **Solution:** Turn faith into a decision expressed in outward obedience: repentance, baptism, and daily guidance by the Holy Spirit.
- **Problem:** You wait for the church to run a campaign or show a video before praying for workers.
  **Solution:** Start praying now — prayer is the strategy, and there is no other strategy.
