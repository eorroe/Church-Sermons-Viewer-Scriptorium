# Whatever You Do Week 5: Whatever You Say

## Overview

This sermon teaches Christians to be intentional with every word spoken, written, or texted by praying for divine appointments, engaging outsiders with grace-filled conversation, encouraging others with specific and personal words, and honoring people intentionally. It challenges followers of Jesus to let their words point others to Christ and give glory to God in all interactions, using Colossians 3:17 and 4:2-18 as the biblical foundation.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need to use your words to point others to Jesus
- When working on building bridges with people who don't know Christ
- When seeking to be more intentional with your speech and communication

## Steps

### Step 1: Pray with Your Words Because They Connect You to God

Colossians 4:2 teaches three aspects of prayer that shape how we speak to others. First, be devoted in prayer by being persistent and focused, not distracted by the 96 daily notifications that fragment our attention. Second, be watchful and alert, practicing eyes-open prayer throughout your day where you are aware of your surroundings and pray through your day for natural opportunities to have spiritual conversations. Third, be thankful, approaching God with gratitude and humility rather than presumption, remembering that God doesn't owe us anything. Pick two people who don't know Jesus, write their names down, and ask God to open a door of opportunity for you to use your words to point them to Him.

### Step 2: Engage with Your Words Because They Build Bridges to Outsiders

Colossians 4:5-6 challenges us to be wise toward outsiders and let our conversation be always full of grace, seasoned with salt. Use words that are full of grace, which represents undeserved kindness and unearned favor, and salt, which preserves, purifies, and enhances flavor. Look for intersections in people's lives where they are naturally more open to spiritual conversation. The intersection of change occurs when people are in transition, such as a new job, moving, marriage, having a baby, or retirement. The intersection of crisis occurs when people face the death of a loved one, difficult health news, job loss, financial hardship, or a breakup. At these intersections, your words have exponentially more significance, and a small gesture of hospitality or an invitation to a local church can have much more impact.

### Step 3: Encourage with Your Words Because They Strengthen Others

Colossians 4:7-9 shows Paul sending Tychicus and Onesimus to encourage the church's hearts. The word encourage means to put courage into someone, to speak courage into them. First, see and say what others miss by asking God to show you where you can recognize and affirm people around you. Second, affirm their value in God's kingdom by speaking to their identity in Christ and how God wants to use their story. Third, speak to their future and not just their past by putting courage into people, giving them strength to keep going, keep believing, and keep faith when they feel overwhelmed. Fourth, be specific rather than generic by not just giving generic compliments, but speaking specifically about each person's unique role and calling.

### Step 4: Honor with Your Words Because They Deepen Your Relationships

Colossians 4:10-18 demonstrates Paul mentioning people by name, honoring them specifically and intentionally. Honor people by being specific, personal, and intentional with your words. Use people's names and recognize their identity in Christ. Let people be seen by you through your words, countering the modern tendency to lose the art of personal recognition in a world of mass communication. Ask yourself at the end of the week, "Who have I honored with my words this week?"

## Examples

### Example 1: Looking for Open Doors in Everyday Life

While working on this sermon, the preaching pastor experimented with praying for an open door instead of continuing to work on his message. He prayed for his server, Michelle, and when she noticed a picture of his grandkids on his iPad, a natural conversation opened that led to him praying for her and her daughter, and recommending a church near her that could support her as a single mom. This demonstrates how praying for an open door creates space for God to work through your words in unexpected moments, and how being watchful for opportunities leads to natural spiritual conversations.

### Example 2: Building Bridges Through Conversation

The pastor attempted to build a bridge with an Uber driver by using the name he assumed the driver had to transition into a spiritual conversation about honesty and the gospel. Although there was an initial misunderstanding about the driver's actual name, the attempt illustrates the principle of using everyday interactions as opportunities for spiritual conversations, even when they don't go as smoothly as planned. The adventure of saying, "God, how do you want to use my words to build a bridge?" is valuable even when the execution is imperfect.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Pray for two people who don't know Jesus and ask God for an open door
- ✅ Look for intersections of change and crisis in the lives of people around you
- ✅ Use words full of grace and salt when talking to outsiders
- ✅ Be specific, personal, and intentional when encouraging others
- ❌ Don't let your words tear down, cancel, or be harsh toward others
- ❌ Don't be generic when encouraging people
- ❌ Don't miss opportunities to speak courage and faith into people's lives at their intersections

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon challenges posture and heart posture more than it provides a rigid formula for spiritual conversations.
- The principles in this sermon require the Holy Spirit's guidance and sensitivity to each unique situation and relationship.
- Be sensitive to the Holy Spirit's leading when engaging in spiritual conversations. Not every moment is the right moment, and discernment is required.
- Respect people's boundaries and personal space. If someone is not open to a spiritual conversation, continue to show Christ's love through your actions and words in other ways.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Words overflow from an uncontrolled heart
  **Solution:** Spend time in devoted, watchful, and thankful prayer so that what's inside you reflects Christ before it comes out in speech.
- **Problem:** Missing divine appointments because you're not looking for them
  **Solution:** Practice eyes-open prayer throughout your day and be intentional about noticing intersections of change and crisis in the lives of people around you.
- **Problem:** Giving generic encouragement that doesn't actually strengthen anyone
  **Solution:** Be specific about what you see in people, affirm their identity in Christ, and speak to their future rather than just their past.
