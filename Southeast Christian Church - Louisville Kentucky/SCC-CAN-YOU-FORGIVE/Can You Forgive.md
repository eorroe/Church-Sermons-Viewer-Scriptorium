# Can You Forgive? - Biblical Forgiveness Through Joseph's Story

## Overview

This sermon is written from a Christian evangelical perspective and presents the Joseph narrative as a typological precursor to Jesus Christ. It provides a structured, four-phase framework (conviction, repentance, sacrifice, grace) that guides the user through reading Genesis 42–45, self-reflection prompts, and application exercises drawn from the sermon "Can You Forgive?" by Southeast Christian Church.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When studying the story of Joseph and his brothers in Genesis 42-45
- When preparing a sermon, Bible study, or small group lesson on forgiveness
- When personally working through hurt or betrayal and want a biblically grounded study framework to process it
- When the user asks about biblical forgiveness, reconciliation, or letting go of bitterness

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Context of Joseph's Story

Read Genesis 37 and Genesis 41–45 from a standard translation such as ESV, NIV, or NASB. All verse references in this skill are from ESV unless otherwise noted. Key facts:
- Joseph was 17 when his brothers threw him into a cistern and sold him into slavery
- After 13 years of slavery and false imprisonment, Joseph became second-in-command of Egypt
- 22 years later, during a famine, Joseph's brothers came to Egypt for grain
- Joseph tested his brothers to see whether they had changed.

### Step 2: Identify the Phase of Conviction (Genesis 42)

When the brothers stood before Joseph, their guilty consciences were awakened. They recognized their past sin of selling Joseph into slavery. Key indicators of conviction:
- Connecting present suffering to past sin
- The weight of guilt becoming unbearable or all-consuming
- Acknowledging wrongdoing even when no one else knows

### Step 3: Recognize the Phase of Repentance (Genesis 43)

Repentance means changing direction. The brothers moved from jealousy and selfishness toward mercy and love. Key indicators of repentance:
- Taking responsibility for past actions
- Willingness to protect the vulnerable (Benjamin)
- Changing behavior, not just words

### Step 4: Observe the Phase of Sacrifice (Genesis 44)

Judah stepped forward and offered himself as a slave in place of Benjamin. Sacrifice is giving up something you love for something you love more. Key indicators of sacrificial love:
- Putting another person's safety, needs, or interests above your own
- Keeping promises even at personal cost
- Acting as a substitute for someone else

### Step 5: Receive the Phase of Grace (Genesis 45)

Joseph revealed his identity and extended mercy instead of revenge. Grace is receiving mercy when you deserve judgment. Key indicators of grace:
- Releasing the right to retaliate
- Interpreting past evil through God's sovereignty
- Providing restoration instead of punishment

### Step 6: Apply Forgiveness to Personal Life

Apply the four-phase framework: (1) name the specific hurt in one sentence, (2) identify which of the four phases (conviction, repentance, sacrifice, grace) most accurately describes your current state based on the indicators listed above, (3) write a 2–3 sentence journal entry in a physical notebook or a digital text file (for example, `.md`, `.txt`, or `.docx`) saved to a location of your choice, and (4) consider sharing it with a pastor or trusted mentor only if the situation involves another person, you have their consent, and you genuinely feel physically and emotionally safe doing so.

Key scriptures to meditate on:
- 2 Corinthians 7:10: "Godly sorrow brings repentance that leads to salvation and leaves no regret, but worldly sorrow brings death."
- Ephesians 5:25: "Husbands, love your wives just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her."
- Matthew 6:14-15: "For if you forgive other people when they sin against you, your heavenly Father will also forgive you. But if you do not forgive others their sins, your Father will not forgive your sins."
- Genesis 45:7: "But God sent me ahead of you to preserve for you a remnant on earth and to save your lives by a great deliverance."

### Step 7: Overcome Barriers to Forgiveness

**Common objection:** "They don't deserve it" — a sentiment brought to forgiveness. **Biblical response:** Mercy means the most when it is deserved the least. Joseph's brothers did not deserve forgiveness.
**Common objection:** "It's too painful" — holding onto hurt feels justified. **Biblical response:** Bitterness is often compared to drinking poison while waiting for the other person to die — a reminder that unresolved hurt typically harms the holder more than the source. This metaphor does not diminish the reality of the pain; it points toward the cost of carrying it long-term.
**Common objection:** "I can't forget it" — the memory feels inescapable. **Biblical response:** Forgiveness is not forgetting; it is releasing the right to retaliate. God remembers your sins no more (Hebrews 8:12).
**Common objection:** "I'm not strong enough" — the task feels impossible alone. **Biblical response:** Identify what is within your power to change (for example, your response) and what is not (for example, the other person's actions), and surrender the latter to God (Dr. Henry Cloud). The Holy Spirit empowers forgiveness.

### Step 8: Share the Gospel Connection

This skill operates from a Christian worldview that reads the Joseph story as typologically pointing forward to Jesus Christ:
- Decades earlier, Judah's brothers sold Joseph into slavery, according to the narrative
- Generations later, Judah's descendant Jesus gave himself on the cross to save sinners
- The innocent became the substitute for the guilty so that the guilty could go free

## Examples

### Example 1: Personal Bible Study

Read Genesis 37 and Genesis 41–45, then for each of chapters 42–45 write down in a personal journal or notebook:
- What happened in this chapter
- Which phase of forgiveness is present (conviction, repentance, sacrifice, grace)
- How this applies to your current situation

Sample entry for Genesis 42: "Joseph's brothers felt convicted when they remembered selling him into slavery. I am in the conviction phase because I keep replaying what my friend said. God is revealing that my anger is masking my own guilt."

### Example 2: Small Group Discussion

Facilitate a discussion using these questions:
1. When have you experienced conviction like Joseph's brothers?
2. What does repentance look like in your relationships?
3. Who needs you to sacrifice for them today?
4. How has God extended grace to you in an unexpected way?

### Example 3: Sermon Preparation

Structure a sermon on forgiveness using the four-phase framework:
- Introduction: The story of Joseph
- Point 1: Conviction - The weight of guilt
- Point 2: Repentance - Changing direction
- Point 3: Sacrifice - Giving up what you love
- Point 4: Grace - Receiving mercy when you deserve judgment
- Conclusion: The gospel connection to Jesus Christ

## Best Practices

- Read the full Genesis 37 and Genesis 41–45 passage before studying individual chapters
- Meditate on the key scriptures provided for each phase
- Journal your responses to the application questions honestly
- Process a real situation through the four-phase framework alongside a trusted spiritual mentor or pastor before applying it independently
- Remember that forgiveness is a process, not a single event
- Extend grace to others as God has extended grace to you (Ephesians 4:32)

## Keep In Mind

- Forgiveness does not always mean restoring the relationship; safety and boundaries are important
- The process of forgiveness can take time; be patient with yourself and others
- Consult a pastor or spiritual director for complex relational or doctrinal questions
- Do not justify staying in abusive relationships; forgiveness and safety are not mutually exclusive but require wisdom

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Confusing forgiveness with forgetting.
  **Solution:** Forgiveness is releasing the right to retaliate, not erasing the memory of the hurt.

- **Problem:** Waiting for the other person to apologize first.
  **Solution:** Joseph forgave his brothers before they asked for forgiveness. Forgiveness is initiated by the offended, not the offender.

- **Problem:** Believing forgiveness means the relationship will be restored automatically.
  **Solution:** Forgiveness is unilateral; reconciliation is bilateral. You can forgive without the other person's participation.

- **Problem:** Using "I'll forgive when I feel like it" as an excuse.
  **Solution:** Forgiveness is a choice of the will, empowered by the Holy Spirit, not a feeling that arrives on its own.
