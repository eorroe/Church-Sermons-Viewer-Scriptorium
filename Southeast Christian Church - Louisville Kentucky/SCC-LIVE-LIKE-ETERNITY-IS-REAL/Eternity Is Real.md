# Eternity Is Real
## Overview
This sermon on Luke 16:19-31 teaches that eternity is real: hell is a literal place that Jesus warns about more than he celebrates heaven, and heaven is a settled seat at the table with Abraham. Working from a funeral-room exercise, the story of the rich man and Lazarus, and the image of a rented cabin you check out of, the emphasis is practical: examine the sentence your life is building, set down what you cannot carry into eternity, re-decide your grudges, money, and time, and warn the people who know you best. It closes with a direct call to put your faith in Jesus, repent, and be baptized, plus pastoral counsel for believers whose loved one died without Christ.

## When to Follow This Sermon
- When you suspect your life has quietly become about comfort, hobbies, and entertainment and you want an honest measure of that life
- When you have faced a death, a diagnosis, or a milestone birthday that has forced the question of what your life will leave behind
- When you can feel the gap between what you say you believe about eternity and how you actually spend your week
- When you are weighing what to do with money, time, or a relationship you cannot take with you
- When you need words for someone who is ready to trust Jesus but feels held back by grief, shame, or a loved one who died without Christ
- When you want to work through the exercise privately, with a trusted friend, or in a small group, and leave room at the end for the invitation, the repentance, and the next-steps conversation in the second Steps block

## Steps
### Step 1: Sit at your own funeral table and name four people who really know you
Picture a round table with five chairs. You sit in one chair, and four people who genuinely know you sit in the other four. Skip the people who first come to mind and choose people who know the real you: a close friend, your spouse if you are married, one of your children if you have children, or a sibling. This is the same room a pastor sits in with family before a funeral, so the four names you choose are the four people who know you the way a grieving family knows the person who died. Name your four out loud or write the four names on paper.

### Step 2: Work the table and write the honest answers
Ask each person at the table: what were they really like, what was this person passionate about, what did they live for, what is their legacy, how will they be remembered? Write the single sentence you believe each of the four would honestly say about you. Do not defend yourself and do not argue against the sentence. Hear each sentence the way the pastor hears answers in a pre-funeral room, as the honest measure of the life you have lived.

### Step 3: Examine the sentence your life is currently building
The pastor's funeral-room stories show what a wasted eulogy sounds like. A man in his 30s died in a car accident, and his entire eulogy was that he loved hats and his hat collection. At a second table, a woman's adult daughter said that her mother never missed an episode of Young and the Restless, and everyone laughed even though an entire human life had been lived. Ask yourself: what sentence has my life been building? Ask yourself what the four people you chose would say, in one sentence, about what you were passionate about and what you lived for.

### Step 4: Read and study Luke 16:19-31 in full
Lay the facts of the text beside your own answer. The rich man was dressed in purple and fine linen and lived in luxury every day. Lazarus, a beggar covered with sores, was laid at the rich man's gate, longing to eat what fell from the rich man's table, while the dogs came and licked his sores. Both men died. The angels carried Lazarus to Abraham's side, while the rich man was buried and was in torment in Hades, looking up and seeing Abraham far away with Lazarus by his side.

### Step 5: Take seriously the reality and the love of the warnings (Luke 16:19-31)
Eternity is real, and hell is a real place, not a metaphor. Jesus warns about hell more than he celebrates heaven, and his warnings are an expression of love. A parent issues constant warnings for the same reason: the stove is hot, the knife is sharp, the ladder needs both hands, and the stove must not be touched. Scripture describes the suffering of hell in three ways. The suffering is emotional, with weeping and gnashing of teeth as the body's physical response to regret. The suffering is relational, with the rich man in total isolation and Lazarus in community at the table with Abraham. The suffering is spiritual, because hell is most simply a place where God is not.

### Step 6: Answer the question 'How could a loving God send people to hell?'
Use the parent-with-a-prodigal analogy. Imagine a parent whose child has rejected the parent completely, refusing the parent's calls, money, advice, and presence. That parent does not throw the child in a trunk and handcuff the child to a beam in the basement. The reason is not that the parent lacks the strength. The reason is that this is not how love works. Here is what a loving parent can do: the parent can send messengers who are close to the child, the parent can write a letter, the parent can get the child's attention and make the current path less comfortable, and the parent can go and live on the street with the child. God did every one of those four actions. He sent Moses and the prophets. He wrote a letter, and that letter is the Bible. He came Himself, leaving heaven, living in this world, bleeding, dying, and rising. Notice that in the story the rich man never says 'it isn't fair.' The objection loses its force on the other side of this life.

### Step 7: Set down what you cannot carry into eternity (Revelation 21:4)
John describes heaven partly by naming what is NOT there: no more death or mourning or crying or pain, for the old order of things has passed away. Name the specific items you have learned to carry and that you can now set down: the pill organizer with the days of the week on the lid, the cane, the walker, the oxygen line you have learned to step over, the folder of records you carry to every appointment, the bitterness, the resentment, the shame, the divorce papers, the death certificate, the test results, the police report, the eviction notice, the pink slip, the restraining order, the bill you cannot pay, the negative pregnancy test. Lazarus set down his sores, his hunger, his shame, his fear, and his loneliness when the angels carried him to Abraham's side, and you can set your list down at that same table.

### Step 8: Return to the four chairs and name what you want them to say
Do not ask only what they would say. Write a single sentence per chair describing what you WANT that person to say about your life, the sentence you are still able to build. The pastor's own hope is that the people at his table would say, 'he lived like he really believed eternity is real.'

### Step 9: Test your belief for congruence
Incongruent values are when you genuinely believe a claim while your daily life flatly contradicts that claim. The classic example is a person who says that nothing matters more to that person than health while the same person skips exercise, eats poorly, and sleeps too little. Apply the same test to heaven: if you truly believe eternity is real, your life should look different, including how you spend your time, how you handle your relationships, and which goals you pursue. Treat this as a daily fight to get your life to line up with what you believe, not a one-time decision.

### Step 10: Reframe your time as a week in a cabin you are checking out of
Eighty years measured against eternity is about a week in a rented cabin. Nobody spends their savings and takes out a loan to gut and remodel a cabin they have rented. Nobody installs mahogany floors, quartz countertops, a hot tub on the deck, and a new mattress on the last day, only to get a voicemail from the rental company that checkout is at 10 a.m. Ask yourself: why am I remodeling a place I am about to check out of? Ask yourself what your life would look like if you lived like checkout is at 10:00 a.m. Measure the email that ruined your weekend by the lens of eternity. Measure the event you fear that has not happened yet by the lens of eternity.

### Step 11: Re-decide your grudges, your money, and your time through the lens of eternity
Name one specific ongoing irritation and ask whether you can carry that irritation into eternity. Since unforgiveness cannot be taken with you, decide now whether you will carry that grudge for another 10, 20, or 30 years. Then evaluate money and time against Jesus' command to store up treasures in heaven. For each decision ask what will still matter in eternity, ask what you are storing in heaven, and ask whether this purchase is an eternal investment or another cabin remodel.

## Steps: Making the Decision and Following Jesus
### Step 12: Tell the four people at your table that it is all real
The moment eternity became real to the rich man, his second request was for a witness: 'send Lazarus to my family, I beg you, please warn them so they will not also come to this place of torment.' Nothing matters more than making sure the people at your table know that eternity is real. Use the pastor's closing inventory as a checklist of what that witness looks like. He lived like he really believed eternity is real. She noticed people when she walked by. The way he gave away money was almost a little irresponsible. She was always telling someone about how Jesus changed her life. He was quick to forgive. When she got the diagnosis she grieved but had a hope and peace that made people uncomfortable. When she said she would pray for you, she meant it. He never made you feel like you were interrupting. He gave away a car and was embarrassed when people found out. She invited me to church 11 times and I came on the 12th. He was the first person to look me in the eye and tell me God loved me. She was the first to apologize. When he forgave, he never brought it back up.

### Step 13: Counsel a believer whose loved one may have died without Jesus (Luke 16:27-31)
If a loved one you loved deeply died and, as far as you know, died without Jesus, hold two truths together. First, nobody wants that loved one in heaven more than Jesus. There are no lengths Jesus will not go to, and you do not know whether a final conversation happened between that loved one and Jesus. The thief on the cross was saved with seconds to spare, and nobody standing on the ground knew that the thief had been saved. Second, if your loved one is not with Jesus, the very first thing that loved one would want the moment eternity became real is for someone to warn you. Believing in Jesus is not betrayal. It is the one thing your loved one would ask you to do.

### Step 14: Recognize that Moses and the prophets are enough (Luke 16:29-31)
Abraham answered that the rich man's brothers have Moses and the prophets and that his brothers should listen to Moses and the prophets. The rich man argued that someone rising from the dead would convince his family, and Jesus answered that if they will not listen to Moses and the prophets, they will not be convinced even if someone rises from the dead. Someone has risen from the dead, and that person is Jesus, so believe the Scriptures. The ultimate witness has already been given.

### Step 15: Put your faith in Jesus, because salvation is a gift and not a wage
Respond by repenting, confessing, and believing. Salvation is a free gift of God through faith in Jesus Christ. The word 'believe' simply means to put your weight on a claim, so put your weight and your hope on Jesus. Like Lazarus the beggar, you can do nothing to earn or deserve salvation.

### Step 16: Settle the decision before you leave, and talk to someone about it
If you have never put your faith and trust in Jesus, do not leave the decision open. Go to the next steps room on the first floor of the church, talk with a staff member or volunteer there, and pray with them so the decision is settled today.

### Step 17: Walk into repentance as a Christian
For those who are already Christians, there is room at the foot of the cross for all of us. Repentance is not about shame or making yourself feel bad. Repentance is about freedom, looking at your life in the light, being honest, and telling yourself the truth. Ask concretely: if I believe eternity is real, what should change about how I spend my time, what should change about my relationships, and what should change about my goals?

### Step 18: End with the legacy sentence you choose to build
Close by returning to the four chairs and the single question this sermon circles: what is the sentence that your life is building? State what you want each of the four people to say, and take the first concrete action toward that sentence this week.

## Examples
### Example 1: Answering the Eulogy Question Honestly at a Funeral Table
Use the pastor's real funeral-room stories. Around the table for a man in his 30s who died in a car accident sat his mom, two brothers, a girlfriend, and buddies. After a long silence his mother said, 'Well, my son loved hats,' and then, 'You should really come by and see his hat collection.' At another table an adult daughter said, 'If there's one thing about my mom, she never missed an episode of Young and the Restless.' Everyone laughed even though an entire human life had been lived. Apply it: these are the answers that break the pastor's heart, and they are the measure of what a life spent on comfort, hobbies, and entertainment produces.

### Example 2: Remodeling a Cabin You Are Checking Out Of
The rented cabin in the Smokies looked great in the pictures, where 'rustic charm' turns out to mean carpet coming up, wallpaper peeling, a popcorn ceiling, and springs breaking through the mattress. The renter burns through savings and takes out a loan to gut it with mahogany floors, new cabinets, quartz countertops, a hot tub on the wraparound deck, and a Sleep Number mattress that arrives on the last day. Then the rental company calls to remind the renter that checkout is at 10 a.m. Apply it: nobody does that with a week-long rental, yet most of us do exactly that with 80 years measured against eternity.

### Example 3: Explaining Heaven by Naming What Is Not There
In an East African village, the pastor could not explain indoor plumbing, a pillow-top mattress, or a roller coaster to a 19-year-old who had never seen any of those three things. So the pastor switched to describing what is NOT there: no season when the food runs out, no dirt floor, no darkness, and no walk to get water because the water is already in the house and a person only turns the handle. John does the same in Revelation 21:4 to make eternity believable. Apply it: when hope feels abstract, name the specific items you will set down when you arrive in heaven.

### Example 4: The Rich Man's Second Request
In Luke 16:27-31, the rich man's first request is for a drop of water, but his second request is for a witness. He begs Abraham to send Lazarus to warn his five brothers. He is not hoping his family joins him in torment. He is begging that his five brothers do not come to this place of torment. Apply it: the clearest test of whether eternity has become real to you is whether your concern has shifted from your own comfort to the people in your life hearing the truth.

### Example 5: The Person Who Will Not Believe Because It Feels Like Betrayal
A person is ready to trust Jesus and be baptized, but is held back because someone they loved died, as far as that person knows, without Jesus, so hoping in Jesus feels like betraying the person who died. Apply the two truths from Step 13: no one wants that loved one in heaven more than Jesus, and final conversations between the dying and Jesus happen more often than the people standing nearby realize, just as the thief on the cross was saved with seconds to spare. And if your loved one is not with Jesus, the first thing that loved one would want is for someone to warn you. Believing is the one thing your loved one would ask for.

## Best Practices
- ✅ Do the funeral-table exercise honestly, and write the four sentences down instead of only thinking about them
- ✅ Do let the sentence you want the four people at your table to say govern how you spend time, money, and energy
- ✅ Do set down the items you cannot carry into eternity, including bitterness, resentment, shame, and old records and diagnoses
- ✅ Do forgive quickly, and never bring the offense back up again once you have forgiven
- ✅ Do tell the people at your table the truth about eternity, and make a specific, countable invitation, like the woman who invited someone to church 11 times and they came on the 12th
- ✅ Do mean it when you say you will pray for someone
- ✅ Do share your story of how Jesus changed your life with people who do not yet know him
- ✅ Do let kindness and love lead people to repentance rather than shame or fear
- ❌ Don't spend your whole life remodeling the cabin you are about to check out of
- ❌ Don't carry a grudge for another 10, 20, or 30 years that you cannot take with you
- ❌ Don't keep your faith private from the people who know you best
- ❌ Don't assume there will be second chances after death, because the chasm between heaven and hell is fixed and set in place
- ❌ Don't treat repentance as shame, because repentance is about freedom and telling yourself the truth
- ❌ Don't attempt to earn or deserve salvation through good works, because salvation is a free gift received by putting your weight on Jesus

## Keep In Mind
- This content is written from a Christian evangelical perspective and presents hell as a real, literal place as taught in Luke 16:19-31.
- Jesus speaks about hell more than heaven, and his warnings come from love, not threat.
- The chasm between heaven and hell is fixed and set in place, so nobody can cross from one side to the other. There is no transfer, no appeal process, no waiting room, and no second chances.
- Heaven is described with table imagery: a feast in paradise with Abraham seated at the table, and Lazarus finally has the seat he was denied at the rich man's gate.
- The most committed atheist still receives every good gift from God, including the blue sky, the sun, laughter, and good meals (James 1:17), and hell is what remains when God is removed.
- In the words of the sermon: for a Christian, this life is the closest thing to hell you will ever experience. For someone without Christ, this life is the closest thing to heaven you will ever experience.
- Repentance is about freedom, not shame: looking at your life in the light and telling yourself the truth.
- The sermon is urgent but not heavy-handed, because the Bible teaches that God's kindness leads to repentance.

## Security & Safety Notes
- This file is a spiritual-practice guide, not a substitute for professional counseling, pastoral oversight, or medical or mental health care.
- The funeral-table exercise deliberately confronts mortality, grief, and how you will be remembered, and it can surface real anxiety or fresh grief. Do the exercise with a trusted friend, pastor, or counselor if you are recently bereaved.
- If grief becomes persistent or overwhelming, or if you experience thoughts of self-harm, contact a trusted pastor, counselor, or local crisis line immediately.
- If you are in an unsafe or abusive situation, prioritize your physical safety and contact local authorities or a trusted support organization before pursuing any relational reconciliation step.
- Do not use the hell and warning material to threaten, coerce, or pressure people into a decision. Present it plainly and let kindness and love draw them.
- If you are helping someone whose loved one may have died without Jesus, do not speculate about that loved one's eternal state. Focus on the two truths in Step 13 and on the living person in front of you.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Problem:** Waiting for a funeral or a crisis to think about what your life is building
  **Solution:** Do the funeral-table exercise today, while you still have time to change the sentence your life is building

- **Problem:** Answering the eulogy question with hobbies and preferences instead of love, generosity, and witness
  **Solution:** Ask what the four people would say you lived for, and measure your current week against the sentence you want them to say

- **Problem:** Believing the sermon is about wealth being evil
  **Solution:** Remember that Abraham was one of the wealthiest people to ever live, and this story is about this particular rich man who lived his whole life for luxury and comfort

- **Problem:** Treating hell as a metaphor, a party, or a temporary holding room
  **Solution:** Take Jesus at his word: hell is a real place of emotional, relational, and spiritual suffering where God is not

- **Problem:** Assuming a loving God could never allow hell, while ignoring that the rich man in the story never claims unfairness
  **Solution:** Follow the prodigal analogy in Step 6: God sent Moses and the prophets, wrote a letter, and came himself, and love does not kidnap or handcuff

- **Problem:** Doing the exercises with conviction but never changing one decision about time, money, or a grudge, which leaves your life just as incongruent as it was before
  **Solution:** Name one grudge, one purchase, and one use of your week, and re-decide each through the lens of eternity

- **Problem:** Using fear, shame, or pressure to get a decision out of someone
  **Solution:** Present the truth plainly and let God's kindness lead people to repentance, because an invitation repeated 11 times does more than a threat ever could

- **Problem:** Believing that hoping in Jesus is a betrayal of a loved one who died without him
  **Solution:** Remember the two truths in Step 13: no one wants that person in heaven more than Jesus, and believing is the one thing that person would ask you to do

- **Problem:** Trying to earn salvation instead of receiving it as a free gift by faith
  **Solution:** Like Lazarus the beggar, you can do nothing to earn or deserve salvation. Put your weight and your hope on Jesus
