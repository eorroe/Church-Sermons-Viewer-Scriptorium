# Trusting God's Ways

## Overview

This sermon teaches believers to trust God's ways even when life's detours, delays, and closed doors do not immediately make sense. Drawing from biblical accounts and real-life testimonies, it reveals that storms and troubles are not meant to stop you but to transport you to a place of greater blessing. By maintaining faith, a good attitude, and a refusal to fight divine redirections, you position yourself to experience the abundance God has prepared on the other side of every closed door.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are walking through a season of confusion, disappointment, or sudden change
- When a door has closed in your life and you cannot understand why
- When you are facing a storm and feel overwhelmed or out of control
- When you need encouragement to keep the faith during delays or setbacks
- When you want to understand how God works behind the scenes in difficult circumstances

## Steps

### Step 1: Acknowledge the Storm

Do not ignore or minimize what you are going through. Name the difficulty, disappointment, or closed door honestly. Recognize that your current situation is real and valid. Acknowledging the storm is the first step toward processing it through faith rather than fear or frustration.

### Step 2: Trust That God Is in Control

Even when the situation makes no sense, choose to believe that God is working behind the scenes. Your assignment is not to understand every detour — it is to trust the One who is directing it. Release the need to control the outcome and instead surrender the situation to God's wisdom and timing.

### Step 3: Keep Your Peace

Refuse to let anxiety, worry, or bitterness take root. Speak peace over your circumstances through prayer, worship, and positive confession. A peaceful heart positions you to hear God's voice and discern His direction, even in the midst of chaos.

### Step 4: Refuse to Fight Closed Doors

Do not waste energy trying to force open a door that God has closed. Closed doors are divine redirections — they are setups for something greater. Instead of pushing against the barrier, pause, pray, and ask God what new path He is opening for you.

### Step 5: Expect Abundance on the Other Side

Hold fast to the conviction that every storm is transporting you, not trapping you. God promises to blow you from barren places to abundant places. Keep an attitude of expectancy, knowing that the delay or detour is preparing you for a level of blessing you could not have reached any other way.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Disciples in the Storm (Mark 6)

Jesus sent the disciples ahead of Him by boat, and while they were crossing the sea, a fierce storm arose. They were terrified, thinking they would perish, even though Jesus was actually on the water walking toward them. The storm looked like destruction, but it was actually transporting them to a moment where they would witness the power and presence of Christ in a new and miraculous way. The storm was not the end — it was the setup for a greater revelation of God's faithfulness.

### Example 2: Coach Tony Dungy

Tony Dungy, former NFL coach, was fired from the Tampa Bay Buccaneers and faced one of the lowest points of his career. The setback looked like the end of his coaching journey, but he refused to lose faith or his good attitude. He later became the head coach of the Indianapolis Colts and led them to a Super Bowl victory, proving that a closed door can be a divine setup for something far greater than what you had imagined.

### Example 3: Joel Osteen's Personal Testimony

Joel Osteen shared that he never planned to go into ministry. His father's sudden death thrust him into the pulpit at Lakewood Church — a role he did not seek or anticipate. What looked like an unexpected, painful detour became the platform for the largest church in America and a global ministry that reaches millions. The detour was not a mistake; it was a divine assignment in disguise.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Acknowledge your feelings honestly before choosing to trust God
- ✅ Speak life and peace over your situation daily through prayer and confession
- ✅ Stay connected to community and supportive believers during difficult seasons
- ✅ Look for what God is teaching you through the storm rather than just wanting it to end
- ✅ Keep a record of past faithfulness to remind yourself during new storms
- ❌ Don't fight against closed doors or force opportunities that aren't meant for you
- ❌ Don't adopt a bitter or negative attitude when things don't go as planned
- ❌ Don't isolate yourself during difficult times — community strengthens faith
- ❌ Don't assume that a closed door means God is against you — it may mean He is for you

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace personal conviction, pastoral guidance, or professional support for mental health and emotional well-being.
- Stop and ask for prayer, counsel, or clarification if you are struggling with trauma, grief, or severe distress.
- Trusting God's ways is a daily choice — it does not mean you will never feel pain, confusion, or doubt, but that you will not let those feelings drive your decisions.
- This sermon is intended for personal spiritual growth and encouragement.
- If you are experiencing thoughts of self-harm, severe depression, or crisis, reach out to a qualified mental health professional or contact emergency services immediately.
- Always apply discernment when sharing personal struggles — choose safe, trusted environments and people of faith.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Feeling abandoned by God during a prolonged storm or delay
  **Solution:** Remember that God's silence is not absence. Many biblical figures waited through seasons before seeing breakthrough. Continue to pray, worship, and trust in God's perfect timing.
- **Problem:** Becoming bitter or cynical after a door closes
  **Solution:** Bitterness poisons your future. Intentionally choose forgiveness and gratitude each day, and ask God to help you release disappointment.
- **Problem:** Trying to force a door open that God has closed
  **Solution:** Pause and seek God's direction through prayer, wise counsel, and scripture. If a door remains closed, trust that God has a better plan and is redirecting you for your ultimate good.
- **Problem:** Using a single storm to define your entire relationship with God
  **Solution:** Look back at God's faithfulness throughout your life. One difficult season does not override the countless ways God has already shown up for you.
