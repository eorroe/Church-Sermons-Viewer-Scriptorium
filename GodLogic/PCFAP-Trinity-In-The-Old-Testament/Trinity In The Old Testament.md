# Trinity In The Old Testament

## Overview

This sermon demonstrates that the doctrine of the Trinity is deeply rooted in the Old Testament, not a later invention of Paul, Constantine, or the New Testament. By systematically working through Genesis, Job, Psalms, and Isaiah, it shows that the Father, the Word (Son), and the Holy Spirit are all identified as the one Creator, that God is addressed in plural terms in the Hebrew, and that the Angel of the Lord repeatedly claims divine identity and receives worship. The intent is to equip believers to confidently present and defend the Trinity in dialogues with Muslims, Jehovah's Witnesses, Mormons, and Unitarians using only Old Testament scripture.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need to defend the Trinity in a dialogue with a Muslim who claims the doctrine was invented centuries after Moses
- When you need to explain the deity of Christ to a Jehovah's Witness using only the Old Testament
- When you need to respond to a Mormon who challenges the plurality of God in the Hebrew Scriptures
- When you need to answer a Unitarian who argues that singular pronouns for God in the Old Testament disprove the Trinity
- When you want to learn how to present Isaiah 48:12-16 or Isaiah 63:7-11 as Trinitarian texts
- When you need to explain who the "Arm of the Lord" is in Isaiah 53 to someone who holds a Jewish, Islamic, or JW view

## Steps

### Step 1: Establish that Creation is Attributed to Three Distinct Persons

Show that the Father, the Word, and the Holy Spirit are each credited as Creator in the Old Testament, yet there is only one God. Reference Psalm 104:29-30 (the Spirit creates and renews the ground), Job 33:4 ("The Spirit of God has made me"), Deuteronomy 32:6 (Yahweh is the Father who created you), and John 1:1-3 (the Word was with God and the Word was God, and all things were made through him). Note that John 1 is the only New Testament reference used in this sermon. Conclude that Father, Spirit, and Word are all identified as Creator while Deuteronomy 6:4 affirms one divine essence.

### Step 2: Show That God Is Referred to in Plural Terms in Hebrew

Demonstrate that the Hebrew language itself contains plural references to God as Creator. Reference Job 35:10, where the Hebrew word *oay* ("my makers") is the plural form of *osa* ("maker"), and Psalm 149:2, which uses the same plural form ("Let Israel be glad in his makers"). Explain that English translations often obscure this plural language, so use an interlinear Bible, Logos, or similar tool to verify the original Hebrew. This shows that God is simultaneously singular in essence and plural in person.

### Step 3: Examine the Angel of the Lord in Genesis 28

Read Genesis 28:10-22, where Jacob has his dream of the ladder. Note that Yahweh stands at the top of the ladder and identifies himself as "I am Yahweh, the God of your father Abraham and the God of Isaac" (verse 13). Observe that Jacob names the place Bethel ("house of God") and makes a vow to Yahweh. This establishes that the figure who appears to Jacob is identified as Yahweh himself.

### Step 4: Show the Angel of the Lord Claims Yahweh Identity in Genesis 31

Read Genesis 31:11-13, where the angel of God speaks to Jacob in a dream: "I have seen all that Laban has been doing to you. I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed a pillar, where you made a vow to me." The angel speaks in the first person as the one to whom Jacob made his vow—the same Yahweh from Genesis 28. Ask the rhetorical question: Who is the only one able to receive sacrifices, altar-building, and worship? The answer is Yahweh alone.

### Step 5: Show That God Himself Commands Worship of the Angel of the Lord in Genesis 35

Read Genesis 35:1: "Then God said to Jacob, arise, go up to Bethel and live there and make an altar there to God who appeared to you when you fled from your brother, Esau." The Father (God) commands Jacob to build an altar to the Angel of the Lord, identifying the Angel as God Almighty worthy of worship. This establishes the Angel of the Lord as Yahweh himself, because only Yahweh can receive altar worship. Draw the parallel to Hebrews 1:8, where the Father calls the Son "God."

### Step 6: Observe Jacob Praying to the Angel of the Lord in Genesis 48

Read Genesis 48:15-16. As Jacob blesses Joseph's sons, he says: "The angel who has redeemed me from evil, bless these boys." Jacob, a patriarch and prophet of God, prays to the Angel of the Lord as the one who kept him, redeemed him, and has the power to bless his descendants. Ask the question: Why is a patriarch praying to an angel as if that angel can create life and command the seed of Israel? The answer is that Jacob knew the Angel of the Lord was not a created angel but Yahweh himself.

### Step 7: Examine Isaiah 48:12-16 for Plurality Within Yahweh

Read Isaiah 48:12-16. Note that no speaker change occurs—Yahweh speaks throughout. Verse 12: "Hear me, O Jacob, even Israel, whom I called. I am he. I am the first, I am also the last." Verse 16: "And now the Lord Yahweh has sent me and his spirit." Highlight that within the single speaker (Yahweh), there is Yahweh the speaker, Yahweh who sends, and the spirit of Yahweh who is also sent. This is plurality within the one God—Yahweh the speaker refers to Yahweh who sends him together with the spirit of Yahweh who is also sent.

### Step 8: Examine Isaiah 63:7-11 for Distinct Persons in Salvation

Read Isaiah 63:7-11. Verse 9: "In all their distress, he was distressed, and the angel of his presence saved them." Verse 9b: "In his love and in his mercy he redeemed them; he lifted them and carried them all the ancient days. But they rebelled and grieved his Holy Spirit." Note that Yahweh, the Angel of His Presence, and the Holy Spirit are each personally active in the one act of saving Israel, and the Holy Spirit's ability to be grieved demonstrates his personhood. Verse 11: "Where is he who put the Holy Spirit in the midst of them? Who caused his glorious arm to go at the right hand of Moses?"

### Step 9: Identify the "Arm of the Lord" as the Pre-Incarnate Christ

Read Isaiah 53:1: "Who has believed our report, and to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?" Read the surrounding verses of Isaiah 53, which describe the suffering servant—"He grew up before him like a tender shoot... He was despised and forsaken of men... He bore our griefs." Connect this to Isaiah 63, where the "arm of the Lord" was the one who split the Red Sea and saved Israel from Egypt. The same "arm of the Lord" who acted in the Exodus is the suffering Messiah of Isaiah 53—none other than the pre-incarnate Christ, Jesus.

### Step 10: Reference Jude 5 to Confirm Jesus as Yahweh

Read Jude 4-5. Verse 5: "Now I desire to remind you, though you know all things, that Jesus having once saved a people out of the land of Egypt subsequently destroyed those who did not believe." This confirms that Jesus—not a created being, not merely "the Father"—is the one who redeemed Israel from Egypt. This aligns with Isaiah 63 (the Angel of His Presence) and the Old Testament witness that Yahweh saved Israel. Some manuscripts say "Lord" instead of "Jesus," but verse 4 identifies Jesus as "our only Master and Lord," so either reading confirms the same point.

### Step 11: Answer the Objection of Singular Pronouns for God

When a Unitarian argues that God is referred to with singular pronouns repeatedly throughout the Old Testament, show Isaiah 44:4-5, where Yahweh says, "I alone stretched out the heavens, by myself." If singular pronouns prove God can only be one person, then Unitarians face a contradiction: they believe Jesus was the agent through whom the Father created all things, yet Isaiah 44:4 says Yahweh did it alone. Turn their argument against them by showing it actually contradicts their own view of Jesus' role in creation. Also show places where God speaks with plural language, demonstrating that the Old Testament itself presents God as both singular and plural.

### Step 12: Answer the Objection of Jesus Not Knowing the Hour (Mark 13:32)

Explain that the Greek word *oiden* in Mark 13:32 ("no one knows the hour, not even the Son") can mean "to make known" or "declare" as well as "to know." Use 1 Corinthians 2:2, where Paul says, "I decided to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ and him crucified"—Paul did not lack all knowledge, he simply chose to declare nothing else. Also reference Luke 20:1-8, where Jesus says, "Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things"—Jesus is not ignorant of his authority, he is withholding the declaration. Finally, compare to Acts 1:7, where Jesus says, "It is not for you to know the times or seasons which the Father has fixed by his own authority." Jesus' refusal to declare the hour is an act of humility and submission to the Father's authority, not a statement of ignorance. He knows his own wedding day; the Father alone announces it.

### Step 13: Answer the Objection of Isaiah 9:6 — "Everlasting Father"

When a Oneness Pentecostal or Unitarian claims that Isaiah 9:6 ("Everlasting Father") identifies the Son as the Father, note three responses: First, ask "Who gave the Son?" Isaiah 9:6 says "a son is given"—for something to be given, there must be at least two parties: a giver and a receiver. The Father and the Son are therefore distinct. Second, explain that "Everlasting Father" (*Aviad*) is a title describing a quality of the Son, not a statement of identity. Abraham is called "father of nations" without being every person's literal biological father. Similarly, the Son is called "Everlasting Father" as the source of eternal life, not as an identity conflation with the heavenly Father. Third, ask "Is the Father a prince?" Isaiah 9:6 also calls the Son "Prince of Peace"—if titles prove identity, then the Father is also a prince and the prince of peace, which is absurd.

### Step 14: Warn Against the Mind/Body/Spirit Analogy

Explain that comparing the Trinity to the human parts of mind, body, and spirit is a flawed analogy that falls into two heresies: partialism (each person is only a part of God) and modalism (the three modes of a single person). The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are each fully God—not parts of God, not modes of one person, but three distinct persons who share the same divine essence. This analogy should not be used.

## Examples

### Example 1: Presenting the Creation Argument to a Muslim or Unitarian

Begin by asking: "Can you name anyone besides God who created?" The answer is no. Then show Psalm 104:29-30 (the Spirit creates), Job 33:4 (the Spirit of God made me), John 1:1-3 (the Word created all things), and Deuteronomy 32:6 (Yahweh, the Father, created you). There is only one Creator—yet three distinct persons are named as Creator. Ask: "Who created all things?" They will say God alone. Then show the texts naming Father, Word, and Spirit. There is one divine essence shared by three distinct persons—this is the Trinity in the Old Testament.

### Example 2: Using the Angel of the Lord with a Jehovah's Witness

Read Genesis 31:11-13. The angel says, "I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed a pillar, where you made a vow to me." Ask: "Who is the only one who can receive altar sacrifices and worship?" Only Yahweh. Then read Genesis 35:1, where God himself commands Jacob to build an altar to "God who appeared to you"—the Angel of the Lord. If only Yahweh can receive worship, and God commands worship of the Angel, then the Angel of the Lord is Yahweh. Ask: "Who does the Angel of the Lord remind you of in the New Testament?"—the Son, who speaks with the Father's authority, receives worship, and claims divine identity.

### Example 3: Explaining Isaiah 48:12-16 to a Unitarian

Read Isaiah 48:12-16 aloud. Verse 12: "I am he, I am the first, I am also the last." (Yahweh speaking.) Verse 16: "And now the Lord Yahweh has sent me and his spirit." Ask: "How many are in this verse?" One: Yahweh the speaker. Two: Yahweh who sends. Three: the spirit of Yahweh who is sent. The Old Testament itself contains plurality within the one God. This is not imported from the New Testament; it is woven through the prophets.

### Example 4: Explaining the Arm of the Lord to a Muslim

Read Isaiah 63:7-11, where the Angel of His Presence and the Holy Spirit both are identified as the ones who saved Israel. Then read Isaiah 53:1: "To whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?" The "arm of the Lord" who split the Red Sea is the same figure described in Isaiah 53 as the suffering servant—despised and forsaken of men, bearing sin. This is not an impersonal force; it is a person. Jude 5 confirms: "Jesus having once saved a people out of the land of Egypt." The one who saved Israel in the Old Testament is the Lord Jesus Christ.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Start with the Old Testament to avoid the claim that the Trinity is a New Testament invention
- ✅ Let the Old Testament texts build the case cumulatively rather than quoting isolated verses
- ✅ Use Hebrew interlinear tools (Logos, Bible software, interlinear Bibles) to expose plural language for God that English translations obscure
- ✅ Ask rhetorical questions during dialogue to lead the other person to the conclusion
- ✅ Learn these passages well so they become second nature in gospel and apologetic conversations
- ✅ Use Isaiah 9:6 ("Everlasting Father") to show that titles describe qualities, not identity
- ✅ Distinguish the "Arm of the Lord" as a person, not a force, by reading Isaiah 53 in context
- ❌ Do not use the mind/body/spirit analogy to explain the Trinity—it leads to partialism and modalism
- ❌ Do not let Unitarians use singular pronouns to defeat the Trinity—turn Isaiah 44:4 back against their own view
- ❌ Do not reduce the Angel of the Lord to a created angel—he claims Yahweh identity, receives altar worship, and is called God
- ❌ Do not claim Jesus "didn't know" the hour—explain Mark 13:32 as Christ's humble submission to the Father's authority to declare it

## Keep In Mind

- The Old Testament does not contain a single verse that says "God is three persons in one essence"—the doctrine is built from a pattern of texts, just as doctrines about the atonement or resurrection are
- Plural language for God in the Old Testament is real and verifiable in the Hebrew, not a New Testament imposition
- The Angel of the Lord is a unique figure—he is called Yahweh, he receives worship, he makes covenants, and he is identified as the God of Bethel; he is not a created angel and not a mere prophet
- Isaiah 48:12-16 is not a proof text taken out of context; no speaker change occurs, and the plurality is within the single speaker (Yahweh)
- "Everlasting Father" in Isaiah 9:6 is a title/quality, not a statement that the Son is the Father; a "son is given"—which requires two distinct persons
- The "Arm of the Lord" in Isaiah 63 and 53 is the same figure—a person, not power—and that person is the pre-incarnate Christ

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A Unitarian points to singular pronouns for God throughout the Old Testament and claims the Trinity is unbiblical.
  **Solution:** Show Isaiah 44:4, where Yahweh says, "I alone created the heavens and the earth, I stretched out the heavens by myself." If singular pronouns prove Unitarianism, this contradicts the Unitarian's own belief that Jesus was the agent of creation. Also show passages where God uses plural language (Job 35:10, Psalm 149:2) so the Unitarian cannot claim the Old Testament presents only strict monadic singularity.

- **Problem:** A Muslim or Unitarian claims Jesus is ignorant because Mark 13:32 says "no one knows the hour, not even the Son."
  **Solution:** Explain that the Greek word *oiden* can mean "to declare or make known," not just "to know." Reference 1 Corinthians 2:2 (Paul "knew nothing" except Christ) and Luke 20:1-8 (Jesus says "neither will I tell you"—he is not ignorant of his authority, he is withholding it). Reference Acts 1:7, where Jesus says the Father alone has fixed the times by his own authority—Jesus submits to the Father's prerogative to announce the hour, not his own ignorance of it.

- **Problem:** A Oneness Pentecostal argues Isaiah 9:6 ("Everlasting Father") means the Son is the Father, collapsing the distinction.
  **Solution:** Ask "Who gave the Son?" Something given requires two parties—a giver and a receiver—so the Father and Son are distinct. Explain that "Everlasting Father" is a title of quality (source of eternal life), not identity, just as "Prince of Peace" is a title. If titles prove identity, the Father would also have to be the Prince of Peace, which is absurd. Isaiah 9:6 explicitly says "a son is given," not "a father is given."

- **Problem:** Someone uses the mind/body/spirit analogy to explain the Trinity, falling into partialism or modalism.
  **Solution:** Explain that mind, body, and spirit are parts of a single human person—if applied to God, this would mean the Father, Son, and Spirit are only parts of God, not fully God (partialism). Alternatively, if they are modes of a single person appearing in different forms, this is modalism. The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are each fully God, distinct persons who share one divine essence. This analogy should be abandoned.

- **Problem:** A Muslim or JW reduces the "Arm of the Lord" to an impersonal force or power of God, missing the Christological identification.
  **Solution:** Show that Isaiah 53 explicitly calls the "Arm of the Lord" a person—"He grew up before him... He was despised and forsaken of men... He bore our griefs." Connect to Isaiah 63, where the same "Arm of the Lord" saved Israel from Egypt, and to Jude 5, which identifies Jesus as the one who did this. The "Arm of the Lord" is a personal title for the pre-incarnate Christ.
