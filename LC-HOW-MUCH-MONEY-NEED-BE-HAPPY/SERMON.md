# How Much Money Do You Need to Be Happy?

## Overview

This sermon by Pastor Chris from Life.Church explores the question of how much wealth is enough to find happiness — being "rich toward God" (Luke 12:21) rather than trapped in the pursuit of "just a little bit more." The phrase "rich toward God" means having your heart oriented toward Him, storing up eternal investments rather than earthly treasures. Drawing on biblical teachings from Luke 12, Matthew 6, Psalm 24, Malachi 3, and Proverbs 20, it challenges the lie that more possessions equal more happiness. The message calls listeners to recognize they are already wealthy compared to the three billion people on planet earth living on two dollars a day, adopt a steward mindset instead of an owner mindset, practice tithing, and invest their time, talent, and money in eternal kingdom purposes rather than temporary earthly things.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel trapped in the endless pursuit of "just a little bit more" income or possessions
- When you struggle to trust God with your finances or feel resistant to giving
- When you want to shift from a consumer mindset to a generosity lifestyle
- When you're discerning your next step of faith in the area of money and stewardship
- When you need biblical perspective on wealth, contentment, and what it truly means to be "rich toward God"

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize You Are Already Rich

Compare your daily problems to those living on two dollars a day — the three billion people on planet earth living on that income. A refrigerator full of food you do not feel like eating, a streaming service that buffers, or an Amazon package taking three days instead of two are all problems that would not exist for someone surviving on that income. God has already blessed you far beyond what most of the world experiences. Acknowledge this honestly instead of minimizing it. Refuse to make excuses for the ways God has blessed you, just as you would never apologize for a blessed marriage or healthy children. This acknowledgment is the starting point for true stewardship.

### Step 2: Shift Your Mindset From Owner to Steward

Psalm 24:1 declares, "The earth is the Lord's, and everything in it." Everything you have — your home, your car, your income, even your own body — belongs to God. You do not own these things; you manage them on His behalf. Ask yourself honestly: do you live as though what you have is yours to own? Begin every day by reminding yourself that you are a steward, not an owner. This mental shift is the foundation for every other step of financial faithfulness.

### Step 3: Return the First Tenth — Practice the Tithe

Bring the first 10% of your income back to God (Malachi 3:10). The word "bring" is intentional — you do not give what is yours; you return what already belongs to God. The tithe is not about the amount; it is about the order. God must be first in your finances, just as He is meant to be first in your day and your week. When you return the tithe before anything else, you declare God's ownership over the rest. This act of obedience is the point where you are stuck in your faith until you learn to trust God in this area, but it is also the point where God changes your mind about what belongs to Him.

### Step 4: Invest in Heaven More Than You Spend on Earth

Matthew 6:19-21 teaches that every moment of every day you have a choice: spend what God has given you, or invest it in something eternal. Your time, your talent, and your money can all be directed toward heaven. Spend time scrolling Netflix and nothing eternal happens; invest that same time praying for your teenage kids or your unsaved boss and you bank an eternal yield. Spend influence building your own brand or invest it making the name of Jesus famous. Spend money on an unnecessary consumer purchase that serves no eternal purpose, or use that same money to pay a single mom's rent. Choose investment over consumption.

### Step 5: Live to Give, Not Live to Get

Make generosity your lifestyle, not a sporadic event. Proverbs 20:22 says, "He who has a generous eye will be blessed." Train your eyes to look for what need you could meet, what ministry you could serve, and where you could be a blessing. This is how you were designed to live — as a conduit of God's blessing, not a collector of things. The blessing is meant to come to you and then flow through you to others.

### Step 6: View Giving as Ministry, Not Philanthropy

Any spiritual gift exercised in alignment with the agenda of heaven is an act of ministry, not philanthropy. Giving is not simply writing a check and receiving a thank-you; it is the exercise of a spiritual gift that should be nurtured, celebrated, and grown. When you give — whether the first 10% of your income or your entire estate — to launch a new church campus, fund missions, or support the local body, you are doing kingdom work. Your generosity becomes part of someone else's story of coming to faith.

### Step 7: Take the Next Step of Faith

Do not wait until you feel ready. If you have never tithed, return the tithe. If you have never fully surrendered your life to Jesus, that comes first — you cannot be a faithful steward until you first belong to God. Call out to Jesus, ask Him to forgive your sin, and surrender your whole life to Him. Then take the concrete step of returning the first 10% of your income to God through your local church. Faith is demonstrated in the moment you act even when your bills are staring back at you. God invites you into the greatest adventure of all time — being written into His story.

## Examples

### Example 1: Pastor Chris and Cindy's Journey With the Tithe

Pastor Chris and his wife Cindy began tithing 26 years ago when they were called into ministry. He was reluctant, skeptical, and even felt like it was a "church scam" designed to extract money from people. He gave out of disobedience, not excitement. But over time, the act of returning the first 10% shifted his mindset entirely. What began as a reluctant act of obedience became a family culture of God's ownership. Today, every financial decision is filtered through the lens of stewardship, and their family has experienced the fruit of living in divine order.

### Example 2: Carrie and Bear's Legacy of Generosity

Carrie and Bear, members of the Legacy Team at Life.Church's South Oklahoma City campus, had no children of their own. They saw their church family as their family, and they chose to name Life.Church as the sole beneficiary of their entire estate — 100% of everything — so that leveraging what God had blessed them with could reach even one more person for the gospel, creating a return that outlasted their own lives. Their generosity will fund future campuses and ministries long after they are gone, continuing to write them into God's story.

### Example 3: The Travel Lesson: Currency That Is Worthless at Home

On a family trip abroad, Pastor Chris's sons exchanged all their American dollars for foreign pesos. They had a wonderful week, but when they returned home, the pesos were worthless. This illustrated a spiritual reality: the things you spend your life obsessing over and collecting may have temporary value in this world but carry no weight in eternity. The question is not whether you can enjoy what God has given you, but whether what you accumulate will matter when you stand before Him.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Acknowledge your blessings honestly rather than minimizing them — it is the gateway to gratitude and stewardship
- ✅ Return the tithe before any other expenses — honor the order God intended
- ✅ View everything you have as God's property entrusted to your care, not as your personal possession
- ✅ Choose eternal investments over temporary spending with your time, influence, and money
- ✅ Make generosity a regular lifestyle practice, not a reactive event triggered by guilt or emotion
- ✅ Celebrate giving as a spiritual gift and an act of ministry within your community of faith
- ✅ Take the next step of faith now — do not wait until you feel fully ready or have it all figured out

## Keep In Mind

- Wealth is a moving target. The moment you reach a financial goal, the bar will move again. Contentment comes when you accept your lot and are happy in your work and toil — a gift from God (Ecclesiastes 5) — not in accumulating more.
- God is not mad that you are rich — He is concerned when what you possess becomes the thing you trust instead of the God who put it in your hands (Luke 12:21).
- Riches create a spiritual disadvantage when they become what you trust rather than the God who gave them.
- You were created to be a conduit, not a collector. Blessing is meant to come to you and then flow through you to others.
- Generosity given today can create campuses where people will be invited, hands will go up, and people will come to faith long after you are gone — as Carrie and Bear's estate gift will do.
- What God invites you to is an adventure, not a demand. He does not need your money — He invites you to have a part in His story.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Giving should always go through your local church or established, trusted ministry channels — never send money in response to unsolicited outreach, urgent emotional appeals, or unverified individuals.
- Before making large estate or legacy decisions, consult a qualified attorney and financial advisor who understands both legal and spiritual stewardship.
- Be cautious of any teaching that pressures giving through fear, guilt, or manipulation. Generosity should flow from a willing heart, not coercion.
- Protect your personal and financial information when engaging with any ministry or charitable organization online.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Comparing your wealth only to those who have more
- **Solution:** Compare your blessings to the three billion people on planet earth living on two dollars a day before desiring more.
- **Problem:** Treating the tithe as a "give" rather than a "bring" — seeing it as losing something rather than returning what belongs to God
  **Solution:** Reframe the tithe as worship and stewardship. You are not losing income; you are acknowledging rightful ownership and building your life in God's divine order.
- **Problem:** Spending everything on earth-based comforts and entertainment, leaving nothing invested in eternal causes
  **Solution:** Audit your time and money. For every hour spent on temporary entertainment, ask whether that same hour could be invested in prayer, service, or reaching someone for Christ.
- **Problem:** Viewing generosity as philanthropy — a nice thing wealthy people do — rather than a spiritual gift and act of ministry
  **Solution:** Recognize that every gift given in alignment with heaven's agenda is ministry. Celebrate generosity in your church community as a spiritual discipline.
- **Problem:** Delaying obedience until you feel "ready" or until you have more money, more time, or more confidence
  **Solution:** Take the step of returning the tithe today. If you have never tithed, return the first 10% of your income. If you have never surrendered to Jesus, surrender to Him first. Faith grows in the acting, not the waiting.
