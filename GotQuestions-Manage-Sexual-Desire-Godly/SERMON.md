# Manage Sexual Desire in a Godly Way

## Overview

This sermon teaches unmarried believers how to honor God with their sexuality by practically managing sexual desire through spiritual disciplines, self-control, and behavioral changes. It affirms that sexual drive is a God-given gift, not inherently sinful, while calling Christians to channel that desire toward godly purposes: serving others in tangible ministry such as serving at church, helping those in need, or discipling other believers, pursuing personal holiness as defined in 1 Peter 1:15-16, honoring God in your thoughts, words, and bodily behavior, and reserving its physical expression for the marriage covenant between one man and one woman. The goal is to honor God with your body and mind, not to live in guilt.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When an unmarried Christian struggles with managing sexual temptation or tension
- When seeking biblically rooted guidance for sexual purity
- When learning to redirect sexual energy into ministry, discipleship, and service to others
- When preparing for marriage or seeking to submit your body and mind to Christ

## Steps

### Step 1: Appreciate Your God-Given Sexuality

Gratefully accept your sexual nature and the purposes God has designed for it within marriage. Do not deny that you have sexual feelings, repress them into shame, or feel guilty about their existence—they are normal for human beings and part of how God designed you. Offer your sexuality to God by dedicating your body, mind, and desires to Him as a living sacrifice (Romans 12:1), refusing to use them for self-gratification outside of marriage or for any sinful purpose, and trusting Him to guide you in honoring Him with your whole person—body, mind, and spirit (1 Thessalonians 5:23). Redirect your energies into ministry, discipleship, and service for the Lord, and seek to glorify Him in your choices and actions.

### Step 2: Cultivate a Mindset That Honors God

Make honoring God and exercising self-control your aim, even with your imagination. Recognize that secular media content—including movies, television, music, advertising, and social media—overemphasizes sexuality and promotes instant self-gratification rather than self-control. Practice self-discipline through daily prayer and regular Scripture reading, putting on the spiritual armor described in Ephesians 6:10-18: the belt of truth, the breastplate of righteousness, the shoes of the gospel of peace, the shield of faith, the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit which is the Word of God, combined with prayer, and trusting Jesus to fight the battle for you.

### Step 3: Yield to the Holy Spirit's Power

Remember that the Holy Spirit dwells within every Christian. Your body is God's temple: the Holy Spirit dwells within every Christian, making your body a holy place belonging to God (2 Corinthians 6:16; 1 Corinthians 6:19). Submit your desires to the Spirit and resist temptation by praying specifically for the Spirit's guidance and self-control, submitting every tempting thought to Him in prayer the moment it arises, asking Him to overcome it, and walking in His strength rather than relying on your own willpower to resist.

### Step 4: Follow Jesus' Example of Self-Discipline

Jesus was "tempted in every way, just as we are—yet he did not sin" (Hebrews 4:15), which means He governed His human desires—including sexual desire—always for the glory of God and never for selfish gratification, as Hebrews 4:15 teaches. He directed all His energies toward serving the needs of others through tangible ministry. Serve alongside Christ by ministering to people in tangible, specific ways—at your local church, in your community, and in your personal relationships—and offer yourself as an instrument of His love, compassion, and truth to the people around you.

### Step 5: Redirect Your Thoughts and Body When Urges Intensify

When a sexual urge grows stronger, immediately redirect your thoughts to something else and reduce the intensity of the desire by removing both the mental fuel (lustful thoughts or fantasies) and the physical fuel (lingering in bed for more than 10 minutes after waking, being alone in a private space, or viewing content that triggers sexual desire). Specifically: remove yourself from the triggering situation within seconds, engage in physical activity: specifically, exercise: running, lifting weights, taking a brisk walk, doing push-ups, or any physical activity that raises your heart rate, pray immediately, or speak with a trusted believer—any concrete action that shifts your focus away from the urge so you can think rationally and make a choice that brings glory to God rather than gratifying selfish desire.

### Step 6: Ruthlessly Avoid Sexual Stimulation Outside of Marriage

Be intentional about what you watch, read, and think. Both men and women should avoid visual media—including photographs, videos, artwork, or illustrations—that depicts nudity or sexual activity, as visual stimuli trigger sexual desire in both men and women. Both men and women are affected by visual stimuli—images that depict nudity or sexual activity—which trigger sexual desire, and both should eliminate sexual stimulation outside of marriage—defined as anything that intentionally or recklessly arouses sexual desire outside of marriage: including pornography, sexually explicit content in any media, media depicting romantic or sexual intimacy that triggers lustful thoughts, and lingering thoughts that stir desire once recognized.

### Step 7: Guard Your Eyes and Heart Against Lust

Follow Jesus' command not to look at anyone with lustful intent (Matthew 5:28). Follow Job's example: "I made a covenant with my eyes not to look lustfully at a young woman" (Job 31:1). Practice chastity by training your mind, thoughts, imagination, and eyes to refrain from lustful looking and lustful thinking.

### Step 8: Get Up When You Wake Up

For people who struggle with lust, lying in bed while drowsy and mentally unoccupied opens the door to sexual arousal and increases temptation. When you wake up in the morning, get up immediately. If you struggle with nighttime temptation in bed, apply the same principle: do not linger in bed when arousal begins—within seconds, get up, leave the room, and engage in a concrete different activity: reading Scripture aloud, praying immediately, calling a trusted Christian friend, or doing physical exercise.

### Step 9: Work Vigorously

Work with your full physical effort during the day. Physical exertion during the day helps you fall asleep quickly at night, reducing the amount of time you spend mentally unoccupied in bed and therefore vulnerable to temptation.

### Step 10: Manage Your Mind with God's Truth

Fill your mind with what is true, noble, right, pure, lovely, and admirable (Philippians 4:8). Intentionally choose thoughts that Philippians 4:8 describes as true, noble, right, pure, lovely, and admirable—specifically: thoughts about God's Word, about serving others, about nature and creation, or about good and honest things—thoughts that crowd out sexual cravings.

### Step 11: Channel Sexual Energy Into Meaningful Service

Sublimate sexual energy into service for others in Christ's name by intentionally redirecting the physical and emotional energy of sexual desire into specific, active, God-glorifying pursuits—specifically: serving at your local church through ministry to others, investing in a discipleship relationship with a newer believer, doing physical labor including yard work or exercise, or engaging in creative work: specifically, writing, painting, drawing, playing a musical instrument, composing music, woodworking, gardening, or creative work such as writing, art, or music—so that the energy that would fuel lust instead fuels love and service to others. Channel it into specific projects and activities that occupy your mind and leave no room for lustful thoughts.

### Step 12: Keep Your Focus on Marriage and Its Godly Purpose

Prepare for the responsibilities of marriage and family by studying Scripture on marriage (Ephesians 5:21-33), keeping your attention on the peace with God, freedom from guilt, and joy in obeying Him that come from sexual purity and self-control. Understand that marriage is not primarily about satisfying sexual desire—it is about Christ-centered partnership, mutual submission, and building a family that honors God (Ephesians 5:21-33)—so do not rush into marriage merely to satisfy sexual tension.

### Step 13: Replace Lustful Imagination with Pure Memories

Use your imagination and memory to recall physical sights, sounds, smells, feelings, and tastes experienced in ways that bring no guilt or shame: the sight of God's creation in nature, the words of a beloved hymn, the smell of fresh bread, the memory of a warm embrace from a family member, or the taste of a meal God has provided. Choose to fill your mind with memories that Philippians 4:8 describes as true, noble, right, pure, lovely, and admirable—memories you can reflect on without guilt or shame—rather than lustful ones.

### Step 14: Change Your Environment When Urges Intensify

When a sexual urge intensifies, change your environment immediately—ideally within seconds of recognizing the temptation. Safe environments include: taking a walk with a Christian friend you trust, going to a public space—specifically a coffee shop, a library, or your local church building during scheduled activities—joining a group activity at church or in your community, or stepping outside for fresh air. Do not remain in the same room or situation where the temptation began.

### Step 15: Develop a Hands-On Hobby

Cultivate a hobby that requires active use of your hands and mind—specifically: woodworking, playing a musical instrument, exercising, serving at your church, or doing volunteer work—so that your hands and mind are occupied in ways that bring glory to God and are not sexual in nature.

### Step 16: Identify and Avoid Triggers

Take note of what triggers your sexual urges, and avoid those triggers. If certain lustful thoughts consistently follow a specific activity—specifically: browsing websites that depict sexual activity outside marriage, watching shows with sexual content, or being alone in a private place late at night—stop engaging in that activity and replace it with an alternative that honors God: exercising, reading Scripture, calling a trusted Christian friend, or going to a public space: specifically your local church during scheduled activities or a coffee shop. Do what you can to change the situation, routine, or relationship that consistently triggers the desire, replacing it with a different routine or environment.

### Step 17: Talk to God About Your Struggles

Share every temptation, urge, and feeling with the Lord Jesus in prayer. Prayerfully bring your temptations before Him and trust Him with your weaknesses.

### Step 18: Confess Sin Immediately and Receive Forgiveness

If you do sin, confess it to Christ without delay and receive His forgiveness by honestly acknowledging your sin before God (1 John 1:9), turning away from it through repentance, and trusting in the finished work of Jesus Christ on the cross—not your own efforts—to cleanse you from all unrighteousness. Do not let shame control you. Thank God that Christ already died for all sin on the cross, that God's love for you is based on His character not your performance, and His mercy is new every morning (Lamentations 3:22-23). Ask God for victory, relying on prayer, Scripture memorization, the Holy Spirit's power, Christian community, and the specific passages of Ephesians 6:10-18, 1 Peter 5:6-11, and James 4:7-8 as His means of victory.

### Step 19: Pursue Accountability With Other Believers

Cultivate close, honest relationships and accountability with other Christians who are grounded in Scripture, accountable to church leadership, and have demonstrated the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23). Ask them to pray for you and be available for weekly conversations, quick texts when temptation strikes, or meeting in person—so they can encourage you with Scripture, hold you accountable by asking about your temptations, and walk with you through the battle for sexual purity.

### Step 20: Acknowledge Struggles to a Trusted Leader

Confide in someone you can trust, specifically your pastor, a mature Christian leader at your church, or a trained Christian counselor. You are not the first person to struggle with managing sexual nature. Hiding your temptation and sin from a pastor or mature Christian who is accountable to church leadership: by concealing the truth about your temptation, by faking spiritual maturity by pretending you have no temptation, and by acting hypocritically by presenting yourself as blameless to others while secretly sinning will each damage your character, undermine your witness, and ultimately destroy your integrity. If you do slip, your confidante can support you with prayer, exhortation, and encouraging guidance.

### Step 21: Be Patient and Persistent in the Process

Realize that striving for obedience in managing sexual desire is difficult and does not produce immediate results; it requires sustained, daily effort. Practice self-control—the ability to govern your desires by the Holy Spirit's power—through the specific means described in this document. As 1 Corinthians 9:26-27 teaches, discipline your body and keep it under control so that you will not be disqualified from the eternal reward God has prepared for those who faithfully follow Him.

### Step 22: Use Music to Shift Your Mind

When you feel sexual cravings, listen to music with lyrics that align with Philippians 4:8—specifically hymns like "Amazing Grace," worship songs about God's holiness and love, or music from Christian artists whose lyrics are rooted in Scripture and promote godly living—to redirect your thoughts and lift your heart to the Lord.

### Step 23: Take It One Day at a Time

Depend on God's grace daily and keep your eyes on Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith (Hebrews 12:2). Trust God for the strength you need right now, taking today's challenges one moment at a time.

### Step 24: Consider Marriage If Control Is Not Possible

If you find yourself consistently unable to control your sexual urges despite prayer, discipline, and accountability for a period of 3 to 6 months—and you have sought wise counsel from mature, spiritually grounded believers—it is good and right to marry, specifically when your motive for marrying is to flee ongoing, unrepentant temptation and to build a Christ-centered partnership where both spouses prioritize following Christ together, love each other as Christ loved the church, and build their relationship on the Bible's teachings about marriage, marriage will provide the God-honoring context for sexual expression (1 Corinthians 7:8-9) to help you flee from ongoing, unrepentant temptation rather than merely escaping personal discomfort, and you are prepared to love and serve your spouse sacrificially. Paul writes, "It is better to marry than to burn with passion" (1 Corinthians 7:8-9). Singleness is a good gift, and so is marriage. However, do not rush into marriage merely to satisfy sexual desire—study God's design for marriage in Ephesians 5:21-33 and understand sex within marriage so you build a strong, biblically grounded relationship with your future spouse.

## Examples

### Example 1: A Single Young Man Battling Visual Temptation

A young unmarried man finds his sexual desire intensified by social media and movies. He applies these steps by deleting apps that trigger lust, scheduling morning workouts to avoid lying in bed, listening to worship music when cravings hit, and meeting weekly with an accountability partner. He experiences greater peace and self-control.

### Example 2: A Single Young Woman Managing Emotional Triggers

A single woman realizes her sexual urges are regularly triggered by certain romantic conversations or by media that depicts sexual activity outside marriage. She begins to avoid those conversations, redirects her energy into serving at church and volunteering, changes her environment by calling a friend when she feels tempted, and confides in her pastor for ongoing guidance.

### Example 3: Repentance and Recovery After a Fall

A believer struggles with recurring sin and feels burdened by shame. After reading these instructions, he confesses his sin to Christ that same day, receives His forgiveness, and opens up to a trusted Christian mentor. Together they identify triggers, establish new habits, and walk in the freedom and grace God provides.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Accept your sexuality as a God-given gift (Genesis 1:27-28), not something shameful or inherently sinful
- ✅ Redirect sexual energy into ministry, discipleship, physical labor, and creative work
- ✅ Be ruthless in eliminating unnecessary sexual stimulation—defined as any media, thought, or situation that intentionally or recklessly arouses sexual desire outside marriage—from your environment
- ✅ Confess sin without delay and receive God's forgiveness
- ✅ Cultivate honest accountability relationships with other believers
- ✅ Meditate on Philippians 4:8 and fill your mind with pure, godly content
- ✅ Take practical action—exercise, physical activity, change of environment—when urges intensify
- ❌ Do not repress or deny your sexual feelings—this is not the path to purity
- ❌ Do not rely solely on willpower without the Holy Spirit's power
- ❌ Do not hide your struggles or live in shame and hypocrisy
- ❌ Do not rush into marriage merely to satisfy sexual desire

## Keep In Mind

- Sexuality is God's good gift, not inherently sinful or dirty
- Self-control is a command, not a suggestion (1 Corinthians 9:24-27; 2 Peter 1:6)
- Wet dreams are involuntary physiological events that occur without conscious control and are not sinful, but they should not be used as an excuse for habitual sin in waking hours—specifically, repeated, intentional sinful behaviors—such as lust, pornography, or other sexual sins committed deliberately. Take responsibility for the thoughts and behaviors you can control, and seek God's grace for the areas you cannot.
- Sexual release through self-gratification outside of marriage reinforces lustful desire rather than providing the fulfillment God intends—peace with God, freedom from guilt, and joy in obeying Him for sexual expression within the marriage covenant
- Fulfillment in marriage comes from serving and giving yourself to your spouse (1 Corinthians 7:4-5), not from self-gratification
- Sexual purity is a lifelong calling for both unmarried and married believers (1 Corinthians 6:18; 1 Thessalonians 4:3-8; Hebrews 13:4)
- Marriage is a good gift, but it is not primarily about sex (Ephesians 5:21-33)—it is about Christ-centered partnership

## Security & Safety Notes

- Guard your heart and mind against sexual temptation by monitoring your media consumption (tracking what you watch, read, and browse online) and thought patterns (noticing when lustful thoughts arise, what triggers them, and how many times they occur each day or week—and setting specific, concrete boundaries: deleting triggering apps, using content filters on all devices, and redirecting your mind the moment an impure thought enters your mind
- Use accountability partners to help you stay safe and honest
- Do not share explicit details of temptation with those who are not trustworthy—defined as those who are grounded in Scripture, accountable to local church leadership, demonstrate the fruit of the Spirit in their own life (Galatians 5:22-23), and have demonstrated discretion, confidentiality, and sound biblical judgment in counseling others through specific, verified examples.
- Establish and maintain specific boundaries in every relationship: specifically, limits on physical contact: specifically, no hugging longer than 10 seconds or sitting on each other's laps, limits on private time alone together: specifically, meeting only in public or group settings, and limits on topics of conversation: specifically, avoiding sexually suggestive topics

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Feeling guilty about having sexual desires as an unmarried person
  **Solution:** Remember that sexual desire itself is not sin. God designed every human being with these drives (Genesis 1:27-28). The issue is not having the desire, but whether you submit it to God's design for its expression within marriage or practice self-control in singleness. Accept it as God's gift and submit it to His Word.

- **Problem:** Relying only on willpower to resist temptation
  **Solution:** Self-discipline is commanded by God, but it must be empowered by the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 9:24-27), not by your own willpower alone. Stay close to God through prayer and Scripture, put on His spiritual armor (Ephesians 6:10-18), and invite Him to direct your desires.

- **Problem:** Falling into sin and being consumed by shame
  **Solution:** Confess your specific sin immediately to Christ (1 John 1:9), receive His complete forgiveness, and walk in the freedom He provides. Shame is not from God (Romans 8:1). His mercy is new every morning (Lamentations 3:22-23).

- **Problem:** Hiding your temptation and sin from a trusted pastor or mature Christian and living a double life
  **Solution:** Open up to a trusted pastor or mature Christian. Concealing your temptation from those who can help you damages your character, while confession brings healing, support, and accountability.

- **Problem:** Considering marriage solely to escape sexual tension
  **Solution:** Marriage is a good gift from God (Hebrews 13:4), but rushing into it solely for sexual release creates a poor foundation. Understand God's design for marriage in Ephesians 5:21-33 first—Christ-centered partnership, mutual service, and building a family that honors Him—then pursue marriage with wisdom and with the right motives.
