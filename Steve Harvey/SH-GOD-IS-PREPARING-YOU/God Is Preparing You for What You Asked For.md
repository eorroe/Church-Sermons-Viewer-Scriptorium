# God Is Preparing You for What You Asked For

## Overview

This sermon teaches that every hardship, setback, and failure you experience is not random — it is God preparing you for the very thing you prayed for. Steve Harvey explains that you cannot appreciate success without first experiencing lack, and you cannot learn how to win without first learning how to lose. The core message is that the difficult seasons of life serve a divine purpose, and when you trust that your trials are part of a larger plan, you gain the resilience and gratitude needed to truly steward the blessings that follow.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are going through a difficult season and don't understand why
- When you feel like giving up on a dream or goal
- When you have experienced loss, failure, or disappointment
- When you need perspective on why things aren't happening as fast as you expected
- When you feel like your prayers aren't being answered
- When you are preparing to receive a major blessing or opportunity

## Steps

### Step 1: Accept That Tests and Hardships Are Part of the Process

Recognize that you cannot have dreams without tests. Everything you are going through right now is God preparing you for what you asked for. Hardships, setbacks, and missteps are not signs that you are on the wrong path — they are proof that you are being shaped for something greater. Do not resist or resent the process; instead, acknowledge that the difficulty has a purpose.

### Step 2: Understand That You Learn Through Failure

You do not learn anything when you are winning. Growth happens through failure. The only way to learn how to get up is to fall. The only way to learn how to get over is to be under. The only way to appreciate laughter is to experience crying. The only way to appreciate sunshine is to go through rain. Embrace failure as a necessary teacher rather than a final verdict.

### Step 3: Trust That Everything Happens in the Exact Order It Should

You may not like the order in which events are unfolding in your life. You may wish things had happened differently or sooner. But trust that God's timing and ordering of your life is intentional. The struggles you face today are building the character, wisdom, and appreciation required for the blessings ahead. Release the need to control the timeline and instead focus on what you are learning along the way.

### Step 4: Develop a Mindset of Gratitude for Your Past Struggles

Look back at the hardest moments of your life and identify what they taught you. Having lost everything twice gives you a much greater appreciation for what you have now. The challenges you have endured are not wasted — they are the foundation of your gratitude and your ability to steward future blessings well. Choose to see your past hardships as preparation rather than punishment.

### Step 5: Keep Going Even When You Feel Like Quitting

You can miss seven out of ten times and still make millions — what matters is the hits, not the misses. A baseball player who succeeds three out of ten times at bat is still highly successful and well-paid.

## Examples

### Example 1: Steve Harvey's Personal Journey

Steve Harvey shares that he did not like being homeless, living in a car, getting divorced, or losing everything twice. None of those experiences were pleasant, but they were all preparing him for the life God had for him. Because he had been without, he now has a much greater appreciation for what God has given him. His story illustrates that even the most painful and shameful seasons can serve a divine purpose when you trust the process.

### Example 2: Michael Jordan's Missed Shots

Michael Jordan took over 900 game-winning shots in his career but only made 147 of them. That means he missed over 750 shots. No one writes about the misses — they only celebrate the wins. But Jordan learned how to make those 147 winning shots by missing 750 times. His misses were not failures; they were the training ground for his legendary success. This demonstrates that the path to achievement is paved with misses, and each one is essential preparation.

### Example 3: The Baseball Player Who Succeeds 3 Out of 10 Times

A professional baseball player can make millions of dollars even if he only gets a hit three out of ten times at the plate. No one cares about the seven times he strikes out or gets out — they pay him for the three hits. This example shows that you can miss the majority of the time and still be wildly successful. What matters is not how many times you fail, but whether you keep showing up and delivering when it counts.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Reframe every setback as preparation for what you asked God for
- ✅ Keep a record of what you have learned through difficult seasons
- ✅ Practice gratitude for your current blessings by remembering your past lack
- ✅ Persist through failure knowing it is part of the learning process
- ✅ Study the stories of successful people who overcame repeated failures
- ❌ Don't assume that hardship means God has abandoned you
- ❌ Don't give up on your dreams just because the process is painful
- ❌ Don't compare your timeline or journey to someone else's
- ❌ Don't waste your pain by failing to extract the lesson from it
- ❌ Don't let past failures define your future potential

## Keep In Mind

- Failure is not the opposite of success — it is part of success. Every miss is a lesson.
- You do not appreciate the sunshine if it never rains. Contrast gives value to everything in life.
- The order of your struggles matters to God even when it doesn't make sense to you.
- Preparation often looks exactly like pain. Trust the process even when it is uncomfortable.
- What you are going through right now is temporary, but what it is producing in you is permanent.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Spiritual encouragement should complement — not substitute — practical action. Use the principles in this sermon to gain perspective, but also take concrete steps to address the challenges in your life.
- Do not use the idea that "everything happens for a reason" to dismiss or minimize genuine suffering, abuse, or injustice. Some hardships require intervention, boundaries, and external support, not just spiritual perspective.
- God's preparation does not mean you must passively accept harmful situations. You can trust the process while also taking wise and practical action to improve your circumstances.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You believe that because you are going through a hard time, God is displeased with you or has forgotten you.
  **Solution:** Remind yourself that hardship is not evidence of God's absence — it is evidence that God is at work shaping you. The fact that you are being tested means God believes in your potential and is investing in your growth.

- **Problem:** You become bitter or resentful toward God or others because of the pain you have experienced.
  **Solution:** Forgiveness and gratitude are the antidotes to bitterness. Actively choose to release resentment and look for the lessons your hardships taught you. Bitterness prolongs pain; gratitude accelerates healing.

- **Problem:** You measure your worth or progress by your current circumstances instead of by your growth and character.
  **Solution:** Shift your metrics from external results to internal development. Ask yourself what you have learned, how you have grown, and how you are becoming stronger — not just what you have achieved or accumulated.

- **Problem:** You give up on your dreams after a few failures because you assume success will come easily if it is from God.
  **Solution:** Realign your expectations with biblical and historical examples. Every great destiny requires passing through difficulty. One or two failures do not disqualify you — they are part of the qualification process. Keep going.
