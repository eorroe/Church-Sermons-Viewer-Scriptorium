# Is Jesus The Only Way?

## Overview

This sermon addresses the question "Is Jesus The Only Way?" through a live question-and-answer session. It explores how to engage with skeptics who demand evidence, examines the nature of faith versus proof, and demonstrates that all people—including atheists—operate by faith when answering life's fundamental questions. The message emphasizes respectful dialogue, the evidence for Christ's reliability, and the logical connection between God's existence and objective human value.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When someone asks whether Jesus is the only way to God
- When engaging with skeptics who say there is not enough evidence for God
- When discussing the difference between faith and blind leap
- When exploring whether atheists also have faith
- When addressing questions about moral relativism versus objective morality
- When someone struggles with doubt despite believing in God

## Steps

### Step 1: Engage in Respectful Dialogue

Listen carefully and take language seriously when engaging with skeptics. Respect others' right to express their views and acknowledge the value of free exchange of ideas. Avoid treating the conversation as a debate or power struggle, and instead seek meaningful conversation through honest, direct questioning.

### Step 2: Acknowledge That All People Have Faith

Recognize that everyone must answer fundamental questions about life's meaning, purpose, and morality. These questions cannot be scientifically proven, so all people—whether atheist, agnostic, Muslim, Jew, or Christian—exercise faith when answering them. Help others see that atheists have faith just as Christians do, but with a different object of trust.

### Step 3: Challenge Skeptics to Identify What They Live For

Ask skeptics what or who they live for and what evidence supports their chosen worldview. Point out that everyone lives for something or someone, and that their chosen values require just as much faith as Christianity. Use questions about flourishing, goodness, and meaning to reveal the assumptions underlying their position.

### Step 4: Present the Evidence for Christ's Reliability

Share the evidence for Christ's reliability through the historical accuracy of the Gospels. Explain that former atheists who examined the evidence found the Gospels to be accurate history and were confronted by the lifestyle, teachings, death, and resurrection of Christ. Present the resurrection as the strongest evidence for Christianity.

### Step 5: Explain That Faith in Christ Is Not a Blind Leap

Clarify that faith in Christ is not a blind leap into the dark but a step into the light based on evidence. The resurrection of Jesus provides solid evidence that He is totally credible, totally trustworthy, and totally reliable. Emphasize that putting your confidence in Christ is a wise decision because He is all-powerful, broke death, and promises eternal life to all who trust in Him.

### Step 6: Use the Example of John the Baptist to Show That Doubt Is Normal

Point to John the Baptist as an example of a great believer who struggled with doubt. While in prison, John sent his disciples to ask Jesus if He was the Christ or if he had made a mistake. Show that doubt is normal even for the greatest believers and that Jesus responds to doubt with evidence of His messianic work.

### Step 7: Point to the Resurrection of Jesus as the Strongest Evidence

Emphasize that the resurrection of Jesus is the strongest evidence for Christianity. Jesus rose from the dead three days after His crucifixion and revealed Himself to hundreds of people so that nobody would ever have a realistic reason to doubt. The resurrection confirms that Jesus is who He claimed to be and that His sacrifice was accepted.

### Step 8: Emphasize That God Promises Eternal Life to All Who Trust in Him

Close by pointing to the promise of eternal life. God created humans to live for eternity, not just 60 to 80 years. Jesus promises to ultimately solve all problems by giving eternal life in heaven to all who humbly and sincerely trust in Him. Encourage listeners to make that decision today.

## Examples

### Example 1: A Person Raised Outside the Church

A person raised outside the church asks if there is evidence for God beyond the Bible. They do not take the Bible as evidence and want to know how someone without a religious upbringing can be convinced. Follow this sermon by explaining that former atheists examined the evidence for God's existence and the historical accuracy of the Gospels, found Christ to be reliable, and chose to put their faith in Him.

### Example 2: John the Baptist Doubted While in Prison

John the Baptist, the forerunner of Christ, had a tremendous ministry and pointed people to Jesus. But when he was thrown into prison and Jesus did not immediately rescue him, John sent his disciples to ask if Jesus was truly the Christ. This example shows that doubt is normal even for the greatest believers and that Jesus responds to doubt with evidence of His power and love.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Respect others' right to express their views and engage in free exchange of ideas
- ✅ Listen carefully before responding and take language seriously
- ✅ Acknowledge your own limitations and doubts
- ✅ Avoid false dichotomies (absolutism vs relativism)
- ✅ Point to the resurrection as the central evidence for Christianity
- ✅ Help skeptics see that all people operate by faith, including atheists

## Keep In Mind

- Engage with respect and intellectual honesty in all conversations about faith
- Avoid dominating the conversation or speaking over others
- Be prepared for difficult questions about suffering, evidence, and doubt
- Respect the sincerely held beliefs of others when discussing matters of faith

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Claiming atheists have no faith
  **Solution:** Explain that all people exercise faith when answering life's unprovable questions about meaning, purpose, and morality

- **Problem:** Avoiding difficult questions about evidence
  **Solution:** Address questions honestly while also turning the focus back to the skeptic's own beliefs and what they live for

- **Problem:** Presenting faith as a blind leap without evidence
  **Solution:** Emphasize that faith in Christ is a step into the light based on the evidence of the resurrection and the reliability of the Gospels
