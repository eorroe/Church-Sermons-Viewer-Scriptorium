# Sermon Title: Give Me an Answer - All Religions Do Not Say the Same Thing. Jesus Is Different From the Rest.

## Overview

This sermon teaches that all religions do not say the same thing and in fact contradict each other on doctrines such as the nature of God, Jesus, salvation, and the afterlife. It argues that because religions like Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, and the Baha'i Faith contradict one another, either all are wrong or one is right. The sermon then makes the case that Jesus Christ is uniquely trustworthy and different from all other religious leaders because of the historical evidence for his resurrection, and that trusting Jesus is the only basis for believing in heaven and eternal life.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When comparing religions and wondering if all religions lead to the same God or heaven
- When seeking truth about Jesus and whether he is unique among religious leaders
- When questions about religious tolerance arise and whether all beliefs are equally valid
- When wrestling with the existence of heaven and why one should believe in it
- When struggling with the problem of evil and suffering in the world
- When considering questions about original sin and human nature
- When someone believes in God but is unsure about believing in Jesus
- When evaluating whether life has ultimate meaning and significance

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize That All Religions Contradict Each Other

Acknowledge that major world religions contradict one another on doctrines such as the nature of God, Jesus, salvation, and the afterlife. Muhammad contradicts Jesus, the avatars of Hinduism contradict Siddhartha Gautama the Buddha, and they all contradict the Baha'i Faith. It is intellectually dishonest to claim that all religions say the same thing. Either all are wrong or one of them is right.

### Step 2: Understand That Heaven Requires Trust

Realize that no human has ever been to heaven or seen it with their own eyes. Belief in heaven requires trusting someone. Just as Austin, Texas is not heaven despite being a nice place, earthly experience cannot prove heaven's existence. The question is not whether you will trust, but whom you will trust.

### Step 3: Study the Evidence for Jesus Personally

Read the gospels for yourself: Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John. Ask whether the historical evidence points to Jesus being the truth. The Apostle John lived closely with Christ for three years and wrote that in him is no sin. In John chapter 8, Jesus looked at his enemies and asked which one of them could prove him guilty of sin, and they were silent.

### Step 4: Evaluate Jesus Based on the Evidence

Do not trust Jesus if the evidence shows he is a fraud, hypocrite, or liar. But if the evidence shows he is totally reliable, that he really did die and rise from the dead, then you can trust him. The resurrection is the falsifiable claim that verifies Jesus' identity. If Jesus is not risen, Christians are fools for believing in him.

### Step 5: Trust Jesus for Eternal Life

If the evidence confirms Jesus is the truth, then put your faith in him. He is the one who promises heaven to everyone who puts their faith in him. Without Jesus, the alternative is that life ends in the same place for everyone - the fertilizer pit - making all decisions ultimately meaningless.

### Step 6: Address the Problem of Evil and Suffering Through Free Will

Understand that evil exists because God, though all-powerful, chose to limit his power by giving humanity free will. When humans use free will to harm others, they cannot blame God. Suffering also exists because when human beings rebelled against God in Genesis chapter three, God honored their free will and took a step back, allowing nature to unravel and chaos to fill the gap.

### Step 7: Recognize Original Sin as a Readiness to Sin Factor

Original sin is not something God gives to people; it is a readiness to sin factor that all humans are born with because we are born into a world where sin has already corrupted everything. This does not remove personal responsibility because every person still has free will and must choose whether to obey or disobey God.

## Examples

### Example 1: Personal Experience of Sin as a Child

The preacher gives an example from his own childhood of being in a sandbox at age four. When another child hurt him, he naturally picked up a metal truck and slammed it on the other child's head. He had never seen this behavior modeled before, yet it came naturally. This illustrates the readiness to sin factor that all people are born with.

### Example 2: The Grape Juice and the Son

The preacher describes his son Stuart sitting in a baby chair with a glass of grape juice in front of him. The preacher would say "Stuart, do not knock this glass of grape juice off this tray," and his son would smile and backhand the glass off the tray. This demonstrates that even very young children display a natural inclination toward disobedience.

### Example 3: College Basketball and Sexuality

The preacher played major college basketball and observed that almost every teammate pursued sexual conquest with as many women as possible. He had to exercise his conscience and rational thinking to decide whether that was how God intended his sexuality to be expressed. He had to contradict his culture and environment when he realized they were wrong.

### Example 4: Skinheads Who Chose Not to Be Racist

The preacher worked with some skinheads who grew up in a culture that taught white superiority. Some of them chose to reject that teaching and decided not to follow their culture. This shows that people can use their conscience and rational mind to contradict even deeply ingrained cultural values.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Study the gospels for yourself: Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John
- ✅ Follow the evidence about Jesus rather than cultural conditioning
- ✅ Trust Jesus if the evidence shows he is reliable and rose from the dead
- ✅ Exercise your conscience and rational thinking to contradict your culture when it is wrong
- ✅ Do not believe in Jesus simply because you grew up in a Christian country or family
- ✅ Recognize that goodness requires a mind to define it, and that moral absolutes point to a moral lawgiver
- ✅ Accept responsibility for your choices rather than blaming your sinful nature or environment
- ✅ Remember that babies and those who cannot understand the gospel will be in heaven

- ❌ Don't believe that all religions say the same thing when they clearly contradict each other
- ❌ Don't trust Jesus if the evidence shows he is a fraud, hypocrite, or liar
- ❌ Don't blame your sinful nature for your sinful choices - you still have free will
- ❌ Don't define good as whatever you or your culture wants if there is no God
- ❌ Don't think that being "born with a desire" makes that desire morally good
- ❌ Don't claim you believe in God simply because you believe in God - believe because there is evidence

## Keep In Mind

- No human has ever been to heaven; belief in heaven requires trusting someone. The question is whom you will trust and why.
- Jesus is the only person in history who was born sinless and lived a perfectly sinless life, according to the evidence.
- The evidence for Jesus is historical, not merely philosophical. His claim to die and rise from the dead gives us a way to verify or falsify his claims.
- If there is no heaven and no hell, then all human lives are ultimately meaningless because Hitler and Mother Teresa end up in the same place.
- Free will explains human evil and suffering, but it does not fully explain animal suffering or suffering of those who did nothing wrong.
- Babies who die will be in heaven, as evidenced by David's words about his dead baby: "I will go to you, you will not come to me."
- Original sin is not an excuse for sin; it is a readiness to sin factor, but humans are still fully responsible for their choices.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not trust Jesus if the evidence shows he is a fraud, hypocrite, or liar. The sermon explicitly warns against following Jesus based on blind faith alone.
- If the historical evidence contradicts Jesus' claims, intellectual honesty requires rejecting him.
- The preacher acknowledges he cannot give a complete answer to why God allows evil and suffering, but he challenges questioners to provide their own consistent solution first.
- The existence of evil and suffering is real and must be addressed honestly, not dismissed.
- Be aware that claiming all religions are the same is intellectually dishonest and disrespectful to the actual contradictory claims of each faith.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that all religions say the same thing when they clearly contradict each other on core doctrines like the nature of God, Jesus, salvation, and afterlife.
  **Solution:** Study the actual claims of each religion and acknowledge their contradictions. Either all are wrong or one is right.

- **Problem:** Believing in Jesus simply because you grew up in a Christian culture or family.
  **Solution:** Examine the evidence for Jesus personally through the gospels and historical investigation. Do not believe based on geography or family tradition.

- **Problem:** Blaming your sinful nature for your choices instead of taking personal responsibility.
  **Solution:** Recognize that while all humans have a readiness to sin, we still possess free will and are responsible before God for our choices. A sinful nature is not an excuse.

- **Problem:** Thinking that life has no ultimate meaning if there is no God or heaven.
  **Solution:** Understand that without eternal consequences, all human actions are ultimately meaningless. Jesus provides eternal significance because there is a heaven, a hell, and a day of judgment.

- **Problem:** Defining good independently of God and then claiming it exists eternally.
  **Solution:** Goodness is an intangible value that requires a mind to define it. If goodness exists eternally, it depends on an eternal mind - God. Goodness cannot exist as a floating eternal concept without a definer.

- **Problem:** Thinking that being born with a certain desire makes that desire morally good.
  **Solution:** Having a natural desire does not make it right. Exercise your conscience and rational thinking to evaluate whether your desires align with God's design.
