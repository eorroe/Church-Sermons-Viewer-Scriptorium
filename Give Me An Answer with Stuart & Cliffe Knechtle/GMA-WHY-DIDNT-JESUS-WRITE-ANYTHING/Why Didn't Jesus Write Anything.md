# Why Didn't Jesus Write Anything?

## Overview

This sermon addresses the toughest questions facing Christians and seekers today: Why didn't Jesus write anything down? If God is good, why create hell? Why does a loving God allow evil? Why didn't Jesus appear to everyone in the world? Through a live Q&A format, these questions are answered with biblical reasoning, historical evidence for the Gospels, and honest engagement with doubt. It exists to equip believers to think clearly about the hardest objections to the Christian faith and to respond with both truth and grace.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When someone asks why Jesus never wrote anything himself
- When wrestling with the problem of hell and God's goodness
- When trying to understand how a loving God can allow evil
- When facing questions about why Jesus only appeared to a small group in ancient Israel
- When someone claims the Gospels are unreliable or full of myths
- When asked whether people who never heard of Jesus can be saved
- When discussing whether Lucifer and hell are part of God's plan
- When helping someone work through doubts about Christianity

## Steps

### Step 1: Address Why Jesus Never Wrote Anything Down

Jesus did not need to write anything because his message was confirmed by his resurrection, not by a written document. The greatest miracle Christ performed was rising from the dead, which validated every claim he made. The Gospels were written by eyewitnesses or those closely connected to eyewitnesses within the first century, not centuries later. When asked why Jesus didn't write anything, the answer is that the evidence for Christ does not rest on a single written text but on the historical resurrection and the trustworthy witness of those who saw him.

### Step 2: Respond to the Question of Hell and God's Goodness

Hell exists because God is good and cannot allow evil to win. Evil will ultimately be destroyed, and hell is the place where that destruction is carried out. God did not create evil; evil is the misuse of good gifts that God gives, made possible by the free will God gave humanity. Free will is precious because love, relationship, and genuine trust are only possible if people are truly free to choose. If God forced everyone to be good, love would be meaningless. Hell is the logical result of a world with real free will and a God who takes evil seriously enough to judge it.

### Step 3: Explain Why Jesus Appeared Only to a Small Group in Ancient Israel

God judges people according to the revelation they have received, not according to what they do not know. Jesus said that those who know their master's will and do not do it will be beaten with more blows, but those who do not know and do things deserving punishment will be beaten with fewer blows. This means people who never heard of Jesus but humbly trusted God to the best of their ability, such as Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Rahab, will be in heaven. Their salvation rests on God's grace and Christ's death, applied to them according to the light they had. Jesus appeared where he did because the evidence of the resurrection was meant to be verified by eyewitnesses in a specific historical context.

### Step 4: Defend the Reliability of the Gospels

The Gospels of Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John were written in the first century by eyewitnesses or their close associates. The earliest fragment of a Gospel, the John Rylands fragment, is dated between 117 and 138 AD, only 30 to 60 years after John was written. Early church fathers Ignatius and Clement quoted Matthew, Mark, and Luke in letters written in 95 and 110 AD. There are over 5,200 Greek manuscripts of the New Testament, and modern translations go directly from the original Greek to contemporary languages, not through a chain of translations. The Gnostic Gospels were rejected in the second century because they were based on Greek philosophy rather than apostolic teaching and were not used by churches across the known world. The four Gospels were accepted because they met three tests: written by an apostle or someone who knew an apostle, orthodox in teaching, and used by churches universally.

### Step 5: Clarify the Nature of Hell and Satan's Role

The Bible does not specify the exact nature of hell, using symbolic language such as fire and outer darkness. Hell was prepared for the devil and his angels, and they do not have physical bodies, so the exact experience is unknown. What is clear is that Jesus used the most serious language possible to describe it and that no one should want anyone to go there. Satan is not a happy ruler of hell; he is a committed enemy of God who tempts people toward evil. God does not send people to hell arbitrarily; because God is just, he must punish evil. If someone rejects Christ, they are rejecting God's offer of forgiveness and help.

### Step 6: Show That Genuine Repentance Requires Making Things Right With People

If you hurt someone, genuine repentance means asking that person for forgiveness, not only asking God. If someone claims to be sorry but refuses to make things right with the person they wronged, there is reason to question whether their repentance is real. This protects against cheap grace and ensures that forgiveness is accompanied by a changed life and a desire to restore what was broken.

## Examples

### Example 1: Someone Asks Why God Would Send Good People to Hell

The question assumes that being "good" is enough, but the Bible teaches that everyone has done evil, and the heart of sin is pride. Religious self-righteousness is especially dangerous because it blinds people to their need for God's mercy. Even those who have lived outwardly moral lives have done wrong, and if they reject Christ's forgiveness, they are rejecting the only means by which they can be made right with God. Heaven is not for people who are good enough; it is for people who recognize they are not and receive God's grace.

### Example 2: Someone Says the Gospels Are Just Made-Up Stories

The Gospels are written in the style of historical narrative, not myth or epic poetry. Luke chapter 3 begins with precise historical references to rulers and dates because Luke was a meticulous historian. The earliest manuscript evidence is close to the original writing, and the Gospels were universally accepted by churches because they were eyewitness accounts. Other so-called gospels were rejected because they taught philosophy contrary to the apostles' teaching and were not used by the church worldwide.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Answer difficult questions directly without avoiding them
- ✅ Use historical evidence and manuscript information when defending the Bible
- ✅ Distinguish between different literary genres in the Bible, such as historical narrative and apocalyptic literature
- ✅ Acknowledge when something is not fully known, such as the exact nature of hell
- ✅ Share personal testimony when appropriate to show that Jesus is trustworthy
- ✅ Explain that God judges according to the light people have received
- ✅ Emphasize that genuine repentance requires making things right with people you have wronged
- ❌ Do not dismiss hard questions as unimportant
- ❌ Do not claim the Bible is perfect in a way that ignores textual variants
- ❌ Do not say that people who never heard of Jesus are automatically condemned
- ❌ Do not portray hell as something God arbitrarily enjoys
- ❌ Do not use overly complex philosophical language when simple clarity works better

## Keep In Mind

- Jesus used harsh language to describe hell because he wanted people to take it seriously, not because he wanted them to live in fear.
- The resurrection is the central evidence for Christianity; without it, the faith is meaningless.
- Free will is required for love to be real, and free will makes evil possible.
- People who never heard of Jesus can still respond to the light they have and be saved by Christ's work on the cross.
- Religious pride is a greater barrier to faith than moral failure because it prevents people from seeing their need for grace.
- Asking hard questions is not a sign of bad faith; it is often a sign of genuine seeking.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not share personal identifying information about students or participants in Q&A settings without consent.
- When discussing sensitive topics like hell and judgment, maintain a tone of warning rather than condemnation.
- Avoid making absolute statements about the eternal destiny of specific individuals.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Someone says, "It's all faith, there is no evidence."  
  **Solution:** Explain that the resurrection is an historical claim backed by eyewitness testimony, early creeds, and the empty tomb, and that faith is trust based on evidence, not belief without evidence.

- **Problem:** Someone claims the Gospels were written hundreds of years after Jesus.  
  **Solution:** Provide the manuscript evidence, including the John Rylands fragment and the quotations from early church fathers, to show the Gospels were written in the first century.

- **Problem:** Someone argues that because there are variants in New Testament manuscripts, we cannot trust the text.  
  **Solution:** Acknowledge the variants but explain that most are minor spelling or grammatical differences, and no cardinal doctrine depends on a disputed text.

- **Problem:** Someone says, "If God is loving, why would he send anyone to hell?"  
  **Solution:** Explain that God does not send people to hell arbitrarily; he respects their free will. If someone chooses to live apart from God, God honors that choice for all eternity.
