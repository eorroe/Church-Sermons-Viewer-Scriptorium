# Cliffe Knechtle's Best Atheist Debates Ever!

## Overview

This sermon compiles Cliffe Knechtle's exchanges with atheists, ex-Christians, and university students who challenge Christianity on morality, the reliability of Jesus, the existence of hell, biblical contradictions, and the uniqueness of Christ as the only religious figure who claimed to be God and rose from the dead. The consistent thread across every exchange is that the truth of Christianity depends entirely on whether Jesus of Nazareth is historically who he claimed to be and whether he rose from the dead — not on the behavior of Christians, not on passion or sincerity, but on whether Jesus is who he claimed to be and whether he actually rose from the dead.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are asked why Christianity is true when other religions also claim truth
- When someone says morality does not require God
- When someone challenges you on apparent contradictions in the Bible
- When someone asks whether people of other faiths go to hell
- When someone says the God of the Old Testament is immoral or hypocritical
- When someone says they cannot believe in Jesus because of Christian hypocrisy
- When someone says being a sincere Muslim or atheist is just as valid as being a Christian
- When someone questions the reliability of the resurrection
- When you want to respond to questions about homosexuality, slavery, or other challenging biblical passages
- When you wrestle with doubt yourself and need honest, unscripted answers

## Steps

### Step 1: Start With the Person of Jesus — Not Christianity as a Religion

Do not defend Christianity as an institution. Some who claimed the name of Christ used Christian identity to justify the Crusades, the Inquisition, and slavery. Place your trust in Jesus Christ himself, not in Christian institutions or traditions. When someone asks why you believe what you believe, point them to the person of Jesus: his sinless life, his ethical teachings, his death on the cross forgiving his enemies, and his resurrection from the dead. Jesus is the only religious figure who claimed to be God and then backed it up by rising from the dead. Muhammad claimed to be a prophet, not God. Buddha did not claim divinity. Only Jesus made the claim and provided the evidence.

### Step 2: Answer the Question "Why Christianity Over Other Religions?" With Evidence, Not Passion

When someone asks why Christianity over other religions, do not say "because I believe it" or "because I'm passionate." Sincerity and passion do not make a belief true — walking on thin ice with sincere faith still results in falling through. The validity of faith is determined by the credibility of its object. Jesus is credible because he lived a sinless life according to God's moral standard, taught the highest ethical standard in history (the Sermon on the Mount, described by Harvard psychology professor Robert Coles as the supreme work of ethics over 2,000 years), died forgiving his enemies, and rose from the dead. If you die and rise from the dead, that is evidence your message is true. No other religious founder made that claim and backed it up.

### Step 3: Address the Morality Question by Grounding It in God's Nature

When someone says "I don't believe in God and I still know rape is wrong — so where does my morality come from?" respond that humans are created in God's image, endowed with a conscience and rational mind that reflect his nature. The conscience convicts us that we have done wrong, and that guilt needs forgiveness. Christianity does not merely say "be good." It says you have already failed and Jesus Christ paid the penalty. Guilt is real, and the gospel answers it. If there is no God, morality is just personal taste, cultural conditioning, or the opinion of a power elite — none of which can account for why something is actually evil rather than merely inconvenient.

### Step 4: Distinguish Between the Moral Law and the Mosaic Theocratic Legislation

When someone raises slavery, polygamy, or harsh Old Testament laws as a problem, distinguish between the moral law (Exodus 20, the Ten Commandments) and the 613 laws of the Mosaic legislation that governed the theocracy of Israel. The Mosaic laws were given specifically to the nation of Israel during the Old Testament theocratic period. The moral law — do not murder, do not steal, love your neighbor — is universal and timeless. Mixing the two is a textual error that leads to false objections.

### Step 5: Respond to "Bible Contradictions" With Perspective, Not Perfection

When someone claims the Bible has contradictions, explain that different perspectives on the same event are not contradictions. If one gospel mentions one angel at the empty tomb and another mentions two, that is two different perspectives — not a contradiction. The New Testament is based on over 5,200 Greek manuscripts, giving us a high degree of certainty about what the authors wrote. Minor textual variants (for example, differences in how closely a manuscript repeats a phrase) do not change any doctrine. Have the humility to say "I don't know" when you do not have an answer — but do not accept a contradiction where there is only a difference in perspective.

### Step 6: Address Hell and Judgment Honestly

When someone says they cannot believe in a God who sends people to hell, do not avoid the question. Hell is a real place of eternal separation from God, as Jesus described it in the New Testament. But the ultimate judgment is based on whether a person has accepted or rejected Jesus Christ as Lord, not on failing to follow a set of rules. You do not know who is going to heaven or hell. Do not claim certainty about anyone's eternal fate — including your own. The question that matters is whether a person has placed their trust in Jesus Christ. Christians who sin and Christians who enslaved people were not living consistently with the gospel — that is hypocrisy, and it grieves you. It does not invalidate Jesus.

### Step 7: Engage Other Religions With Respect and Intellectual Honesty

When someone asks whether Muslims, Hindus, or people of other faiths are wrong, be honest: if the atheist speaks the truth, you are wrong. If the Muslim speaks the truth, you are wrong. The question is not which religion is most passionate or most sincere, but which one is actually true. Read the primary texts of other faiths if you claim to love people in those faiths. Muhammad lived more than 500 years after Jesus and never met him. The eyewitnesses of Jesus (Matthew, Mark, John, Peter, James) all insisted Jesus claimed to be God. Muhammad explicitly rejected the deity of Christ in the Quran — but if Jesus truly claimed to be God in the first century, then Muhammad's claim contradicts the eyewitness testimony. You cannot have it both ways. Either Jesus was who he claimed to be, or he was a liar. There is no middle ground.

### Step 8: Call People to Read the Gospels Themselves

When someone says they cannot believe, do not argue them into faith. Point them to the historical record: read the Gospels of Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John — not as the word of God (if they do not believe that), but as history. Ask: does the evidence of the way Jesus lived, taught, died, and rose from the dead point to him being reliable, or does it not? Do not believe in Jesus because someone else does. That is a terrible reason. Believe because you have examined the evidence for yourself.

### Step 9: Acknowledge the Cost of Authentic Faith

Christian faith is not a ticket to comfort. Following Jesus means dying to selfishness, greed, and the desire to be your own god. If you live for money, status, or pleasure, Christianity will feel like an intrusion — because it is. A vague, non-authoritative conception of God permits people to live however they choose without accountability. The God of the Bible is definite and authoritative, which means you must change. That is the real reason many people reject Christianity — not because of intellectual objections, but because they do not want to surrender control of their lives. Be honest about that.

### Step 10: Be the Kind of Christian That Makes the Gospel Credible

People reject Christianity for many reasons, but one of the most common is Christian hypocrisy. Christians who have enslaved others, Christians who have committed sexual violence, Christians who live for money while claiming to follow Christ — these are real stumbling blocks. Live consistently with what you claim to believe. If you are struggling with materialism, greed, or selfishness, say so. Humility and honesty go further than a polished argument. The world needs to see Christians who are honest about their own sin and who show the love of Christ in how they live.

## Examples

### Example 1: Responding to "Why Christianity over Other Religions?"

An atheist student asks: "Why Christianity over other religions? A Muslim would be just as passionate about his faith." Respond: "Passion does not make something true. If I walk on thin ice with sincere faith, I fall through. What matters is whether the object of faith is reliable. Jesus Christ is the only religious figure who claimed to be God and then rose from the dead. Muhammad denied Jesus' deity despite Jesus' own claims, which contradicts the eyewitness accounts. Either Jesus is who he said he is, or he was a liar. Read the Gospels, examine the evidence, and decide for yourself."

### Example 2: Responding to "I Don't Believe in God but I Still Know Rape Is Wrong"

An ex-Christian asks: "If God doesn't exist, where does my morality come from? I don't need God to know that rape is wrong." Respond: "You are right that you know it is wrong — but the question is why. Is it just your culture, your taste, or a power elite that taught you? If there is no God, there is no objective standard of good and evil. But you do experience guilt when you do wrong. That guilt is the conscience God implanted in you, and the gospel answers it: Jesus Christ absorbed the penalty for the wrong you have done. You do not have to earn your way to heaven. You just have to trust the one who died for you."

### Example 3: Responding to "Doesn't the Old Testament Show God Is Immoral?"

An atheist challenger says: "God commanded child sacrifice in the Old Testament, and then judged people for doing the same thing. That's hypocrisy." Respond: "That is not what the text says. God explicitly told the Jewish people not to sacrifice their children — that was the practice of the surrounding Canaanite cultures. God judged Israel when they disobeyed and did what the surrounding nations did. The facts are: they disobeyed God, they sacrificed their children, and God judged them through the Assyrians and Babylonians. Stick to the facts. Do not go off into hypotheticals."

### Example 4: Responding to "Christians Are All Hypocrites — Why Should I Believe?"

A student says: "I was in church for a long time and I don't buy it. Christians enslave people, Christians rape, Christians are hypocrites." Respond: "You are right. Christians sin. Every follower of Christ sins — including me. I cannot tell you with certainty that any particular person is a genuine Christian. That is between them and God. But the question is not whether Christians are perfect. The question is whether Jesus Christ is trustworthy. Look at his life, his teachings, his death, and his resurrection. Do not judge Jesus by his followers. Judge him by who he actually is."

## Best Practices

- ✅ Put your faith in Jesus Christ, not in Christianity as an institution
- ✅ Anchor every answer in the person and work of Jesus Christ
- ✅ Use historical evidence (resurrection, eyewitness testimony, ethical teachings) rather than personal experience to defend the faith
- ✅ Distinguish between the moral law of God and theocratic Mosaic legislation when responding to Old Testament objections
- ✅ Explain that different perspectives on the same event are not biblical contradictions
- ✅ Be honest and say "I don't know" rather than making up an answer
- ✅ Read the primary texts of other faiths if you claim to love people in those faiths
- ✅ Acknowledge the reality of Christian hypocrisy without excusing it
- ✅ Point people to read the Gospels themselves rather than arguing them into faith
- ✅ Be honest about the cost of following Jesus — it requires dying to self
- ✅ Remember that sincerity and passion do not make a belief true; the credibility of its object does

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Defending "Christianity" as a religion instead of Jesus Christ as a person.
  **Solution:** When challenged, immediately distinguish between the failures of Christians and the reliability of Jesus. Say: "Christianity has been used to justify terrible things. I put my faith in Jesus Christ, not in Christianity."

- **Problem:** Responding to "Why your religion?" with "Because I believe it" or "Because I'm sincere."
  **Solution:** Replace sincerity-based arguments with evidence-based arguments: Jesus' life, teachings, death, and resurrection. Sincerity cannot make an untrue belief safe or reliable, just as walking on thin ice with sincere faith still results in falling through.

- **Problem:** Conflating the moral law with Old Testament theocratic legislation when responding to slavery, polygamy, or other difficult passages.
  **Solution:** Learn to distinguish between the universal moral law (Exodus 20) and the 613 theocratic laws of Israel (Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy). They are not the same.

- **Problem:** Trying to resolve every apparent Bible "contradiction" with a pat answer.
  **Solution:** Supposed contradictions are often simply different perspectives on the same event. If one gospel mentions one angel and another mentions two at the empty tomb, both can be true. Do not over-explain — acknowledge what you do not know.

- **Problem:** Claiming certainty about who is in hell or heaven.
  **Solution:** You do not know. Even Cliffe Knechtle, after 32 years of campus ministry, will not say with certainty whether any specific person is saved or lost. Humility here is not weakness — it is honesty.

- **Problem:** Dismissing other faiths with caricature rather than engaging their actual claims.
  **Solution:** If you seek to love Muslims genuinely, read the Quran. If you claim to love an atheist, listen carefully to why they do not believe. Engage the strongest version of their position, not the weakest.

- **Problem:** Making the gospel about rules, morality, or religious performance rather than grace.
  **Solution:** The Christian message is not "be good and go to heaven." It is "you have sinned, Jesus Christ paid the penalty, and he offers you forgiveness as a free gift." Keep the gospel central.
