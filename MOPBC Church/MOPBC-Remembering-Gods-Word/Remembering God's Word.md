# Remembering God's Word: 2 Peter 1:12-19

## Overview

On August 1, 2026, Dr. Teresa Henderson taught the Mount Olive Baptist Church Sunday School lesson "Remembering God's Word," drawn from 2 Peter 1:12-19. The lesson opens by reading 2 Peter 1:3-7 (NLT and KJV) to establish that divine power has given believers everything needed for life and godliness. Dr. Henderson explains that remembering God's word requires having first experienced it — both good and bad times — because memory regains knowledge of what has already happened. The message centers on three themes: the permanence of God's word, the call to stir up dormant spiritual gifts, and the urgency of Peter's own reminder to the church as he faces impending martyrdom under Nero. Believers are challenged to remain established in present truth, to walk humbly, and to recognize that God reveals Himself in ways only He can accomplish.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you cannot remember what God has done for you because your circumstances have caused you to forget
- When you have been in church for years but still feel like you do not truly know God's word
- When you are aging and your body is getting weaker — when rising each day feels harder than it used to
- When you are young and do not yet understand that the hard experiences ahead will teach you to rely on God
- When you want to discern whether a message or prophetic word truly comes from God
- When your spiritual gifts are dormant because you fear what other people will say or because you have delayed your calling
- When you need to understand how to add to your faith the seven qualities from 2 Peter 1:5-7: moral excellence, knowledge, self-control, patience, godliness, brotherly affection, and love
- When you are tempted to compare your spiritual walk to others or assume you have already arrived in your faith
- When you need to be reminded to recognize church order and not bypass your pastor or spiritual authority

## Steps

### Step 1: Remember That You Must Experience Before You Can Remember

Recognize that you cannot remember something you have never experienced. Both the good times and the bad times you have lived through are the foundation for remembering God's word. When you call to mind how God kept you through the night, how He brought you through a difficult situation, or how He answered a prayer, you regain knowledge of what God has done. Your memories are the proof of His faithfulness.

### Step 2: Accept That God's Word Will Never Fade Away

Know that God's word is eternal and will never be void, regardless of how long you have been in church. It is your responsibility to teach and remind the younger generation — "these babies here" — that God's word stands the test of time. Divine power has given you everything you need for life and godliness through your knowledge of Him (2 Peter 1:3). Do not assume that because you have attended church for years you already know enough.

### Step 3: Grow by Adding to Your Faith

Study 2 Peter 1:3-7 and actively supplement your faith with moral excellence, knowledge, self-control, patience, godliness, brotherly affection, and love. Understand that godly knowledge must supersede worldly knowledge. You need practical knowledge — like knowing how to drive a car — but that knowledge must be submitted to the knowledge of God. Do not neglect self-control when your temper flares or patience when you have to wait on God's plan to unfold. This growth is not automatic; it requires daily intentional effort.

### Step 4: Stir Up the Gift Within You

Do not allow your spiritual gifts to remain dormant because you fear what other people will say or because you have delayed your calling. Identify what God has placed in you — singing, ushering, cooking, helping — and actively use those gifts to move forth the kingdom of God. Even if you cannot give financially, you can serve: clean the floor, help clean up, or do whatever task God has for you. Your time, your talents, and your treasures are all gifts. Do not let other people's opinions delay your calling.

### Step 5: Trust That God's Promises Are Always on Time

Hold onto the promises of God even when they do not come when you want them. God has given you great and precious promises that enable you to share in His divine nature (2 Peter 1:4), but those promises come when you need them, not necessarily when you want them. He is always on time. Be cautious when people prophesy over you or promise you things — test every message with a shadow of a doubt that it is from God. The only way to know for sure is to know God personally, because when God moves in your life, the circumstances will be so extraordinary that no one but God could have done them.

### Step 6: Stop Trying to Force God to Speak to You

Stop striving and trying so hard to get a message from God. When God is ready to reveal Himself to you, He will do it in a way that only He can accomplish. You cannot tell God when or how to speak to you. The proof that God is talking to you is that what is happening cannot be done by anyone except God. Paul's conversion on the road to Damascus is the pattern: God knocked him off a donkey and changed his life in an undeniable way. Let God move on His timeline, not yours.

### Step 7: Remain Established in the Present Truth

Like Peter, who knew his death was imminent under Nero's coming persecution, be diligent about putting others in remembrance of God's word. Peter said, "I would not be the man that I am, the godly man that I am, if I do not tell you the things that have got me through." Work hard to prove that you are among those God has called and chosen. Stay alert and ready, for Christ is coming back "like a twinkling of an eye." Recognize that you do not simply walk into ministry on your own — God calls you into service. Do not go past your pastor or spiritual authority to do things on your own. Respect the God-given order in the church.

## Examples

### Example 1: Peter's Final Teaching Before Martyrdom

Peter, knowing that Nero's persecution was coming and that his own death was near, made it his priority to remind believers of the truth that had set him free. He said, "I would not love you if I did not tell you these things" — meaning that failing to share what got him through would make him less of the godly man God called him to be. He wrote, "Wherefore I will not be negligent to put you always in remembrance of these things, though you know them, and be established in the present truth" (2 Peter 1:12). This shows that remembering God's word is not passive — it requires intentional teaching and reminding, even when you think others already know.

### Example 2: The Elder Teaching Through Lived Experience

Dr. Henderson shared how her mother told her that as she lived longer, she would understand more about life and God's goodness. Even when the body gets tired and things become harder — when it is more difficult to get out of bed in the morning — the believer must remember that God is still good and still allows them to rise each day. This illustrates how godly elders impart wisdom that only comes through lived experience. A younger believer cannot fully understand God's faithfulness until they have lived long enough to see it for themselves.

### Example 3: Abraham and Lot Choosing the Wrong Path

Dr. Henderson referenced Abraham and Lot to show the danger of not remembering God's word when making decisions. Abraham offered Lot the choice of land, and Lot looked at the green pastures and chose what appeared to be the better option. That choice led Lot straight into Sodom and Gomorrah — the wrong place at the wrong time. The lesson: do not be led by what looks attractive on the surface. Remember God's word and let it guide your decisions, because the right way does not always look pretty all the way.

- ✅ Be intentional about teaching and reminding others of God's word, as Peter did knowing his death was near

- ✅ Test all prophesies and messages with a shadow of a doubt, ensuring the message is truly from God before you receive it

## Keep In Mind

- The title of a Sunday school lesson carries more meaning than you realize — "Remembering God's Word" is not just a topic but a call to action
- God calls you into service — you do not simply walk into ministry on your own terms. God calls, and He expects you to respond sincerely (Ephesians 1)
- The path to heaven is narrow. Jesus said many will say "Lord, Lord" and He will respond, "Depart from me, I never knew you" (Matthew 7:23)
- God is righteous, eternal, merciful, everlasting, magnificent, omnipresent, and divine — and He is also grace. We should thank Jesus Christ daily because without His grace, we would not be here
- You must worship God, obey God, and rely on God daily — not just on Sundays
- Order matters in the church — recognize and respect the God-given authority structure; do not go past your pastor to do things on your own
- The devil is on a mission to kill and destroy the word of God in the hearts of believers — he wants you to forget what God has taught you
- You cannot understand the complexity of God, so do not try. It is all baby steps. God can change a person in the twinkling of an eye, as He did with Paul on the road to Damascus
- When you are emotional and pray openly, be careful what you say — the world does not need to spread your business, and you have already told God your thoughts before you spoke them

## Security & Safety Notes

- Be discerning when receiving prophetic words or messages from others — ensure with a shadow of a doubt that the message is from God before you receive it. The only way to know is to know God personally
- Be careful about sharing personal business openly and publicly, especially when you are emotional — vulnerability in prayer or testimony can lead to unnecessary exposure if you are not in a safe, trusted setting
- Recognize church order and do not bypass your pastor or spiritual authority to do things on your own
- Guard against false teachings by knowing the whole counsel of God's word — do not rely on a single verse or message without context
- Do not assume that people in difficult situations — the homeless, those in crisis, those who seem unlikely — do not know God's word. They may know more scripture and have deeper faith than you realize

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Thinking you have arrived and know everything about God's word because you have been in church a long time
  **Solution:** Remember that even the mature Christian is still growing. The moment you think you know everything, the devil has you — because even the devil does not know when God is coming back.

- **Problem:** Allowing your spiritual gifts to remain dormant because of fear, laziness, or concern about what other people will say
  **Solution:** Stir up the gift. Identify what God has placed in you — singing, ushering, cooking, helping — and use it to advance His kingdom regardless of what others may think. Other people can delay your calling, but they cannot stop it.

- **Problem:** Trying too hard to force a message or revelation from God on your timeline
  **Solution:** Stop striving. When God is ready to reveal Himself to you, He will do so in undeniable ways that cannot be attributed to anyone but God. Do not tell God when to speak to you — He decides when and how.

- **Problem:** Comparing your spiritual walk to others or feeling discouraged by hard times and aging
  **Solution:** Remember how God has kept you through the night. Young people, if you live long enough, you will understand that God is always good even when life gets harder and your body gets weaker. Keep living, and you will understand.

- **Problem:** Forgetting to pray when your day starts off chaotic and you are running late
  **Solution:** Develop a consistent habit of beginning each day in prayer. Even when you are running late, take a moment to thank God and ask for His guidance. He will make you remember Him.

- **Problem:** Assuming people do not know God's word based on their appearance, housing situation, or church attendance
  **Solution:** Do not judge others' spiritual knowledge. Homeless people, those in difficult situations, and those who seem unlikely may know more scripture and have deeper faith than you realize. John the Baptist was not dressed up, but he was doing the work and will of God.

- **Problem:** Speaking carelessly about Bible characters without understanding their context
  **Solution:** Remember that Bible characters did not have the complete written word with many translations and study notes that we have today. Do not put your mouth on people in the Bible without understanding the full context of what they lived through.

- **Problem:** Letting emotional vulnerability in public prayer become unnecessary exposure
  **Solution:** Be careful when praying openly and publicly — if you are emotional, you may tell all your business, and the world does not need to spread it. You have already told God your thoughts before you spoke them.
