# The Funeral Stopping Business

## Overview

This sermon establishes the divine calling for believers to intervene in communities affected by death, despair, and systemic destruction. Based on Luke 7:11-17, it teaches that God calls every believer into the "funeral stopping business" like Jesus, who stopped a funeral procession to restore life to a widow's only son. The sermon provides a three-step actionable framework for how churches and individuals can actively combat spiritual and physical death in their neighborhoods.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When your church or community is experiencing spiritual death, despair, or hopelessness
- When you feel called to address systemic issues like gun violence, drug epidemics, or poverty in your neighborhood
- When you need a biblical framework for community outreach and intervention
- When preparing for pastoral installation or new ministry leadership
- When seeking to understand the church's role in social justice and community transformation

## Steps

### Step 1: See What Is Happening Around You

Believers must develop spiritual eyes to see the death and destruction happening in their communities. Just as Jesus saw the widow of Nain weeping as she followed her dead son, you must look past the surface of your neighborhood and acknowledge the hurting people and situations directly around your church. This means paying attention to drug houses, violence, poverty, and people who have lost hope. Do not ride through your community looking past hurting people. Take time to observe what is transpiring in the lives of the people who live there, because God has called you to minister in that community. The former pastor of Mount Olive demonstrated this by buying property in the neighborhood to address the blight and prepare for God's future work.

### Step 2: Stop and Touch What Is Broken

After seeing the need, you must be willing to stop and intervene, even when it is risky or uncomfortable. Jesus did what no law-abiding Jew would do—He touched the deceased casket, risking being declared unclean and excommunicated from the community. You must be willing to touch what is broken in your community. This means engaging with people others avoid, entering spaces that seem dangerous, and getting personally involved in messy situations. Do not spend too much time analyzing how a person got into their situation before you help them. If a person is hungry, feed them first, then discuss how to get their next meal. Stop the funeral by reaching out, touching, and intervening on behalf of those who are dying spiritually and physically.

### Step 3: Speak Life Into Dead and Dying People

Once you have seen and stopped, you must speak life into those who are dead and dying. Jesus did not just stop and touch the young man—He spoke to him and said, "Young man, I say unto you, get up." You are commissioned to speak positive words of life into people regardless of their past or how long they have been doing wrong. Preach the word that Jesus is alive and in the saving business. Tell them that the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life. Listen to people, including children, so you can understand what is on their hearts and minister to them effectively. Speak life over your church, your city, and the people God has entrusted to your care. Choose this day to release from your mouth what you will carry in your spirit.

## Examples

### Example 1: Pastor Duke and the Liquor Store Owner

Pastor Kenneth Duke was passing out flyers in a neighborhood when the Lord told him to stop at a liquor store. The owner, Mr. Johnny, explained that he used to go to church but his church's covenant did not allow selling or consuming liquor. Pastor Duke told him that in the Primitive Baptist church, they could sell it and drink it, which made Mr. Johnny laugh and pick up his pen to write it down. Mr. Johnny eventually came to church, got saved, and attended Wednesday night Bible class. This demonstrates how stopping to speak life to someone in an unexpected place can lead to eternal transformation.

### Example 2: Mount Olive Church Buying Property in the Neighborhood

The former pastor of Mount Olive Primitive Baptist Church did not just come to the community—he saw what was going on and had a vision. He saw drug houses and blight in the neighborhood, so he bought those houses and even property on 15th Street to prepare for God's future work. The church now owns property on the block and in the neighborhood. This demonstrates how seeing the need and stopping to invest in the community can create long-term transformation and stop death before it starts.

### Example 3: The Widow of Nain

In Luke 7:11-17, Jesus saw a widow weeping as she followed her only dead son being carried out of the city gate. Despite the risk of touching a dead body and becoming unclean, Jesus stopped the funeral procession, touched the casket, and spoke life into the young man, raising him from the dead and giving him back to his mother. This is the ultimate example of the funeral stopping business—seeing, stopping, and speaking life to restore what death had stolen.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Develop the habit of observing your community with spiritual eyes before you judge it
- ✅ Stop what you are doing when God directs you to intervene, even if it seems inconvenient or risky
- ✅ Speak life-giving words to people, especially those who have been written off by society
- ✅ Feed people first before analyzing how they got into their situation
- ✅ Pray for your pastor and church leaders daily, knowing they are under constant spiritual attack
- ✅ Be patient with new pastoral leadership as they learn the congregation and community
- ✅ Participate actively in ministry alongside your pastor rather than expecting them to do everything alone
- ✅ Use your prophetic voice to speak on social issues like affordable housing, voter suppression, poverty, education inequality, and gun violence
- ✅ Teach your people consistently so they can understand and apply the word of God
- ✅ Stand in the gap as an intercessor for your pastor, your church, and your community

## Keep In Mind

- God calls ordinary people to accomplish extraordinary things in the funeral stopping business
- Pastoring is a calling, not a job—hirelings run at the first sight of trouble, but called shepherds remain
- Your pastor is not a performer, a savior, or a lone ranger—he is a shepherd who needs your prayers, patience, and participation
- The church is made up of flawed people seeking the Great Physician, so expect imperfection in yourself and others
- Prayer changes things—Moses stood in the gap and God relented from destruction, proving that intercessors can change the mind of God
- Your words matter—death and life are in the power of the tongue, so choose to speak life over your assignment
- God's hand is on your life and He will keep you, fight your battles, and complete the good work He started in you
- When you feel weak or alone, remember that God is your strength and He will never leave you nor forsake you

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not put cameras on church property to catch people doing wrong—instead, build relationships with the community so they know the pastor cares about them
- Be cautious about who you share sensitive ministry challenges with, but remain transparent with those you trust
- Protect your pastor and his family's privacy—they live in a glass house and are under constant scrutiny
- When dealing with dangerous situations or people, seek wisdom from God and godly counsel before acting alone

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Analyzing why a person is in their condition instead of helping them first
  **Solution:** Feed the hungry first, then discuss how to get their next meal. Action precedes analysis.
- **Problem:** Being too familiar as a pastor and losing the respect necessary for effective leadership
  **Solution:** Be firm, be frank, be friendly, but never be familiar. Maintain appropriate boundaries while remaining approachable.
- **Problem:** Trying to do ministry alone without the support of the congregation
  **Solution:** Remember that you did not call the pastor to do your job. Participate alongside him in carrying out God's great commission.
- **Problem:** Losing sight of your identity as an intercessor when you become consumed with the title of first lady or pastor
  **Solution:** Serve faithfully, sow seeds continually, and stand steadfast in prayer, ensuring you never neglect your own soul or your primary assignment from God.
- **Problem:** Preaching without teaching, assuming the congregation understands the Word
  **Solution:** Never stop teaching your people. They cannot understand your sermon if you do not teach them the foundational Word of God ahead of time.
- **Problem:** Becoming discouraged when ministry ideas do not take off as hoped
  **Solution:** Trust God's process and timing. Anoint your knees to pray about it, your hands to hold on to God's word, and your mind to make decisions that align with His will.
