# It's Only a Test

## Overview

This sermon is the second week of the "Let's Go Back to School" series at MOPBC (Montala Primitive Baptist Church) / Mount Olive. Using Genesis 22 and the story of Abraham and Isaac, the sermon teaches that life's trials are not random attacks but divinely allowed tests designed to reveal whether the lessons of faith—such as obedience, patience, and trust—have taken root in the believer's heart. The core message is that God is the teacher, life is the classroom, the Bible is the textbook, and faith will be tested—but with God's help, every believer can pass the test.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are going through a trial, test, or difficult season and need biblical perspective
- When you do not understand why something is happening and need reassurance that God is still in control
- When you need to strengthen your trust in God's character even when His instructions or timing do not make sense
- When you want to learn how to respond to life's challenges with faith rather than fear

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand that every test is not an attack on you

Tests reveal what you have already learned rather than teaching something entirely new. When a trial comes, resist the assumption that God is against you. Instead, recognize that the test is showing you whether the lessons of obedience, patience, and trust have taken root in your heart. Ask yourself specific questions such as: Where am I struggling to trust God? What area of my life am I holding back from Him? What has God been teaching me recently that I need to apply now?

### Step 2: Let the test challenge what you have learned

A test will challenge what you have learned. Like Abraham, you may be asked to surrender the very thing God promised you. Do not quit when the test asks for your deepest trust. Instead, remind yourself of the specific lessons God has already taught you about obedience, promise, patience, and faithfulness through your past experiences. Choose to live out those lessons under pressure.

### Step 3: Move forward even when you do not understand the test

Tests often have questions that you cannot fully understand. Abraham obeyed without knowing the full outcome and without arguing or negotiating with God. You do not need to understand every detail of God's plan or have a complete explanation before you take the next step. Psalms 119:105 teaches that God's word is a lamp to your feet and a light to your path—it gives enough light for the next step, not necessarily the whole journey. Obey the specific instruction God has given you in this moment, even when the full picture is unclear.

### Step 4: Trust the teacher when the test gets quiet

During a test, the teacher gets quiet, and those around you may also become quiet. Do not panic when explanations stop and you are left to rely on what you have already learned. Trust God's character when you cannot understand His hand. Abraham trusted that even if he had to surrender Isaac, God could raise Isaac from the dead. When you do not understand what God is doing, trust God's unchanging character, faithfulness, and love.

### Step 5: Keep moving through the entire test

Abraham traveled three days with the weight of his assignment and did not turn back. Do not quit midway through your test. Continue in obedience even when the journey is long, emotionally heavy, or mentally exhausting. Faith has to keep moving even when God does not explain everything along the way.

### Step 6: Remember that God's provision is already prepared

The provision you need is already prepared for the moment when you obey. Abraham did not see the ram until he was already on the mountain, but God had already prepared it. If you stop early, quit halfway, or refuse to follow through on your assignment, you may miss the provision God has already arranged. Do not give up before you reach the place where God's answer is waiting for you.

### Step 7: Remember that your test has an expiration date

Tests have time limits. You are not going to be tested, crying, struggling, or in the valley forever. God has set a time limit on your current trial, and He will bring you through it. Stop treating temporary trouble like it is your permanent address. Keep pushing, keep fighting, and keep learning, because the test will end.

### Step 8: Take your next step using the Bible as your textbook

Just as a student can find the answers by reading the book, you can find direction, comfort, and answers by reading the Bible. Do not rely solely on quick spiritual fixes, motivational quotes, or advice from others. Read the word for yourself, study the lessons personally, and apply them directly to your situation. The answers to every test you face are in the book.

### Step 9: Declare your closing declaration daily

When facing a test, declare your trust in God. Say: I will not panic during the test. I will remember what God has taught me. I will obey even when I do not understand. I will trust what I cannot see. And I will believe that God has already provided everything that I need. Speak that declaration over your situation as a reminder of who God is and what He has promised.

## Examples

### Example 1: Financial hardship

If your finances are being tested, remember that the test is temporary. Do not panic or give up on God. Obey what you have already learned about stewardship and giving. Trust that God's provision is already prepared, even if you cannot yet see it. Continue moving forward step by step, using Scripture as your guide, and declare that God will make a way out of no way.

### Example 2: Relationship difficulty

When your patience or commitment is being tested in a relationship, resist the urge to quit or retaliate. Instead, remember what God has taught you about love, forgiveness, and grace. Obey even when the situation does not make sense. Trust the teacher, keep moving forward, and believe that God can restore what the enemy meant for harm.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Remember that tests reveal what you have already learned
- ✅ Trust God's character when you cannot understand His hand
- ✅ Obey even when you do not have all the answers
- ✅ Keep moving forward even when God gets quiet
- ✅ Read the Bible daily as your textbook for life's tests
- ✅ Declare your trust in God out loud during difficult seasons
- ✅ Remember that your test has an expiration date
- ✅ Rest in God's presence when you feel overwhelmed

## Keep In Mind

- Tests are temporary seasons, not permanent addresses
- God's provision is already prepared for the moment when you obey
- Faith does not require all the answers before taking the next step
- The same God who allowed the problem already knows the solution
- When you pass the test, you will know God in a deeper and more personal way
- God is the teacher, life is the classroom, the Bible is the textbook, and faith will be tested

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not base your trust solely on what other people say; verify everything against the Bible
- Do not rely on quick spiritual fixes, motivational quotes, or advice from others; read the book for yourself
- Do not give up on your faith because you do not understand the current test
- Do not assume that God has abandoned you during a quiet or difficult season

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Quitting midway through the test because it becomes too difficult or unclear
  **Solution:** Keep moving forward in obedience, remembering that the provision is at the moment of your obedience and the test has an expiration date
- **Problem:** Assuming God is against you because of the hardship you are facing
  **Solution:** Remember that every test is not an attack; it is an opportunity for your faith to grow and for God to reveal His character to you
- **Problem:** Waiting for God to explain everything before taking action
  **Solution:** Faith does not require full understanding. Take the next step in front of you using the light God has already given you through His word
- **Problem:** Comparing your test to someone else's and feeling like yours should be easier
  **Solution:** Every believer has a different test timed by God. Trust the teacher and focus on what God has taught you, not on what others are going through
