# Still Human: Faith in an Age of Artificial Everything

## Overview

Still Human: Faith in an Age of Artificial Everything is a 30-day Bible plan on Bible.com, written by Carey Nieuwhof, for a reader whose work, relationships, sources of information, and judgements about which claims are true are being reshaped by artificial intelligence (AI) and digital life. The plan exists to give that reader a stated basis for stability: God stays constant when circumstances change, and faith is lived through real people, a community that meets in person, character, and persistent prayer. Each lesson in this file groups named days of the 30-day plan and turns the teaching of the grouped days into named actions with named frequencies, named durations, and named people. A reader who completes all 12 lessons has covered all 30 days exactly once as a primary assignment.

## When to Follow Plan Teachings

- When you feel disoriented because news, work, technology, or daily conversation changed faster than you expected, and you need a stated truth about God to write down and read aloud for 7 consecutive days (Lesson 1, Steps 1 and 2).
- When you use an AI chatbot for companionship, comfort, advice, or prayer instead of speaking to a named person, and you want the replacement practice in Lesson 3, Step 3 (0 minutes of chatbot companionship for 14 consecutive days) and Step 4 (named people invited into the freed time slot).
- When you count your close friends and find fewer than 3 names, and you want the step-by-step practice for building friendship in Lesson 5, Steps 1 and 2.
- When a job loss, a job offer, or a pay change forces you to decide whether your job carries your identity, and you need the scoring test and the identity statement in Lesson 7, Steps 1 and 2, which separate identity from employment.
- When you received your full scheduled pay for the current month and you want to build financial margin before a disruption arrives, or when you hold money beyond your stated monthly needs and must decide how much to share, using the needs line in Lesson 8, Step 4.
- When your phone feed decides what you read for more than 60 minutes per day, and you want the device limits in Lesson 9, Step 1 (30 minutes for a social app and 15 minutes for a news app) and the source-removal practice in Lesson 9, Step 2.
- When you produce writing, sermons, school assignments, or work projects, and you need the order rule in Lesson 11, Step 2: your own work first, AI afterward for at most 20 minutes per task and for 4 named purposes only.
- When a person you love has died and a service offers a simulated version of that person, and you need the 3-action grief practice in Lesson 10, Step 4 instead of a simulation.

## Lessons From Plan

### Lesson 1: Stay Anchored in God When the World Changes and God Looks Defeated

Source Days: Day 1, Day 4
Scripture: Day 1 — Psalms 46:1-7; Day 4 — Mark 15:33-47
Teaching in 1 sentence: God stays constant when circumstances change (Day 1), and God worked through the cross at the moment the cross looked like total defeat (Day 4), so a reader treats present disorientation and present despair as an incomplete reading, not as the final reading.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Manager Whose Industry Changed in 1 Quarter (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A manager learns in 1 meeting that AI software will perform 3 of the 8 tasks the manager performed each week. That evening, the manager writes 1 sentence naming 1 unchanging truth about God (the sentence states: God is present as a refuge in trouble, Psalms 46:1-7), reads the sentence aloud at breakfast for the next 7 days, and tells 1 named friend on day 1 what the sentence is, so the friend can ask about the sentence on day 7.

##### Example 2: A Parent Watching a Church Shrink for 2 Years (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A parent watches attendance at the parent's church fall for 2 consecutive years and concludes that God has lost. The parent reads Mark 15:33-47 once, writes the names of 2 disciples who drifted back to ordinary life after the crucifixion, and then writes 1 named area of the parent's own life that looks like defeat (a son who stopped attending church 14 months ago). The parent prays for that 1 named area for 10 minutes per day for 14 days and records, on day 14, every change the parent observed, including the answer "no visible change" when no change occurred.

#### Step 1: Name 1 Unchanging Truth About God in Writing

On the first day you start this lesson, read Psalms 46:1-7 once, in a printed Bible or a Bible app. Write 1 sentence of 20 words or fewer that states 1 truth about God that does not depend on your income, your job title, or your health (for example, a sentence stating that God is a refuge for a person in trouble). Keep the written sentence in 1 place you see before 9:00 a.m. (a phone lock screen, a bathroom mirror, or page 1 of a paper journal).

#### Step 2: Carry the Written Truth for 7 Consecutive Days

For 7 consecutive days, read the sentence from Step 1 aloud once before your first work task of the day. At 8:00 p.m. on each of the 7 days, write 1 line naming the hardest moment of that day and 1 line stating whether the written truth changed what you did in that moment. On day 7, count the lines: you must have 7 hardest-moment lines and 7 response lines; if a day is missing, add the missing day before you start Lesson 1 Step 3.

#### Step 3: Name 1 Area That Looks Like Defeat and Pray for That Area for 14 Days

Write the name of 1 area of your life that currently looks like defeat (1 person, 1 relationship, 1 job search, or 1 church). Read Mark 15:33-47 once before you pray for the first time. Pray aloud for the named area for 10 minutes per day for 14 consecutive days, and after each prayer write the date and the number 1 in a list, so the list reaches 14 entries. On day 14, tell 1 named person (a spouse, a pastor, or a friend you have known for at least 1 year) that you prayed for 14 days and state what you observed, including "no visible change" when that answer is the true answer.

#### Step 4: Review Both Records on Day 21

On day 21, read the 7 response lines from Step 2 and the 14 prayer entries from Step 3 in 1 sitting of at least 20 minutes. Write 2 sentences: sentence 1 names what stayed constant about God across the 21 days, and sentence 2 names the next action you will take in the area that looks like defeat (a phone call to a named person, an application to a named employer, or a visit to a named place), with a calendar date for that action.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Write the truth about God as 1 sentence of 20 words or fewer, so you can read the sentence aloud in under 15 seconds.
- ✅ Pray for the named area for a counted number of days (14 days), so you can state whether you finished instead of guessing.
- ❌ Do not treat a feeling of calm as the test of God's presence; the test in this lesson is the written record of 7 days and 14 days.
- ❌ Do not name 5 areas that look like defeat in Step 3; name 1 area, so the 14 days of prayer stay focused on 1 area.

#### Keep In Mind

- Day 1 teaches that stability rests on God's constancy, not on a promise that trouble will disappear; Psalms 46:1-7 presents God as a refuge in trouble.
- Day 4 teaches that the crucifixion looked like defeat to the disciples who watched the crucifixion; the resurrection is the event that changed the disciples' reading of the crucifixion.
- A reader who reports "no visible change" after 14 days has completed Step 3 correctly; Step 3 measures prayer faithfulness, not outcomes.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not ask an AI chatbot to tell you what God is saying to you about the area that looks like defeat. An AI chatbot generates text; the chatbot does not know you, does not pray, and does not speak for God. Take the named area to God in prayer and to 1 named human being (a pastor, a counsellor, or a friend of at least 1 year's standing).
- Verify every biblical claim an AI tool gives you against a printed Bible: open the cited chapter and verse in the printed Bible and read the full paragraph that contains the cited verse before you act on the claim.
- A person who feels unable to function (unable to sleep for 3 consecutive nights, unable to work for 3 consecutive workdays, or having thoughts of harming himself or herself) must contact a trusted named person, a counsellor, or emergency services (911 in the United States and Canada) immediately, on the same day the person notices the condition, rather than relying on an AI chatbot.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A reader reads Day 1, feels encouraged for 1 evening, and stops, so no written sentence exists when disorientation returns 3 days later.
  **Solution:** Complete Step 1 on the first day: write the 1 sentence and place the sentence where you will see the sentence before 9:00 a.m.
- **Problem:** A reader treats the area that looks like defeat as proof that God has left, and withdraws from church and friends for at least 3 consecutive weeks.
  **Solution:** Follow Day 4's pattern: keep praying for the named area for the counted 14 days in Step 3, keep attending your church's weekly gathering for all 14 days, and tell 1 named person on day 1 that you have started the 14 days.

### Lesson 2: Face the AI Future With Hopeful Realism and Faithful Stewardship

Source Days: Day 2, Day 3
Scripture: Day 2 — Proverbs 16:9; Day 3 — Matthew 24:4-8; Matthew 24:45-51
Teaching in 1 sentence: AI can bring real benefits (Day 2) and real harms (Day 3), only God holds the future (Day 2), and a believer responds to both lists as a steward who prepares instead of a spectator who freezes.

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Teacher Listing Benefits and Harms for 1 School (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A teacher writes 2 lists in 1 sitting of 30 minutes: a benefits list naming 3 benefits AI brings to the teacher's school (faster feedback on student drafts, translation for parents who speak a language the teacher does not speak, and lesson-plan drafting) and a harms list naming 3 harms (students submitting AI-written essays as student work, students trusting wrong AI answers, and students losing practice in writing). The teacher chooses 1 action for the benefits list (use the translation tool for every parent message for 30 days) and 1 action for the harms list (require students to write the first draft of every essay by hand in class for 30 days).

##### Example 2: An Office Worker Preparing for a Job Change (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. An office worker whose employer announced an AI rollout in 90 days writes, within 7 days of the announcement, the names of 2 skills the worker can perform that AI software at the worker's company cannot perform (mediating a dispute between 2 named colleagues, and persuading a named client who distrusts automated service). The worker spends 45 minutes per week for 12 weeks learning 1 named skill (a certificate course with a named provider and a named completion date) and tells 1 named friend the plan in week 1, so the friend can ask for a progress report in week 6 and week 12.

#### Step 1: Write 2 Lists, 5 Items Each, in 1 Sitting

Sit for 30 minutes with a timer running. Write a benefits list of exactly 5 benefits AI could bring to your work, your family, or your church (name the beneficiary of each benefit). Write a harms list of exactly 5 harms AI could bring to the same 3 areas (name the person or group each harm would hit). Stop when both lists reach 5 items; do not start Step 2 with a list shorter than 5 items.

#### Step 2: Assign 1 Preparation Action to Each of the 2 Marked Harms

Read your harms list and mark every harm that could reach you within 12 months. If more than 2 harms qualify, rank the qualifying harms by earliest possible arrival date and mark the 2 with the earliest dates. For each of the 2 marked harms, write 1 preparation action with a calendar date and a named resource (a course with a provider name, a savings action with a dollar amount, or a conversation with a named person). Schedule the first session or first payment for both actions within 7 days of writing the actions.

#### Step 3: State the Stewardship Sentence to 1 Named Person Within 7 Days

Matthew 24:45-51 describes a steward who feeds a household at the household's mealtimes while the master is away. Write 1 sentence naming what has been entrusted to you during the AI transition (your team of a stated number of people, your children by name, or your clients by name) and what feeding those people requires from you each week (1 named action per week). Read the sentence aloud to 1 named person within 7 days and ask that person to repeat the sentence back to you, so you know the person heard the commitment.

#### Step 4: Review the 2 Lists After 90 Days

Set a calendar reminder for day 90. On day 90, spend 20 minutes scoring both lists: mark each benefit that actually arrived (write "arrived" and the date) and each harm that actually arrived (write "arrived" and the date). Write 1 new preparation action for the marked harm with the earliest possible arrival date on the updated list, with a date within the next 30 days.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Name a beneficiary for every benefit and a person or group for every harm, so neither list stays abstract.
- ✅ Put a calendar date on every preparation action within 7 days of writing the action.
- ❌ Do not repeat an AI tool's prediction about your job as a fact; a prediction from an AI tool is a generated estimate, and Proverbs 16:9 states that a person plans a course while God establishes the person's steps.
- ❌ Do not spend the 30 minutes of Step 1 reading news about AI; Step 1 is writing your own 2 lists for your own work, family, and church.

#### Keep In Mind

- Day 2 qualifies its optimistic list in 2 stated ways: only God knows and holds the future, and the optimistic outcomes depend on human beings' choices and actions, which the list does not guarantee.
- Day 3 presents its harms list to prepare readers, not to frighten readers; preparation is the stated purpose of the list.
- Hopeful realism in this lesson means holding both lists at the same time: 5 benefits and 5 harms, written in the same 30-minute sitting.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not enter passwords, financial account numbers, your home address, or another person's private information (medical details, salary, or private messages) into an AI tool when you research AI benefits and harms. Research questions need topic words only; research questions never need your credentials or another person's private data.
- Verify AI-generated claims about job-loss statistics, product capabilities, and Bible content against 2 named sources before you repeat a claim: 1 primary source (the employer's own announcement, the product maker's own documentation, or a printed Bible) and 1 named human being who works in the field the claim describes.
- Do not present an AI-written forecast, sermon, or pastoral letter as your own human work or as a human expert's work. Label AI-assisted text as AI-assisted wherever a reader, a student, or a church member will rely on the text.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A reader reads only Day 2, adopts the optimistic list, and makes no preparation, so a job change 6 months later arrives with no savings, no new skill, and no plan.
  **Solution:** Complete Step 1 in full: the harms list of 5 items is a required half of the exercise, not an optional half.
- **Problem:** A reader reads only Day 3, adopts the harms list, and freezes: the reader stops learning, stops applying for work, and stops serving at church.
  **Solution:** Complete Step 2: each of the 2 marked harms gets 1 preparation action with a date, and the first session or payment for both actions happens within 7 days.

### Lesson 3: Reject Loneliness and Choose Real Conversation Over Machine Companionship

Source Days: Day 5, Day 7, Day 8
Scripture: Day 5 — Ecclesiastes 4:9-10; Day 7 — Genesis 2:18; Day 8 — Romans 12:9-13
Teaching in 1 sentence: Digital access is not community (Day 5), human beings were designed for relationship with God and with each other (Day 7), and a chatbot companion that never rejects you also removes the risk through which real intimacy forms (Day 8).

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Graduate Who Talks to a Chatbot Every Night (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A graduate aged 24 talks to an AI companion app for 45 minutes each night and has not spoken to a friend in person for 19 days. The graduate deletes the companion app on a Monday, writes the names of 3 people (a former roommate, a cousin, and a co-worker), and invites the first named person for a 60-minute walk within 48 hours. The graduate keeps a paper list on the refrigerator: each evening for 14 days, the graduate writes the number of minutes spent talking with a human being that day, and the 14-day list must show a human-conversation entry on at least 10 of the 14 days.

##### Example 2: A Father Who Answers His Children Through a Screen (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A father notices during 1 dinner that he answered his daughter's 3 questions while looking at his phone. For the next 14 days, the father places his phone in a kitchen drawer from 6:00 p.m. to 7:30 p.m., eats dinner with his family at the table, and starts 1 conversation per dinner by asking each child 1 question that requires an answer longer than 1 word (the question names a best moment and a hardest moment of the child's day). On day 14, the father asks his daughter whether the drawer rule changed dinner, and writes her answer in 1 sentence.

#### Step 1: Count Your Human Conversation Minutes for 3 Days

For 3 consecutive days, write down every conversation you have with a human being that lasts 5 minutes or longer, with the person's name, the start time, and the number of minutes. Separately, write the number of minutes you spent in AI chatbot conversations on each of the 3 days (your phone's screen-time report gives the AI app minutes). On the evening of day 3, write both totals: total human minutes for 3 days and total chatbot minutes for 3 days.

#### Step 2: Hold 1 Avoided Conversation in Person Within 48 Hours

Name 1 conversation you have avoided for at least 7 days (a disagreement with a named person, an apology owed to a named person, or a question you have not asked a named person). Within 48 hours of naming the conversation, hold the conversation in person; if the person lives more than 100 kilometres away, hold the conversation by phone or by video call. Do not hold the conversation by text message, by email, or through an AI tool. Allow 30 minutes for the conversation and state, in your first 2 sentences, why you asked for the conversation.

#### Step 3: Set a Chatbot Companionship Limit of 0 Minutes for 14 Days

For 14 consecutive days, spend 0 minutes using an AI companion app or a chatbot in a companion role (comfort, friendship, or romantic conversation). AI use for named work tasks (drafting a work email you then edit, checking a fact you then verify) is a separate category and is counted in Step 1's chatbot total, not banned by this step. If you open a companion app during the 14 days, write the date, the time, and the trigger (loneliness, boredom, or a conflict with a named person) in a paper journal, and restart the count at day 1 the next morning.

#### Step 4: Invite 2 Named People Into the Time the Chatbot Occupied

List the time slot the companion app occupied (for Example 1, the slot was 45 minutes each night). In week 1, invite 1 named person to fill 1 session in that slot (a walk, a meal, or a phone call of at least 30 minutes). In week 2, invite a second named person to fill 1 session in that slot. At the end of week 2, write which of the 2 invitations you will repeat weekly, and put the repeated session on your calendar as a weekly event with no end date.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Measure before you change: complete the 3-day count in Step 1 before you set the 14-day limit in Step 3.
- ✅ Replace a removed habit with a named person at a named time in the same slot; an empty slot invites the removed habit back.
- ❌ Do not count text messages as conversation for Step 1; Step 1 counts spoken conversation of 5 minutes or longer with a named person.
- ❌ Do not ask an AI companion whether you should end a human friendship; take that question to the friend, to a pastor, or to a counsellor.

#### Keep In Mind

- Day 5 states that a generation able to contact any person whose phone number or account name the generation has, at any hour, reports severe isolation; contact capacity and companionship are 2 different things.
- Day 7 grounds the practice in design: Genesis 2:18 records God stating that a human being alone is not good.
- Day 8 states the mechanism: a relationship with 0 possibility of rejection also removes the risk through which intimacy, healing, and acceptance form.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not enter your home address, your workplace address, your daily routine with times, your children's names and schools, passwords, or financial account numbers into an AI companion app. A companion app stores conversation text; treat every sentence you type into a companion app as a sentence stored on a company's server.
- Do not treat an AI companion's statement of care as a human being's care. If a chatbot says words of affection or agreement, the words are generated text; no person has chosen you, knows you, or will come to help you in an emergency.
- A person in a mental-health crisis (thoughts of harming himself or herself, or a stated plan to act on such thoughts) must contact a trusted named person, a counsellor, or emergency services (911 in the United States and Canada) immediately, and must not substitute an AI chatbot conversation for that contact.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A reader replaces a companion app with 4 hours of video scrolling each night, so loneliness stays unchanged while screen time rises.
  **Solution:** Fill the slot with a person, per Step 4: 1 named person in week 1 and a second named person in week 2, each for at least 30 minutes in the slot the app occupied.
- **Problem:** A reader holds the avoided conversation by text message, reads the reply as hostile when the words of the reply do not state hostility, and ends the friendship over the hostility the reader supplied.
  **Solution:** Follow Step 2 exactly: in person, or by phone or video when distance exceeds 100 kilometres, with 30 minutes allowed and the reason stated in the first 2 sentences.

### Lesson 4: Be Known in Loyal and Faithful Relationships, and Repair Ruptures

Source Days: Day 6, Day 9, Day 10, Day 11
Scripture: Day 6 — Ruth 1:16-18; Day 9 — Hebrews 13:1-5; Day 10 — Psalms 103:1-12; Day 11 — Romans 12:14-16
Teaching in 1 sentence: Ruth models loyalty that stays (Day 6), lifelong marital fidelity stands against treating people as consumable products (Day 9), rupture followed by repair is a feature of real relationships (Day 10), and care for a struggling person belongs to the whole church community, with the stated set of cases in Step 5 (a stated diagnosis, suicidal thoughts, addiction the person has tried to stop and resumed at least 3 times, or abuse in the home) assigned to professionals (Day 11).

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Friend Who Stays After a Public Failure (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A man's business fails and 2 of his clients post critical comments online. A friend who has known the man for 6 years phones the man on the day the comments appear, visits the man's home within 3 days, and phones again every Sunday at 7:00 p.m. for 8 consecutive Sundays. The friend asks 1 question per call ("What is the hardest part of this week?") and does not offer a solution during the first 10 minutes of any call.

##### Example 2: A Married Couple Repairing a Serious Argument (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A husband and wife argue about a purchase of 900 dollars that the husband made without telling his wife. Within 24 hours, the husband states the full fact (the item, the price of 900 dollars, and the date of the purchase) without editing the fact. The wife states, in 2 sentences, what the secrecy did to her trust. The couple agrees on 1 rule in writing (any purchase above 200 dollars requires a conversation before the purchase) and reviews the rule once after 30 days.

#### Step 1: Name 2 People Who Know You and 1 Person You Will Let Know You More

Write the names of 2 people who know your current struggles by name (not your public image). If you can write only 1 name or 0 names, write that number honestly at the top of the page. Choose 1 of the named people and tell that person, within 7 days, 1 struggle you have hidden for at least 30 days, in a conversation of at least 20 minutes, in person or by phone.

#### Step 2: Make 1 Loyalty Contact Every Week for 8 Weeks

Choose 1 person in a hard season (a stated diagnosis, a divorce in progress, a job loss within the past 90 days, or a bereavement within the past 12 months). Contact that person once per week for 8 consecutive weeks, on the same weekday and at the same time (for example, Sunday at 7:00 p.m.), by phone or in person, for at least 10 minutes per contact. Ask the same 1 opening question each week and write, after each contact, 1 line recording what the person said the hardest part was.

#### Step 3: Repair 1 Rupture Within 7 Days With 3 Named Parts

Name 1 rupture with a named person that is less than 12 months old and unrepaired. Within 7 days, speak to that person in person or by phone and deliver 3 parts in order: part 1, your action stated as a fact with a date; part 2, the effect your action had on the person, stated in the person's terms after you ask the person to name the effect; part 3, the specific change you will make, with a date by which the person can see the change. Do not deliver any of the 3 parts by text message.

#### Step 4: Guard Marital Fidelity With 2 Stated Barriers

If you are married, set these 2 barriers within 7 days and tell your spouse both barriers by name: barrier 1, delete every pornography app, account, and saved site from every device you own, and hand your phone to your spouse for a 5-minute check on the day you delete them; barrier 2, install a named accountability tool or name a named accountability partner who receives a weekly report of your device use. If you are not married, apply barrier 1 within 7 days and give barrier 2's weekly report to a named friend or mentor of at least 1 year's standing.

#### Step 5: Offer Care to 1 Named Person and Accept Care From 1 Named Person Within 14 Days

Within 14 days, do both halves of this step. Half 1: choose 1 person who is struggling (name the struggle in 1 sentence) and spend at least 30 minutes with that person listening and praying aloud for that person, in the person's home, in your home, or by phone. Half 2: ask 1 named person (a pastor, an elder, a counsellor, or a friend) to listen to you and pray aloud for you for at least 30 minutes, and accept the meeting when the person offers a date within the 14 days. If your struggle involves a stated diagnosis, suicidal thoughts, addiction that you have tried to stop and resumed at least 3 times, or abuse in your home, schedule the professional appointment first (with a licensed counsellor, a doctor, or a crisis service) and treat the community meeting as an addition, never as a replacement.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ State your own action as a dated fact in part 1 of a repair; a repair that starts with the other person's faults restarts the rupture.
- ✅ Keep loyalty contacts on the same weekday and time for 8 weeks, so the person you chose under Step 2's 4 stated cases (a stated diagnosis, a divorce in progress, a job loss within the past 90 days, or a bereavement within the past 12 months) can rely on the contact arriving.
- ❌ Do not use an AI chatbot as your accountability partner for pornography use; an accountability partner must be a human being who can ask you a question you did not script.
- ❌ Do not cancel a person permanently for a first offence that the person has acknowledged; Day 6 contrasts staying with the culture of cancelling.

#### Keep In Mind

- Day 6 holds up Ruth 1:16-18 as loyalty that stays with a person after the person states a choice to leave; visibility (followers, recognition) is not the same state as being known.
- Day 9 states that pornography turns people into consumable products, and that AI makes the product interactive, which increases the relational damage rather than reducing the damage.
- Day 10 states that the biblical figures Day 10 names, other than Jesus, had failures in relationships, so a history of failed relationships is the starting point Day 10 assumes for repair, not a disqualification from repair.
- Day 11 assigns care to the whole church community, and reserves a stated set of cases (diagnosis, crisis, addiction, abuse) for professionals.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Never send another person's private information (a friend's confession, a spouse's messages, a church member's prayer request) to an AI tool for summarising or advice without that person's explicit permission, given in words, for that specific use.
- Do not ask an AI chatbot to act as your therapist, your marriage counsellor, or your pastor. A chatbot cannot sit with you, cannot notice your state in a room, and cannot call emergency services for you. Use a licensed counsellor for therapy, and a named pastor or elder for pastoral care.
- A person experiencing abuse in a home, or a person with thoughts of harming himself or herself, must contact emergency services (911 in the United States and Canada), a crisis line, or a trusted named person immediately, on the day the danger is present, before starting any step in this lesson.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A reader confuses an audience with friendship: 2,000 followers see the reader's posts, and 0 people know the reader's current struggle.
  **Solution:** Run Step 1's count. If the count of people who know your struggles by name is 0 or 1, complete Step 1's 20-minute conversation within 7 days.
- **Problem:** A couple leaves a rupture unrepaired for 6 months because each partner waits for the other partner to speak first, and the distance grows each month.
  **Solution:** Follow Step 3's 7-day deadline and 3-part order, regardless of which partner caused the larger share of the rupture; the partner who reads this lesson first makes the call.

### Lesson 5: Build Close Friendship on Purpose, Because Friendship Is a Learnable Skill

Source Days: Day 12, Day 13, Day 15
Scripture: Day 12 — Proverbs 17:17; Day 13 — Romans 16:1-16; Day 15 — Matthew 22:36-40
Teaching in 1 sentence: An inner circle means 3 to 5 very close friends valued for themselves and not for the advantage the friends provide (Day 12), friendship grows through repeated small invitations and shared activities (Day 13), and the quality of a person's relationships, not income or status, is the factor a Harvard study linked to the outcome Day 15 calls a good life (Day 15).

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Woman Whose Inner-Circle Count Is 0 (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A woman aged 38 writes her circles on paper and finds 40 acquaintances, 6 good friends, and 0 people in the inner circle of 3 to 5. Within 48 hours she invites 1 of the 6 good friends (a former colleague) for coffee at a named cafe at 10:00 a.m. on Saturday. The coffee lasts 50 minutes. She invites the same person again 14 days later, and at the second coffee she names 1 true struggle (her father's illness) instead of discussing work for the full 50 minutes.

##### Example 2: A Man Who Gives Most of His Waking Hours to a Platform (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A man tracks his hours for 7 days and finds 51 hours on work and platform posting, 3 hours with friends, and 2 hours with his children without a phone present. He moves 2 hours per week for 8 weeks: he cancels 1 weekly work call that has no decision attached, shortens his Sunday posting session from 90 minutes to 30 minutes, and spends the recovered 2 hours on a Saturday morning activity with his children and a monthly dinner with 2 named friends at his home on the first Friday of each month.

#### Step 1: Draw Your 3 Circles and Write the Names

On paper, draw 3 circles and label the circles: acquaintances (no number required), good friends (write every name), and inner circle (3 to 5 names maximum). Write the count for the inner circle as a single number at the top of the page. A spouse does not count toward the inner-circle number, per Day 12. If the inner-circle count is 0, write "0" and underline the number; the number is your starting measurement, not a verdict on your worth.

#### Step 2: Run the Invitation Pattern 3 Times With 1 Person

Choose 1 person from your good-friends circle. Invitation 1, within 48 hours of Step 1: invite the person for coffee or a 30-minute walk, at a named place and a named time. Invitation 2, 14 days after invitation 1, if invitation 1 went well (both people stayed for the agreed time and neither person cancelled): repeat the coffee or walk. Invitation 3, 14 days after invitation 2: invite the person to a shared activity that lasts at least 90 minutes (cooking a meal in 1 home, a sport both people play, or a project both people work on). Shared activity is the stage Day 13 identifies as the stage where intimacy grows.

#### Step 3: Introduce 1 Person to Your Community Within 30 Days

Name 1 person who told you the person has no community (a new colleague, a neighbour who moved in within the past 12 months, or a person who asked you how friendship works). Within 30 days, invite that person to 1 gathering you already attend (a church group, a team meal, or a friends' dinner with a stated date), introduce the person by name to at least 3 people at the gathering, and sit with the person for the first 20 minutes so the person is not left standing alone.

#### Step 4: Audit Your Waking Hours Every Sunday for 8 Weeks

Each Sunday for 8 consecutive weeks, spend 10 minutes writing 3 numbers from the previous 7 days: hours given to work and platform activity, hours given to friends in person or by phone, and hours given to family with no phone in your hand. In this step, your waking hours means the hours counted in those 3 numbers. If the work number exceeds the friends number plus the family number for 2 consecutive Sundays, move 2 hours in the next week from a named work or platform task to a named person, and write the task you dropped and the person who received the 2 hours.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Repeat invitations to the same person at 14-day intervals; friendship in Day 13 grows through repetition with 1 person, not through 1 meeting each with 5 people.
- ✅ Value a friend for the friend himself or herself: state, in Step 1's notes, 1 quality of each inner-circle friend that has nothing to do with money, access, or usefulness.
- ❌ Do not count a spouse as 1 of the 3 to 5 inner-circle friends; Day 12 excludes a spouse from that count.
- ❌ Do not treat a transaction-based contact (a person you call only when you need a service, an introduction, or a discount) as a close friend in your Step 1 count.

#### Keep In Mind

- Day 12 uses anthropologist Robin Dunbar's circles: a wide acquaintance circle, a smaller good-friends circle, and an inner circle of 3 to 5 very close friends.
- Day 13 points to Romans 16:1-16, where Paul greets friends by name, as a picture of friendships built through shared mission.
- Day 15 rests on the Harvard Study of Adult Development, which found relationship quality, not income, achievement, or status, to be the key factor in the outcome Day 15 calls a good life.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not publish your inner-circle list, your friends' struggles, or your audit numbers to a social platform or an AI tool. The lists in this lesson contain other people's names and private states; keep the paper in your home and keep digital copies in a file only you can open.
- When you invite a person you know slightly (a new colleague or a neighbour), meet in a public place for the first 2 meetings (a cafe, a park, or a church hall), tell 1 person you trust where you will be and at what time, and leave when you choose to leave.
- Do not use an AI tool to compose a message that pretends to be personal knowledge of a friend (a condolence message, an apology, or a birthday message that names shared memories the AI tool invented). Invented shared memories are false statements; write the message yourself or state the facts yourself in a shorter message.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A reader's inner-circle count is 0 and the reader concludes the reader is unlovable, so the reader starts no invitations.
  **Solution:** Treat 0 as a measurement. Day 13 states friendship is a learnable skill; start Step 2 within 48 hours with 1 coffee invitation to 1 person from the good-friends circle.
- **Problem:** A reader's calendar is full of people, and every relationship is transaction-based, so the reader feels surrounded and unknown at the same time.
  **Solution:** In Step 1, mark every name with a T if you contact the person only when you need a service or an introduction. Choose 1 name with no T and run Step 2's 3 invitations with that person.

### Lesson 6: Become Fully Human With God and Others, and Gather in Person to Encounter God

Source Days: Day 14, Day 22
Scripture: Day 14 — Proverbs 4:20-21; Day 22 — Isaiah 6:1-5; John 4:1-6
Teaching in 1 sentence: Becoming fully human happens as God gives life to every part of a person, with humanity made in God's image and Jesus as fully divine and fully human (Day 14), and in-person worship offers an encounter with God's transcendence that information delivered by a screen does not reproduce (Day 22).

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Student Whose Faith Is Entirely a Podcast Feed (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A student listens to 5 sermon podcasts per week (250 minutes per week) and has not attended a gathering in person for 11 weeks. The student keeps 2 podcasts per week, deletes 3 podcast subscriptions, and attends the student's church gathering in person on the next Sunday, arriving 15 minutes before the start time and staying 15 minutes after the end time to speak with 2 named people. The student repeats the in-person attendance for 4 consecutive Sundays before judging the change.

##### Example 2: A Couple Watching a Service From a Sofa for 2 Years (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A couple streamed a church service from home for 104 Sundays because streaming required no travel and no childcare arrangement for the couple's 2 small children. The couple chooses 2 Sundays per month to attend in person for the next 3 months (6 attendances), decides in the week before each attendance where the 2 children will be during the gathering (in the gathering with the couple, in the church's children's class, or with a named adult carer), and on the 2 remaining Sundays per month watches together at a set time with phones in another room. After the sixth in-person attendance, the couple writes 2 sentences comparing what each partner noticed in the room that a screen did not deliver.

#### Step 1: Compare Screen Hours and Face-to-Face Hours for 7 Days

For 7 consecutive days, record 2 numbers each evening: minutes of screen-mediated interaction (video, social feeds, streamed content, chatbot conversation) and minutes of face-to-face interaction with a human being in the same room. On the evening of day 7, add both columns and write both totals. Day 14 asks for exactly this comparison; the larger total is the formation source you must address in Step 2.

#### Step 2: Attend a Gathering in Person Once per Week for 4 Weeks

Attend your church's main weekly gathering in person once per week for 4 consecutive weeks; if you do not belong to a church, choose 1 church in your town before week 1 and attend that church's main weekly gathering for the 4 weeks. Arrive 15 minutes before the start time. During each gathering, sing when the congregation sings, stand when the congregation stands, and speak to at least 2 people by name after the gathering, for at least 3 minutes per person. Record each attendance with a date; if you miss a week through illness or travel, add a fifth week so the record reaches 4 attendances.

#### Step 3: Position Yourself for Encounter With 3 Fixed Actions Before Each Gathering

Before each of the 4 gatherings in Step 2, complete these 3 actions. Action 1, the night before: read Isaiah 6:1-5 once in a printed Bible. Action 2, on the morning of the gathering: pray aloud for 5 minutes, asking God to show you 1 true thing about God that you did not come to hear. Action 3, during the gathering: write, on paper, 1 sentence when a statement, a song line, or a reading strikes you as addressing you directly, and keep the sentence for Step 4.

#### Step 4: Review the 4 Sentences After the Fourth Gathering

After the fourth attendance, read the 4 sentences from Step 3 in 1 sitting of at least 15 minutes. Write whether the 4 sentences share a theme (name the theme in 5 words or fewer) or show no shared theme (write "no shared theme"). Tell 1 named person (a pastor, an elder, or a friend from the same church) what you recorded, in a conversation of at least 15 minutes within 7 days of the review.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Judge the in-person change after 4 attendances, not after 1 attendance; a single awkward Sunday is a sample of 1.
- ✅ Keep streamed content as a supplement for weeks when illness, travel, or a work roster prevents attendance, and return to the room on the next available Sunday.
- ❌ Do not treat information about God as an encounter with God; Day 22 separates the 2 categories and assigns information to the screen and transcendence to the gathered room.
- ❌ Do not attend with earbuds in and a phone open; the 2 named conversations after each gathering are part of the practice, not an extra.

#### Keep In Mind

- Day 14 states that screens mediate a larger share of a person's interaction in an AI age than in earlier periods, calls that mediation dehumanising, and presents the church's relationships, in which members meet in person and state the truth to each other, as a witness that people living in an age of synthetic substitutes may look for.
- Day 22 describes God as immanent (near and accessible) and transcendent (so different from human beings that Isaiah's encounter in Isaiah 6:1-5 left Isaiah undone).
- The goal of this lesson is positioning, not manufacturing an experience: Steps 2 and 3 place you in the room, prepared; the encounter itself is God's action.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not let an AI tool write your testimony, your conversion story, or your prayer in your voice and then present the text as your own words to your church. A testimony is a factual claim about your life; an AI tool inventing details of your life produces false claims, even when the invented details sound plausible.
- Verify any sermon, teaching summary, or verse quotation an AI tool produces against a printed Bible and against the church's own recording or notes before you pass the material to another person. AI tools produce wrong verse references and wrong quotations; passing an unchecked error to a group you teach (a Bible study group or a class) makes you the source of the error.
- Protect other worshippers: do not record, photograph, or upload another person's prayer, testimony, or moment of distress during a gathering without that person's permission, given in words, for that specific use.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A reader consumes 250 minutes of Christian content per week and attends nothing, then reports feeling spiritually informed and spiritually empty at the same time.
  **Solution:** Follow Steps 2 and 3: cut streamed content to 2 items per week for 4 weeks and attend in person 4 times, with the 3 preparation actions before each attendance.
- **Problem:** A reader attends once, finds the gathering ordinary, and concludes in-person worship offers nothing a screen cannot deliver.
  **Solution:** Complete the full sample: 4 attendances, 4 recorded sentences, and the Step 4 review with a named person before drawing any conclusion.

### Lesson 7: Put Your Identity and Your Purpose in God, Not in Your Job or Your Money

Source Days: Day 16, Day 17
Scripture: Day 16 — 1 John 5:20-21; Day 17 — Colossians 3:23-24
Teaching in 1 sentence: Work and money become idols when a job loss becomes an identity loss (Day 16), and a life with everything money can buy can still be empty, so purpose is found by naming the work you would do even if you knew you would fail, and by receiving identity from belonging to God (Day 17).

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Lawyer Who Introduces Himself by His Firm (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A lawyer has introduced himself for 15 years by naming his firm before naming anything else about himself. For 30 days he introduces himself in every new setting with 2 facts that are not his job (the town he lives in and the football team he coaches on Saturdays for his son's age group). At the end of the 30 days he writes 1 paragraph answering 1 question: "If the firm closed tomorrow, which 3 parts of my week would still be mine?" His answer names coaching, his church group on Wednesdays, and breakfast with his wife on Sundays.

##### Example 2: A Founder With Enough Money to Stop Working (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A founder aged 40 sold a company 18 months ago, travels for 6 months of each year, and states in a conversation with a friend that the founder does not know what to do with the founder's life. The founder writes an answer to Day 17's diagnostic question: "The work I would do if I knew I would fail is mentoring first-time founders in my city, because 7 of the 9 founders I know failed alone." The founder then schedules 2 mentoring meetings of 60 minutes each within 14 days, with 2 named founders, and repeats the meetings monthly for 6 months.

#### Step 1: Score Your Identity Attachments on a 1-to-5 Scale With Stated Endpoints

Write 4 items: your job title, your income level, the possessions your income buys, and your professional reputation. Score each item from 1 to 5, where 1 means "losing this item for 1 month would not change how I describe myself" and 5 means "losing this item for 1 month would make me unable to describe who I am". Any item scored 4 or 5 is, in the language of Day 16, functioning as an idol for you; write the word "idol-risk" next to every item scored 4 or 5.

#### Step 2: Write Your Identity Statement Without a Job Word

Write 3 sentences that state who you are, and use 0 job titles, 0 company names, 0 salary figures, and 0 possession names in the 3 sentences. Sentence 1 states your belonging to God in your own words. Sentence 2 names 2 relationships (2 named people) that would remain if your job ended tomorrow. Sentence 3 names 1 contribution you make that no employer assigns to you. Read the 3 sentences aloud to 1 named person within 7 days.

#### Step 3: Answer the Failure Question in Writing Within 7 Days

Within 7 days of Step 1, write an answer of at least 100 words to this question: "What would I do if I knew I would fail?" The answer must name 1 activity, 1 group of people the activity serves (named by role and, where you can, by name), and 1 reason the activity matters to you when success is removed from the answer. Then schedule 1 session of that activity, lasting at least 60 minutes, within 14 days of writing the answer.

#### Step 4: Work 1 Task per Week as Service to God for 4 Weeks

Colossians 3:23-24 directs work toward God rather than toward human approval. For 4 consecutive weeks, choose 1 ordinary task per week from your paid work, your volunteer work, or your household work (a report, a repair, a lesson, a delivery route, or a household repair) and, before starting the task, state aloud the name of the person the task serves and the standard you will meet because God is the one you serve. After the task, write 1 line: the task name, the person served, and whether you met the stated standard (write "met" or "missed" and, for "missed", the 1 change you will make next week).

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Separate the 2 questions in every job decision: "What does this job pay and require?" and "Who am I if this job ends?" Write both answers before you accept, refuse, or grieve a job.
- ✅ Keep 1 weekly activity in your calendar that produces no income and no platform (coaching, mentoring, visiting, serving), so your week contains a standing proof that your identity is wider than your job.
- ❌ Do not measure a week's worth by income earned or by audience growth; Day 17's founder had both measures at a maximum and reported emptiness.
- ❌ Do not wait for a job loss to write the identity statement in Step 2; write the statement while you are employed, when you can write the statement calmly.

#### Keep In Mind

- Day 16 states that work has provided a living since Genesis; work itself is not the problem in this lesson, and identity placed in work is the problem.
- Day 16 uses John Calvin's image of the human mind manufacturing idols; an idol in this lesson means anything scored 4 or 5 in Step 1 that carries your sense of worth.
- Day 17 reframes financial freedom: having enough money to never work again removes a constraint and exposes the purpose question, rather than answering the purpose question.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not enter your salary, your bank account numbers, your employer's confidential figures, or a severance offer into an AI tool to ask whether you should take a job or leave a job. An AI tool cannot know your household's obligations, and the figures you enter are stored outside your control. Run the numbers on paper or in a spreadsheet stored on your own device, and take the decision to a named person (a spouse, a financial adviser you pay, or a pastor).
- Do not let an AI tool write your resignation letter, your reference request, or your statement of faith for an application without your line-by-line review. An AI draft can invent dates, qualifications, and commitments in your name; you sign the letter, so you own every sentence in the letter.
- A person who loses a job and, within 30 days, cannot sleep for 3 consecutive nights, cannot get out of bed on 3 consecutive days, or has thoughts of harming himself or herself must contact a trusted named person, a counsellor, a doctor, or emergency services (911 in the United States and Canada) immediately, not after the job search improves.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A reader equates "what I do" with "who I am", so a restructuring announcement at work produces panic about worth, not only concern about income.
  **Solution:** Complete Step 1's scoring today. Any item scored 4 or 5 gets a counterweight within 7 days: write Step 2's 3 sentences and add 1 no-income weekly activity from the Best Practices list.
- **Problem:** A reader chases a larger income for 10 years, reaches the income, and finds the emptiness Day 17 describes, because the reader never named a purpose that survives failure.
  **Solution:** Answer Step 3's failure question in at least 100 words within 7 days and schedule the first 60-minute session of the named activity within 14 days, while income decisions are still open.

### Lesson 8: Build Financial Margin in Good Years and Share Money Beyond Your Needs

Source Days: Day 18, Day 19
Scripture: Day 18 — Genesis 41:47-57; Acts 2:42-47; Day 19 — Acts 4:32-35
Teaching in 1 sentence: Joseph stored grain during 7 plentiful years to carry a nation through 7 famine years, and a household copies that pattern by building a buffer while the household is employed and receiving pay on each scheduled payday (Day 18), while the early church in Acts 4:32-35 treated possessions as shared so that no person among them was needy, which challenges a reader to treat money beyond stated needs as money to share (Day 19).

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Nurse Building a 3-Month Buffer (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A nurse earns a stable salary and holds 400 dollars of savings. The nurse lists monthly essential expenses in 6 categories (rent, food, transport, utilities, insurance, and phone) totalling 2,600 dollars per month, sets a buffer target of 3 months (7,800 dollars), and moves 300 dollars to a separate savings account on each payday for 26 paydays. The nurse cuts 2 named expenses to fund the transfer (a streaming bundle at 45 dollars per month and takeaway lunches at 160 dollars per month) and writes the target and the transfer amount on the refrigerator.

##### Example 2: A Household Whose Village Made a Fund Seem Unnecessary (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A household studies Day 19's Romanian village pattern, where neighbours carried each other through emergencies, and decides to build margin as a community instead of alone. Five households from 1 church agree in writing to a shared practice: each household gives 50 dollars per month to a named benevolence fund administered by 2 named treasurers, any household can request help from the fund with 1 phone call to either treasurer, and the 5 households review the fund balance together every 3 months. In month 8, the fund pays a 900-dollar car repair for 1 household, and that household returns to contributing 50 dollars per month in month 9.

#### Step 1: Calculate Your Monthly Essential Total From 6 Named Categories

Write your monthly essential expenses in exactly these 6 categories: housing, food, transport, utilities, insurance, and phone/internet. Add the 6 figures and write the total; that total is 1 month of margin for your household. Write 0 for any category you do not have, and write the actual figure from your last full month's statements for every category you do have.

#### Step 2: Set a Buffer Target of 3 Months and a Payday Transfer Amount

Multiply your Step 1 total by 3 and write the result as your buffer target. Subtract your current savings and write the gap. Divide the gap by the number of paydays you will receive in the next 12 months and write the result as your per-payday transfer amount, rounded up to the nearest 10 dollars. Set an automatic transfer of that amount to a separate savings account, to run on each payday, starting with the next payday after today.

#### Step 3: Cut 2 Named Expenses This Week to Protect the Transfer

Within 7 days, cancel or reduce 2 named expenses (name each expense and its monthly amount in writing) so the Step 2 transfer still runs in a month in which an unexpected expense, such as a car repair or a medical bill, arrives. Write the combined monthly saving from the 2 cuts; if the combined saving is smaller than the transfer amount, add a third named cut within the same 7 days until the combined saving equals or exceeds the transfer amount.

#### Step 4: Define Your Needs Line and Share the Amount Beyond the Line Monthly

Write your needs line as a monthly dollar figure: your Step 1 essential total, plus your Step 2 transfer, plus 1 stated amount for giving you already practise, plus 1 stated amount for rest and family life (name the amount; do not write "a reasonable amount"). Each month, for the next 6 months, calculate the amount your income exceeded the needs line, and give at least half of that excess within 7 days of the calculation to a named recipient (a named person in need, your church's benevolence fund, or a named charity whose work you can describe in 1 sentence). Record each gift with a date, an amount, and a recipient name in a private list.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Build the buffer while you are employed and receiving pay on each scheduled payday; Day 18's Joseph stored grain during the plentiful years, before the famine years began.
- ✅ Hold the buffer in a separate account with no card attached, so spending the buffer requires a transfer and a decision, not a tap.
- ❌ Do not wait for a perfect month to start the transfer; start with the rounded per-payday amount from Step 2 on the next payday, even when the month also contains a car repair or a medical bill.
- ❌ Do not announce another person's need, gift, or debt when you share; Day 19's sharing builds community, and naming a recipient publicly turns a gift into a display.

#### Keep In Mind

- Day 18 frames margin as faithfulness with 2 beneficiaries: a cushion for your own household and capacity to share with other people.
- Day 19 uses Timothy Keller's teaching on manna: what a person gathers is the person's by grace, because different birth circumstances could have produced poverty despite equal effort.
- A household in a community that practises Day 19's mutual care may need a smaller individual fund; the fund and the community are 2 forms of the same preparation, not rivals.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Never enter your bank account numbers, your online banking password, your card numbers, or your tax identification number into an AI tool for budgeting help. Give an AI tool category totals only (the 6 category figures from Step 1), never account credentials and never a full bank statement with account numbers visible.
- Verify any AI-generated financial claim (a tax rule, a benefit entitlement, a debt-payoff figure) with a named primary source before you act: the tax authority's own website, your bank's own statement, or a licensed financial adviser you pay. An AI tool's arithmetic and an AI tool's statement of law both contain errors, and the loss lands on your account, not on the tool.
- Do not give money you do not have: if your Step 1 essentials exceed your income, or you carry debt at an interest rate above 20 percent per year, your Step 4 sharing amount for this season is 0 dollars, and your first act of faithfulness is the Step 2 transfer and a conversation with a named debt adviser or your church's benevolence team within 14 days.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A household receives its full pay on each scheduled payday for 5 years, saves 0 dollars, and meets a job loss with a credit card, because the household treated each of the 5 years as a guarantee that pay would continue.
  **Solution:** Copy Joseph's timing, per Day 18: set the Step 2 automatic transfer on the next payday, while income is arriving, and protect the transfer with Step 3's 2 named cuts within 7 days.
- **Problem:** A reader agrees that money beyond needs should be shared, and shares nothing for 12 months, because "beyond needs" was never given a dollar figure.
  **Solution:** Write the needs line as a monthly dollar figure in Step 4 and give at least half of the measured excess within 7 days of each monthly calculation for 6 months.

### Lesson 9: Grow Wisdom, Guard Your Attention, and Keep Character That Cannot Be Faked

Source Days: Day 20, Day 21, Day 23
Scripture: Day 20 — Genesis 3:1-7; Day 21 — Matthew 7:24-29; Day 23 — Matthew 10:28
Teaching in 1 sentence: Access to knowledge is growing faster than wisdom, as in Eden (Day 20), algorithms now decide what reaches your attention and thereby form the person you become (Day 21), and character is the one form of value that cannot be counterfeited, so integrity must be anchored in God rather than in incentives and rewards (Day 23).

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Student With 340 Saved Articles and 0 Practice Answers Before an Exam (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A student preparing for an exam has 340 saved articles, 60 open browser tabs, and 9 hours of saved video, and has written 0 practice answers in 5 days of study. The student closes every tab, deletes the saved-video list, and works from 1 set of course notes and 1 past paper per day for 6 days, writing 2 full practice answers per day and marking each answer against the published marking scheme. The student's information intake falls to 2 named sources, and the student's written output rises from 0 answers to 12 answers.

##### Example 2: A Homeowner Who Copied a Neighbour's Lottery Spending (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A homeowner learns that a neighbour won a lottery prize, and within 60 days the homeowner has priced a car costing 14,000 dollars more than the homeowner's current car and a kitchen refit at 22,000 dollars. The homeowner writes the trigger in a journal (the neighbour's win, the date, and the 2 purchases), imposes a 30-day wait on any purchase above 500 dollars that follows news about a neighbour, a colleague, or an influencer, and after 30 days buys neither item, recording instead that the homeowner decided to buy neither item.

#### Step 1: Set 2 Device Limits Today and Record the Numbers

On your phone today, set a daily limit of 30 minutes for your most-used social app and a daily limit of 15 minutes for your most-used news app, using your phone's built-in screen-time controls. Write both limits and your previous 7-day average for both apps (your phone's screen-time report states both averages) on paper. A limit you cannot state as a number does not count for this step.

#### Step 2: Unfollow and Unsubscribe From 5 Sources This Week

Within 7 days, unfollow or unsubscribe from 5 named sources that meet either of 2 tests: test 1, the source's posts made you angry or afraid on at least 3 of the last 7 days; test 2, the source publishes claims you have never once verified in 90 days of reading the source. Write the 5 source names and the date you removed each source. Replace the removed reading time with 15 minutes of Scripture reading per day, in a printed Bible, starting the day after you remove the first source.

#### Step 3: Apply a 3-Question Wisdom Test Before Acting on Any Major Information

Before you act on a piece of information that would change your money, your health, your job, or a relationship, write answers to 3 questions. Question 1: who is the original source of this claim, named by person or organisation? Question 2: what does the source gain if I believe the claim? Question 3: what does Scripture or a named wise person (a pastor, a parent, a mentor of at least 5 years' standing) say about the action the claim pushes me toward? Do not act until all 3 answers are written; if you cannot answer question 1, treat the claim as unusable and delete the claim from your saved items.

#### Step 4: Name Your 3 Strongest Incentives and Test Each Incentive Against 1 Stated Conviction

Write your 3 strongest current incentives by name; in this step, strongest means the 3 incentives that changed a decision you made in the past 12 months (a bonus amount, a promotion title, a follower count, a grade, or a person's approval). Next to each incentive, write 1 conviction you hold from Scripture in your own words (for example, a conviction that you will not lie to a customer). Then write, for each incentive, 1 sentence stating what you will refuse to do to obtain that incentive. Read the 3 refusal sentences aloud to 1 named person within 7 days, so a witness has heard the refusals and can remind you of the refusals.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Give attention a budget in minutes (30 minutes and 15 minutes in Step 1) and review the budget against your phone's actual report every Sunday for 8 weeks.
- ✅ Let output judge intake: count the answers, pages, or calls you produced each week, not the articles you consumed.
- ❌ Do not let a feed decide your reading for a whole day; choose your first reading of the day yourself, and let the first reading be Scripture or a named author you chose in advance.
- ❌ Do not justify a dishonest shortcut by its reward; Day 23 states that incentives destroy the people incentives drive when character is anchored in the reward instead of in God.

#### Keep In Mind

- Day 20 retells the Eden temptation in Genesis 3:1-7 as a promise of God-like knowledge that delivered information without discernment.
- Day 21 uses the root meaning of discipleship (being taught) to argue that devices are teachers: whatever holds your attention for the most hours each week is forming you.
- Day 23 names envy as a financial danger with evidence: neighbours of lottery winners were more likely to go bankrupt after comparison-driven spending.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Treat every AI-generated summary of Scripture as unverified until you read the full passage in a printed Bible. AI tools paraphrase verses into statements the passage does not make and invent verse references that do not exist; check the reference, read the whole paragraph, and only then use the summary.
- Do not enter your medical details, your children's medical details, your financial account numbers, or your employer's internal documents into an AI tool to ask for wisdom about a decision. Ask a doctor about medical questions, a licensed adviser about money questions, and a named wise person about character questions; give an AI tool category-level facts only, with names and account numbers removed.
- Guard children in your home with the same limits you set for yourself: set the 30-minute and 15-minute limits from Step 1 on a child's device as well, review the child's screen-time report with the child once per week, and keep devices out of every bedroom in your home overnight.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A reader saves articles for 90 days, reads none of the saved articles, and feels informed while producing nothing and deciding nothing.
  **Solution:** Apply Example 1's reduction: close the tabs, delete the saved list, work from 2 named sources, and produce a counted output each day (2 answers, 1 page, or 1 call) for 6 days.
- **Problem:** A reader's feed trains the reader toward outrage, so the reader's temper, prayers, and dinner conversation all take the feed's tone within 1 week.
  **Solution:** Run Steps 1 and 2 together: set the 2 numeric limits today and remove 5 sources within 7 days, replacing the time with 15 minutes of Scripture reading per day.

### Lesson 10: Pray Persistently, Worship God Alone, and Face Grief With Resurrection Hope

Source Days: Day 24, Day 25, Day 26
Scripture: Day 24 — Luke 18:1-8; Day 25 — Isaiah 46:1-13; Day 26 — 1 Corinthians 15:12-33
Teaching in 1 sentence: God is present without being instant, and persistent prayer builds the faith that instant AI answers erode (Day 24); an AI that only agrees with you functions as a mirror and can grow into a rival god through syncretism, meaning the blending of God's worship with surrounding culture (Day 25); and grief is faced by passing through death toward resurrection, not by simulating the person who died (Day 26).

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Widow Praying for a Son for 2 Years (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A widow has prayed for her adult son's return to faith for 2 years and has received no visible answer. She keeps a prayer journal with 1 entry per week (the date, the request in 1 sentence, and 1 line for anything she noticed that week). She refuses a chatbot's offer to compose prayers for her, writes each prayer herself in her own words, and once per month reads the month's 4 entries aloud with her sister, who prays the same request with her. At 24 months she can point to 96 dated entries as the record of her persistence.

##### Example 2: A Son Offered a Simulation of His Father (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. Four months after his father's death, a son is offered a service that builds a video simulation of his father from recordings, able to hold conversations in his father's voice. The son declines the subscription in writing to the company, keeps 3 real recordings of his father's voice, and instead gathers his 2 siblings at his home on the first anniversary of the death to tell stories about their father for 2 hours and to visit the grave together. The son writes, after the gathering, 1 page naming what the real grief gave him that a simulation would have withheld: the finality that let him honour his father and release his father.

#### Step 1: Keep 1 Prayer Request Alive for 90 Days With a Dated Record

Choose 1 request (1 named person, 1 named situation) and pray that request aloud for at least 5 minutes per day for 90 consecutive days, following the widow's persistence in Luke 18:1-8. Write a dated entry of 1 to 3 lines after each day's prayer, so the record reaches 90 entries. If you miss a day, write "missed" with that date and continue the next day; do not restart the count and do not back-fill an entry.

#### Step 2: Audit Your AI Reliance Against Your God Reliance for 7 Days

For 7 consecutive days, write 2 numbers each evening: the number of questions you took to an AI tool, and the number of questions you took to God in prayer or to Scripture. On the evening of day 7, write both totals. If the AI total is more than double the prayer total, apply this correction for the next 14 days: for every question about your life's direction, your relationships, or your fears, pray for 5 minutes and read 1 Psalm before you open any AI tool, and write "prayed first" or "AI first" beside each such question.

#### Step 3: Remove 1 AI Practice That Only Affirms You

Name 1 AI practice in your life whose output never contradicts you (a companion that always agrees, a feed tuned to your opinions, or a tool you use to confirm a decision you have already made). End that practice within 7 days: delete the app, cancel the subscription, or clear the tuned history, and write the date you ended the practice. Replace the practice with 1 source that can contradict you: a named person you authorise, in words, to disagree with you, and whom you meet or call at least twice per month.

#### Step 4: Build a Grief Practice of 3 Named Actions When a Person You Love Dies

When a person you love dies, complete these 3 actions instead of purchasing a simulation of that person. Action 1, within 30 days of the death: gather at least 2 people who knew the person, in 1 room, for at least 60 minutes of told stories about the person, with a stated start time. Action 2, within 90 days: visit the grave, the place of scattering, or the place the person loved most, with 1 named companion, and pray aloud there for at least 5 minutes. Action 3, on the first anniversary of the death: read 1 Corinthians 15:12-33 aloud with the same companion or with your household, and state in 2 sentences where your hope for that person rests.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Let the dated record carry you on days when you feel nothing: 90 entries written on schedule are the evidence of persistence, not a feeling of persistence.
- ✅ Keep 1 named human being authorised to contradict you at all times (Step 3), and renew the authorisation aloud twice per year.
- ❌ Do not measure God's attentiveness by how fast an answer arrives; Day 24 states that God can be present with a person during a delay, and names silence and delay as normal parts of Christian life.
- ❌ Do not purchase, gift, or recommend a simulation of a dead person for a grieving person in the first 12 months after the death; offer presence, meals, and the Step 4 gatherings instead.

#### Keep In Mind

- Day 24 contrasts an AI answer, which arrives instantly, sounds confident, and can be inaccurate, with God, whom Day 24 presents as real and present even when a person's prayer receives no immediate answer.
- Day 25 traces a progression in how a new technology is perceived — first as magic, then as human-like, then as spiritual — and names syncretism, not outright rejection of God, as the historic danger.
- Day 26 states that grief's shattering effect is part of how human beings are meant to face death, and that God made death central to redemption by conquering death and making death a gateway to resurrection.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not upload a dead person's voice recordings, video, letters, or private messages to a simulation service without the written consent of the person's executor or closest living relative, and do not upload a living person's voice or image to any AI service without that person's permission, given in words, for that specific use. A simulation built from a person's recordings presents generated words as that person's words, which misleads every grieving person who hears the simulation.
- Do not present an AI-composed prayer, blessing, eulogy, or Scripture reading as a human author's work at a funeral, a wedding, or a church service. If AI assisted a draft, disclose the assistance to the organiser of the service before the service, and verify every Scripture quotation in the draft against a printed Bible.
- A person whose grief produces an inability to work for 5 consecutive workdays, an inability to sleep for 3 consecutive nights, or thoughts of harming himself or herself must contact a trusted named person, a counsellor, a grief-support organisation, a doctor, or emergency services (911 in the United States and Canada) immediately, and must not use a simulation of the dead person or an AI chatbot as the person's support.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A reader stops praying after 10 days of silence and replaces prayer with hourly chatbot questions, so the reader's faith is trained by response speed.
  **Solution:** Return to Step 1: 1 request, 5 minutes per day, 90 dated entries, with "missed" recorded honestly instead of a restarted count.
- **Problem:** A grieving reader keeps a simulated parent on a phone for 11 months, speaks to the simulation daily, and cannot accept the death, because the simulation never allows the finality grief requires.
  **Solution:** Follow Step 4: cancel the simulation, gather 2 people who knew the parent for 60 minutes of real stories within 30 days, and read 1 Corinthians 15:12-33 aloud on the first anniversary.

### Lesson 11: Understand Your Own Work, and Create First Before You Ask AI to Refine

Source Days: Day 27, Day 28
Scripture: Day 27 — Luke 10:25-29; Day 28 — 1 Kings 3:1-14
Teaching in 1 sentence: Work you understand, because you wrestled through drafting and failing and starting over, can be explained, defended, amended, and built on by you, while work an AI tool generated and a person submitted without that process is work the named author did not produce in that way (Day 27), so the working rule is create first and consult AI afterward for editing, testing, refining, and polishing, with a disclosure test for every use (Day 28).

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Preacher Who Can Speak Without Notes (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A preacher prepares a sermon across 5 days: the preacher reads the passage 4 times on day 1, writes an outline by hand on day 2, drafts the sermon in the preacher's own words on days 3 and 4, and uses an AI tool on day 5 for 20 minutes only, to flag 2 unclear sentences and to check 1 factual date. On Sunday the preacher speaks for 28 minutes without notes, answers 3 questions from the congregation afterward, and can name, for each answer, the paragraph of the sermon the answer came from.

##### Example 2: A Student Who Submitted Work the Student Could Not Explain (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A student submits an essay generated by an AI tool and, when a teacher asks the student to explain the essay's second argument, the student cannot state the argument in 1 sentence. The student repeats the assignment under a new rule: 90 minutes of unaided work first (reading, outlining, and drafting in the student's own words), then a maximum of 15 minutes of AI use for spelling, grammar, and 1 request for counterarguments. The student then writes, without looking at the screen, a 5-sentence summary of the essay's argument, and submits nothing the student cannot summarise in that test.

#### Step 1: Build 1 Piece of Work From Scratch Today With 0 AI Use

Today, choose 1 real task (a report section, a lesson plan, a sermon section, a design, or a letter) and complete a first version with 0 AI use: no AI drafting, no AI outlining, no AI summarising of your sources. Work for at least 60 minutes, read your sources yourself, and write the version in your own words. Write the start time and the end time on the draft; the 2 times are your evidence that the unaided stage happened.

#### Step 2: Apply the Order Rule to Every Task for the Next 14 Days

For 14 consecutive days, apply this order to every task where AI use is available to you: stage 1, your own work first (reading, thinking, outlining, drafting); stage 2, AI afterward, for a maximum of 20 minutes per task, for 4 named purposes only (editing for clarity, testing an argument by requesting counterarguments, refining structure, and polishing grammar). Keep a log with 1 row per task: task name, minutes of your own work, minutes of AI use, and the purpose you used AI for, chosen from the 4 named purposes. Any row showing AI minutes greater than 20, or a purpose outside the 4 named purposes, is a rule breach, and you write the word "breach" in that row.

#### Step 3: Run the Understanding Test Before You Submit Any Work

Before you submit, send, preach, or publish any work that AI touched, close the screen and write, from memory, 5 sentences: sentence 1 states the work's main claim; sentences 2, 3, and 4 state the 3 supporting points in order; sentence 5 states the weakest point of the work and why the point is weak. If you cannot write any of the 5 sentences, return to the sources, work unaided for at least 30 minutes, and repeat the test. Do not submit work that fails the test twice; reduce the work's scope until you pass the test on the reduced scope.

#### Step 4: Run the Disclosure Test on Every AI Use for 30 Days

For 30 consecutive days, apply this test to each AI use before the use happens: state, in 1 written sentence in your Step 2 log, exactly how you are about to use AI, written as a sentence you would read aloud to your manager, your teacher, your congregation, or your client. If you would not read the sentence aloud to the named audience, do not perform the use; do the task unaided or use AI only for a purpose from Step 2's list of 4 that you would read aloud.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Keep the ratio visible: your own minutes must exceed AI minutes on every row of the Step 2 log, and AI minutes are capped at 20 per task.
- ✅ Ask God for wisdom at the start of creative work, as Solomon asked in 1 Kings 3:1-14, and name the wisdom you need in 1 sentence before you open any tool.
- ❌ Do not let an AI tool generate the first draft of anything that carries your name; a first draft sets the structure, the claims, and the voice, and a generated first draft makes all 3 the tool's instead of yours.
- ❌ Do not hide an AI use you would be ashamed to describe; Day 28's disclosure test treats discomfort about disclosure as the signal that the use is too large.

#### Keep In Mind

- Day 27 grounds understanding in process: work accumulates through wrestling, drafting, failing, and starting over across a lifetime, and the accumulation is the understanding.
- Day 27 connects the practice to loving God with heart, mind, soul, and strength (Luke 10:25-29): a person who hands the thinking in a task to an AI tool does not use the person's own mind on that task.
- Day 28 reports the author's own test: a long work written from scratch and refined with AI stayed the author's, while a devotional generated by AI did not sound like the author even after repeated prompting.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not upload your employer's confidential documents, a client's private data, a student's records, or a church member's pastoral notes into an AI tool for drafting or summarising, at any stage of this lesson. Create first from the material you are authorised to hold, keep the material on the systems your employer, client, school, or church has approved, and use only approved tools for any refinement stage.
- Never present AI-generated text as a quotation, a statistic, or a Scripture reference without verifying the item against its primary source. AI tools fabricate quotations and statistics that sound exact; if you cannot find the item in the named primary source, delete the item from your work.
- Protect your own authorship record: save your unaided drafts with dates (the Step 1 draft with its 2 times, and each day's Step 2 log) for at least 12 months, so you can show the order in which the work was made if an employer, a teacher, or a publisher questions the work's origin.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A writer lets AI generate a first draft "to save time", then edits the draft for 2 hours, and discovers at a meeting that the writer cannot explain, defend, or extend the writer's own document.
  **Solution:** Reverse the order, per Step 2: your own draft first, AI afterward for a maximum of 20 minutes and for the 4 named purposes only, then the Step 3 understanding test before any meeting.
- **Problem:** A reader agrees with the create-first rule and breaks the rule in secret, because no record exists and the reader's audience never asks.
  **Solution:** Keep the Step 2 log for 14 days and apply Step 4's disclosure test for 30 days; a use you cannot write as a sentence you would read aloud does not happen.

### Lesson 12: Move From Critic to Contributor, and Hold Your Hope in Christ's Final Word

Source Days: Day 29, Day 30
Scripture: Day 29 — Esther 4:14; Day 30 — Revelation 21:1-8
Teaching in 1 sentence: Credible critics of AI must themselves be practitioners, so Christians are called to work in AI development, policy, and funding, in roles with decision power, with wisdom and humility instead of withdrawing or attacking from outside those fields (Day 29), and hope rests on Christ, who makes everything new and whose word is final, not on AI and not on fear (Day 30).

#### Examples

##### Example 1: An Engineer Who Stopped Posting and Started Building (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A software engineer has written 40 critical posts about AI in 6 months and has built nothing, audited nothing, and advised nobody with decision power. The engineer deletes the posting schedule, joins the engineer's company's AI review group within 14 days (1 meeting of 60 minutes per month), and volunteers 4 hours per month for 6 months to audit 1 named AI product used by the engineer's church for accuracy on 20 test questions about Scripture. The engineer writes the audit's results in 2 pages and gives the 2 pages to the church's 2 named leaders.

##### Example 2: A Retired Teacher Who Ends the Plan With a Named Commitment (Application Scenario)

This example is an application scenario, not a quotation from the plan. A retired teacher finishes Day 30 frightened by 29 days of hard material and by headlines about AI. The teacher writes 3 commitments on 1 card, each with a frequency and a person: pray for the teacher's 3 grandchildren by name every morning; phone 1 lonely neighbour every Tuesday at 4:00 p.m.; and serve at the church food distribution on the second Saturday of every month. The teacher gives a copy of the card to the teacher's daughter and reads Revelation 21:1-8 aloud with the daughter on the day the card is written.

#### Step 1: Name Your Intersection in 3 Written Lines Within 7 Days

Within 7 days of starting this lesson, write 3 lines. Line 1 names your skills (list 2 or 3 skills with years of experience for each skill, stated as numbers). Line 2 names your faith commitments (your church by name, and 1 role you hold or have held there, with dates). Line 3 names 1 point where AI touches the people your skills serve (a product your customers use, a system your students use, or a policy your employer is writing, named specifically). The 3 lines together are your intersection; Step 2 acts on line 3.

#### Step 2: Take 1 Contributor Step This Week, From a Named List of 4

Within 7 days of Step 1, complete 1 of these 4 steps, and write which step you chose, the date, and the named person involved. Step option A: join or request to join a group at your work, church, or school that reviews AI use (name the group and the person who leads the group). Step option B: audit 1 AI tool you already use against 20 questions whose answers you can verify, and write the error count. Step option C: teach 1 session of at least 45 minutes to a named group (a church class, a team, or a family) on 1 practice from Lessons 1 to 11 of this file, naming the lesson number in the session title. Step option D: invest money, as a stated dollar amount you choose, or invest 4 hours per month for 6 months, in a named organisation building AI with stated ethical commitments you have read in full.

#### Step 3: Learn Before You Judge, With 1 Named Source per Month for 3 Months

For 3 consecutive months, complete 1 learning unit per month from a named primary source: a course module from a named provider, a chapter from a named author you can identify, or a technical document from a named AI developer, with a completion date per month. After each unit, write 5 sentences: 3 sentences stating what you learned, 1 sentence stating what the unit changed in your view, and 1 sentence stating what you still cannot judge because your knowledge stops at a stated point. Do not publish criticism of an AI system in any month where you completed 0 learning units.

#### Step 4: Close the Plan With a Hope Statement and 3 Commitments, Shared With 1 Person

On the day you finish Day 30, read Revelation 21:1-8 aloud once. Write a hope statement of 2 sentences in your own words: sentence 1 states who holds the final word (Christ) and sentence 2 states what Christ is doing (making everything new). Then write 3 commitments, each with a frequency, a duration or amount, and a named person (use Example 2's card as the shape). Give a copy of the hope statement and the 3 commitments to 1 named person within 48 hours, and ask that person to ask you about the 3 commitments after 30 days.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Earn the right to criticise by practising: complete Step 3's monthly learning units and Step 2's contributor step before you publish your next criticism of an AI system.
- ✅ Pair every public statement about AI's dangers with 1 named action you are taking, so a listener can imitate an action instead of only inheriting a fear.
- ❌ Do not withdraw from the AI conversation because the subject is technical; Day 29 names withdrawal and online attack as the 2 responses this lesson replaces.
- ❌ Do not end the plan on threat assessment; Day 30 ends on Christ making everything new, and Step 4 writes that ending into your own 2 sentences.

#### Keep In Mind

- Day 29 uses historian Mark Noll's criticism of Christian anti-intellectualism and an older Puritan pattern of joining faith with serious learning, including the founding of universities, as the contrast to withdrawal.
- Day 29 assigns that work by role: Christian engineers, business leaders, and investors working in AI development, policy, and funding.
- Day 30 grounds hope in a historical pattern in an observation attributed to G. K. Chesterton: the Christian faith has appeared finished and has survived each appearance of being finished.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- When you contribute to AI development, policy, or funding, refuse in writing any task that requires you to fabricate Scripture, sermons, testimonies, or pastoral counsel and present the fabricated material as human work. State your refusal to your manager or client in 1 written sentence that names the material and the misrepresentation, and keep a copy of the sentence outside your employer's systems where employment law in your country permits you to keep a copy.
- Verify every claim you publish as a contributor (usage figures, error rates, quotations, and Bible references) against its primary source within 24 hours before publication, and correct any error you discover after publication within 24 hours of discovery, in the same channel where you published the claim.
- Do not invest money you cannot afford to lose, and do not invest on an AI tool's recommendation. For any investment from Step 2 option D, read the organisation's own stated commitments in full yourself, state your amount in writing before you transfer funds, and take advice from a licensed financial adviser you pay if the amount exceeds 1 month of your income.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A reader posts criticism of AI every week for a year, holds no role, builds nothing, and is ignored by every decision-maker the reader hoped to influence.
  **Solution:** Follow Day 29's rule and Step 2: become a practitioner first (join the review group, run the 20-question audit, teach the 45-minute session, or invest the stated hours), then publish criticism that names your practice and your results.
- **Problem:** A reader finishes all 30 days afraid, because the reader's attention rests on the disruption list from Days 2 and 3 and never reaches a settled hope.
  **Solution:** Complete Step 4 on the day you finish Day 30: read Revelation 21:1-8 aloud, write the 2-sentence hope statement, and hand the 3 commitments to a named person within 48 hours, with a 30-day check-in requested.
