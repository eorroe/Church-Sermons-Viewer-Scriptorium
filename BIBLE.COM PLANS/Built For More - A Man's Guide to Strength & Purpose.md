# Built for More: A Man's Guide to Strength & Purpose

## Overview

This plan is a 7-day Bible study plan for men, written by Steve Robinson and published on Bible.com, and the 7-day plan exists to teach a man the 5 traits named in 1 Corinthians 16:13-14: watchfulness, firm faith, courageous action, strength, and love. The reader in this plan means 1 man who reads 1 lesson on 1 day and completes the action in that lesson on that same day. This plan moves in 1 fixed order, from Day 1, which names childish ways a man must leave, to Day 7, which combines all 5 traits into 1 daily practice. In this plan, a trusted person means 1 named adult whom the man knows in person and whom the man can contact by phone or in person on the same day the contact is required. In this plan, local means the city or county where the man is located when the action is required, and licensed means holding a license to practice in the state or province where the professional practices.

## When to Follow Plan Teachings

- When a man makes a decision in 5 minutes that a man will regret for 5 years, and the man needs a pause rule before the man acts.
- When a man avoids a conversation at home or at work for 7 consecutive days, where the conversation requires the man to admit a fault, to apologize, or to state a disagreement, and the man needs a dated plan to start the conversation.
- When a man has no named accountability partner, and the man needs 1 named man to contact 1 time per week.
- When a man faces pressure from coworkers, friends, or online content to violate a belief the man holds, and the man needs a written standard to check before the man responds.
- When a man leads a spouse, a child, a coworker, or a friend, and the man needs to check that the man's words and actions show love before the man speaks or acts.
- When a man feels shame after a failure, and the man needs 1 written next step, stating 1 action, 1 date, and 1 time, to complete within 24 hours instead of withdrawal from family and friends.

## Lessons From Plan

### Lesson 1: Day 1 — Put Away Childish Ways and Pursue Maturity, 1 Corinthians 13:11

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Man Stops Blaming a Spouse for the Man's Own Spending

Application scenario: A man spends 80 dollars on a video game on Friday at 8:00 p.m. without telling the man's spouse, and the spouse asks about the charge on Saturday at 9:00 a.m. The man names the purchase, names the amount of 80 dollars, apologizes for hiding the purchase within the Saturday 9:00 a.m. conversation, and moves 80 dollars from the man's personal spending category to cover the charge on Saturday before 12:00 p.m.

##### Example 2: A Man Replaces Perfection Pressure With 1 Measurable Step

Application scenario: A man missed prayer on 4 of 7 mornings in 1 week and calls the entire week a failure. The man writes 1 step for Monday: pray for 5 minutes at 6:30 a.m. before checking a phone. The man marks Monday complete or incomplete at 9:00 p.m. on Monday, and the man repeats the same 5-minute step on Tuesday regardless of the Monday result.

#### Step 1: Name 2 Childish Behaviors in Writing

On Day 1 at 7:00 p.m., the man sits alone for 15 minutes with 1 sheet of paper and 1 pen. The man writes 2 behaviors, and each behavior entry includes the behavior name, the date of the most recent occurrence, and the person affected. The man writes a behavior entry in a full sentence, for example, a sentence that names the behavior, the date, and the affected person.

#### Step 2: Choose 1 Replacement Behavior for Each Childish Behavior

On Day 1 at 7:15 p.m., the man writes 1 replacement behavior beside each childish behavior from Step 1. Each replacement behavior states an action, a time, and a place. The man practices replacement behavior number 1 on Day 2 and replacement behavior number 2 on Day 3.

#### Step 3: Tell 1 Named Person the 2 Replacement Behaviors Within 24 Hours

Within 24 hours after Day 1 at 7:00 p.m., the man sends 1 message to 1 named accountability partner. The message lists both replacement behaviors and the 2 dates from Step 2. The man asks the accountability partner to ask 1 question on Day 4: did the man complete both replacement behaviors on the assigned dates, yes or no.

#### Step 4: Score Progress at the End of 7 Days

At 8:00 p.m. on Day 7, the man writes whether the man completed replacement behavior number 1 on Day 2, yes or no, and whether the man completed replacement behavior number 2 on Day 3, yes or no, for a total completed count out of 2 scheduled practices. The man writes 1 sentence naming progress the man made, and the man writes 1 sentence naming the next behavior the man will address in the following 7 days.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do write each behavior with a date, a time, and a named affected person.
- ✅ Do measure progress as a count of completed replacement behaviors out of the 2 scheduled replacement behaviors in Step 2.
- ❌ Don't judge a 7-day result as total success or total failure with no count in between.
- ❌ Don't hide a failure from the accountability partner named in Step 3.

#### Keep In Mind

- Maturity in Lesson 1 means the man takes responsibility for the man's own action within 24 hours after the action occurs.
- Progress in Lesson 1 means the count of completed replacement behaviors rises between the first 7-day period and the second 7-day period.
- The man in Lesson 1 is the reader; the accountability partner in Lesson 1 is a second man whom the reader names in Step 3.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use Lesson 1 to shame a spouse, a child, a coworker, or a friend for a behavior of that person; Lesson 1 applies to the reader's own 2 behaviors only.
- A man who is in crisis, meaning a man who plans to harm the man himself or harm another person, must contact a trusted person in the man's home or community and a licensed counsellor immediately, on the same day and within the same hour, before continuing Lesson 1.
- A man who struggles with addiction, meaning repeated use of alcohol, drugs, or pornography that the man has tried to stop at least 3 times, must contact a pastor, a licensed counsellor, or a medical professional within 7 days and must not rely on Lesson 1 alone.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A man writes a vague goal, for example, a goal with no date, no time, and no count.
  **Solution:** The man rewrites the goal with 1 action, 1 date, 1 time, and 1 count, following the format in Step 2.
- **Problem:** A man stops the entire plan after 1 missed day.
  **Solution:** The man marks the missed day as incomplete, resumes the next numbered day on the next calendar day, and records the miss in the Day 7 score from Step 4.

### Lesson 2: Day 2 — Be Watchful, 1 Peter 5:8

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Man Guards Late-Night Phone Use

Application scenario: A man views pornography on a phone between 11:00 p.m. and 12:00 a.m. on 3 nights in 1 week. The man places the phone in a kitchen drawer at 10:30 p.m. on each of the next 7 nights, and the man tells the man's accountability partner the plan before the first night begins.

##### Example 2: A Man Pauses Before a Short-Sighted Purchase

Application scenario: A coworker offers to sell a man a motorcycle for 3,000 dollars on Monday, and payment is due on Tuesday. The man waits 24 hours, checks the household budget with the man's spouse on Tuesday at 7:00 p.m., and gives the coworker an answer on Wednesday at 9:00 a.m.

#### Step 1: List 3 Entry Points for Temptation

On Day 2 at 7:00 p.m., the man writes 3 situations in which the man experiences an urge to commit a sin the man has named. Each situation entry names a place, a time range, and a device or a person involved. The man ranks the 3 situations from highest risk, rank 1, to lowest risk, rank 3.

#### Step 2: Block the Rank 1 Situation for 7 Nights

Starting on the night of Day 2, the man applies 1 physical block to the rank 1 situation for 7 consecutive nights. A physical block means a change a second person can verify, for example, a phone placed in a named room, an application deleted from a phone, or a door left open during a defined time range.

#### Step 3: Pray and Read for 10 Minutes Before 9:00 a.m.

On each morning from Day 3 to Day 7, the man prays for 5 minutes and reads 1 Peter 5:8 aloud repeatedly for 5 minutes, and the man completes both actions before 9:00 a.m. The man places a check mark for each completed morning on a paper calendar.

#### Step 4: Report the 7-Night Result to 1 Accountability Partner

At 8:00 p.m. on Day 8, the man reports to the accountability partner the number of nights, out of 7 nights, in which the physical block from Step 2 stayed in place. The man names the first night that failed, if a failure occurred, where a failed night means a night in which the physical block from Step 2 did not stay in place, and the man states the new block the man will apply for the next 7 nights.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do place a physical block that a second named person can verify.
- ✅ Do decide a purchase answer after a 24-hour wait and a budget check with a named person.
- ❌ Don't remain in the rank 1 situation from Step 1 between 11:00 p.m. and 12:00 a.m. with no physical block in place.
- ❌ Don't keep a temptation entry point secret from the accountability partner named in Step 4.

#### Keep In Mind

- Watchfulness in Lesson 2 means the man names a risk before the risk arrives, and the man places a block before the risk time range begins.
- The enemy in Lesson 2 refers to Satan, as named in 1 Peter 5:8, and temptation in Lesson 2 refers to a situation in which the man experiences an urge to commit a sin the man has named in Step 1.
- A short-sighted choice in Lesson 2 means a choice made in under 5 minutes that creates a consequence lasting longer than 30 days.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use Lesson 2 to monitor, control, or accuse a spouse, a child, or a coworker; the blocks in Lesson 2 apply to the reader's own devices and the reader's own schedule only.
- A man who receives a threat of violence from any person must contact local emergency services and a trusted person immediately, within the same hour.
- A man with a mental-health crisis, meaning panic, depression, or thoughts of self-harm lasting longer than 24 hours, must contact a licensed counsellor or a medical professional on the same day the crisis is recognized.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A man names a risk with no place, no time range, and no device or person named, and places no block, so the risk returns at the same time on the next night.
  **Solution:** The man applies Step 2 again with a physical block, a named room, and a start time of 10:30 p.m. for 7 consecutive nights.
- **Problem:** A man makes a purchase above 100 dollars in under 5 minutes and creates debt that lasts longer than 30 days.
  **Solution:** The man applies a 24-hour wait to every purchase above 100 dollars, and the man completes a budget check with the man's spouse or accountability partner before payment.


### Lesson 3: Day 3 — Stand Firm in the Faith, Ephesians 4:14

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Man States a Belief at Work Without Attacking a Coworker

Application scenario: At a lunch break on Tuesday at 12:30 p.m., 3 coworkers mock a belief the man holds about honesty in expense reports. The man states in 1 sentence that the man reports exact amounts, the man does not insult the 3 coworkers, and the man stays at the table until the lunch break ends at 1:00 p.m.

##### Example 2: A Man Follows the Example of Elijah in 1 Kings 18

Application scenario: A man is the only person in a group of 8 friends who objects to a plan to cheat on a work certification test on Friday. The man names the objection once on Thursday at 6:00 p.m., offers to study with the group for 60 minutes on Thursday at 7:00 p.m., and declines to cheat on Friday even if all 7 friends proceed.

#### Step 1: Write 3 Beliefs and 1 Scripture Reference for Each Belief

On Day 3 at 7:00 p.m., the man writes 3 beliefs the man will not compromise. Beside each belief, the man writes 1 Scripture reference that supports that belief. The man keeps the paper in a wallet or in a phone case for 30 days.

#### Step 2: Read Ephesians 4:14 Aloud 1 Time per Day for 5 Days

On each day from Day 3 to Day 7, the man reads Ephesians 4:14 aloud 1 time before 9:00 a.m. The man places a check mark on a paper calendar after each reading, for a total of 5 check marks.

#### Step 3: Prepare a 1-Sentence Response Before a Pressure Situation

Before the next work meeting or social event on the man's calendar, the man writes 1 sentence that states the man's belief without insulting another person. The man rehearses the sentence aloud 3 times at home. If pressure occurs at the meeting or event, the man says the rehearsed sentence 1 time and does not add an insult.

#### Step 4: Review 1 Pressure Situation With an Accountability Partner Within 7 Days

Within 7 days after a pressure situation, the man describes the situation to the accountability partner in a call or a meeting lasting at least 15 minutes. The man states 2 facts: what the man said, and what the other person or group said in reply. The man asks the accountability partner to rate the man's response on firmness from 1, meaning the lowest firmness, to 5, meaning the highest firmness, and on kindness from 1, meaning the lowest kindness, to 5, meaning the highest kindness.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do anchor each belief in Step 1 to 1 written Scripture reference.
- ✅ Do state a belief 1 time in words that name the belief and the action the man will take, and remain present after stating the belief.
- ❌ Don't compromise a belief from Step 1 to gain approval from a group in a single meeting.
- ❌ Don't insult, mock, or threaten a person who disagrees with a belief from Step 1.

#### Keep In Mind

- Standing firm in Lesson 3 means the man holds a belief from Step 1 when holding the belief costs the man approval, money, or status.
- A compromise in Lesson 3 means the man acts against a belief from Step 1 in order to be accepted by a group.
- Elijah in Lesson 3 is the prophet in 1 Kings 18 who stood alone, 1 person, against a group of people who opposed Elijah's message, and Elijah serves in the plan summary as a named example of firm faith.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use Lesson 3 to justify violence, threats, or harassment against a person who holds a different belief; firmness in Lesson 3 is verbal and non-violent in every case.
- A man who faces abuse at home or at work, meaning physical harm, threats of physical harm, or coercion repeated on at least 2 occasions, must contact a trusted person, a pastor, a licensed counsellor, or local emergency services within 24 hours.
- A man who is pressured to break a law must decline the illegal action and must contact a supervisor, a compliance officer, or a lawyer within 5 business days.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A man cannot state why the man holds a belief, so the man changes position during the first argument.
  **Solution:** The man completes Step 1 again and writes 1 Scripture reference beside each of the 3 beliefs before the next argument occurs.
- **Problem:** A man states a belief with insults, and listeners reject the delivery instead of hearing the belief.
  **Solution:** The man uses the 1 rehearsed sentence from Step 3, apologizes for the insult within 24 hours, and restates the belief without an insult.

### Lesson 4: Day 4 — Act Like Men With Courage, Humility, and Community, Proverbs 27:17 and 1 Corinthians 16:13-14

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Man Asks 2 Men to Form an Accountability Group

Application scenario: On Sunday at 10:00 a.m., a man asks 2 named men from the man's church to meet for 45 minutes every Tuesday at 6:30 a.m. for 4 consecutive weeks. Each meeting covers 3 questions: 1 temptation faced, 1 action completed, and 1 action planned for the next 7 days.

##### Example 2: A Man Leads at Home by Taking a Task Without Being Asked

Application scenario: A man's spouse is ill on Monday. The man cooks dinner on Monday at 6:00 p.m., washes the dishes by 7:30 p.m., and puts 2 children to bed by 8:30 p.m., and the man does not ask the spouse to manage any of the 3 tasks.

#### Step 1: Invite 2 Named Men to a Weekly Meeting

Within 48 hours after reading Lesson 4, the man sends 1 invitation message to 2 named men. The invitation states a day, a start time, a meeting length of 45 minutes, and a duration of 4 consecutive weeks. For each invited man, the man writes 1 of 3 entries within 72 hours after sending the invitation: yes, no, or no reply received.

#### Step 2: Admit 1 Fault to the Group at the First Meeting

At the first meeting, the man states 1 fault in 1 sentence, names the person affected by the fault, and states 1 repair action with a date. The man completes the repair action by the date the man stated.

#### Step 3: Accept Correction Without Leaving the Conversation

When a group member corrects the man, the man listens for the full correction, repeats the correction back in 1 sentence, and waits 10 minutes before deciding a response. The man does not leave the room or end the call during the correction, unless a person threatens physical harm.

#### Step 4: Take 1 Courageous Action Within 7 Days

Within 7 days after Lesson 4, the man completes 1 action the man has avoided for at least 14 days. The action must have a named recipient, for example, an apology to a named person, a conversation with a named supervisor in which the man admits a fault, apologizes, or states a disagreement, or a protective action for a named family member. The man reports completion to the group at the next weekly meeting.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do meet with 2 named men for 45 minutes 1 time per week for 4 consecutive weeks.
- ✅ Do state a fault, a named affected person, and a dated repair action in the same conversation.
- ❌ Don't attempt Lesson 4 alone with no group for longer than 14 days.
- ❌ Don't use courage as a reason to dominate, shout at, or frighten a family member or a coworker.

#### Keep In Mind

- Acting like a man in Lesson 4 means the man takes a courageous action and accepts accountability for the action; acting like a man does not mean physical size, income level, or job title.
- Community in Lesson 4 means 3 men in total, the reader plus 2 named men, meeting on a fixed schedule defined in Step 1.
- Proverbs 27:17 in Lesson 4 describes men sharpening men, meaning 1 man improves a second man's conduct through honest speech and correction.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use Lesson 4 to pressure a man to disclose abuse, trauma, or a crime in a group meeting; a man may disclose a sensitive matter to a pastor or a licensed counsellor in a private meeting instead.
- A man who learns in a group meeting that a child is being abused must contact local child protective services or local emergency services on the same day the man learns the information.
- A man in a group who threatens physical harm to a person must be reported to a pastor, to 1 of the 2 other men in the man's accountability group from Step 1, or to local emergency services within 1 hour after the threat.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A man invites 2 men once, receives 2 refusals, and forms no group.
  **Solution:** The man invites 2 different named men within 48 hours after the second refusal and repeats the invitation process a maximum of 3 rounds until 2 men accept.
- **Problem:** A man attends 4 meetings and speaks only about other people's faults.
  **Solution:** At the next meeting, the man states 1 fault of the man's own, following Step 2, before the man comments on any other person.


### Lesson 5: Day 5 — Be Strong Through Resilience, Responsibility, and Stewardship, Genesis 2:15

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Man Repairs a Household Problem on the Day the Problem Appears

Application scenario: A kitchen faucet leaks on Wednesday at 7:00 a.m. The man shuts off the water valve under the sink by 7:15 a.m., buys a replacement part on Wednesday before 6:00 p.m., and completes the repair or schedules a plumber by Thursday at 12:00 p.m. The man does not leave the leak unaddressed past Thursday.

##### Example 2: A Man Admits Exhaustion and Schedules Rest and Help

Application scenario: A man sleeps 5 hours per night for 5 consecutive nights and shouts at the man's children on the fifth night. The man tells the man's spouse on the fifth night at 9:00 p.m. that the man's sleep total is 5 hours per night, the man goes to bed at 10:00 p.m. for the next 3 nights, and the man schedules an appointment with a medical doctor within 7 days after the 14th consecutive night if sleep stays below 6 hours per night for 14 consecutive nights.

#### Step 1: List 4 Areas of Stewardship With 1 Duty for Each Area

On Day 5 at 7:00 p.m., the man writes 4 area headings: family, work, health, and faith. Under each heading, the man writes 1 duty the man owns, 1 due date within the next 7 days, and the name of the person who benefits from the duty.

#### Step 2: Complete the Earliest Due Duty First

On Day 6 at 8:00 a.m., the man selects the duty from Step 1 with the earliest due date. The man works on the selected duty for 30 uninterrupted minutes before checking a phone or email. The man repeats a 30-minute work block a maximum of 3 times in 1 day until the duty is complete.

#### Step 3: Schedule Health Care for Body and Mind for 7 Days

On Day 5, the man writes 3 health actions for the next 7 days: sleep from 10:30 p.m. to 6:30 a.m., a walk of 20 minutes on 5 of the 7 days, and 1 conversation of at least 20 minutes with a spouse, a friend, or a counsellor in which the man names the man's emotion with 1 emotion word, for example, sad, angry, or afraid. The man marks each completed action on a paper calendar.

#### Step 4: Name 1 Weakness and 1 Source of Help Within 24 Hours

Within 24 hours after Day 5, the man writes 1 weakness in 1 sentence and names 1 source of help for the weakness, choosing from a pastor, a licensed counsellor, a medical doctor, or an accountability partner. The man contacts the named source of help within 72 hours and schedules a meeting or a call with a stated date and time.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do assign a due date within 7 days and a named beneficiary to each duty in Step 1.
- ✅ Do state a weakness in writing within 24 hours and contact a named source of help within 72 hours.
- ❌ Don't wait for another person to notice and fix a duty the man owns in Step 1.
- ❌ Don't hide exhaustion, pain, or sadness for longer than 7 days without telling a named person.

#### Keep In Mind

- Strength in Lesson 5 means the man completes duties the man owns and asks for help for a weakness the man names; strength does not mean lifting a maximum weight or hiding pain.
- Stewardship in Lesson 5 means care for a person, a task, money, or a place that God has placed under the man's responsibility, as Genesis 2:15 describes a man working and keeping a garden.
- Resilience in Lesson 5 means the man resumes a duty within 24 hours after a failure, interruption, or criticism.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use Lesson 5 to continue physical exertion after a condition named in this bullet begins; a man with chest pain, an injury that includes a broken bone, a head injury, or bleeding that the man cannot stop must contact local emergency services within 5 minutes after the condition begins, and must stop physical exertion.
- A man with symptoms of depression lasting 14 consecutive days or longer, meaning loss of interest, sleep change, or hopelessness on at least 8 of those 14 days, must contact a medical doctor or a licensed mental-health professional within 7 days.
- A man must not use strength in Lesson 5 as a reason to stay in an abusive situation without help; a man facing abuse must contact a trusted person, a pastor, a licensed counsellor, or local emergency services within 24 hours.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A man waits until the man wants to start a duty before starting the duty, and the duty remains undone for 14 days.
  **Solution:** The man starts Step 2 with a 30-minute work block at 8:00 a.m. on the next calendar day, regardless of whether the man wants to start at 8:00 a.m.
- **Problem:** A man treats asking for help as failure, so a weakness continues for 30 days with no source of help contacted.
  **Solution:** The man applies Step 4 within 24 hours, names 1 source of help, and schedules a meeting or a call within 72 hours.

### Lesson 6: Day 6 — Do Everything in Love, 1 Corinthians 16:14

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Man Serves a Spouse Before Being Asked

Application scenario: On Saturday, a man washes 1 load of laundry at 9:00 a.m., prepares lunch at 12:00 p.m., and asks the man's spouse at 6:00 p.m. to name 1 task for Sunday. The man completes the named task on Sunday before 8:00 p.m.

##### Example 2: A Man Listens to a Spouse for 15 Minutes Without Fixing

Application scenario: Following the reference to 1 Peter 3:7 in the plan summary, in which a husband learns a need that the wife names in words, a man listens to the man's spouse for 15 minutes at 7:30 p.m. The man asks 2 questions about the emotion the spouse names, using the emotion word the spouse uses, the man repeats the spouse's main point in 1 sentence, and the man offers a solution only if the spouse asks for a solution.

#### Step 1: Choose 1 Act of Service per Day for 5 Days

On Day 6 at 7:00 p.m., the man writes 5 acts of service, 1 act for each day from Day 7 to Day 11. Each act names a recipient, an action, and a completion time. The man completes each act before the completion time written for that act.

#### Step 2: Listen for 15 Minutes Without Interrupting

On 3 evenings within the next 7 days, the man listens to a spouse, a child, or a friend for 15 minutes. The man sets a timer for 15 minutes, the man asks 2 questions during the 15 minutes, and the man does not give advice during the 15 minutes unless the speaker asks for advice.

#### Step 3: Check Words Against Love Before Sending a Message That Criticizes a Person

Before the man sends a text message, an email, or a spoken correction that criticizes a person, the man waits 10 minutes. During the 10 minutes, the man reads the message 1 time and removes any insult, threat, or mocking word. The man sends the revised message only after the full 10 minutes have passed.

#### Step 4: Ask 1 Recipient for a Rating Within 7 Days

Within 7 days after Day 6, the man asks 1 recipient of an act of service from Step 1 to rate the man's helpfulness from 1, meaning not helpful, to 5, meaning the highest helpfulness, and to name 1 change the recipient wants. The man writes the rating and the requested change, and the man applies the requested change in the next act of service within the following 7 days.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do complete 1 named act of service per day for 5 consecutive days, with a named recipient for each act.
- ✅ Do listen for a full 15 minutes and repeat the speaker's main point in 1 sentence before responding.
- ❌ Don't perform an act of service and then demand praise, repayment, or agreement from the recipient.
- ❌ Don't send a critical message in anger without the 10-minute wait in Step 3.

#### Keep In Mind

- Love in Lesson 6 means a chosen action that benefits a named recipient at a cost to the man's time, money, comfort, or pride; love in Lesson 6 is measured by the action, not by a feeling alone.
- Understanding in Lesson 6, drawn from 1 Peter 3:7 as named in the plan summary, means the man learns a need that the spouse names in words during the listening period in Step 2, instead of assuming the man knows the need.
- Empathy in Lesson 6 means the man names the emotion a speaker expressed, using the emotion word the speaker used, for example, sad, angry, or afraid, before the man responds.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use love in Lesson 6 to excuse abuse by any person; a man or a spouse who experiences physical harm or threats of physical harm must contact a trusted person, a pastor, a licensed counsellor, or local emergency services within 24 hours, and within 1 hour if danger is present at that moment.
- A man must not use an act of service to control a recipient's decisions, movements, money, or friendships; a recipient may decline any act of service at any time, and the man must accept the refusal in the same conversation.
- A man who cannot control anger, meaning the man shouts, breaks an object, or frightens a family member on at least 2 occasions in 30 days, must contact a licensed counsellor or an anger-management professional within 7 days.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A man equates love with agreement and stops speaking to a spouse or a friend and stops completing acts of service for that spouse or friend when that spouse or friend disagrees.
  **Solution:** The man completes the next scheduled act of service from Step 1 on the scheduled date, even after a disagreement, and the man states the disagreement in words without withdrawing the service.
- **Problem:** A man listens for 2 minutes and then gives advice the speaker did not request.
  **Solution:** The man applies Step 2 again, sets a timer for 15 minutes, asks 2 questions, and gives advice only after the speaker requests advice.


### Lesson 7: Day 7 — Embrace All 5 Traits Daily and Ground Identity in Christ, 1 Corinthians 16:13-14

#### Examples

##### Example 1: A Man Runs a 10-Minute Morning Check of All 5 Traits

Application scenario: At 6:30 a.m. on Monday, a man spends 10 minutes with a paper checklist of 5 traits. For each trait, the man writes 1 action for Monday: guard a phone at night for watchfulness, state a belief at work for firm faith, make a phone call that the man has avoided for 7 days because the phone call requires an apology, for courageous action, complete a repair for strength, and serve a spouse for love.

##### Example 2: A Man Recovers From a Failure Within 24 Hours Without Shame Withdrawal

Application scenario: A man shouts at a child on Tuesday at 7:00 p.m. and feels shame. The man apologizes to the child by Wednesday at 7:00 p.m., states the specific wrong in 1 sentence, tells the man's accountability partner about the failure on Wednesday, and attends the family dinner on Wednesday at 6:00 p.m. instead of withdrawing to a separate room.

#### Step 1: Build a 5-Trait Checklist With 1 Action per Trait

On Day 7 at 7:00 p.m., the man writes 5 trait headings: watchfulness, firm faith, courageous action, strength, and love. Under each heading, the man writes 1 action, 1 date within the next 7 days, and 1 completion time for that action.

#### Step 2: Complete and Mark 1 Action per Day for 7 Days

For each day in the 7-day period after Day 7, the man completes at least 1 action from the checklist in Step 1 before 9:00 p.m. The checklist in Step 1 contains 5 actions in total, 1 action per trait, so on 2 of the 7 days the man repeats 1 action that the man completed on an earlier day. The man writes the completion time beside the action for each day. At the end of 7 days, the man counts distinct completed checklist actions out of a maximum of 5 actions, the exact total the man scheduled in Step 1.

#### Step 3: State Identity in Christ in Writing 1 Time per Day for 7 Days

On each morning for 7 consecutive mornings, before 9:00 a.m., the man writes 1 sentence that states the man's identity in Christ, meaning who the man is as a follower of Christ, separate from job title, income, and the results of the previous day. The man reads the sentence aloud 1 time after writing the sentence.

#### Step 4: Hold a 30-Minute Review With an Accountability Partner on Day 14

On Day 14 at a time agreed in advance, the man meets the accountability partner for 30 minutes. The man reports 3 numbers: distinct completed actions from Step 2 out of a maximum of 5 actions, missed actions from Step 2 calculated as 5 minus the distinct completed action count, and mornings completed from Step 3 out of 7 mornings. The man and the accountability partner write the next 7-day checklist before the 30-minute meeting ends.

#### Best Practices

- ✅ Do review all 5 traits together on Day 7, because the 5 traits form 1 framework from 1 Corinthians 16:13-14.
- ✅ Do report 3 exact numbers at the Day 14 review: completed actions, missed actions, and completed mornings out of 7.
- ❌ Don't select 1 of the 5 traits, practice only that 1 trait, and ignore the other 4 traits for longer than 7 days.
- ❌ Don't define the man's worth by a job title, an income figure, or a single day's score from Step 2.

#### Keep In Mind

- Identity in Christ in Lesson 7 means the man's standing before God rests on the man's relationship with Christ, not on job performance, income, physical strength, or approval from other people.
- Integration in Lesson 7 means the man practices all 5 traits within the same 7-day period, with at least 1 dated action per trait.
- A fall in Lesson 7 means a missed action or a sin the man confesses; a fall ends a streak, where a streak means the count of consecutive days in Step 2 on which the man completed at least 1 checklist action, and a fall does not end the man's participation in the plan.

#### Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use Lesson 7 to claim that a man with depression, addiction, or trauma needs faith alone and no professional care; a man with any of the 3 conditions named in this bullet must contact a pastor for spiritual care and a licensed professional for clinical care within 14 days, and within 24 hours if the man's safety is at risk.
- A man who plans to harm the man himself or harm another person must contact local emergency services and a trusted person immediately, within the same hour, and must tell the accountability partner within 24 hours after the immediate danger has passed.
- A man must not use the 5 traits to demand submission, money, sex, or silence from a spouse, a child, or any other person; a person who experiences that demand must contact a trusted person, a pastor, a licensed counsellor, or local emergency services within 24 hours.

#### Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** A man completes Lesson 7, completes checklist actions on Day 8 and Day 9 only, and schedules no action after Day 9.
  **Solution:** The man completes Step 1 on Day 7 with dated actions covering the full next 7 days, and the man schedules the Day 14 review from Step 4 before Day 8 ends.
- **Problem:** A man misses 3 actions in 1 week and concludes that the man has failed as a man.
  **Solution:** The man reports the exact count from Step 4, confesses any sin involved within 24 hours, completes the next scheduled action on the next calendar day, and starts a new 7-day checklist at the Day 14 review.
