# Jesus Said 2,000 Words That Changed Human History

## Overview

This sermon examines the Sermon on the Mount, a collection of Jesus' teachings that has shaped the lives of billions of people throughout history. The sermon explores how Jesus organized his teachings into a deliberate three-part structure centered on the Lord's Prayer, and how those teachings reveal the arrival of God's Kingdom among the poor, weak, and powerless. Understanding this context helps readers see why Jesus' words continue to challenge and inspire people today.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When studying the life and teachings of Jesus of Nazareth
- When exploring how the Sermon on the Mount has influenced human history
- When seeking to understand the connection between God's Kingdom and the poor in spirit

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Historical Context

Jesus lived in a Jewish world where he grew up meditating on the Hebrew Bible, the sacred Scriptures of Israel. These scriptures told the story of God creating a well-ordered world and appointing humans to rule it on God's behalf. When humans rule with God's wisdom and love, and when justice and peace prevail, and there is enough for everyone, that is God's Kingdom and God's will being done on Earth as it is in Heaven.

### Step 2: Recognize the Problem of Rebellion

The sermon teaches that humans foolishly rebel and start building their own kingdoms by their own wisdom. In Jesus' day, Israel was under the thumb of Roman oppressors, and God's Kingdom was nowhere to be seen. Many religious leaders offered different explanations: scribes and Pharisees thought God's Kingdom was not coming because Israel was not being faithful enough to the Torah; Sadducees thought Israel should cooperate with Rome; zealots ran for the hills to become freedom fighters against Rome; others withdrew to the desert to wait for God to start a new Israel.

### Step 3: See Where God's Kingdom Begins

Jesus went to the hill country of Israel where normal people were figuring out their lives as best they could. Most people were barely hanging on, with poor and sick people who had lost their land to Roman occupiers and struggled to pay heavy taxes. They were powerless and hopeless. Jesus went to these people, healing the sick and announcing that God's Kingdom was arriving. Jesus went up to a tall hill and said the arrival of God's Kingdom was starting there and then with them.

### Step 4: Embrace the Central Blessing

The very first line of the Sermon on the Mount is "Blessed are the poor in spirit." This means God's presence and blessing spring to life among the poor, not among the rich or powerful. Jesus tells listeners to look where people are poor, where they feel crushed and defeated, because God's Kingdom is beginning with those people.

### Step 5: Study the Deliberate Structure

The Sermon on the Mount is not a random collection of Jesus' teachings. It has been organized in a beautiful way so that it is easier to remember and meditate on. There are three main parts of the sermon, the middle of which has three parts, and then each of those middle parts themselves have three parts. Right at the center of the center is the famous prayer that Jesus taught his followers: "Our Father in Heaven, may your name be treated as holy. May your Kingdom come, may your will be done on Earth as it is in Heaven."

## Examples

### Example 1: Jesus Teaching About God's Kingdom

Jesus went to the hill country of Israel where normal people were figuring out their lives as best they could. Most people were barely hanging on, with poor and sick people who had lost their land to Roman occupiers and struggled to pay heavy taxes. They were powerless and hopeless. Jesus went to these people, healing the sick and announcing that God's Kingdom was arriving. People gathered from all over to hear his teachings.

### Example 2: The Lord's Prayer as the Center

The Sermon on the Mount has an intentional design. It begins with Jesus pronouncing blessings on his listeners. Right at the center of the center is the famous prayer that Jesus taught his followers: "Our Father in Heaven, may your name be treated as holy. May your Kingdom come, may your will be done on Earth as it is in Heaven." This prayer sits at the heart of Jesus' three-part structure, showing that the sermon has been carefully designed.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Study the Hebrew Bible to understand the context of Jesus' teachings about God's Kingdom.
- ✅ Look for God's Kingdom among the poor, weak, and powerless rather than among the rich and powerful.
- ✅ Recognize that the Sermon on the Mount has a deliberate, organized structure with three main parts.
- ✅ Meditate on the Lord's Prayer as the center of Jesus' teaching about God's will being done on Earth.
- ❌ Do not assume the Sermon on the Mount is a random collection of sayings.
- ❌ Do not look for God's Kingdom only among the rich, powerful, or religious elite.
- ❌ Do not ignore the historical context of Roman oppression and Jewish expectations in Jesus' day.


## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Reading the Sermon on the Mount as a random collection of disconnected sayings.
  **Solution:** Study the sermon's deliberate three-part structure, with three main sections, each containing three parts, and the Lord's Prayer at the center of the center.

- **Problem:** Expecting God's Kingdom to arrive through human power, wealth, or political force.
  **Solution:** Follow Jesus' teaching that God's Kingdom begins among the poor in spirit, the powerless, the weak, and the nobodies, not among the rich or powerful.
