# This Is Who Experiences the Good Life (According to Jesus)

## Overview

The Sermon on the Mount is the largest collection of the teachings of Jesus, all about his good news that God's heavenly Kingdom has touched down here on Earth. This sermon examines the introduction of the Sermon on the Mount, focusing on the nine announcements called the Beatitudes, which reveal who is experiencing the good life according to Jesus. The Beatitudes are divided into three parts: the first three announce God's Kingdom coming to the least likely people, the next three describe the character of those who will be most at home in God's Kingdom, and the last three address the difficult path for those who choose to follow Jesus.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When studying the Sermon on the Mount and the Beatitudes
- When wanting to understand who experiences the good life according to Jesus
- When seeking to understand the character of those who will be most at home in God's Kingdom
- When learning about the identity of Jesus' followers and their calling

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize the Nine Beatitudes

The Beatitudes describe who is experiencing the good life according to Jesus. They reveal that God's Kingdom is coming to the powerless, the grieving, and the unimportant. Jesus announces that God's heavenly reality where everything is done according to God's good will is being brought to earth for people to experience now.

### Step 2: Understand the First Three Beatitudes

These announce that God's heavenly Kingdom is coming to the least likely people:
- "How good is life for the poor in spirit" - those lacking energy and power, both internally (feeling downcast or discouraged) and externally (those on the margins of society), for theirs is the kingdom of the skies.
- "How good is life for those who grieve" - for they will be comforted.
- "How good is life for the unimportant" (Greek: "praus," often translated "the meek") - those with no influence in society, those who are looked down on and discarded, for they will inherit the land.

### Step 3: Understand the Next Three Beatitudes

These describe the character of those who will be most at home in God's Kingdom:
- "How good is life for those who hunger and thirst for righteousness" - living in right relationship with God and others, treating people with fairness and justice, for they will be satisfied.
- "How good is life for those who show mercy" - generosity and extravagant kindness that goes beyond what is required, for they will be shown mercy.
- "How good is life for the pure in heart" - living from pure motives with desires aligned with God's desires in all actions, for they will see God.

### Step 4: Understand the Last Three Beatitudes

These are about the difficult path for those who choose to follow Jesus:
- "How good is life for the peacemakers" - those who enter conflicts and work creatively to open up a just and generous path forward for others, for they will be called children of God.
- "How good is life for those who have been persecuted on account of doing what is right" - for theirs is the kingdom of the skies.
- When people insult you, persecute you, and spread evil lies against you on account of Jesus, celebrate and shout for joy, because your reward is great in the skies, for this is how they persecuted the prophets before you.

### Step 5: Live as Salt and Light

Followers of Jesus are called to be salt of the land and light of the world:
- As salt, preserve the life of creation by preserving the covenant.
- As light, let your light shine before people so they can see your good works, and so they can give honor to your Father who is in the skies.
- Good works are acts of service for people in need and generosity to the poor.

## Examples

### Example 1: Being Poor in Spirit

Someone feeling downcast or discouraged, or those on the margins of society without external power, can experience God's Kingdom as Jesus offers it to them first. Instead of being last, Jesus offers God's Kingdom to them first.

### Example 2: Hunger and Thirst for Righteousness

Being unsatisfied with the disorder in the world and the broken relationships between people, having a holy discontent and longing to see relationships made right all over. When people treat each other the way Jesus taught, it leads to healthy, satisfying relationships where things are made right.

### Example 3: Showing Mercy

Mercy is a step beyond doing what is simply right or fair. It is generosity and extravagant kindness that goes beyond what is required. The degree to which we show mercy is connected to how much mercy we'll receive.

### Example 4: Being a Peacemaker

Entering into conflicts and working creatively to open up a just and generous path forward for others. This may push against the status quo and result in resistance, but if this peacemaking reflects the loving way of Jesus and is done for his reputation, it's worth it.

### Example 5: Being Salt and Light

Preserving the life of creation through covenant faithfulness, like salt preserves food. Letting your light shine through acts of service for people in need and generosity to the poor, so the whole world gets to experience the goodness of God.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Recognize that the good life is not about material comfort or power but about experiencing God's Kingdom
- ✅ Hunger and thirst for right relationships with God and others
- ✅ Show mercy as generosity that goes beyond what is required
- ✅ Keep pure motives, aligning your desires with God's desires in all actions
- ✅ Work as a peacemaker, entering conflicts to create just and generous paths
- ✅ Be prepared for persecution but celebrate it as part of bearing witness to God's Kingdom
- ✅ Preserve the life of creation through covenant faithfulness (salt)
- ✅ Let your light shine through good works and service to others
- ✅ Avoid being "unsalty" by remaining faithful to the covenant
- ✅ Avoid doing good works for public praise rather than pure motives

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon is a commentary on the Beatitudes in the Sermon on the Mount and does not replace personal Bible study, prayer, or expert spiritual guidance.
- Pause and reflect on which Beatitude God is calling you to live out more fully in this season.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Misunderstanding "poor in spirit" as only internal poverty
  **Solution:** Recognize it also refers to those lacking external power and on the margins of society.
- **Problem:** Thinking the good life is about material comfort
  **Solution:** The Beatitudes teach that the good life is about experiencing God's Kingdom, which often comes to the least likely people.
- **Problem:** Confusing mercy with simply doing what is right or fair
  **Solution:** Mercy is generosity and extravagant kindness that goes beyond what is required.
- **Problem:** Thinking pure in heart means being perfect
  **Solution:** It means living from pure motives and aligning your desires with God's desires, not being without sin.
- **Problem:** Avoiding peacemaking because it can be dangerous
  **Solution:** Peacemaking may push against the status quo and result in resistance, but it reflects the loving way of Jesus and is worth it.
- **Problem:** Fearing persecution for doing what is right
  **Solution:** Jesus says to celebrate and shout for joy when persecuted for following him, as this is how the prophets before you were persecuted.
- **Problem:** Being "unsalty" by violating the covenant
  **Solution:** Remain faithful to the covenant to preserve the life of creation, as God is renewing his salty crew through Jesus.
- **Problem:** Hiding your light instead of letting it shine
  **Solution:** Let your light shine before people through good works so they can see and give honor to God.
