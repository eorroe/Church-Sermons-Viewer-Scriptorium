# What Jesus Taught in the Sermon on The Mount

## Overview

The video teaches that the Sermon on the Mount reveals the character and calling of God's Kingdom through a three-part structure. It explains how Jesus' teachings fulfill the Torah and call followers to live with integrity in their relationship with God, others, and material possessions. Viewers learn that true righteousness is about becoming whole people who reflect God's blessing, and the sermon concludes by calling each listener to choose between following Jesus' wisdom or relying on their own.

## When to Follow This Sermon

Follow this sermon when preparing to study, teach, or meditate on Jesus' teachings about Kingdom righteousness and how to live in right relationship with God and neighbor. It is also helpful when discerning how to apply the Beatitudes, Jesus' teaching on the Torah, or the call to store up treasure in the sky rather than on the land. Return to this sermon whenever you need clarity on what it means to be salt and light in the world.

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize the Surprising Identity of God's Kingdom

Begin with the introduction, where Jesus announces the surprising identity of those in God's Kingdom. Receive the nine sayings called the Beatitudes, which redefine who experiences the good life as the lowly outsiders who hunger for righteousness and suffer as peacemakers. Accept Jesus' call to be "salt of the land" and "light of the world," recognizing that he and his followers are fulfilling Israel's covenant promise to reflect God's light, peace, and blessing to all nations.

### Step 2: Understand Righteousness and the Torah

Move into the first part of the main body, where Jesus teaches that his righteousness fulfills the Torah rather than setting it aside. Study the six case studies Jesus offers on anger, lust, divorce, telling the truth, revenge, and enemy love. Embrace Jesus' summary of this way of life as "teleios," a Greek word meaning complete or whole, recognizing that the purpose of the Torah is to teach God's wisdom so people become mature, whole people who spread God's blessing to the world.

### Step 3: Pursue Righteousness in Religious Practices

Turn to the second part of the main body, where Jesus warns that living in right relationship with God and others results in reward, but doing righteousness for public praise causes you to miss the real reward God has in store. Apply Jesus' teaching on three practices where devotion can go sideways: generosity to the poor, prayer, and fasting. Express your devotion in subtle ways that do not attract attention, and store up treasure in the sky rather than treasure on the land, knowing that the real reward is knowing and being known by the loving God of the universe.

### Step 4: Live Righteousness in Relationships with God and Neighbor

Engage the third part of the main body, where Jesus addresses how righteousness is expressed in relationships with God and neighbor. Address the obstacle of money and possessions, which claim allegiance and cause worry, and receive Jesus' parables about the healthy generous eye versus the evil stingy eye, and the choice between two masters: God or money. Trust the generous God of creation who cares for you, rather than chasing security from things that are ultimately insecure. Do not judge others, remembering the parable about not removing the speck from someone else's eye when you have a log in your own. Use discernment when trying to help and correct others, as illustrated by the parable about not tossing holy pearls to pigs and dogs. Ask God for the wisdom you need, knowing that when you ask, seek, and knock, the door will be opened for you. Apply the Golden Rule: do to others what you want them to do to you, for this simple teaching is what the Torah and Prophets are all about.

### Step 5: Make a Decision About How to Live

Respond to the conclusion, where Jesus calls you to make a choice about how you are going to live your life. Consider the three images illustrating this choice: the two paths with two gates, one leading to life and the other to ruin; the two kinds of trees, one good and one bad, by which you will know a tree by its fruit; and the two types of houses, one built on sand by ignoring Jesus and using your own wisdom, and one built on rock by listening to Jesus and using his wisdom. Choose to build your house on the rock.

## Examples

### Example 1: Applying the Six Case Studies of Righteousness

Jesus explores six case studies showing how his righteousness fulfills the Torah: anger, lust, divorce, telling the truth, revenge, and enemy love. A small group or Bible study can walk through each case study, reflecting on how Jesus' teachings bring the Torah to completion by addressing the heart behind each action rather than merely external obedience. This helps participants understand that "teleios" — being complete or whole — is the goal of the law, not merely following rules.

### Example 2: Practicing the Golden Rule in Community

Jesus ends the third part of the main body with the Golden Rule: "Do to others what you want them to do to you." A community group or family can use this as a framework for resolving conflicts, making decisions, and evaluating how they treat one another. Because Jesus says this simple teaching is what the Torah and Prophets are all about, it can serve as a unifying standard for relational ethics in any context where people are learning to live out righteousness together.

## Best Practices

- Righteousness is about living in right relationship with God and with others, not merely following external rules.
- Express devotion to God in subtle ways that do not attract public attention, so you do not miss the real reward.
- Store up treasure in the sky, not treasure on the land, because the greatest reward is knowing and being known by the loving God of the universe.
- Trust the generous God of creation who cares about you more than you can imagine, rather than chasing security from things that are ultimately insecure.
- Do not judge others, especially when you have your own issues to address, and use discernment when trying to help and correct others.
- Ask, seek, and knock, trusting that the door will be opened and wisdom will be given.
- Apply the Golden Rule as the summary of the Torah and Prophets: do to others what you want them to do to you.

## Keep In Mind

- If you are experiencing spiritual distress or conflict while engaging with these teachings, consider seeking guidance from a trusted spiritual leader or counselor.

## Common Pitfalls

- Doing righteousness for public praise: When generosity, prayer, or fasting are done to be seen by others, you miss the real reward God has in store. Practice these disciplines privately.
- Letting possessions claim your allegiance: Our stuff tends to claim our allegiance and causes us to worry, becoming one of the biggest obstacles to healthy relationships with God and other people. Guard against this by storing up treasure in the sky.
- Judging others while ignoring your own issues: The parable of the speck and the log warns against trying to remove a speck from someone else's eye when you have a log in your own. Address your own heart before correcting others.
- Building on sand: Ignoring Jesus' teachings and building your life by your own wisdom is like building a house on sand. When the storm comes, it will not stand. Choose to listen to Jesus and build on the rock of his wisdom.
