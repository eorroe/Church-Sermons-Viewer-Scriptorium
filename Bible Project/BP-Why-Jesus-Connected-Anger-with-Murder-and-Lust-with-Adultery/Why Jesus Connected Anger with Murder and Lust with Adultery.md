# Why Jesus Connected Anger with Murder and Lust with Adultery

## Overview

This sermon explores Jesus' teaching from the Sermon on the Mount, where he connects anger with murder and lust with adultery to reveal deeper principles about righteousness. Jesus shows that God's wisdom in the ancient laws is aimed at our core desires and motivations, calling us to treat every person as a fellow image of God with ultimate value.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When studying Jesus' teachings on righteousness and the Sermon on the Mount
- When exploring Christian ethics regarding anger, murder, and verbal abuse
- When learning about Jesus' teachings on lust, adultery, and sexual integrity
- When examining Jesus' teaching on marriage, divorce, and the dignity of women
- When seeking to understand how ancient biblical laws apply to modern relationships

## Steps

### Step 1: Study God's Wisdom in Ancient Laws

Jesus taught that righteousness can be learned through studying God's wisdom found in the ancient Scriptures, the Torah and the Prophets. He demonstrated this by first quoting a command from the Torah and then revealing the deeper wisdom underneath the command.

### Step 2: Examine the Case Study on Murder and Anger

Jesus quoted the command "you shall not commit murder" and then extended its meaning: "everyone who is angry with his brother will be guilty in the court." He shows that murder, anger, and verbal abuse are all outward expressions of a deeper problem: how much you value, or don't value, someone's existence. The wisdom of this command is about treating everyone as valuable, not just avoiding physical violence.

### Step 3: Examine the Case Study on Adultery and Lust

Jesus quoted "you shall not commit adultery" and then taught that "everyone who keeps on staring at a woman in order to stir up sexual desire, he's already committed adultery with her in his heart." He is not talking about merely finding someone attractive, but about objectifying someone by looking at them like they exist for your own pleasure. When we do this, we strip a fellow human of their God-given dignity. Jesus uses hyperbole ("cut off your eye or hand") to emphasize that the real problem is in our hearts.

### Step 4: Examine the Case Study on Divorce

Jesus addressed the debated divorce law in the Torah. In his day, only Israelite men could initiate divorce, and this led to abuse. Jesus sided with those who limited legitimate divorce to cases of sexual immorality (understood as adultery or unchastity), stating that any man who sends away his wife except for a case of sexual immorality makes her the victim of adultery. This protects the dignity of women as images of God and challenges treating marriage as disposable.

### Step 5: Apply the Core Principle of Righteousness

All three case studies illustrate that for Jesus, real righteousness and wisdom means treating every human as if they possess ultimate value. The commands are aimed at our core desires and motivations that affect how we treat people. God's wisdom invites us to honor the image of God in every person we meet.

## Examples

### Example 1: Applying the Murder/Anger Principle

Instead of only avoiding physical violence, examine your heart for attitudes of superiority and contempt toward others. When you feel angry or are tempted to verbally abuse someone, recognize that this reveals a failure to value their existence as a fellow image of God.

### Example 2: Applying the Adultery/Lust Principle

When you find yourself objectifying others through lingering looks or sexualizing thoughts, recognize that this strips them of their God-given dignity. Redirect your focus to see them as a person made in God's image, not as an object for your pleasure.

### Example 3: Applying the Divorce Principle

In marriage, treat your spouse as a covenant partner who reflects God's image. Avoid using divorce as a casual solution to dissatisfaction. In your church and civil communities, advocate for protections that prevent the oppression of women through misuse of divorce laws.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Treating Jesus' hyperbole about cutting off body parts as literal commands rather than rhetorical emphasis on heart change.
  **Solution:** Recognize that Jesus was a master of hyperbole and that his point is always about internal transformation, not literal physical mutilation.

- **Problem:** Assuming Jesus' teaching on divorce means there are no legitimate grounds for divorce beyond adultery.
  **Solution:** Jesus was addressing a specific cultural abuse of his day. Seek broader biblical counsel and professional guidance for complex marital situations involving neglect or abuse. Note also that Jesus' full statement (Matthew 5:32) indicates that the man who marries a divorced woman also commits adultery, making the teaching balanced rather than one-sided. Consider also how this intersects with civil laws and church policies regarding remarriage, which vary by jurisdiction and tradition.

- **Problem:** Only focusing on outward rule-keeping while ignoring inner attitudes of contempt and objectification.
  **Solution:** Jesus' righteousness is about heart transformation. Regularly examine your motivations and how you value the dignity of others.
