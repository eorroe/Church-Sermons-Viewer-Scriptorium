# How Can You Know What's Right? Here's What Jesus Thought.

## Overview

This sermon explores Jesus' teaching on righteousness from the Sermon on the Mount, explaining how to discern right action by learning God's wisdom through the Torah and Prophets. It reveals Jesus as the fulfillment of Scripture who provides a practical vision for building healthy communities and transforming broken relationships through wholehearted love and respect.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need to understand what true righteousness means
- When working through difficult moral decisions and ethical dilemmas
- When the user asks about how Jesus interpreted the Old Testament law

## Steps

### Step 1: Ask the Right Question About Righteousness

Recognize that the core question is: "How do we know what it means to do right by everyone in any given situation?" A righteous person is someone who consistently does right by God and right by others. Jesus said those who live this way are entering the kingdom of the skies.

### Step 2: Learn God's Wisdom Through the Torah and Prophets

For Israelites, finding God's wisdom meant meditating on the Scriptures called the Torah and Prophets. The Torah (the first five books of the Bible) means "teaching" in Hebrew, though often translated as "law." These books aren't just laws—they tell a story about God's plan to partner with humans in ruling the world.

### Step 3: Understand the Story of the Torah

When humans ruined that partnership through stupidity and violence, God began a rescue plan. First by choosing and then rescuing the Israelites, and then by inviting them into a covenant relationship. The story of Moses on Mount Sinai is where they received commands to live by. The Torah was given centuries earlier when Israelites could live free on their own land, but in Jesus' time they were occupied by Romans and couldn't follow many laws as originally stated.

### Step 4: See How the Prophets Continued the Story

After the Torah come the Prophets who continued the story. They showed how Israel failed to live by God's wisdom by violating those commands, but they also wrote poetry about how that failure was not the end of the story. God was going to bring this partnership to its fulfillment.

### Step 5: Meditate on Scripture Day and Night

The Torah and the Prophets reveal God's wisdom to anyone who will meditate on them day and night. The 613 laws in the Torah are not a comprehensive rule book. Teachers of the Torah called scribes and Pharisees would fill in the gaps by discerning God's wisdom underneath the laws so they could apply it to other areas of life. For instance, the Torah said Israelites should stop working one day a week, but the Torah doesn't give a full definition of what counts as work, so scribes would study the Scriptures and provide a comprehensive list.

### Step 6: Embrace Jesus as the Fulfillment of the Scriptures

Jesus claimed he was taking the story forward, showing how to be righteous and live by God's wisdom. He said, "Don't suppose that I've come to dismantle the Torah or the Prophets. I haven't come to dismantle them, but to fill them full." Jesus even said his vision of righteousness was more in line with God's wisdom than anyone else in his day: "Unless your righteousness far surpasses that of the scribes and Pharisees, you won't enter the kingdom of skies."

### Step 7: Live Out Jesus' Vision of Righteous Community

Jesus is offering a bold and beautiful vision of human life: how to build healthy communities, heal broken relationships, and transform hostility into honor and respect. He demonstrates this through six case studies where he quotes a command from the Torah and then explains how it reveals God's wisdom so that we can learn how to consistently love God and do right by our neighbor.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Question of Righteousness

When life is messy and situations are complicated, the temptation is to rely on external rule-following. But Jesus calls us to a deeper righteousness that comes from meditating on the whole story of Scripture and understanding God's wisdom beneath the commands.

### Example 2: The Torah as Teaching, Not Just Law

Instead of seeing the Torah as a burdensome legal code, recognize it as God's teaching about partnership with humanity. This reframes obedience from rule-keeping to participating in God's redemptive story.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Meditate on the Torah and Prophets day and night to discern God's wisdom
- ✅ Look for the story and purpose behind biblical commands, not just the literal rules
- ✅ Apply God's wisdom to new situations by understanding the principles beneath the laws
- ✅ Study how Jesus fulfilled the Scriptures to understand true righteousness
- ✅ Seek to build healthy communities and heal broken relationships as Jesus taught
- ❌ Don't treat the Torah as merely a comprehensive rule book for every situation
- ❌ Don't assume righteousness is just external compliance with religious traditions
- ❌ Don't dismiss the Old Testament as outdated—Jesus claimed to fulfill it
- ❌ Don't rely on human traditions alone instead of meditating on Scripture

## Keep In Mind

- This content is for spiritual formation and ethical reflection, not professional counseling or legal advice.
- Seek qualified spiritual direction when applying these teachings to complex life situations.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Treating the 613 laws as a complete rule book for every modern situation
  **Solution:** Follow the example of Jesus and the scribes by seeking the wisdom and principles beneath the commands
- **Problem:** Thinking Jesus rejected the Old Testament
  **Solution:** Jesus said he came to fulfill the Torah and Prophets, not abolish them
- **Problem:** Reducing righteousness to external rule-following
  **Solution:** Righteousness is about consistently doing right by God and others from the heart
- **Problem:** Applying ancient laws directly without understanding their story context
  **Solution:** Meditate on the whole narrative of Scripture to understand God's wisdom
