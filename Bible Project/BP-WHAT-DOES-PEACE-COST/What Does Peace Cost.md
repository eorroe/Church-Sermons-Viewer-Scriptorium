# What Does Peace Cost?

## Overview

This sermon explores Jesus' radical teachings on peacemaking from the Sermon on the Mount, revealing that true peace requires costly, creative love rather than retaliation or avoidance. It examines how covenant laws on oaths, retaliation, and love for neighbors are deepened into a call for radical honesty, nonviolent resistance, and indiscriminate generosity that imitates God's love for all people. The central message is that followers of Jesus are called to become peacemakers who actively mend broken relationships, even with enemies, because creative love is the only pathway to real peace.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When navigating conflict in personal relationships, friendships, or family dynamics
- When responding to someone who has wronged, insulted, or mistreated you
- When tempted to retaliate, seek revenge, or "get even" with an enemy
- When struggling to forgive or love someone who hates or opposes you
- When discerning how to respond to systemic injustice or oppression without violence
- When seeking to build peace in divided communities, workplaces, or social circles
- When feeling spiritually called to a higher standard of love beyond your comfort zone

## Steps

### Step 1: Commit to Radical Honesty and Integrity

Make your "yes" mean "yes" and your "no" mean "no" without using God's name, reputation, or religious oaths to manipulate people into trusting you. Let your character and consistency be the basis of trust, not empty swearing.

### Step 2: Refuse to Retaliate

When someone harms you, do not pay back evil with evil. Resist the natural urge to get even. Instead, choose to value peace more than your own wounded pride or sense of justice through revenge.

### Step 3: Respond Creatively and Nonviolently to Evil

When confronted with hostility or injustice, respond with bold, nonviolent action that exposes injustice and asserts your humanity. This includes turning the other cheek to force recognition of your equal dignity, giving generously to shame the oppressor, and going beyond what is demanded to subvert power dynamics.

### Step 4: Love Your Enemies

Extend the same love you have for friends and family to those who mistreat you. Pray for their well-being, seek their good, and treat them as fellow human beings made in God's image, not as enemies to be destroyed.

### Step 5: Imitate God's Indiscriminate Generosity

Follow the model of God's love, which is shown to both the evil and the good, the righteous and the unrighteous. Let your love be impartial and generous, flowing freely to all people regardless of how they treat you.

### Step 6: Become a Peacemaker

Actively work to repair broken relationships at every level in your community. Invite others into reconciliation, seek to mend divisions, and live as a person who is "complete" in love—reflecting the fullness of God's character.

## Examples

### Example 1: Turning the Other Cheek

In the ancient Roman world, a slap on the right cheek with the back of the hand was a degrading act of domination. Jesus' call to "turn the other cheek" is not passive submission but a bold, creative act that forces the oppressor to strike you as an equal—with an open hand—rather than as an inferior. By offering the left cheek, you refuse to be dehumanized and assert your dignity as a fellow human being, even at personal cost.

### Example 2: Going the Extra Mile

A Roman soldier could force a subject to carry his pack for one mile, a humiliating act of occupation. Jesus instructs his followers to go a second mile voluntarily. The first mile may be done under coercion and dehumanization, but the second mile is a choice to treat the soldier as a friend and assert one's own humanity. This creative generosity can subvert the power game and even transform the relationship, opening the door for genuine peace.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Let your "yes" be "yes" and your "no" be "no" without manipulation or hidden agendas
- ✅ Choose peace over retaliation when wronged, even when you feel justified in revenge
- ✅ Respond to evil with creative, nonviolent action that exposes injustice
- ✅ Love and pray for those who mistreat you as a regular spiritual discipline
- ✅ Extend generosity to enemies as a witness to God's indiscriminate love
- ✅ Actively seek reconciliation and peacemaking in broken relationships
- ❌ Don't use oaths, swearing, or religious language to manipulate trust
- ❌ Don't retaliate or seek revenge when you have been wronged
- ❌ Don't respond to hostility with more hostility or violence
- ❌ Don't limit your love to friends, family, or those who benefit you
- ❌ Don't stand by when you have an opportunity to mend broken relationships
- ❌ Don't confuse peacekeeping with peacemaking—avoidance is not the same as reconciliation

## Keep In Mind

- Creative nonviolent resistance may place you in personal danger or provoke further hostility from aggressors; exercise wisdom and seek counsel when engaging in high-risk peacemaking situations.
- Loving enemies does not mean remaining in abusive or harmful relationships without boundaries—it means responding with generosity while protecting your own safety and well-being.
- Peacemaking in contexts of systemic injustice requires discernment to avoid enabling oppression; seek the guidance of wise, experienced mentors in your community.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Mistaking passive avoidance of conflict for peacemaking, when Jesus calls for active engagement and creative confrontation.
  **Solution:** Recognize that true peacemaking requires courageous presence and action, not silence or retreat. Step into the conflict with a heart for reconciliation.

- **Problem:** Believing that loving enemies means tolerating abuse or remaining in harmful situations without boundaries.
  **Solution:** Love your enemy through prayer and outward generosity, but maintain healthy boundaries and seek safety. Creative love does not require self-destruction.

- **Problem:** Using Jesus' teachings on nonretaliation as an excuse for passivity or inaction in the face of injustice.
  **Solution:** Jesus' call to turn the other cheek and go the extra mile is a radical, provocative form of action—not passivity. Engage the injustice creatively and courageously, even when it costs you.
