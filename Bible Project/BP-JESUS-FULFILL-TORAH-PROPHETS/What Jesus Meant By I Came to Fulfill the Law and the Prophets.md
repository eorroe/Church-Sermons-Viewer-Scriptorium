# What Jesus Meant By "I Came to Fulfill the Law and the Prophets."

## Overview

This sermon explores Jesus' teachings on the Torah and the Prophets, explaining what Jesus meant by fulfilling the Torah and the Prophets rather than abolishing them, and how his followers can live out this higher standard of righteousness that has been at the heart of the Hebrew Scriptures all along.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When studying the Sermon on the Mount in Matthew chapter 5
- When exploring Jesus' relationship to the Hebrew Bible (Torah and Prophets)
- When seeking to understand the Kingdom of Heaven ethic and what it means to be "great" in God's kingdom

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand What "Torah and the Prophets" Means

The phrase "Torah and the Prophets" refers to Jesus' Bible, the Hebrew Bible. These Scriptures tell a story about God calling all of humanity to partner with him to steward the world in a way that brings justice and peace. God called one nation, Israel, to lead the way with the hope that he would make all the world new and unite Heaven and Earth.

### Step 2: Recognize Jesus' Mission to Fulfill the Scriptures

Jesus claims he is renewing Israel as God's partner so they can invite everyone else into the redemptive mission of restoring the relationship between Heaven and Earth, and so bring the biblical story to completion. This is a key part of what he means by saying he came to fulfill the Torah and Prophets.

### Step 3: Address Misconceptions About Jesus and the Law

Israel's leading Bible scholars of the day, called the scribes and Pharisees, sometimes disagreed with Jesus' views on God's instruction in the Torah, like the commands about the Sabbath or ritual purity. They accused Jesus of not honoring God's word. Jesus responds by affirming the permanence of the Torah.

### Step 4: Understand the Permanence of God's Word

Jesus says: "Until the sky and the land pass on, not one dot or one squiggle will pass on from the Torah until all things have taken place." The sky and land will undergo a divinely orchestrated transformation and emerge as a renewed creation. But God's word that brought the sky and land into being is above and beyond time. God's purpose and word revealed in the Torah endure forever.

### Step 5: Understand the Meaning of "Dots and Squiggles"

The "dot" is the Greek word iota, and the "squiggle" is keraia. These refer to the tiniest pen strokes used in writing Hebrew letters to distinguish one Hebrew letter from another. Even the tiniest details of the Torah are important because they express God's wisdom. For Jesus, all the Torah and Prophets, including God's commands to Israel, reveal God's wisdom to guide the lives of his people in every age.

### Step 6: Live Out the Higher Standard of the Kingdom

Jesus says: "Whoever undoes one of the least of these commands and teaches people to do the same, they will be called least in the kingdom of the skies. But whoever does them and then teaches people, this one will be called great in the kingdom of the skies." To undo the Torah is to reject its commands as useless or out of date. Those who undo the Torah are least in the kingdom of the skies.

### Step 7: Embrace the Heart of the Torah

The Kingdom of the skies is God's heavenly domain, full of right relationships and life and goodness. Jesus said that heavenly reality was establishing his Kingdom on Earth through himself as he formed communities of disciples who live by God's wisdom in new and creative ways. Jesus is flipping the social order: the scribes and Pharisees thought of themselves as the greatest, but God's Kingdom turns the whole thing upside-down so that the last are first and the first are last.

### Step 8: Pursue Righteousness Beyond External Compliance

Jesus says: "Unless your righteousness far surpasses that of the scribes and Pharisees, you won't be entering the kingdom of the skies." The Torah and Prophets point to an even more radical heart transformation that produces behavior change. The Scriptures ought to lead us to a Kingdom of God ethic that is more generous, more just, and more merciful than we could ever imagine.

## Examples

### Example 1: Renewing Israel's Calling

Jesus renews Israel as God's partner to restore the relationship between Heaven and Earth, showing that fulfilling the Torah means living out its deepest purpose rather than abandoning it.

### Example 2: The Permanence of God's Word

Just as God's word created the sky and land, his purposes in the Torah endure forever, beyond the passing of creation. This means every detail of Scripture matters for guiding God's people in every age.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Take the Torah and Prophets seriously as God's enduring revelation for all ages
- ✅ Understand that fulfilling the law means living out its deepest purpose, not abandoning it
- ✅ Pursue a righteousness that surpasses external religious compliance
- ✅ Teach others the ways of the Kingdom with humility and generosity
- ✅ Recognize that even the smallest details of Scripture express God's wisdom
- ❌ Don't reject the commands of the Torah as outdated or useless
- ❌ Don't rely on external religiosity without heart transformation
- ❌ Don't assume the scribes and Pharisees had the highest standard of righteousness
- ❌ Don't misunderstand "least" and "great" as worldly rankings rather than Kingdom values

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Misunderstanding Jesus' statement as rejecting the Torah
  **Solution:** Recognize that Jesus came to fulfill the Torah, not do away with it, by revealing its deepest purpose in the Kingdom of God.
- **Problem:** Assuming the "dots and squiggles" are trivial details without significance
  **Solution:** Understand that even the smallest details of Scripture express God's wisdom and are important for guiding his people in every age.
- **Problem:** Thinking righteousness is about external compliance with rules
  **Solution:** Embrace that the Torah points to a transformation of the heart that produces a more generous, just, and merciful way of life.
