# Jesus' Perspective on Wealth

## Overview

This sermon reframes wealth as a spiritual obstacle and calls followers to redirect their attention, trust, and generosity toward God's Kingdom rather than material accumulation, so they can experience the union of Heaven and Earth in their daily lives.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need to evaluate your relationship with material possessions
- When working through anxiety about financial security or daily needs
- When the user asks about Jesus' teachings on money, generosity, or worry

## Steps

### Step 1: Release attachment to earthly storage

Stop storing up treasures on earth where moth and nibbler destroy and thieves break in and steal. Recognize that material accumulation demands your attention and loyalty, pulling you away from the Kingdom of God.

### Step 2: Redirect your treasure toward the eternal

Store up for yourself heavenly treasure—the kind of treasure that is secure and cannot be threatened. Cultivate the kind of wealth that God's heavenly Kingdom is made of, because the more you collect it, the more you will experience that Kingdom here on Earth now.

### Step 3: Align your heart with your treasure

Remember that where your treasures are, there too will be your heart. Let this truth shape your priorities, because what you store up has a way of slowly shaping you in its image for better or worse.

### Step 4: Cultivate a generous eye

Practice having a "good eye"—a figure of speech for being generous—rather than a "bad eye," which is a metaphor for being stingy and hoarding material possessions. Jesus warns that a bad eye is the quickest way to ruin yourself, while a generous spirit radiates light and life.

### Step 5: Choose one master

Acknowledge that you cannot serve two masters. Wealth is like a tyrant that demands your loyalty. Choose to serve God alone, because whatever you serve will shape your identity and destiny.

### Step 6: Release worry and trust the Father

Do not worry about your life, what you will eat or drink, or what you will wear. Consider how God has packed creation with abundance—each creature having just what it needs. Trust that your Father in the skies knows you need all these things and will provide.

### Step 7: Prioritize the Kingdom first

Rather than seeking material provision first, seek for the Kingdom of God and do right by God and all people. When you love God and love your neighbor, your needs will be met and you will find the rich peace of Heaven and Earth uniting in your own life.

## Examples

### Example 1: Choosing generosity over hoarding

When faced with a financial bonus or unexpected gift, instead of protecting or saving it all, practice storing up heavenly treasure by giving generously to those in need. This act of kindness shapes you toward generosity and reflects the Kingdom values Jesus taught.

### Example 2: Trusting God instead of worrying about provision

When anxiety rises about bills, job security, or unmet needs, pause and consider the birds of the air and the lilies of the field. Trust that your Father in the skies feeds them and clothes them, and He will do the same for you as you prioritize seeking His Kingdom.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Redirect your attention from material accumulation to eternal values and relationships
- ✅ Practice generosity as a way to store up heavenly treasure
- ✅ Trust God as a generous Father who knows your needs
- ✅ Let your heart follow where your treasure is placed
- ❌ Don't serve wealth as a master alongside God
- ❌ Don't hoard resources that can be destroyed or stolen
- ❌ Don't let anxiety about provision distract you from God's Kingdom

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon addresses spiritual formation and is not financial advice. Consult qualified financial advisors for material investment decisions.
- Trust in God's provision does not negate personal responsibility or prudent planning.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Worrying about material needs instead of trusting God
  **Solution:** Remember that your Father in the skies knows you need these things, and each day has enough trouble of its own.

- **Problem:** Trying to serve both God and wealth at the same time
  **Solution:** Recognize that wealth demands total loyalty and choose to serve God alone, because no one can serve two masters.

- **Problem:** Storing up treasures that can be destroyed or stolen
  **Solution:** Redirect your investments toward eternal values—acts of kindness, forgiveness, compassion, and loving relationships that last into the renewed creation.
