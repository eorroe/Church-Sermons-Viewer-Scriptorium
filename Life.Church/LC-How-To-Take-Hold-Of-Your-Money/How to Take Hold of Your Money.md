# How to Take Hold of Your Money

## Overview

Dave Ramsey teaches that how we spend our money often determines how we spend our lives. He shares his personal journey from being a "beer drinking hellraising hillbilly" who got rich at 26 and then lost everything through debt, to surrendering to God and discovering biblical principles for managing money. The sermon explains that money magnifies the character you already have — it amplifies generosity or exposes greed, kindness or anger, faithfulness or foolishness. The sermon provides five actionable steps from Scripture to help you take hold of your money.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need to start budgeting and managing your money wisely
- When working through debt that feels overwhelming or out of control
- When you want to honor God with your finances but don't know where to start
- When you feel stuck in a cycle of earning and losing money
- When you want to build a financial plan that aligns with biblical principles

## Steps

### Step 1: Get on a Written Plan (Budget)

Create a written budget that tells your money where to go instead of wondering where it went. Jesus said not to build a tower without first counting the cost (Luke 14:28). Write down every dollar coming in and every dollar going out. Control the controllables. Manage your money intentionally rather than accidentally. A budget is an act of winning, not a restriction.

### Step 2: Get Out of Debt

Quit borrowing money for anything for any reason. The borrower is slave to the lender (Proverbs 22:7). Debt gives other people control over your life and limits your freedom. Cut up credit cards, sell assets that cost you more money than they produce, such as vehicles with payments larger than your rent or investment properties that bleed cash every month, and learn to say "no" to purchases you cannot afford with cash. Get out of debt so that your income is truly yours to manage and invest.

### Step 3: Foster High Quality Relationships

You become who you hang around with. Evil company corrupts good habits (1 Corinthians 15:33). Choose your closest circle carefully because social network research consistently shows that over a decade, your income tends to fall within 10 to 15 percent of the average income of your ten closest friends. Hang out with generous people to become generous. Hang out with people who read the Bible to grow in your faith. Surround yourself with people who reflect the character and habits you want to build.

### Step 4: Save and Invest

Build an emergency fund of three to six months of expenses first. The house of the wise stores up choice food and oil (Proverbs 21:20), while the foolish devour all they have. Spend everything you make and you are living like a fool, biblically speaking. Once your emergency fund is in place, invest for the future. Save consistently so that you are prepared for unexpected events and positioned to build lasting wealth.

### Step 5: Be Outrageously Generous

Give cheerfully and generously once your own household needs are met — bills paid, food on the table, and no looming debt payments. Generosity is a heart posture, not a bank balance threshold. God loves a cheerful giver (2 Corinthians 9:7). Look for practical opportunities to bless others unexpectedly. Generosity changes your heart and the lives of people around you. When you are not broke and have your own life taken care of, you can reach out and help others in tangible ways.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Waffle House Blessing

On the way to a holiday feast, stop somewhere alone. Order coffee, pay for it, and secretly leave three or four $100 bills on the counter before slipping out. Watch how a stranger's entire month is changed by unexpected generosity. This kind of spontaneous blessing demonstrates what God does when we give cheerfully.

### Example 2: The $300 Date Night

After taking care of your household and getting out of debt, take your spouse out for a nice $300 date night. Learn to enjoy money as a gift from God rather than hoarding it out of fear. If even evil people know how to give good gifts (Matthew 7:11), how much more will your Father in heaven bless you when you manage His resources with generosity and joy.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Create and follow a zero-based budget every single month
- ✅ Quit borrowing money entirely and destroy or cancel all credit cards
- ✅ Evaluate your closest relationships and intentionally spend time with people who have the financial habits and faith you want
- ✅ Save three to six months of expenses in an emergency fund before aggressive investing
- ✅ Give cheerfully and look for unexpected opportunities to bless others
- ✅ Track every dollar coming in and going out
- ✅ Choose your "crew" carefully because you will become like them

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon provides spiritual guidance and does not replace personalized financial counsel from a qualified advisor.
- Seek wise counsel and clarification when making major financial decisions.
- Apply these principles prayerfully, trusting God for provision as you step out in obedience.
- Your journey with money is personal. Progress, not perfection, is what God honors.
 This sermon is for spiritual and educational guidance on personal finance and does not constitute professional financial advice.
- Financial decisions should be made prayerfully and with the guidance of the Holy Spirit and trusted advisors.
- When applying these principles in small groups or Bible studies, avoid pressuring others to share specific financial numbers or account details.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Continuing to use credit cards or borrow money while trying to get ahead.
  **Solution:** Cut up credit cards, close revolving accounts, and commit to a cash-only lifestyle until you are fully out of debt.
- **Problem:** Making a budget once and never updating it.
  **Solution:** Review and adjust your budget every single month. Income and expenses change, and your plan must change with them.
- **Problem:** Surrounding yourself with people who are financially reckless or spiritually distant.
  **Solution:** Intentionally choose your closest circle. You become like the people you spend the most time with.
- **Problem:** Waiting to give until you feel "rich enough."
  **Solution:** Start giving now, even if it is small. Generosity is a heart posture, not a bank balance threshold.
