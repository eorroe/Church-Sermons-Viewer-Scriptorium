## Lesson 1: Complete the Litmus Test Self-Assessment

### Overview
Before charting your course to wealth, you must understand exactly where you stand today. The Litmus Test is a brutal, honest self-assessment that separates Game Facers (who take control) from Blamers (who make excuses) and Dreamers (who never act).

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Answer six core questions honestly:**
   - Are you living a life by *your* design or someone else's?
   - What wealth stage are you in? (Broke, Accumulation, or Rich)
   - Are you in the right "platform" (industry) that connects to your goals, skills, and superpower?
   - What is your risk tolerance?
   - Do you need to take a step backward to take two steps forward?
   - Should you be an employee or an employer?

2. **Write down your answers** — do not keep them in your head. The act of writing forces clarity.

3. **Identify which mindset you default to:**
   - **Blamers** — make excuses, feel no control over their future
   - **Dreamers** — wait for a "someday" that never comes
   - **Game Facers** — build their lives deliberately through total control of the process

4. **Commit to becoming a Game Facer** — accept that the truth may be painful, but it is necessary.

### Best Practices
- ✅ Answer every question with brutal honesty. No one else will see this but you.
- ✅ Write your answers in a dedicated notebook or digital document.
- ✅ Revisit the Litmus Test every 6–12 months. Your answers will change as you grow.
- ❌ Do not rationalize or soften your answers to feel better about yourself.
- ❌ Do not skip questions because they feel uncomfortable.

### Common Pitfalls & Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| "I already know the answers." | You may know them intellectually, but writing them down creates accountability. |
| Being too harsh on yourself | Self-awareness without self-compassion leads to inaction. Be honest, but also be kind. |
| Treating the test as a one-time event | Life changes. Revisit your Litmus Test quarterly. |

## Lesson 2: Build a Complete Monthly Expense Map

### Overview
Most people *think* they know what their lifestyle costs. When they put it on paper, they almost always discover they need **a lot more money than they thought**. This exercise is foundational to everything that follows.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Gather 3 months of bank statements, credit card statements, and receipts.**

2. **Fill in every expense category** below, using your actual numbers:
   - **Monthly income (take-home pay, after taxes):** $______
   - **Monthly expenses by category:**
     - Mortgage/rent (including property taxes)
     - Home/renters insurance
     - Auto (payment, insurance, fuel)
     - Birthday gifts
     - Clothes
     - College savings
     - Food (groceries at home, dining out)
     - Health insurance
     - Housekeeper
     - Life insurance
     - Lawn care
     - Music lessons
     - Pool cleaner
     - Retirement savings
     - Schooling (tuition, tutor, activities, supplies)
     - Select sports (registration fees, travel)
     - Toys (boat, ATV, etc. — payment, fuel, insurance)
     - Utilities (gas, electric, water)
     - Vacations
   - Add any categories unique to your situation.

3. **Calculate your total monthly expenses.**

4. **Multiply your total monthly expenses by 12** to get your required annual after-tax income.

5. **Compare your current income to your required income.** Note the gap.

### Best Practices
- ✅ Use actual bank data, not estimates from memory.
- ✅ Include irregular but real expenses (annual subscriptions, car maintenance, holiday gifts).
- ✅ If a category doesn't apply to you, enter $0 — do not skip it.
- ✅ Be truthful. Game Facers are not afraid of the truth.
- ❌ Do not underestimate discretionary spending (dining out, entertainment, subscriptions).
- ❌ Do not forget to account for debt payments and savings goals as "expenses."

### Common Pitfalls & Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| "I'll just estimate — it's close enough." | Estimation fails. Use real bank data. The gap between estimate and reality is where financial surprises live. |
| Forgetting annual or quarterly bills | Divide annual costs by 12 and include them as monthly line items. |
| Ignoring lifestyle inflation | Compare this month's numbers to last year's. Are you spending more without earning more? |

## Lesson 3: Evaluate Whether You Are in the Right Platform (Industry)

### Overview
Your skills only earn maximum money when applied in the right industry ("platform"). A world-class sandwich maker at Subway will never earn what they need to own a Lamborghini — not because they aren't talented, but because the platform caps earnings.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Define your current platform** — what industry do you work in right now?

2. **Ask the critical question:** "Are there people in my platform who are currently achieving what I want and making the kind of money I need?"

3. **Evaluate your platform on three criteria:**
   - **Proof of success:** Are there *real* people (not just stories) earning what you need inside this platform?
   - **Growth trajectory:** Is this platform expanding or contracting? (e.g., early Uber drivers made fortunes; now the platform is saturated.)
   - **Your ability to win:** Can you realistically rise to the top within this platform given your skills, network, and work ethic?

4. **Study proven platforms with long-term staying power:**
   - **Enduring industries:** Banking, Insurance, Real Estate, Transportation/Logistics, Manufacturing, Medicine/Health Care, Housing, Writing/Storytelling/Video Editing
   - **Emerging opportunities:** Cyber Security, Artificial Intelligence, Virtual Reality, Automation

5. **Identify one successful person in your target platform** and study their path. Can you follow a proven producer rather than trying to invent a new path?

6. **Make a decision:** Stay in your current platform, pivot within it, or switch platforms entirely. Recognize that switching may require taking a temporary step backward.

### Best Practices
- ✅ Look for *multiple* high earners in a platform, not just one outlier.
- ✅ Research the industry's trajectory. Is it growing? Are the incumbents aging out?
- ✅ Follow someone who is already winning. Do not try to be the 1-in-100-million pioneer like Zuckerberg unless you truly are.
- ✅ Use platforms that are underpinned by real economic value (housing, food, energy, data security).
- ❌ Do not chase flash-in-the-pan trends (e.g., ride-sharing after saturation).
- ❌ Do not assume your skills transfer automatically to a new platform without research.
- ❌ Do not let pride keep you in a platform where you cannot win.

### Common Pitfalls & Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| "I love my industry, so it must be right." | Love is not a financial strategy. Separate passion from platform viability. |
| Only one person is winning | One success story may be luck. Need multiple data points. |
| Chasing a "cool" startup over a boring stable industry | Many fortunes have been made in "boring" industries with aging management. |
| Ignoring platform longevity | Ask: will this industry exist, in a recognizable form, in 10 years? |

## Lesson 4: Conduct Deep Research to Discover Your True Risk Tolerance

### Overview
Fear of risk is actually fear of the *unknown*. Your perception of risk is directly proportional to the amount of research and analysis you have done. The most financially secure people appear to take big risks — but they have removed the uncertainty through homework.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Before any investment or career move, conduct massive research:**
   - Read articles, reports, and books on the subject
   - Consult subject-matter experts (your CPA, attorney, trusted advisors)
   - Talk to people who have already done what you are considering
   - Gather real data, not hype

2. **Document your findings** in a risk assessment matrix:
   - Potential upside (best case)
   - Potential downside (worst case)
   - Probability of each outcome
   - Whether you can financially survive the worst case

3. **Ask yourself two diagnostic questions after research:**
   - "If I am wrong about this, what is the worst that can happen?"
   - "Am I financially in a position to handle that outcome?"

4. **Factor in the cost of inaction** — what will you miss out on if you don't act?

5. **Determine your role:** Are you the **captain** (active decision-maker) or the **passenger** (passive investor)? Both are valid. Both require research. Passive investors must especially verify that their partners are credible experts with winning track records.

6. **After completing research, reassess your gut:**
   - Still confident → proceed
   - Still uncertain → either do more research or pass

### Best Practices
- ✅ Never invest based on a friend's word alone. Do your own homework.
- ✅ Talk to multiple sources (CPA, attorney, industry veterans).
- ✅ Build a "buffer" of money to protect yourself in worst-case scenarios.
- ✅ Accept that risk is personal — a billionaire's tolerance differs from yours, and that is okay.
- ❌ Do not skip research because an opportunity feels "time-sensitive."
- ❌ Do not confuse familiarity with safety. Research what you *don't* know.
- ❌ Do not let FOMO (fear of missing out) override rigorous analysis.

### Common Pitfalls & Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Investing because a friend said it's good | Anecdotes ≠ data. Do your own research every time. |
| Only researching the upside | Force yourself to research the downside scenario and plan for it. |
| Thinking risk is static | Risk perception changes with knowledge. More research = less unknown risk. |
| Skipping expert consultation | CPAs, attorneys, and seasoned investors catch what enthusiastic amateurs miss. |

## Lesson 5: Apply the "Step Back to Take Two Steps Forward" Principle

### Overview
Most people are trapped by short-term income. They cannot make a strategic move backward because their current paycheck feels too valuable. But Game Facers recognize that a temporary step backward can create a massive leap forward.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Acknowledge your current income trap:** Are you staying in a job or industry because the money is *good enough* right now, even though it blocks your long-term vision?

2. **Define your Lifestyle by Design** — what does your ideal life actually require, time-wise and financially?

3. **Identify the gap:** What would you need to change (career, industry, location, education) to close that gap?

4. **Calculate the short-term cost:** How much income would you sacrifice to make the change? For how long?

5. **Build a reserve account** (detailed in Chapter 5) to create a financial cushion that makes the step backward survivable.

6. **Make the move when:** Your research shows the long-term upside exceeds the short-term sacrifice, AND you have enough reserves to survive the transition.

7. **Commit fully** — half-measures rarely work. Like Sean in the story, success requires "bustin' your butt" during the transition period.

### Best Practices
- ✅ Timing matters. Build your reserve *before* making the leap.
- ✅ Frame the step back as an *investment*, not a loss.
- ✅ Use the Doorman Principle: protect your Core Values (family, health, freedom) as non-negotiable.
- ✅ Surround yourself with people who have successfully made similar transitions.
- ❌ Do not take a step back impulsively. This requires research, planning, and a financial buffer.
- ❌ Do not stay in a high-paying job that destroys your health, family, or soul.

### Common Pitfalls & Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| "I make too much to leave." | Compare your income to your *required* income from Lesson 2. If your current income exceeds your needs, a pay cut may be survivable. |
| Taking the step without a reserve account | You will panic and reverse course. Build the buffer first (see Chapter 5). |
| Changing platforms without researching the new one | Research the target platform's earning potential before moving. |
| Expecting overnight success | Like Sean, you may need 1–2 years to fully replace your old income. Plan accordingly. |

## Lesson 6: Use the 10-Question Employee-or-Employer Framework

### Overview
The single most important fork in the road is whether to remain an employee, earn extra income on the side, or become your own boss. This framework forces data-driven decision-making instead of emotional dreaming.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
Answer each question honestly:

1. Does your current industry offer you the opportunity to earn what you need?
2. Assess your value points — what unique value can you bring to an organization based on your skill set?
3. Is anyone within your industry *currently* earning what you need? (You need real examples, not promises.)
4. Are you bringing *exceptional* (not middle-of-the-road) value to your organization right now?
5. Do you have the money to make a change if your current job cannot meet your needs?
6. If you don't have personal capital, do you have a network of people with money to fund your idea?
7. What options do you have? Money follows the deal — if you have a good deal, the funding will come.
8. Do you have the skills built up yet, or do you need to skill up first?
9. Have you done the research and analysis required to make a change?
10. Are you truly a business owner at heart, or would you be better off skilling up and rising through a great organization?

**Final decision questions:**
- Can you make the money you need in a regular job if you deliver more value?
- If your regular job won't cut it, do you have a business idea, and have you researched it enough to get it off the ground?

### Best Practices
- ✅ Answer each question in writing. Binary (yes/no) answers are fine.
- ✅ If you answer "no" to 3 or more questions, you are not ready to be your own boss yet.
- ✅ Consider being the #2–#10 employee at a great company over being the CEO of a failing small business.
- ✅ Recognize that being an exceptional employee can still make you wealthy (e.g., senior executives at Facebook).
- ❌ Do not assume that hating your boss justifies starting your own business.
- ❌ Do not overestimate how well your employee skills translate to business ownership.

### Common Pitfalls & Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Quitting based on frustration, not data | Complete all 10 questions before making any irreversible decision. |
| Believing "I'd rather be my own boss" is a strategy | It is a feeling. The math is the strategy. |
| Thinking a great individual skill = a great business | Salespeople who leave to start their own firm almost always earn less. You need to be great at *all* business functions. |
| Forgetting benefits, 401(k) matching, and overhead costs | Before quitting, calculate ALL costs of self-employment (health insurance, payroll, marketing, equipment, debt service). |

## Lesson 7: Do the Full Entrepreneurship Math Before Quitting Your Job

### Overview
The #1 mistake aspiring entrepreneurs make is believing their employee success equals entrepreneurial success. The math almost always proves otherwise. Before you resign, calculate what it would actually take to replace your income as a business owner.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Set your target:** What is your current total compensation (salary + benefits + bonuses)?

2. **Calculate your business revenue requirement:**
   - How many deals/clients would you need to close?
   - What revenue would you need to generate?
   - What is your cost of goods sold?
   - What are payroll expenses (even if just you)?
   - What are marketing expenses?
   - What is the cost of capital equipment and debt service?
   - Can you get the funding you need?
   - Can you compete with established players in the marketplace?
   - How long before you can pay yourself a salary?

3. **Factor in employer benefits you will lose:**
   - Health insurance
   - 401(k) matching
   - Company car
   - Travel and expense accounts
   - Paid time off
   - Job security and stability

4. **Compare your projected business take-home to your current total compensation.**

5. **Make the decision only when the math clearly favors the leap.**

### Best Practices
- ✅ Be conservative in revenue projections. Assume you will sell 30–50% less than you expect in Year 1.
- ✅ Include every cost you can think of, then add a 20% contingency buffer.
- ✅ Talk to people who have actually done what you are planning. Not success stories — real stories.
- ✅ Recognize that as a business owner, you will no longer just "sell." You will be accounting, HR, marketing, IT, and operations — and your sales will drop because your attention is divided.
- ❌ Do not assume clients follow you. Clients buy from companies, not individuals.
- ❌ Do not underestimate the time and money required to build a brand from scratch.
- ❌ Do not ignore that 50% of businesses fail in Year 1, and 90% fail by Year 5.

### Common Pitfalls & Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| "My current salary is $X, so I need to make $X as a business owner." | No. As a business owner, you need to generate *revenue* that covers your salary *plus* all business expenses. You may need $2X–$3X in revenue to net $X. |
| Believing client loyalty transfers | Clients follow the best overall solution, not you personally. Amazon customers don't buy from Jeff Bezos. |
| Forgetting the employee had resources | As an employee, you had marketing, IT, operations, finance. As a solo owner, you are all of those. Your sales productivity will drop. |
| Romanticizing entrepreneurship | Being your own boss is overrated for most people. The data does not lie. |

## Lesson 8: Build a Network of Successful People in Your Target Platform

### Overview
If you cannot see anyone winning in your current platform, it is time to find successful people and follow them to their platform. Your network is your navigation system — successful people show you what is actually possible and how to get there.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Identify your target platform** (the industry where you want to build wealth).

2. **Find 3–5 people who are already achieving what you want in that platform.** Use LinkedIn, industry events, podcasts, or social media.

3. **Study their path:** How did they get there? What skills did they build? What sacrifices did they make? How long did it take?

4. **Reach out with specific, thoughtful questions** — not "can you mentor me?" but "I noticed you moved from X to Y in your career. Given my background in Z, what skills do you think I should prioritize?"

5. **Learn from their mistakes too.** Ask what they would do differently.

6. **Absorb their mindset, habits, and frameworks.** You become the average of the people (and ideas) you spend the most time with.

### Best Practices
- ✅ Offer value before asking for favors. Share an article, make an introduction, or contribute to their community.
- ✅ Be specific in your outreach. Generic requests get ignored.
- ✅ Follow proven producers. If no one is winning in your current platform, your ceiling is low.
- ✅ Ask about platforms, not just tactics. "What industry should I be in?" is a better question than "What job should I apply for?"
- ❌ Do not ask a successful person to be your mentor in the first interaction.
- ❌ Do not surround yourself only with people at your current level. You need people ahead of you.

### Common Pitfalls & Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| "I don't know any successful people." | Follow them on social media. Listen to podcasts. Attend industry meetups. Online access removes geographic barriers. |
| Only following people in your current company/industry | You may need to follow people into a new platform. Expand your network intentionally. |
| Treating networking as transactional | Relationships compound over time. Focus on genuine curiosity, not immediate ROI. |

## Lesson 9: Master the Research-and-Analysis Protocol Before Every Major Decision

### Overview
This is the single habit that underpins every successful move in this chapter. The author built a "unyielding process" of research and analysis that he applies to every investment, career move, and business decision.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Before any significant action (investment, career change, business launch), create a research checklist:**
   - What do I not know about this opportunity?
   - Who are the experts I can consult?
   - What are the historical outcomes for people who have done this?
   - What are the worst-case scenarios, and can I survive them?
   - What data would change my mind?

2. **Consult at least three independent sources:**
   - A subject-matter expert (e.g., CPA, attorney, industry veteran)
   - Written research (articles, books, industry reports)
   - People who have actually done what you are considering

3. **Document your analysis.** Write down the pros, cons, potential payout, and potential loss.

4. **Test your risk perception:** After completing your research, ask: "Do I still feel uncertain, or do I feel confident because I know exactly what to do?"

5. **If still uncertain after exhaustive research, pass.** Do not let FOMO override your own analysis.

### Best Practices
- ✅ Make research a non-negotiable habit, not a one-time event.
- ✅ Use research to reframe risk. Uncertainty is the enemy; data is the antidote.
- ✅ Build a "research toolkit" — trusted advisors, go-to sources, and a standard analysis framework.
- ✅ Apply the same rigor to passive investments as you do to active ones.
- ❌ Do not confuse "feeling good about an opportunity" with having done the research.
- ❌ Do not let enthusiasm for an idea short-circuit your analysis process.

### Common Pitfalls & Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Research feels tedious and slow | Reframe it: research is what separates confident investors from gamblers. The billionaire in the story did his homework. |
| Information overload | Set a deadline. Research is not an excuse for inaction — it is a prerequisite for smart action. |
| Only seeking confirming evidence | Actively look for data that contradicts your thesis. If the deal survives scrutiny, it is strong. |
