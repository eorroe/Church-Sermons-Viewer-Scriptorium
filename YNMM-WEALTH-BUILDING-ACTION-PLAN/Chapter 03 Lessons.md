# Chapter 3: Lifestyle by Design — Actionable Lessons

## Lesson 1: Identify Your Core Values

### Lesson Title
Define 4–6 Non-Negotiable Core Values That Govern Your Life

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Take a piece of paper and a pen.
2. Answer the following questions honestly:
   - What matters to you most in this life?
   - What are you willing to do for your success?
   - What are you **not** willing to do for your success?
   - What factors outside of making money are important to you? (Family, health, travel, religion, etc.)
   - Who are the most important people in your life? What do you want to provide for them?
   - What are your personal, nonnegotiable values?
3. Take your answers and distill them into **four to six clear statements**.
4. Write them out clearly on paper.
5. Place them in a place of prominence in your home or workplace so you see them every day.
6. Use these values as the foundation for every future decision.

### Best Practices
- **DO** keep values simple, personal, and unwavering.
- **DO** share your values with others so they understand your boundaries.
- **DO** revisit and reaffirm your values regularly.
- **DON'T** let other people's expectations dictate your core values.
- **DON'T** create more than six — keep them focused and memorable.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Values feel generic or borrowed from others | Answer the reflection questions honestly before distilling statements |
| Forgetting values day-to-day | Post them visibly at home and work; review them weekly |
| Wavering on values when faced with pressure | Remind yourself: values are nonnegotiable and meant to guide decisions |

## Lesson 2: Daily Visualization Practice

### Lesson Title
Spend 10 Minutes Daily Visualizing Your Ideal Life in Specific Detail

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. After defining your Core Values, set aside **10 minutes each day** — either first thing in the morning or right before bed.
2. Close your eyes and dream about what you really want. Do not worry about whether it is realistic or how you will get there.
3. Visualize each area of your life with extreme specificity:
   - **Passion:** What makes you come alive? What do you need to do this?
   - **Family:** What kind of spouse do you want? Do you want children? Who are the most important people?
   - **Friends:** What kinds of people do you want around you? How do they make you feel?
   - **Health:** Do you want to lose weight, build muscle, quit smoking?
   - **Lifestyle:** Travel? Work six months a year? Board positions?
   - **Travel:** Where do you want to visit? How often? At what level of comfort?
   - **Education:** What level of education do you want for yourself, your children, or grandchildren?
   - **Income:** Run the financial numbers in tremendous detail — what will travel, living, and spending cost?
4. Write down key details after each session so they do not fade.
5. Review your written vision regularly to reinforce it.

### Best Practices
- **DO** be as specific and detailed as possible — vague goals produce vague results.
- **DO** engage emotion when visualizing — see and feel the life you want.
- **DON'T** judge your vision as realistic or unrealistic during the exercise.
- **DON'T** skip areas of life — the exercise only works if you cover all dimensions.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Visualization feels like empty daydreaming | Anchor visions with written details and cost calculations |
| Skipping difficult areas (finances, health) | Force yourself through every category — discomfort signals importance |
| Losing detail over time | Write summaries after each session and review weekly |

## Lesson 3: Install the Doorman Principle

### Lesson Title
Vet Every Opportunity and Activity Against Your Core Values Before Accepting It

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Define your Core Values (from Lesson 1) — the Doorman has nothing to filter against without them.
2. Mentally (or physically) place a "Doorman" in front of every request, meeting, phone call, or opportunity.
3. Before saying yes to anything, ask: **"Does this activity align with my Core Values?"**
4. If it does not align, decline it — politely but firmly.
5. If it does align, accept it and schedule it.
6. Protect high-value routines (e.g., family dinners) by blocking conflicting activities in advance.
7. Replace in-person meetings with shorter phone calls when possible to maximize time aligned with efficiency values.

### Best Practices
- **DO** pause before automatically saying yes to requests.
- **DO** communicate your boundaries clearly so others learn what to expect.
- **DO** protect routines that reinforce your Core Values (e.g., family dinners, exercise, deep work).
- **DON'T** agree to activities out of guilt, obligation, or FOMO.
- **DON'T** use the Doorman as an excuse to avoid all discomfort — stretch activities can still align with growth values.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Feeling guilty for declining requests | Remind yourself that every yes to the wrong thing is a no to the right thing |
| Forgetting to apply the filter in the moment | Practice a standard response: "Let me check and get back to you" |
| Over-applying and becoming isolated | Periodically audit whether the Doorman is blocking meaningful connection |

## Lesson 4: Set and Write Down Your Goals Daily

### Lesson Title
Write Down and Review Your Goals Every Single Day to Achieve Big-Time Success

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Using your Core Values and visualized ideal life, define your goals across time horizons:
   - **50-year vision**
   - **5-year goals**
   - **1-year goals**
   - **Monthly goals**
   - **Weekly goals**
   - **Daily goals**
2. Write your goals down **every morning** before starting your day.
3. Review your goals **again in the afternoon or evening** — twice per day total.
4. Make goals **big and specific** — include targets like number of employees, people helped, annual revenue, and net worth.
5. Consider using the **10X Calendar** (by Grant Cardone) or a dedicated goal journal to structure daily writing.
6. When reviewing goals, ask: **"Am I spending today on high-value activities that move me toward these goals?"**

### Best Practices
- **DO** set goals that seem almost unachievable — this expands your capacity.
- **DO** write goals by hand daily — the physical act reinforces commitment.
- **DO** tie every goal back to your Core Values and visualized lifestyle.
- **DON'T** set small, "safe" goals that don't require real effort.
- **DON'T** set goals once a year and forget them — daily review is nonnegotiable.
- **DON'T** clutter goals with trivial tasks — focus on high-impact objectives.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Setting the same goals repeatedly without progress | Make goals specific and measurable; track metrics, not just intentions |
| Forgetting to review goals mid-day | Set phone reminders or tie review to existing habits (lunch, commute) |
| Goals feel disconnected from daily life | Map daily tasks directly back to larger goals to maintain alignment |

## Lesson 5: Take Decisive and Massive Action

### Lesson Title
Push Harder Than You Have Been Pushing to Accelerate Your Lifestyle by Design

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. After setting your goals, identify the **one or two highest-leverage actions** you can take today.
2. Take action on those items first — before checking email, before meetings, before anything else.
3. Push harder than your current baseline. If you have been working 40 hours, step up to 50 or 60 on purpose.
4. Decide today what you want tomorrow, then act with urgency.
5. Use your daily goal writing as a trigger — each time you write your goals, pick the top action and execute immediately.
6. Do not wait for motivation or perfect conditions. Act now.

### Best Practices
- **DO** front-load your day with the hardest, highest-impact tasks.
- **DO** measure effort and output, not just time spent.
- **DO** channel discomfort — if it feels hard, you are probably pushing enough.
- **DON'T** confuse busywork with action — completing low-value tasks does not move goals forward.
- **DON'T** wait until you feel ready — action creates momentum, not the other way around.
- **DON'T** abandon your Core Values in pursuit of goals — the Doorman still applies.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Getting stuck in planning without executing | Set a timer: 10 minutes to plan, then immediately act |
| Burning out from unsustainable effort | Pair action with rest and Core Values (health, family) — this is not a sprint forever |
| Taking the wrong action | Filter every action through the Doorman and tie it directly to a written goal |

## Lesson 6: Write Out Your Full Lifestyle by Design Roadmap

### Lesson Title
Document What Your Next 1, 3, 5, and 50 Years Look Like in Detail

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. After completing Core Values, Visualization, Doorman, and Goal Setting, synthesize everything into a written document.
2. Create separate sections or pages for each time horizon:
   - **1 year from now**
   - **3 years from now**
   - **5 years from now**
   - **50 years from now**
3. For each horizon, describe:
   - What your daily life looks like
   - Where you live and what your surroundings are
   - Who is around you
   - What you are working on
   - What your financial situation looks like
   - How you are spending your time
4. Write in present tense — as if it is already happening.
5. Put the document in a place where you will read it regularly.
6. Schedule a weekly time to dream about and review this roadmap.

### Best Practices
- **DO** write in vivid, sensory detail — make the future feel real.
- **DO** use this document as the source of your yearly, monthly, and daily goals.
- **DO** update it as you grow and your vision sharpens.
- **DON'T** write a vague wish list — every area should be specific enough to create goals from.
- **DON'T** create it once and shelve it — revisit and revise it weekly.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Roadmap feels unrealistic or intimidating | Write it anyway — realism is not the point; direction is |
| Forgetting the roadmap exists | Set a recurring weekly calendar event to review and refine it |
| Goals and roadmap drift apart | Derive all goals directly from the roadmap to keep alignment |
