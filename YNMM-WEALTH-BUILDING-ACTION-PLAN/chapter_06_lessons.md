# Chapter 6: Surround Yourself with Game Facers — Actionable Lessons

---

## Lesson 1: Build a "Game Facers" List and Activate It

### Overview
Identify and connect with successful people you admire so you can learn from their experience, model their behavior, and receive guidance to accelerate your financial growth.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Make a list of people you look up to or admire for their grit, skills, or sheer success.
   - They do not need to be personal connections yet.
   - Focus on individuals who have achieved the financial level you want.
2. Call them and request a short meeting (ten minutes, phone or in person).
   - State clearly that you are in "Accumulation Mode" and want to make more money.
3. Ask for advice and follow their lead.
   - Model what they do and take action on their answers.
4. If you cannot find a mentor in person, find them in books, tapes, videos, or courses.
5. Continue asking for introductions after each meaningful connection.

### Best Practices
- Be honest and humble about your current situation.
- Offer to pay for lunches or meetings yourself.
- Build genuine relationships rather than transactional ones.
- Always be authentic—never exaggerate your success to impress.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Fear of rejection from successful people | Remember that most rich people want to help others get rich. Ask confidently. |
| Appearing phony or fake | Drop the BS and be honest about where you are. Vulnerability builds trust. |
| Feeling intimidated or out of place | Embrace being out of your depth as an opportunity to learn. |
| Stopping after one connection | Keep building. Ask each connection for further introductions. |

## Lesson 2: Apply the Mentor Selection Checklist

### Overview
Before investing time and trust in a mentor, evaluate them against a proven checklist to ensure they are qualified, trustworthy, and aligned with your goals.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Identify a potential mentor.
2. Verify they meet all five criteria:
   1. They have something you want (success, lifestyle, skills).
   2. They are more successful than you in the specific area you seek advice in.
   3. They are trustworthy.
   4. They do not have chemical addictions of any kind.
   5. They are willing to help you with little in return.
3. If any criterion is not met, keep looking.
4. Be patient—it takes time to find the right mentors.

### Best Practices
- Trust your intuition; if something feels off, it probably is.
- Look for mentors with verifiable, proven success.
- Avoid seeking marriage advice from someone with three divorces, or money advice from someone with no money.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Ignoring red flags because you want guidance | Stick to the checklist and do not compromise on trustworthiness or success. |
| Rushing the search process | Be patient. Ask around and keep looking until you find the right people. |
| Choosing mentors based on popularity rather than results | Prioritize results, track record, and the qualities listed above. |

## Lesson 3: Eliminate the "Keeping Up with the Joneses" Mind-Set

### Overview
Replace comparison-based thinking with self-improvement and self-judgment to free up mental energy for wealth building.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Notice when you are comparing your life or success to others.
2. Shift your focus inward:
   - It is your life, not theirs.
   - Stop caring what others think of you.
   - Increase self-judgment and decrease judgment of others.
3. When you see others succeed, genuinely be happy for them.
4. Ask successful people how they achieved what they have.
5. Stop wasting time worrying about what others think of you.

### Best Practices
- Remove judgment of others completely.
- Learn from the success of others rather than envying it.
- Focus on being better today than you were yesterday.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Falling into comparison traps | Remind yourself that the wealthiest people do not care what others think; keep up with yourself, not the Joneses. |
| Feeling bitter about others' success | Reframe it as a learning opportunity. Ask "How did they do it?" |
| Seeking external validation | Build internal validation by measuring your own progress. |

## Lesson 4: Follow the Money — Network Intentionally

### Overview
Actively seek out and connect with people who already have what you want (wealth, success, lifestyle) by leveraging networks, events, and personal outreach.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Identify successful people you already know or want to know.
2. Ask them out to lunch (on your dime).
3. Tell them explicitly: "I am looking to network with highly successful individuals who can help me make more money."
4. Attend relevant conferences, workshops, and conventions.
5. Once you gain expertise, apply to become a speaker at industry events.
6. Ask every new contact, "Who do you know in [your city/industry] that I should meet?"

### Best Practices
- Be direct about your goals ("I want more money").
- Always pay for the meal or event.
- Treat every interaction as a way to learn, not just to sell.
- Follow up with introductions within days.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Feeling awkward being direct about money | Being upfront is refreshing and respected. Most people appreciate honesty. |
| Attending events without a strategy | Go with the specific goal of meeting 2-3 new people and getting at least one introduction. |
| Forgetting to follow up | Send a thank-you note or email within 24 hours and reconnect quarterly. |

## Lesson 5: Master Every Aspect of Your Industry Without Overstepping

### Overview
Become exponentially more valuable by learning how every department and function impacts your work, while respecting boundaries and staying humble.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Choose one area outside your direct role (e.g., IT, accounting, sales, operations).
2. Spend ten minutes a day researching or talking to people in that area.
   - Ask how it impacts your work.
   - Google questions like "How does IT affect marketing?"
3. Ask to shadow or sit in on relevant meetings.
4. Take department heads to lunch to understand their challenges.
5. File the knowledge away for later rather than immediately acting on it.
6. Do not interfere in other people's jobs or announce half-baked ideas.
7. Stay humble and ask for information, rather than giving advice on how they could do it differently.

### Best Practices
- Focus on understanding how everything works, not how to do everything.
- Be a sponge, not a critic.
- Respect organizational hierarchies and boundaries.
- Play to your strengths when you open your mouth.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Stepping on coworkers' toes | Observe and learn quietly. Do not boss people outside your department around. |
| Becoming known as a know-it-all | Keep your mouth shut unless you are contributing to your own work. |
| Spreading yourself too thin | Focus on learning, not doing. Acquire knowledge, then apply it strategically. |

## Lesson 6: Assemble Your Gritty Team of Advisors

### Overview
Build a permanent advisory team of five professionals to support your financial and business success. Even employees should have these relationships in place.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. **Accountant:** Find a gritty, out-of-the-box thinker who helps minimize taxes legally. Update them monthly and keep accurate books.
2. **Insurance Agent:** Find an expert in your industry. Ask about general liability, product liability, life, health, downtime, breakdown, gap, and disability insurance.
3. **Lawyer:** Find a tough, nasty, gritty lawyer—not just a nice one. Build the relationship before you need them.
4. **Finance Partner:** Build a relationship with a banker or independent finance company before you need credit or advice.
5. **Mentor:** Find someone in your industry who has accomplished what you want and has contacts who can help you.
6. Schedule quarterly check-ins with all team members, whether you need them or not.

### Best Practices
- Hire gritty advisors who challenge you and think strategically.
- Build relationships in advance, not in crisis mode.
- Make sure every advisor has your best interests in mind.
- Update your accountant monthly to keep books accurate.
- Keep your advisor team small, trusted, and high-quality.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Trying to do everything yourself | You cannot be an expert in everything. Borrow knowledge from experts. |
| Waiting until a crisis to find advisors | Build relationships now. You need your lawyer, banker, and accountant ready before trouble arises. |
| Hiring a "nice" but weak lawyer | You need someone tough and demanding for the dirty work. |
| Only updating your accountant at tax time | Update monthly and seek proactive profit-driving recommendations. |

## Lesson 7: Embrace Honesty and Authenticity to Break Through Insecurities

### Overview
Drop the false personas and self-limiting beliefs that keep you from asking for help, networking up, and achieving your full financial potential.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Acknowledge your self-defeating mind-sets (e.g., the "underdog" complex, fear of inadequacy).
2. Stop lying to yourself and to others about your success.
3. Admit when you do not have a clue and are out of your depth.
4. Ask for advice from people who have achieved more than you.
5. Believe you are worthy of the help and success you seek.
6. Take action on the advice you receive.

### Best Practices
- Authenticity builds trust faster than any sales pitch.
- Admitting vulnerability is a sign of strength, not weakness.
- Your past does not dictate your future. You are awake, alive, and in control of your money situation.
- Most rich people want to help—you just have to ask.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Feeling like an imposter when networking up | Everyone starts from somewhere. Your hunger and grit are assets. |
-| Protecting your ego at the cost of learning | Drop the BS. If you pretend you have it all figured out, you will miss opportunities. |
| Waiting too long to ask for help | Do not wait. Start today. One honest conversation can change your trajectory. |

## Lesson 8: Maximize Opportunities by Being Present and Prepared

### Overview
Show up to events, programs, and networking opportunities ready to learn, not just to impress. Prepare in advance so you can seize opportunities when they arise.

### Step-by-Step Instructions
1. Apply to selective programs, incubators, or industry events even if you feel underqualified.
2. If accepted, prepare a honest introduction: admit you are new, hungry, and there to learn.
3. Focus entirely on learning rather than impressing others.
4. Take detailed notes and commit to implementing at least one thing you learn.
5. Build relationships with speakers and participants who resonate with you.
6. Prepare thoughtful gifts or gestures based on personal interests to stand out authentically.
7. Follow up within days of the event and maintain the relationship.

### Best Practices
- Go all-in on learning, not on ego.
- Prepare small, thoughtful gestures based on personal knowledge (e.g., a gift related to a known interest).
- Reconnect with contacts years later if the relationship lapsed.
- Honor the investment you made in yourself by putting learning into action.

### Common Pitfalls and Solutions
| Pitfall | Solution |
|---|---|
| Wasting a paid opportunity by trying to impress | Be honest about your situation. The truth is more memorable and powerful than a fake success story. |
| Forgetting to follow up | Send a note or call within days. Relationships die without attention. |
| Over-preparing to the point of inauthenticity | Prepare, but let your genuine curiosity and hunger show through. |
