# God Wants You to Pursue Your Purpose | Tony Evans Highlight

## Overview

This sermon teaches that God has a divine plan and purpose for every person's life, and that pursuing this God-given vision requires intentional planning, unwavering commitment, and spiritual determination. Using biblical examples from Numbers, Nehemiah, and the life of Martin Luther King Jr., the message emphasizes that life is like a coin—you only get to spend it once—so you must invest it in something eternal rather than wasting it on trivial pursuits. The core message is that visions from God inspire plans, and rejecting your vision leads to wandering in circles while embracing it with faith leads to fulfilling God's promises.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are seeking to understand or discern God's purpose for your life
- When you have received a vision from God but don't know how to pursue it practically
- When facing opposition, obstacles, or negative counsel regarding a God-given calling
- When feeling discouraged or overwhelmed by the size of a vision or task
- When tempted to abandon a vision because it seems impractical or too difficult
- When you need to recommit to the vision God has placed in your heart
- When making major life decisions that align with your faith and calling

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize That God Has a Divine Plan for Your Life

Understand that God is a God of purpose and planning. From the foundation of the earth, God has had a plan for humanity, and He has a specific plan for your life. Accept that your life is not random or accidental—you were created on purpose, for a purpose. This foundation gives meaning to your decisions, goals, and daily choices.

### Step 2: Receive the Vision God Has Given You

Allow God to give you a vision for your life. This vision may come through Scripture, prayer, circumstances, or counsel. Like Moses receiving the vision of the Promised Land, or the vision of building a house, let God paint a picture in your mind of what He wants to accomplish through you. Let this vision captivate your heart and keep you up at night with holy anticipation.

### Step 3: Develop a Strategic Plan to Fulfill the Vision

Visions do not negate plans—they inspire them. If God has given you a vision, He expects you to develop a practical plan for fulfilling it. Just as a builder hires an architect to plan construction, you should seek wise counsel and create detailed strategies for pursuing your God-given vision. Break the vision down into actionable steps, timelines, and milestones.

### Step 4: Surround Yourself with Wise Counsel, but Don't Vote on God's Will

Seek godly counsel and advice when developing your plan, but never put God's will up for a vote. Moses made the mistake of allowing the people to vote on whether to pursue the Promised Land, and the negative majority report led to 40 years of wandering. When you know a vision is from God, it is not up for democratic decision. Counsel helps refine the plan; it does not override God's direction.

### Step 5: Reject the "Grasshopper Mentality" and Compare Yourself to God's Promise

When the spies returned from scouting the land, the majority said, "We were in our own sight as grasshoppers." They were looking at their own ability rather than God's promise. When pursuing your vision, do not compare yourself to the obstacles or your own limitations. Look at God's faithfulness and power. The question is not whether you can do it, but whether the vision came from God. If it came from God, no fortified city or giant obstacle can stop it.

### Step 6: Stay Committed to the Vision Through Prayer and Dependence on God

Pursuing your purpose requires persistent prayer and dependence on God. Like Nehemiah, who prayed throughout the entire process of rebuilding the wall, you need God not only to start the vision but to sustain you through it. Your commitment to the vision must outlast difficulties, delays, and discouragement. Stay connected to God through prayer, knowing that He will give you the strength and wisdom to complete what He started.

### Step 7: Press Forward with Determination, Knowing God Is Watching

Live with the awareness that God is watching your pursuit of His purpose. This should affect how hard you go, how determined you are, and how you invest your one life. Like Caleb, who at 85 years old still claimed the mountain God had promised, you should pursue your vision with passion until the very end. Do not settle for a lesser version of what God has called you to. Keep dreaming, keep planning, and keep moving forward until you possess what God has for you.

## Examples

### Example 1: Building a House

If you have a vision for a new house—5,000 square feet, specific rooms, and a particular layout—that vision inspires a plan but does not replace the need for an architect. Without proper planning and architectural design, you may end up with an ugly 5,000-square-foot house that you still have to pay for. Similarly, a God-given vision requires careful planning and wise execution to be built properly.

### Example 2: Martin Luther King Jr. and the Civil Rights Movement

Martin Luther King Jr. embodied the principle of pursuing God-given purpose. The burden of racism and injustice led to a prayer meeting that sparked the Montgomery Bus Boycott. From that prayer meeting came a strategic plan that spread across the nation. King's vision was not a vague dream—it was a God-inspired call that required planning, courage, and commitment to justice that changed history.

### Example 3: Moses and the Twelve Spies

In Numbers 13-14, God gave Moses a vision of the Promised Land—a land flowing with milk and honey. Moses sent 12 leaders to spy out the land and develop strategies for taking it. When they returned, 10 gave a pessimistic report focusing on the giants and fortified cities, while Caleb and Joshua gave the minority report of faith: "We should by all means go up and take possession of it, for we shall surely overcome it." The people believed the negative report, and as a result, their corpses fell in the wilderness for 40 years of wandering. This illustrates the consequences of rejecting a God-given vision.

### Example 4: Caleb at 85 Years Old

Forty years after the incident with the spies, Caleb was still pursuing the vision God had given him. At 85 years old, he came to Joshua and said, "Give me that mountain. I have continued to follow the Lord, and I am as strong now as I was at 40." Caleb did not accept a rocking chair or a lesser version of God's promise. He claimed the mountain he had been dreaming about for decades, demonstrating that commitment to God's vision should continue throughout your entire life.

### Example 5: The Goof-Off Football Player

A high school football player who was a fourth-string halfback rarely practiced and was considered a goof-off. During a championship game, all the other halfbacks got hurt in the first quarter, and he was called into the game. He played with extraordinary athleticism, running over, under, and around opponents. After the game, the coach asked how he was able to perform at that level. The player explained that his blind father, who had died the day before, was watching from the stands. When you know someone significant is watching, it changes how you play. In the same way, when you know God is watching your life, it should affect your determination, your work ethic, and how you pursue the purpose He has given you.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Invest your one life in something eternal and meaningful, not just consume it on trivial pursuits
- ✅ Develop a concrete, detailed plan when God gives you a vision
- ✅ Get wise counsel and expert advice (like an architect for a house) to help execute your vision properly
- ✅ Stay in persistent prayer throughout the entire journey, not just at the beginning
- ✅ Compare yourself to God's promise and power, not to the obstacles or your own limitations
- ✅ Reject negative, discouraging reports that come from a place of fear
- ✅ Keep dreaming and pursuing God's vision at every stage of life
- ✅ Remember that God is watching, and let that truth fuel your determination

## Keep In Mind

- You only get one life—spend it wisely on something that matters
- God's sovereignty means He has a plan, but He invites you to be involved in its fulfillment
- Timing matters: God's plan unfolds in His perfect timing, but you must be ready and willing when the moment comes
- The vision God gives you is sure, but the path to its fulfillment will require faith and perseverance
- God will keep you alive and relevant until He accomplishes His purpose through you, even if others try to stop it
- Don't settle for a lesser version of what God has called you to—keep dreaming His dreams
- This sermon provides spiritual and motivational guidance based on religious text and principles; it is not professional financial, legal, or career advice
- The teaching is rooted in Christian theology and biblical interpretation; apply these principles within your own faith tradition and context
- Discernment and wise counsel are emphasized—always verify that visions and plans align with both your faith community and sound practical judgment

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Taking a vote on God's will or allowing others to decide whether your vision is valid
  **Solution:** Seek wise counsel for advice, but never put God's calling up for a democratic decision. When you know a vision is from God, move forward in faith regardless of popular opinion.

- **Problem:** Adopting a "grasshopper mentality"—focusing on your own limitations and the size of the obstacles
  **Solution:** Stop comparing yourself to the giants and fortified cities. Look at God's promise and faithfulness. If the vision came from God, your inadequacy is not the determining factor—His power is.

- **Problem:** Listening to and spreading negative, discouraging reports about a vision
  **Solution:** Be careful what you receive and what you transmit to others. Discouraging words from fearful people can derail a God-given vision. Surround yourself with people of faith who will give the minority report of optimism.

- **Problem:** Abandoning the vision when opposition or difficulty arises
  **Solution:** Opposition is expected, but it does not cancel God's plan. Like Caleb, hold fast to what God has promised and continue to move forward in faith, even if it takes years or decades.

- **Problem:** Having a vision but failing to develop a practical plan
  **Solution:** Visions inspire plans; they do not replace them. Get the right people, strategies, and timelines in place to execute the vision well. Without planning, even the greatest vision can result in a poorly built outcome.
