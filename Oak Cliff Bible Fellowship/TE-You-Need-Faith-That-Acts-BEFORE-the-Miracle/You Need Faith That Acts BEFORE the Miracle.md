# You Need Faith That Acts BEFORE the Miracle | Tony Evans Sermon

## Overview

God calls the believer to act in faith by preparing room for Him to work before any visible provision appears—faith is demonstrated in action before the miracle arrives, not after. This sermon teaches that when the seeker feels dry, discouraged, and confused, they are called to "dig your ditches" by taking practical, faith-driven steps in the midst of uncertainty, creating capacity for God to fill what He alone can provide.

**Key Definitions:**
- **Prophetic word** = a specific, personal communication from God that goes beyond general teaching and addresses an individual's current situation directly
- **Jehoshaphat** = a king described in 2 Chronicles 20 who modeled seeking God's guidance through a prophet
- **Dig your ditches** = taking concrete, practical steps of preparation that create visible readiness for God to act

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When the believer is in a season of drought—emotionally, spiritually, financially, or relationally
- When one problem compounds into another and things go from bad to worse
- When the believer is tempted to blame God for the difficulty they are facing
- When the seeker needs a specific, personal word from God rather than just general teaching
- When the believer has been waiting for a miracle without taking any faith-driven action
- When the seeker feels defeated before the battle is over
- When the believer needs encouragement to keep going when there is no visible sign of hope

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop Blaming God and Seek Spiritual Clarity

When the believer faces a crisis, do not default to blaming God for your situation. Instead, follow the example of Jehoshaphat, who said, "Is there not a prophet of the Lord here, that we may inquire of the Lord by him?" (2 Kings 3:11). Look for someone who can hear from God and bring clarity to your specific situation. A prophetic word is a specific, personal communication from God that goes beyond general teaching and addresses an individual's current situation directly.

**Prerequisite:** Look for a pastor or spiritually mature believer in your church community whom you trust and whose discernment you respect. You will know you have found the right person when their counsel aligns with Scripture and you can identify at least one practical next step they have helped you clarify.

### Step 2: Understand the Difference Between a Sermon and a Prophetic Word

A preached word is for everyone; a prophetic word is for the believer's specific situation. If the seeker only shows up for a sermon, they will only get a sermon. To receive a prophetic word, the believer must be part of a church community where members actively practice personal ministry—such as praying for one another, sharing scripture-based encouragement, and creating space for the Holy Spirit to speak. This means a congregation where personal ministry is a regular practice, not just a weekly sermon.

### Step 3: Create an Atmosphere for God to Speak

Before Elisha received a prophetic word in 2 Kings 3:15, he asked for a minstrel—a musician. The purpose of worship and music in church is not simply to be a prelude to a sermon; it is to set the atmosphere where God feels comfortable to express Himself on a personal level. If the believer wants a prophetic word, be a worshipper, not just an attender. Engage in honest, personal worship practices—such as prayer, scripture meditation, and singing—for at least 15 minutes without rushing. You will know you have engaged in genuine worship when you feel a shift from anxiety to peace or when you receive a thought or scripture that directly addresses your situation.

**Prerequisite:** Join a church community that encourages personal ministry and prophetic encouragement. You will know this atmosphere exists when members share scripture-aligned encouragement with one another at least weekly, and that encouragement includes specific application to individual situations rather than generic statements.

### Step 4: Receive a Specific Word from God and Act on It

When God gives the believer a prophetic word, it may not make natural sense. Elisha told the armies to dig ditches in a valley with no rain in sight (2 Kings 3:16-19). The word was specific and actionable. Do not wait for a sign before you obey—obedience itself is the sign of faith. Whatever specific instruction God gives you for your situation, begin to act on it within 24 hours—taking one concrete step that directly responds to that instruction.

**Verification:** You will know God is speaking when the message aligns with Scripture and you can point to at least one specific decision or action it has helped you identify.

**Timeframe:** During the first 48 hours of a crisis, seek spiritual clarity and begin to act on the specific word God gives you.

### Step 5: Dig Your Ditches Before the Miracle Arrives

God told the people to fill the valley with ditches so that He could fill them with water. He did not dig the ditches for them. God provides for the believer who has taken practical steps of preparation. The action itself demonstrates faith and positions the believer to receive and recognize God's provision when it arrives. Your action creates the capacity for His provision. Keep digging even when there is no rain, no wind, and no visible sign that anything is changing. Your faith in action demonstrates that you trust God before the miracle manifests.

**Modern Application:** "Digging your ditches" means updating your resume, making phone calls, seeking wise counsel, taking a class, making a difficult phone call, or whatever practical step God has shown you to take. Taking a specific step of faith gives God a tangible place to work.

**Timeframe:** Continue your practical steps consistently for at least 30 days.

**Completion Criteria:** Begin within 24 hours, taking one concrete step.

### Step 6: Don't Quit and Don't Box God In

Don't become discouraged and stop digging. Don't limit how God can work or tell Him how He must show up. God can bring water without wind or rain, and He can meet your need in ways you did not expect. Stay in faith, keep acting, and trust that God is at work even when you cannot see it.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Three Kings in 2 Kings 3

Three kings—Jehoram of Israel, Jehoshaphat of Judah (described in 2 Chronicles 20 as a king who modeled seeking God's guidance through a prophet), and the king of Edom—went to war against Moab but encountered a devastating drought with no water for their armies or their cattle (2 Kings 3:1-9). Rather than blaming God, Jehoshaphat sought spiritual clarity through the prophet Elisha. Elisha called for a musician, and after worship, received a prophetic word: make the valley full of ditches. The people obeyed and dug, and by the next morning, the valley was filled with water without any rain or wind—just as God had promised (2 Kings 3:20).

### Example 2: The Power Strip Illustration

Tony Evans compares spiritual capacity to a power strip. When you accept Jesus Christ, certain things come "on the house"—your eternal destiny is secure, and His presence is with you. But other blessings—such as answered prayers for specific needs, opportunities for ministry, or tangible provision—require expanded capacity that comes through faith-driven action. Just as plugging a power strip into the wall expands your ability to do more than what naturally came with the house, taking action in faith expands the believer's capacity to recognize and receive specific blessings, provision, and direction from God. Ephesians 3:20 reminds us that God is able to do exceedingly abundantly above all we could ask or think—but we must position ourselves to receive by acting in faith.

**Application:** Taking a specific step of faith gives God a tangible place to work, just like plugging into a power strip gives you access to more power.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Seek spiritual clarity instead of blaming God when things go wrong
- ✅ Look for someone who can inquire of the Lord for your specific situation—a pastor or spiritually mature believer whom you trust
- ✅ Create an atmosphere of genuine worship, not just sermon attendance
- ✅ Act on the specific word God gives you before the miracle arrives
- ✅ Dig your ditches—take practical, faith-driven steps (such as updating your resume, making phone calls, or seeking wise counsel) even when there is no visible sign
- ✅ Find a Jehoshaphat—someone described in 2 Chronicles 20 who modeled seeking God's guidance—who will encourage you to act, not just talk about faith
- ✅ Don't box God in by deciding how or when He must show up
- ✅ Keep going even when you feel low, discouraged, or defeated
- ✅ Verify any prophetic word by confirming it aligns with Scripture and the counsel of mature believers

## Keep In Mind

- What feels overwhelmingly big to you is a small thing in the hands of God
- A prophetic word is specific to your situation—seek it, don't just rely on general teaching
- God wants to see your faith at work before He fills what you have prepared
- You need at least one righteous influence in your life—a Jehoshaphat—who will encourage you to act in faith
- God can meet you in your drought, your battle, your discouragement, your depression, your frustration, and your singlehood, marriage, career, finances, and family—wherever you are
- Don't quit digging your ditches while you wait for the miracle
- You will know God is speaking when the message aligns with Scripture and brings peace or direction

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon provides spiritual guidance and encouragement; it is not professional advice for medical, legal, financial, or mental health situations
- When facing serious personal crises, seek guidance from qualified professionals alongside spiritual counsel
- Be discerning about who you allow to speak prophetic direction into your life—align any personal instruction with the teachings of Scripture
- Do not neglect practical action in favor of waiting passively for a miracle
- Always verify prophetic words by confirming they align with Scripture and the counsel of mature believers

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Being an emergency worshipper—only turning to God when you are in crisis
  **Solution:** Build a lifestyle of standing before God daily, not just showing up when you need something

- **Problem:** Blaming God when things go from bad to worse
  **Solution:** Follow Jehoshaphat's example (2 Chronicles 20) and seek spiritual clarity through someone who knows God, rather than assigning blame

- **Problem:** Attending church and hearing sermons without ever taking faith-driven action
  **Solution:** Go beyond sermon attendance—create an atmosphere of worship and act on the specific word God gives you

- **Problem:** Waiting for a sign before obeying what God has already told you to do
  **Solution:** Obedience is the sign of faith—dig your ditches before the rain comes

- **Problem:** Quitting before the miracle arrives because there is no visible change yet
  **Solution:** Stay in faith and keep digging; God fills the ditches that are already there

- **Problem:** Limiting how God can work by deciding exactly how or when He must show up
  **Solution:** Don't box God in—He can bring water without wind or rain, and He can work in ways you did not expect

- **Problem:** Acting on a prophetic word without verifying it
  **Solution:** Confirm any prophetic word aligns with Scripture and the counsel of mature believers before acting on it

- **Problem:** Expecting immediate results without consistent effort
  **Solution:** Continue your practical steps consistently for at least 30 days; faith requires persistence
