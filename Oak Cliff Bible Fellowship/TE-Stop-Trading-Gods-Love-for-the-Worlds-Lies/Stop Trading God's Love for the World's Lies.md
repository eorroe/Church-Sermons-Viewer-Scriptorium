# Stop Trading God's Love for the World's Lies

## Overview

This sermon teaches believers how to recognize and reject worldliness by understanding that "the world" is a spiritual system headed by Satan that leaves God out, and that loving this world costs us the experiential love and presence of the Father. It provides an eternal perspective framework for living in the world without being crushed by its pressures, helping Christians maintain intimacy with God while navigating earthly existence.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel pressured to conform to worldly values, standards, or behaviors
- When you struggle with temptation related to pleasure, possessions, or pride
- When you sense a distance in your relationship with God and want to restore intimacy
- When making decisions between honoring God and pursuing worldly gain
- When you need encouragement to maintain your faith in a secular or hostile environment
- When you want to understand the spiritual battle between loving God and loving the world

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand What "The World" Really Means

The Greek word for "world" is "cosmos." In 1 John, it refers not to the physical earth, but to the spiritual reality on the earth—a system headed by Satan that systematically excludes God. This system operates independently of God's will and seeks to draw you away from Him. Recognize that when the Bible says "do not love the world," it is warning you against loving a spiritual system that is ruled by God's enemy.

### Step 2: Recognize the Cost of Loving the World

If you love the world, the love of the Father is not in you (1 John 2:15). This does not mean you lose your salvation, but you do lose your experiential relationship with God—His manifest presence, His love, and His will operating in your life. God saved you to deliver you from this present world (Galatians 1:4), not for you to fall in love with it. Choose daily whether you want the closeness of God or the approval and pleasures of the world.

### Step 3: Identify the Three Worldly Desires to Resist

Everything in the world falls into one of three categories:

- **Lust of the flesh**: The craving for pleasure outside of God's will. This includes legitimate pleasures (like eating) that become sinful when pursued illegitimately (like gluttony). Ask yourself: Am I seeking pleasure in a way that excludes or dishonors God?

- **Lust of the eyes**: The illegitimate desire for possessions. This is the craving for stuff that leads to debt, poor stewardship, and choosing material things over honoring God with your finances. Ask yourself: Do I have to have things I cannot afford to impress people I do not know?

- **Pride of life**: The illegitimate pursuit of position or status. This is the desire to advance independently of God, even through compromising values or priorities. Ask yourself: Am I willing to compromise my integrity to get ahead?

### Step 4: Remember the World Is Temporary

The world and its lusts are passing away (1 John 2:17). What looks exciting and desirable today will fade—new cars become old cars, new houses become just houses, new clothes become just clothes. Do not invest your love in something temporary. Adopt the eternal perspective: this world is a place to visit, not a place to call home. As Jim Elliot said, "He is no fool who gives what he cannot keep to gain what he cannot lose."

### Step 5: Develop Eternal Perspective Through God's Presence on the Inside

You may not be able to change your environment, coworkers, or circumstances. But if you get enough God on the inside, He can neutralize the pressure coming at you from the outside. Like a submarine that can withstand deep ocean pressure because the internal pressure equals or exceeds the external pressure, you can exist in this world without being crushed when you live with an eternal perspective. Maximize your time here in legitimate ways, but do not love the world you are in—love the world you are going to.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Pressured Employee

You face pressure at work to compromise your values, use dishonest practices, or mock your faith to fit in. Instead of conforming (loving the world), you allow God's presence on the inside to give you strength to stand firm. You may not change your coworkers, but you will not be crushed by the pressure because your identity and allegiance belong to Christ.

### Example 2: The Debt Cycle

You see something you want on TV and feel compelled to buy it, even though you cannot afford it. This is the lust of the eyes. You recognize that owning the item will not bring lasting satisfaction and will actually dishonor God by making you a slave to debt. You choose contentment and stewardship over possession, preserving your ability to honor the Lord with your finances.

### Example 3: The Career Compromise

You are offered a promotion that requires you to compromise your values, work against your priorities, or abandon your faith commitments. This is the pride of life. You choose to decline the promotion or seek an alternative path that honors God, because you want the experiential relationship with the Father more than worldly advancement.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Separate legitimate enjoyment of earthly things from loving them
- ✅ Evaluate every decision by asking: Does this choose God or the world?
- ✅ Build your life on eternal values rather than temporal success
- ✅ Practice contentment with what God has given you
- ✅ Spend time in God's presence to increase the "internal pressure" that withstands worldly pressure
- ✅ Remember that the world's approval is temporary and fading
- ✅ Be willing to be different, even if the world rejects you
- ❌ Do not assume that having material things is wrong—loving them is the sin
- ❌ Do not seek the world's approval at the expense of God's will
- ❌ Do not assume you can love both God and the world simultaneously

## Keep In Mind

- You can be IN the world and benefit from it without loving it (John 17:15)
- Your salvation is secure once you are truly born again, but your experience of God's presence can be lost through worldliness
- The world is not evil because it is physical—it is evil because it is spiritual and Satan-driven
- Satan uses what looks good, feels good, and seems desirable to draw you in
- The pressure to conform increases in more secular environments
- God wants to maximize your earthly years in legitimate ways, but He calls you to live with an eternal perspective

## Security & Safety Notes

- No financial, technical, or data security risks apply to this sermon. The only risk is spiritual—choosing the world over God damages your experiential relationship with Him and puts you in enmity with God (James 4:4).
- The enemy's goal is to get you to fall in love with the world system so that you lose the Father's love and presence.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Thinking that "the world" means the physical earth or physical things
  **Solution:** Understand that the world is a spiritual system headed by Satan that leaves God out. You can enjoy physical things without loving the spiritual system behind them.

- **Problem:** Believing you can love both God and the world at the same time
  **Solution:** Recognize that loving the world means choosing it over God when faced with a decision. The love of the Father and the love of the world are mutually exclusive in practice (1 John 2:15, James 4:4).

- **Problem:** Assuming material blessings mean God is pleased
  **Solution:** God does not have a problem with you having things; He has a problem with you loving them. The test is whether the thing has displaced God in your affections and priorities.

- **Problem:** Desiring the world's approval so strongly that you compromise your values
  **Solution:** Accept that if the world hated Jesus, it will hate you too (John 15:18-19). Do not embrace the world to avoid rejection—embrace God even if it means the world rejects you.

- **Problem:** Forgetting that the world is temporary
  **Solution:** Regularly remind yourself that the world and its lusts are passing away. Invest in what is eternal, not what fades.
