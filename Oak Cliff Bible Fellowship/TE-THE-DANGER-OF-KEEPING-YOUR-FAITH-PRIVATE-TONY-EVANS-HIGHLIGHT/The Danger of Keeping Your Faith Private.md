# The Danger of Keeping Your Faith Private

## Overview

This sermon challenges Christians to move beyond private belief to public confession of Jesus Christ as Lord, emphasizing that true discipleship requires openly identifying with Christ despite potential social rejection, loss of relationships, or other personal costs. It teaches that hidden faith is ineffective and that every believer will ultimately bow before Christ, making voluntary confession now the only wise choice.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When tempted to keep your Christian faith private out of fear of social rejection
- When evaluating whether your life reflects genuine submission to Christ as Lord or a watered-down cultural version of Christianity that lacks true obedience
- When facing pressure to compartmentalize faith away from work, neighborhood, or public life
- When seeking to understand the cost and necessity of public discipleship

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand What Confession Means

Confession is the verbal and visible identification of yourself as a follower of Jesus Christ. It is not merely saying "God" or acknowledging a vague deity, but specifically confessing Jesus Christ as Lord. Because Jesus is the unique Son of God sent to earth to reveal the Father, confessing Him specifically by name as Lord is essential rather than merely acknowledging a vague or general deity.

### Step 2: Examine Whether Your Faith Is Private or Public

Ask yourself: if you were accused of being a Christian, would people in your daily life—at work, in your neighborhood, among your friends and family—have enough clear evidence from your words and actions to conclude that you are a follower of Christ? A private or "secret agent" Christian who denies Christ publicly to avoid rejection is not living in true discipleship. Public identification through baptism is the initial step of this commitment.

### Step 3: Address the Lordship Issue

True confession requires that Jesus is Lord of your life—not in word only, but in obedience. Luke 6:46 asks, "How do you call me Lord and do not do what I say?" If you prioritize family, career, social approval, or personal comfort above obedience to Christ's commands, you are not worthy of Him (Matthew 10:37). You cannot confess Christ while refusing to adjust your life to His commands.

### Step 4: Recognize the Social Cost and Embrace It

Publicly identifying with Christ as Lord—beginning with baptism as the initial public step—may lead to social division—at work, in your neighborhood, or even within your own family. Many who reject Christ will also reject, oppose, or distance themselves from you when you publicly identify with Him. But when you lose because of your confession, you actually win with Christ. Be willing to bear the social rejection, mockery, exclusion, loss of relationships, or other opposition that comes with publicly identifying as a follower of Jesus Christ.

### Step 5: Live as Useful Salt

Christians who compromise their witness to please the world become like tasteless salt—useless and thrown out. Do not let the opposition, mockery, or rejection you experience from unbelieving people because of your faith in Jesus Christ cause you to compromise or water down the truth of God's Word. Instead, remain effective in your witness by keeping your confession of Christ clear, loving, compassionate, and unwavering, just as salt preserves and flavors food.

### Step 6: Understand the Ultimate Consequence

Every knee will bow and every tongue will confess that Jesus is Lord—voluntarily in this life now or mandatorily at the final judgment when every person stands before Christ. If you deny Christ before men, He will deny you before the Father (Matthew 10:33). The choice is yours: confess Him now and receive His endorsement, or face the certainty of judgment.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Rulers Who Believed but Would Not Confess

John 12:42-43 describes many Jewish rulers who believed in Jesus but refused to confess Him publicly for fear of being put out of the synagogue. They loved the approval of men more than the approval of God. Their example shows that belief alone is insufficient—public confession is required for true discipleship.

### Example 2: Stephen's Bold Confession

Acts 7:59 records Stephen calling on the name of the Lord as he was being stoned to death. Stephen's willingness to publicly identify with Christ even unto death demonstrates the kind of fearless confession Jesus calls His followers to. Unlike the secret believers in John 12, Stephen did not hide his faith despite lethal consequences.

### Example 3: Political, Cultural, and Convenient Christians

The sermon identifies three modern categories of compromised believers: "political Christians" who wrap their faith in national flags rather than the cross, "cultural Christians" who wrap their faith in race rather than the primacy of Christ, and "convenient Christians" who want God's blessings but refuse to represent Him outside of church. Each represents a failure to confess Christ fully and publicly.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Publicly identify with Jesus Christ in word and lifestyle, not just privately believe in God
- ✅ Be willing to suffer social rejection, division in relationships, loss of opportunities, or other personal costs for the sake of your confession of Christ
- ✅ Examine your life to ensure your actions align with your profession of Christ as Lord
- ✅ Use baptism as a public declaration of your willingness to be identified with Christ
- ✅ Let your confession be clear, loving, compassionate, and appropriate
- ✅ Stand on God's Word and Jesus's perspective on the issues of the day without compromising truth
- ❌ Don't hide behind vague references to "God" or "spirituality" to avoid the social cost, opposition, or personal inconvenience of specifically identifying with Jesus Christ
- ❌ Don't let fear of people outpace your fear of the Lord
- ❌ Don't love the approval of men more than the approval of God
- ❌ Don't be a "secret agent Christian" who denies Christ publicly to avoid social cost
- ❌ Don't compromise truth to make a society or culture that has rejected Christ and His teachings comfortable or accepting
- ❌ Don't expect God to fully bless, guide, and commit Himself to your life if you are ashamed of Him and refuse to confess Him publicly

## Keep In Mind

- Confession is not just verbal but lived out—your daily conduct, decisions, and relationships must visibly align with your profession of Christ
- Jesus said, "If you confess me before men, I will also confess you before my Father who is in heaven. But whoever denies me before men, I will also deny him before my Father who is in heaven" (Matthew 10:32-33)
- The only reason Christ can be your Savior is because He is Lord; you cannot accept Him as Savior while rejecting His Lordship
- When you lose social standing, relationships, or opportunities for the sake of your confession of Christ, you actually gain eternal reward and favor with Christ
- A complete cultural transformation that honors God must begin with the church taking a clear public stand for Christ
- Repentance is available for realignment when you have failed to confess Christ; do not remain in denial

## Security & Safety Notes

- Public confession of Christ may expose you to genuine social rejection, loss of friendships, family conflict, professional consequences, or even persecution—be prepared to count these costs (Luke 14:26-27)
- In countries, cultures, or social environments where Christianity is opposed, restricted, or illegal, openly identifying as a Christian can lead to persecution, loss of employment, imprisonment, or physical danger; assess your specific situation with wisdom and the guidance of your local church community
- Avoid causing unnecessary offense by confessing Christ in a loving, respectful, and wise manner rather than in a reckless, provocative, or unnecessarily confrontational way that dishonors the gospel message
- Maintain strong ties with a local church body for support and accountability when facing opposition
- Repentance is available for realignment when you have failed to confess Christ; do not remain in denial

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Keeping faith private to avoid social rejection
  **Solution:** Remember that every knee will bow eventually; choose voluntary confession now rather than mandatory confession at the final judgment, and trust that the loss you experience for Christ is temporary while the gain is eternal.

- **Problem:** Compartmentalizing faith so it only exists in church but not at work or in the neighborhood
  **Solution:** Evaluate whether people in your daily contexts—at work, in your neighborhood, among your friends and family—would have enough clear evidence from your words and actions to conclude that you are a Christian; let your confession be consistent across all areas of life.

- **Problem:** Loving approval from people more than approval from God
  **Solution:** Take courage from the fact that God's approval is eternal, while human approval is fleeting; the rulers in John 12 chose people's approval over God's and thus remained secret believers.

- **Problem:** Using vague terminology like "God" while avoiding the name of Jesus Christ
  **Solution:** Recognize that confessing a general "God" is not enough; you must specifically confess Jesus Christ by name as Lord, who is the unique Son of God sent to earth to reveal the Father.

- **Problem:** Treating Christianity as a means to personal blessing rather than a call to Lordship
  **Solution:** Accept that Christianity involves taking up your cross and following Christ; you cannot use God for your convenience while denying Him publicly.
