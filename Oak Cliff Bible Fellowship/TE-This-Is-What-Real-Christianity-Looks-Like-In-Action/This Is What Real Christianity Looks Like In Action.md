# This Is What Real Christianity Looks Like In Action

## Overview

This sermon by Tony Evans teaches that biblical love (agape) is the defining mark and "dress code" of true Christianity. Drawing from John 13:35 and 1 John 4:7-21, it explains that love is not optional for believers but a direct commandment from God. The message emphasizes that God is love, and when Christians abide in Christ and allow His nature to overflow through them, they become living expressions of His character to the world.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you want to understand what truly identifies a Christian before the world
- When you struggle with viewing Christianity as mere religious attendance or knowledge
- When you desire a deeper experience of God but feel your spiritual life is stagnant
- When you need clarity on the difference between cultural love and biblical (agape) love
- When preparing for the judgment seat of Christ and want to live with eternal perspective
- When you want to move from being a consumer of blessings to a conduit of God's love

## Steps

### Step 1: Abide in Christ to Receive More of Him

Remain intentionally connected to Christ through daily prayer, Scripture reading, and dependence on the Holy Spirit. Jesus taught in John 15 that abiding in Him produces greater spiritual experience and deeper intimacy with God. Many Christians experience limited spiritual growth because they seek God only for personal benefit rather than for the benefit of others.

### Step 2: Understand That Biblical Love Is Your Required Wardrobe

Recognize that love is not a suggestion but a command from God (1 John 4:21). Biblical love (agape) is the visible mark God has called all His children to wear so that the world can unmistakably recognize them as followers of Jesus Christ (John 13:35). Without this love, Christians risk becoming unrecognizable to those who do not know Christ.

### Step 3: Know God as Love to Love Correctly

Understand that God is love (1 John 4:8, 1 John 4:16) by His very nature, before creation. Because God is triune — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — love has always existed within the Trinity. To love as God loves, you must study and know the character of God through Scripture. If your understanding of God's nature is unclear, you risk using the word "love" in ways that contradict His identity.

### Step 4: Put On Agape Love as a Lifestyle

Wear biblical love (agapao) as your daily lifestyle. Agape is the self-determined decision to express God's will and seek the well-being of another, regardless of your feelings. This is distinct from eros (romantic/sexual love) and phileo (friendship love). God is looking for servants who actively seek opportunities to love, not celebrities who only want blessings for themselves.

### Step 5: Make Your Love Visible, Beneficial, and Just

Ensure your love has three characteristics:
- **Visible:** Biblical love must be demonstrated through action. Do not love with words alone without backing them up with deeds (1 John 3:18).
- **Beneficial:** Love must improve the situation of the one being loved, bringing light to darkness or life to death.
- **Just:** Love does not overlook sin or wrongdoing. It addresses wrongs and holds to a moral standard. Agape involves a deliberate choice to love even when emotions are absent, just as God loved humanity while people were still sinners (Romans 5:8).

### Step 6: Love Others to Experience God More Fully

The more you actively love others, the more God will develop His love within you and increase His presence in your life. If you only seek God for personal gain, you limit how much of Him you will experience. As you actively express love to others, God will entrust you with more of His presence and character. This is how loving others deepens your abiding relationship with God (1 John 4:12, 1 John 4:15-17).

### Step 7: Build Character Relationships That Will Witness for You in Eternity

Intentionally invest in people so that on the day of judgment, they can affirm your character before God. As Jesus illustrated in Luke 16:1-9, the unjust steward made lasting relationships by reducing the debts of his master's debtors. Christians should similarly make eternal investments by serving and blessing others. Your record of love will be reviewed at the judgment seat of Christ, and those you have loved will affirm your faithfulness (Luke 16:1-9).

## Examples

### Example 1: The Unjust Steward (Luke 16:1-9)

A financial manager was dismissed by his master for poor performance. Rather than despairing, he went to his master's debtors and reduced their bills, creating relationships that would support him after his termination. Jesus concluded, "Make friends for yourselves by means of the wealth of unrighteousness, so that when it fails, they will receive you into the eternal dwellings" (Luke 16:9). Even a worldly person understood the lasting value of investing in relationships.

### Example 2: Cain vs. Abel (1 John 3:12)

The Apostle John used Cain as an example of hatred. Cain killed his brother Abel because Cain's deeds were evil and Abel's were righteous. Hatred means to hurt or harm another person. When revenge or selfishness motivates your actions toward others, you have departed from love. Christians are called to the opposite: sacrificially seeking the well-being of others as Christ did for humanity.

### Example 3: The Trinity's Eternal Love Relationship (John 5:19-20, John 17:24)

Before creation, the Father loved the Son, the Son loved the Father, and the Spirit glorified the Son. This eternal, perfect love within the triune God is the source of all genuine love. Christians have been invited into this relationship through Christ, and God wants to shape believers with the nature of agape love that has always existed within Him (Ephesians 1:4-6).

### Example 4: God Demonstrated Love on the Cross (Romans 5:8)

While humanity was still in a state of sin, Christ died for people. Biblical love is not based on the worthiness of the recipient but on the nature of the Giver. God did not wait for people to clean up their lives before sending His Son. He loved people in their sin, providing atonement (satisfaction for sin) through the cross. This is the model for how Christians are to love others.

## Best Practices

- Abide in Christ daily through prayer, Scripture, and dependence on the Holy Spirit
- View every person as someone God has placed in your life to love and serve
- Put on love as your first response, not as a last resort
- Love sacrificially, seeking the spiritual and physical well-being of others
- Allow the Holy Spirit to convict you when you fall short in love
- Serve in ministries and opportunities that allow you to express love practically
- Remember that worship and service are designed to display God to others through your love
- Cultivate relationships that will affirm your character at the judgment seat of Christ

## Keep In Mind

- Biblical love is a **command**, not a suggestion or encouragement (1 John 4:21)
- God is looking for **servants** who love, not celebrities who only seek blessings
- Your life's record of love is kept in heaven, and your actions will be reviewed at the judgment seat of Christ
- How much of God you experience is directly connected to how much of His love you express to others
- Agape love is a **deliberate decision**, not an emotion. It may not always feel pleasant, but it is always right
- The Holy Spirit's work is to make God more real to you. Abiding in love enables Him to accomplish that work
- You cannot love biblically if you do not know the character of God. Study His Word to understand who He is

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not neglect the just nature of biblical love. True love does not ignore sin or harmful behavior in the lives of others. Loving someone means addressing issues that need correction, not enabling destructive patterns.
- Guard against using "love" as an excuse to avoid necessary confrontation or accountability. True love communicates truth with compassion (Ephesians 4:15).
- Beware of substituting emotional feelings for genuine agape love. Feelings are unreliable; the deliberate choice to love by God's standard is consistent and faithful.
- Do not limit your experience of God by only seeking Him for personal benefit. Selfish seeking of God will restrict how much of Him He reveals to you.
- Ensure your love is rooted in Christ and not in your own strength. Apart from abiding in Him, your attempts at love will fall short of God's standard.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Coming to church or reading the Bible only to receive a personal blessing, with no intention of serving or loving others. This self-focused approach limits your experience of God. **Solution:** Shift your focus from receiving blessings to being a blessing to others. Ask God to show you who you can serve and love this week.
- **Problem:** Confusing cultural or emotional love with biblical agape love. Cultural love often prioritizes feelings and mutual benefit, while agape love seeks the other person's well-being at cost to yourself, regardless of emotion. **Solution:** Study 1 John 4 and Romans 5 to understand that biblical love is a decision rooted in the character of God, not your fluctuating emotions.
- **Problem:** Using the word "love" illegitimately to justify behavior that contradicts God's nature. If your definition of love is not rooted in who God is, you may call harmful actions "love." **Solution:** Ground your definition of love in Scripture and the character of God. Ask, "Is this consistent with how God loves?"
- **Problem:** Believing that loving others will drain you rather than fill you. Some Christians avoid loving others because they feel they have nothing left to give. **Solution:** Remember that abiding in Christ is the source of love. The more you love through Him, the more of His love He pours into you. You cannot outgive God.
- **Problem:** Thinking that knowledge, Bible reading, or church attendance replaces the need for love. You can know the Bible intellectually and still fail at loving others. **Solution:** Evaluate your life by your love output, not your religious input. Ask God to make you known by your love for others, just as the disciples were known.
