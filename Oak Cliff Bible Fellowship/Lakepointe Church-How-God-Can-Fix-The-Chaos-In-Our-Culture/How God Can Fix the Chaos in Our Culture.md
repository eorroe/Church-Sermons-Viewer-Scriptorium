# How God Can Fix the Chaos in Our Culture

## Overview

This sermon addresses the root cause of cultural chaos and confusion by distinguishing between surface-level symptoms and the spiritual foundation beneath them. Using the analogy of mopping an overflowing sink while the stopper remains in place, it teaches that visible cultural breakdowns stem from invisible spiritual causes. The message calls for a radical return to the Lord God—not merely acknowledging God's power, but restoring a personal relationship with Him—as the only lasting solution for individuals, families, churches, and nations.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you or your community are overwhelmed by cultural confusion, divisiveness, or moral chaos
- When you recognize that surface-level solutions (politics, programs, economic fixes) are not producing lasting change
- When you sense that the church has become marginalized or ineffective in culture
- When you want to understand the connection between spiritual health and societal well-being
- When you are called to lead others through a season of distress or disorder

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify the True Source of the Problem

Stop treating symptoms and locate the root cause. Just as mopping an overflowing sink without removing the stopper fails to solve the problem, addressing cultural chaos without addressing the spiritual cause will not bring lasting change. Ask: Is God being treated as a true God in my life, family, church, and nation, or have idols taken His place?

### Step 2: Recognize the Three Spiritual Failures Leading to Chaos

Examine whether these three conditions exist, as summarized in 2 Chronicles 15:3-6:

- **No true God**: Has God been replaced by idols such as race, politics, materialism, or comfort? Remember that idols are anything or anyone that receives the allegiance of your heart, time, and priorities in place of the Lord.
- **No teaching priest**: Has the Word of God been abandoned as the final authority? When pulpits replace biblical truth with human opinion or secular thinking, the church loses its voice and its power.
- **No law**: Have personal opinions and feelings replaced God's standards? When everyone does what is right in their own eyes, society loses its moral compass.

### Step 3: Understand the Nature of God's Response

Distinguish between God's active wrath (direct judgment) and His passive wrath. Today's cultural distress often reflects God's passive wrath—He has stepped back to let a people experience life without Him because they have rejected Him. This is not the end, but an invitation to return. God allows disturbances to grow until they become so severe that people are forced to look up to Him as their only solution.

### Step 4: Return to the Lord God—Not Just God

Make a radical return that involves both power and relationship. Recognize God not only as the all-powerful Creator (Elohim) but as the Lord God (Yahweh)—your covenant-making, relational God. Revival is not merely about external religiosity; it is about restoring a personal relationship with the God who loves you.

### Step 5: Assemble Solemn Gatherings

Gather the people of God together for a solemn assembly. Halt normal activity and bring the community together to reinvite God into your midst. This is not a casual gathering but a deliberate, unified gathering focused on seeking the Lord.

### Step 6: Speak With One Voice on Biblically Clear Issues

Identify issues that are clearly addressed in Scripture and speak with unity and clarity. Do not remain silent on what the Bible plainly teaches. The church must present a united front rather than fractured opinions, allowing the world to see that God's people stand on the authority of His Word.

### Step 7: Act to Benefit Your Community

Move from talk to action by doing tangible good in your community. Adopt practical expressions of kingdom love—such as active kindness initiatives where church members perform acts of service—that demonstrate the gospel at work. Let your good deeds speak as loudly as your words.

### Step 8: Break Huddle and Invade the Culture

Do not stop at gathering. The church must break huddle and go public, showing the world what God looks like when His people live out His values in everyday life. A huddle that never breaks is useless; the world is watching to see the difference your faith makes.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Sanitarium Closet Test

A crude European test for cognitive readiness placed an inmate in a closet with a running sink and a mop. If the inmate only mopped without removing the stopper, they were deemed not ready for society. This illustrates how we often deal with cultural messes by mopping while leaving the spiritual root cause untouched.

### Example 2: Walmart and the Discount God

The preacher desired to be first in and first out of Walmart, only to find a store-wide sale in progress. He realized this mirrors our culture's desire for God on discount—people want God's name, blessings, and benefits while avoiding His full cost, principles, and authority. When God comes at full price, they shop elsewhere.

### Example 3: The Horse and the Rider

A young Jonathan Evans was given a horse named Crazy Horse at camp. He tried to command the horse to go forward while pulling back on the reins, confusing the animal. The wrangler explained that the horse needed a rider who was clear about direction. Similarly, people come to church saying "ride on, King Jesus" while negating His Word, and they wonder why Jesus does not show up.

### Example 4: The Mousetrap on the Farm

A mouse warned a chicken, pig, and cow that Farmer Brown had bought a mousetrap. They dismissed it as not their problem. The mousetrap caught a poisonous snake, which bit the farmer's wife. To care for her, the farmer killed the chicken for soup, then the pig for ham, and finally the cow for funeral food. The mouse lamented: "I tried to tell them." When God's Word is ignored, the repercussions ripple outward, affecting everyone.

### Example 5: The Lowered Basketball Hoop

At age eleven, Jonathan Evans dunked a basketball at camp—but only because the janitor had lowered the hoop to six feet. The preacher used this to illustrate that God does not lower His standards; He calls us to rise to them. Do not conform God's standards to your ability; let God's standards stretch your capacity.

### Example 6: Nineteen Men and the True God

Nineteen men in the name of a false god changed America forever after 9/11. If such dedication to a false cause can transform a nation, how much more can people who love Jesus Christ accomplish when they invade culture for the glory of God, with God made at home in their hearts, families, and communities?

## Best Practices

- ✅ Address the spiritual root before attempting to fix cultural symptoms
- ✅ Return to the Lord God—power and relationship together—not merely religious activity
- ✅ Submit every area of life to the authority of God's Word
- ✅ Assemble solemnly and intentionally when facing national or community crisis
- ✅ Speak with one clear, biblical voice on issues the Church has been silent about
- ✅ Demonstrate kingdom values through tangible acts of kindness and service
- ✅ Break huddle and take your faith into the workplace, schools, neighborhoods, and public square
- ✅ Let God's presence, not human opinion, shape your decisions and identity
- ✅ Recognize that God allows distress to draw you back to Him—respond while He is near

## Keep In Mind

- Cultural chaos is not primarily a political, economic, or social problem—it is a spiritual problem.
- God is not distant from culture; He is the author of government, family, and society. He will not skip the church to fix the White House.
- The Word of God has been dumbed down and stripped of authority in many pulpits; returning to its authority is essential.
- When God is recognized only as a powerful force but not as a relational Lord, revival cannot happen.
- God's passive wrath allows a people to experience life without Him so they can see their need for Him.
- A church that only gathers but never goes public is useless to the culture.
- Revival is corporate as well as individual; it requires communities of churches moving together.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Treat all Scripture with integrity; do not twist or manipulate biblical texts to fit cultural preferences.
- Guard against using the sermon's call to action as justification for political partisanship or cultural domination.
- Ensure that acts of service and community engagement are motivated by genuine love, not by a desire for recognition or control.
- Do not abandon speaking the truth in the name of sympathy; speak the truth in love, with compassion and clarity.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trying to mop up cultural chaos while leaving the spiritual stopper in place.
  **Solution:** Identify and remove the spiritual root cause—idolatry, abandoned truth, or lawlessness—before expecting lasting change.

- **Problem:** Confusing God's power (Elohim) with a personal relationship with the Lord (Yahweh).
  **Solution:** Pursue both the power of God and the person of God; revival requires relationship, not just religious acknowledgment.

- **Problem:** Treating God's Word as optional or secondary to personal opinion.
  **Solution:** Submit every decision to the authority of Scripture; let God's Word reign supreme in your life and community.

- **Problem:** Keeping faith private and failing to break huddle.
  **Solution:** Move from private conversation to public demonstration; let the world see the difference Christ makes in your life, family, and community.

- **Problem:** Adopting the surrounding culture while only sprinkling a little Jesus on top.
  **Solution:** Let the kingdom value system transform every area of life rather than conforming to the world's patterns.

- **Problem:** Remaining silent on biblically clear issues out of fear or compromise.
  **Solution:** Speak with one voice on what the Bible plainly teaches; the church's silence is part of the problem.

- **Problem:** Lowering God's standards to make them more achievable.
  **Solution:** Allow God's standards to stretch you; He calls you to rise to what He has set, not to lower the bar to where you are.
