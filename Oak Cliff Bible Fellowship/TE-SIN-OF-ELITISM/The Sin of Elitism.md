# The Sin of Elitism

## Overview

This sermon examines the sin of elitism — an attitude of superiority that lifts oneself or certain others above people deemed unworthy, using illegitimate criteria such as race, class, culture, education, or wealth as the basis for judgment. Anchored in James 2:1-13, it teaches that favoritism and partiality in the church are not mere social problems but sins that blaspheme the name of Christ. The poor, far from being spiritually inferior, are rich in faith and heirs to God's Kingdom — and the elitist who dishonors them miscalculates both people's true worth and the coming reversal of status in eternity.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you find yourself judging others based on outward appearances, wealth, education, or social status
- When you are tempted to give preferential treatment to certain people while ignoring or sidelining others
- When political, racial, cultural, or class divisions are fracturing relationships within the church
- When you need to understand that elitism is a spiritual sin, not just a social issue
- When you want to cultivate genuine mercy, humility, and cross-class relationships in your Christian walk

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize and Confess Elitism in Your Heart

Examine your attitudes toward others. Elitism is defined as "an attitude of superiority that lifts yourself or somebody else up while demeaning people who you do not feel worthy to be recognized, acknowledged, or treated equitably." Ask God to reveal where you have used illegitimate criteria — race, class, culture, education, wealth, or personal preference — to judge others and treat them as inferior.

### Step 2: Stop Making Distinctions Based on External Criteria

When people enter your community or church, do not make value judgments based on how they look, what they wear, or how much money they have. Both the rich and the poor come to the same church, and both deserve equal dignity and fair treatment. Honor is due to those in positions of recognized, accountable authority — such as church elders, civic officials, or organizational leaders — who have been placed there through processes that include public accountability, clear criteria, and submission to Scripture as the final authority. Elevating someone illegitimately based solely on wealth, status, or personal connection, while pushing others down, is the spirit of elitism.

### Step 3: Understand the Spiritual Wealth of the Poor

God has chosen the poor of this world to be "rich in faith" and "heirs of the Kingdom" (James 2:5). Those who have less materially must trust God daily for their needs, and that dependence routinely produces a more consistent, active faith than comfort can allow, because those with less must trust God daily for their needs. This is a general pattern, not an absolute rule about every individual. Recognize that a poor person rich in faith holds spiritual wealth you cannot buy — and you may need to borrow from their faith when your own resources fail. Associate with the lowly, as the Bible instructs, because God has endowed them with a trust in Him that wealth can erode in others.

### Step 4: Remember the Eternal Reversal

God holds back full earthly abundance from faithful poor people because He has appointed them as heirs to far greater riches in His Kingdom. "Tables will be turned" — those who love God and remain faithful in poverty will experience a reversal of status in eternity. Treat every poor brother or sister with the honor due an heir of the Kingdom, because how you relate to them now will matter when Christ's kingdom is fully revealed.

### Step 5: Fulfill the Royal Law — Love Your Neighbor as Yourself

The "Royal Law" — to love your neighbor as yourself — rules all other laws. When you fulfill this one law, you fulfill all the others. Fulfilling it means refusing partiality, refusing to elevate yourself illegitimately, and refusing to push others down. Speak and act as those who are to be judged by the "law of liberty" (James 2:12) — the liberating law that sets you free as you set others free from illegitimate abuse and discrimination.

### Step 6: Build Up Your Character Witnesses by Showing Mercy

"For judgment will be merciless to the one who has shown no mercy; mercy triumphs over judgment" (James 2:13). God will parade before Him the people you showed mercy to — the rejected, the despised, the ignored — as character witnesses on your behalf. Go out of your way to find people to show kindness to: those pushed aside by society, the poor, the disenfranchised. God offers "extra credit" to those who touch the ignored, and your acts of mercy will be your testimony when you stand before Him.

### Step 7: Do Not Let Politics or Worldly Alliances Divide the Kingdom

Political affiliations — whether Republican, Democrat, independent, or any other party or movement — must never divide the church or override your loyalty to Christ. The same God who condemns murder and adultery also condemns elitism and discrimination (James 2:10-11). You cannot selectively adopt your party's platform while ignoring the parts that conflict with Kingdom values, and you cannot allow party loyalty to override your obedience to Christ's commands on justice, mercy, and equity. Your commitment to God and His standards of justice, mercy, and equity must outweigh all political alliances.

## Examples

### Example 1: Mahatma Gandhi and the Church Usher

Mahatma Gandhi, as a college student in India, was drawn to the teachings of Jesus Christ and visited a Christian church seeking to learn more. At the door, an usher told him, "Excuse me, sir, but you can't worship here — you need to go worship with your own kind." Gandhi turned and walked out and never entered a Christian church again, concluding that if Christianity had its own caste system, he might as well remain a Hindu. This single act of elitism — based on Gandhi's race and social standing — cost the church a soul and shaped a movement. Ushers and every church member are warned: how you treat people at the door determines whether they ever return.

### Example 2: The Wealthy Man Who Wanted Unearned Leadership

A wealthy man at Oak Cliff Bible Fellowship approached Pastor Tony Evans and said he could do a lot for the church financially and reputationally because of his status in the community. He then asked to be appointed as a leader — without going through the normal vetting and serving process. Pastor Evans refused, explaining that leadership must be earned the old-fashioned way: by serving. The man's desire to be honored purely based on who he was and what he had, rather than demonstrated character and service, exposed the spirit of elitism — the same spirit the Pharisees had when they demanded the best seats in the synagogue.

### Example 3: The Pope and the Limousine Driver (The "Chauffer" Illustration)

A police officer once pulled over a car being driven wildly. When asked why he didn't give the driver a ticket, the officer replied, "I don't know who was in the car, but I know the Pope was his chauffer." This illustration is used to make a teaching point about how you treat people. The point: if you treat a poor person with contempt, you may be mistreating someone Jesus Himself is serving. The poor person you dismiss may have Jesus as their "chauffer" — and when you dishonor them, you dishonor the One who stands with them.

### Example 4: Seminary Extra Credit and God's Grace

Pastor Evans shared that during seminary, he had the goal of graduating with an A average and academic honors. When he fell short in a class, he would go to his professor and ask for extra credit assignments to boost his grade. In most cases the professor granted the request, and Evans used those moments as a picture of God's grace: many people are missing the grade — failing in commitment, in following Christ, in sensitivity to others — but God gives extra credit to those who are willing to touch the ignored, reach the disenfranchised, and show mercy to those pushed aside. Your acts of mercy become your extra credit before God.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Judge people by the legitimacy of their faith and character, not by their clothes, car, zip code, or educational credentials
- ✅ Give honor to legitimate authorities and leaders, but never use honor as a tool to elevate yourself illegitimately over others
- ✅ Intentionally associate with and befriend people from different economic, cultural, and social backgrounds
- ✅ See every person as an heir of God's Kingdom if they love the Lord, regardless of their earthly status
- ✅ Remember that money cannot solve every problem — cultivate relationships with people whose faith can carry you when your resources cannot
- ✅ Refuse to let political affiliations divide your love for and commitment to fellow believers
- ✅ Actively seek out people to show mercy to — the rejected, the ignored, the despised — so you build spiritual "character witnesses" for the day of judgment
- ✅ Fulfill the Royal Law — love your neighbor as yourself — as the overriding principle that governs all your relationships

## Keep In Mind

- God is not against the rich, nor is He against the poor — He is against the spirit of elitism that makes some illegitimately superior and others illegitimately inferior
- The Bible allows for enjoying wealth if it was earned ethically and is used righteously (1 Timothy 6:17-19; Deuteronomy 8:18) — there is no condemnation for being rich in itself
- Not all poverty is caused by irresponsibility — sickness, economic shifts, segregation, inferior education, and lack of opportunity can all keep good people trapped in poverty
- Countless faithful grandparents and ancestors died without ever seeing this world's wealth — not because they lacked faith, but because God held back their earthly reward to give them greater abundance in eternity
- Political and cultural elites — not the poor — are the primary ones oppressing Christians, dragging them into court, blaspheming the faith, and working to remove prayer from public life
- Being rich in money does not mean you are rich in faith — and when crisis strikes, your money may be useless while someone else's faith can carry them through
- You may currently feel life is unfair — if you are rich in faith and love the Lord, your full story has not yet been written
- The church that welcomes all people regardless of status reflects the true heart of Christ; an elitist church has been infiltrated by worldly values

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Using your wealth, education, or social status as the basis for treating others as inferior or for demanding unearned honor and leadership positions
  **Solution:** Remember that leadership must be earned through service and demonstrated character — not purchased or assumed based on reputation or resources.

- **Problem:** Dividing the church along political lines — rejecting fellow believers because they vote differently, and allowing worldly political alliances to override Kingdom loyalty
  **Solution:** Recognize that the same God who gives moral law on issues you care about also commands justice and equity. Your commitment to Christ must outweigh every political affiliation.

- **Problem:** Associating only with people at your own social, economic, and educational level, thereby missing the faith-filled people God has placed around you who can sustain you in crisis
  **Solution:** Intentionally build relationships across class and culture lines. The poor who love God are rich in faith — and you will need to borrow from their faith bank when your own resources run dry.

- **Problem:** Treating elitism as a minor, secondary issue compared to "bigger sins" like murder or adultery, when Scripture places discrimination and partiality on the same level
  **Solution:** Understand that breaking one law makes you a transgressor of the whole. Elitism is not a side issue — it cracks the foundation of your entire obedience and is worthy of the same serious treatment as any other sin.

- **Problem:** Waiting for God to grant you mercy on judgment day without having first shown mercy to others — expecting God to overlook your elitism while you have refused to overlook others
  **Solution:** Go now and seek out people to show mercy to. Build your character witnesses intentionally, because on the day of judgment, the measure of mercy you receive will be determined by the measure you have shown.
