# Your Breakthrough May Start With Helping Someone Else

## Overview

This sermon teaches that coming to church or engaging in religious activity solely to be blessed is insufficient for experiencing God's movement in your circumstances. Using Isaiah 58 as the central passage, it explains that God chooses a fast that involves actively improving the lives of others—loosing the bonds of wickedness, feeding the hungry, and covering the naked—rather than mere religious ritual. The core message is that your connection with God is tied to your care for others, and the more you seek to be a blessing, the more God is open to hearing and responding to your own needs.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you have been praying, fasting, and serving in church but feel like God is unresponsive or distant
- When you need breakthrough in your circumstances and want to understand why your requests may be delayed
- When you feel self-consumed by your own problems and need to refocus on being a blessing to others

## Steps

### Step 1: Shift Your Motivation From Being Blessed to Being a Blessing

Ask God directly: "God, how do you want me to be a blessing?" instead of only asking God to bless you. Acknowledge that it is okay to come to church to be blessed, but it must not be your only reason for being there. Recognize that if you want to see God move in your circumstances, you must position yourself as a tool He can use for the benefit of others.

### Step 2: Understand the Fast God Actually Chooses

Study and apply Isaiah 58:6-7. God's chosen fast is not merely abstaining from food or religious ritual; it is actively:
- Loosening the bonds of wickedness
- Undoing the bands of the yoke
- Letting the oppressed go free
- Breaking every yoke
- Dividing your bread with the hungry
- Bringing the homeless poor into your house
- Covering the naked
- Not hiding yourself from your own flesh

### Step 3: See, Feel, and Meet the Need God Places Before You

God will bring opportunities to you in a specific order: a need you see, a need you feel, and a need you can meet. When all three align, that is God telling you to handle that situation. Do not be like the preacher or Levite in Luke 10 who saw the need but passed by. Be like the Samaritan who stopped and addressed the need.

### Step 4: Treat Others as You Want to Be Treated

In Matthew 7:7-12, Jesus connects asking, seeking, and knocking with treating others the way you want to be treated. Skipping verse 12 jeopardizes verses 7-9. If you ask God for compassion, deliverance, and provision for yourself, you must be equally willing to minister those same things to someone else in the same situation.

### Step 5: Do the Assignment Vertically and Horizontally, THEN Expect God's Response

Isaiah 58:8-10 makes clear that breakthrough comes after you do the assignment. God says:
- IF you remove the yoke from your midst, the pointing of the finger, and speaking wickedness, THEN your light will break out like the dawn
- IF you give yourself to the hungry and satisfy the desire of the afflicted, THEN your light will rise in darkness and your gloom will become like midday

Do not reverse the order. Do the assignment first, and then expect God to release guidance, direction, provision, and breakthrough in your situation.

### Step 6: Show Mercy to Others to Release Mercy Over Your Own Life

James 1:13 teaches that mercy overrides judgment. When God sees that you have shown mercy to others, especially those in situations similar to your own struggles, it pushes an override button in His heart. Like character witnesses in a courtroom who speak to your good character despite your mistakes, your acts of mercy serve as character witnesses before God when you cry out to Him for breakthrough.

### Step 7: Position Yourself as a Conduit, Not a Cul-de-sac

Understand that you can limit God, not His power, but His choice. When you do not operate the way God wants you to operate, you limit His ability to work through you. God needs a conduit—someone He can use to touch others—not a cul-de-sac where blessing goes in but does not flow through to others. The less concerned you are about being a blessing to others, the less you will hear from heaven about your own life.

## Examples

### Example 1: Isaiah 58 — The People Who Fasted but Saw No Results

In Isaiah 58:3, the people were deeply religious—they were fasting, praying, reading their Bibles, and going to church. Yet they cried out, "Why have we fasted and you do not see? Why have we humbled ourselves and you do not notice?" The reason was revealed in verse 3: on the day of their fast, they still found their own desire and drove hard their workers. They were engaged in religious activity without the relational assignment God had chosen. Their religion was self-serving, and because of that, God could not hear them or use them.

### Example 2: The Good Samaritan — Passing the Test

In Luke 10, a lawyer asked Jesus how to inherit eternal life. Jesus told the story of a man beaten on the Jericho road. A preacher passed by after worship, and a deacon passed by, both seeing the need but refusing to engage. Then a Samaritan—a person of another race—stopped, felt compassion, and met the need. Jesus made clear that the neighbor is the one who shows mercy. The preacher and Levite failed the test of seeing, feeling, and meeting the need, while the Samaritan passed it. Christians fail the same test every day while looking for their own blessing.

### Example 3: The Starfish on the Beach

A father was walking on a beach picking up washed-up starfish and throwing them back into the water. His son asked, "Daddy, there are so many starfish on the beach. Why does it matter that you pick up just one?" The father replied, "It matters to the one I threw back." You cannot solve all the world's problems, but you can pick up the starfish you can reach. It matters to the one you help, and it matters to you when you need someone to pick you up and throw you back.

### Example 4: Nehemiah — Remember Me for What I Did for Others

Throughout the book of Nehemiah, as he rebuilt the city of Jerusalem and improved the lives of the people, Nehemiah repeatedly prayed, "God, remember me." He understood that his cry for God's remembrance was backed by his tangible service to others. When you need God to remember you, you can point to the lives you have touched and improved as evidence of your heart.

### Example 5: Matthew 25:31-46 — The Sheep and the Goats

When Jesus separates the sheep and the goats, He says, "When I was hungry, you fed me; when I was naked, you clothed me; when I was homeless, you gave me a place to stay." The righteous ask, "When did we do this?" Jesus answers, "When you did it to the least of these, you did it to me." Your treatment of others is directly tied to your connection with Christ.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Ask God, "How do you want me to be a blessing?" rather than only asking Him to bless you
- ✅ Be as concerned about improving the lot of other people as you are about your own circumstances
- ✅ Engage in religion that is tied to relationship, not just ritual without spiritual reality
- ✅ See, feel, and meet the needs God places before you
- ✅ Treat others the way you want to be treated if you were in their situation
- ✅ Do the assignment God gives you (vertical with Him and horizontal with others) before expecting breakthrough in your own life
- ✅ Show mercy to others so that mercy can override judgment in your own life
- ✅ Be a conduit through whom God can touch others, not a cul-de-sac that only receives
- ✅ Look for character witnesses—let your good works for others speak for you before God

## Keep In Mind

- God looks at relationship on two levels: vertically with Him and horizontally with others. Both are required.
- Your connection with God is intricately tied to your care for somebody other than you.
- The more you need God to do something for you, the more you must let God use you to do something for someone else.
- The less concerned you are about being a blessing to others, the less you will hear from heaven about your own life.
- When God cannot use you to touch somebody else, He closes His hearing to your own requests.
- You may not be in a crisis today, but keep living—there will be a time when you will need compassion and breakthrough yourself.
- Religion without relationship is like putting Bengay on a broken leg: it may make it feel better, but it does not fix anything.
- Rules without relationship will always lead to rebellion.
- God does not simply want to be used; He wants to use us.
- You are in the life improvement business. Seek opportunities to improve the lives of others.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not limit God by refusing to operate in the way He has called you to operate. Your unbelief or self-consumption can restrict His ability to work in your circumstances.
- Do not assume that religious activity alone is enough. Rituals without relational connection can leave you vulnerable to spiritual stagnation and unanswered prayer.
- Beware of becoming self-consumed with "me, myself, and I." A life focused only on your own blessing positions you to miss the very breakthrough you are seeking.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You are fasting, praying, reading your Bible, and going to church, but nothing is changing in your circumstances.
  **Solution:** Examine whether your religious activity is tied to improving the lives of others. Isaiah 58 shows that God chooses a fast of justice and mercy, not just personal ritual. Shift from asking only "What can God do for me?" to asking "How does God want me to be a blessing?"

- **Problem:** You want to ask, seek, and knock for your own breakthrough, but you refuse to treat others the way you want to be treated.
  **Solution:** Matthew 7:12 is the key that unlocks verses 7-9. If you skip treating others, you jeopardize your own ask, seek, and knock. Begin ministering to others in the same way you want God to minister to you.

- **Problem:** You see a need but pass by it because you are too busy with your own life or afraid of getting involved.
  **Solution:** Recognize the pattern: a need you see, a need you feel, and a need you can meet. When all three align, God is telling you to handle it. The preacher and Levite in Luke 10 failed the test by seeing the need but doing nothing.

- **Problem:** You feel your situation is too small or insignificant to make a difference in a broken world.
  **Solution:** You cannot solve all the problems, but you can pick up the starfish you can reach. It matters to the one you help, and God remembers what you did. Do the part before you.

- **Problem:** You are waiting for God to move in your situation, but you have not moved to help someone else in their situation.
  **Solution:** Isaiah 58:8-10 is clear: breakthrough comes after you do the assignment. God says, "IF you give yourself to the hungry... THEN your light will rise in darkness." Do the assignment first, then expect God to respond.
