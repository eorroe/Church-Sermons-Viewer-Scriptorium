# Don't Let Your Problems Distract You From God's Promises

## Overview

This sermon teaches that believers can remain in the will of God and still encounter overwhelming storms—circumstances that are out of their control, emotions that become terrifying, and theological doubts that question whether God truly cares. Using Mark 4:35-41, Tony Evans explains that trials are not a sign that you are outside of God's will, but rather a journey of discovery to deepen your understanding of who God is. The core message is that knowing who you are dealing with will shift your fear from the circumstance to the right thing—reverence for God—and keep you from letting problems distract you from His promises.

**Key Definitions:**
- **Circumstantial storm** = a crisis caused by events completely outside your control, such as a medical diagnosis, financial collapse, or relationship breakdown
- **Emotional storm** = the terror, anxiety, and instability that rise inside you when uncontrollable circumstances invade your life
- **Theological storm** = the crisis of faith that occurs when you begin to question whether God is faithful, caring, or present amid your pain
- **Hypostatic union** = the doctrine that Jesus Christ is both fully human and fully divine in one person, unmixed and forever

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are walking in God's will but suddenly face a devastating, unexpected crisis
- When your problem feels life-threatening and you don't know if you will make it
- When you are terrified, anxious, or emotionally overwhelmed by circumstances you cannot control
- When you have begun to question God's care, presence, or faithfulness because of what you are going through
- When you feel like God has set you up for defeat or abandoned you in your time of need
- When you need to remember that being in God's will does not exempt you from storms
- When you are tempted to let your problems become bigger than your trust in God

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize That Storms Can Happen in God's Will

Jesus told His disciples to get in the boat and go to the other side—they were obeying His command. Yet while they were in the boat with Jesus, a fierce gale arose (Mark 4:37). Being in the will of God does not guarantee a problem-free journey. Your circumstance may be out of your control, but it is not out of God's control. The first step is to stop blaming God for the storm and instead remember that He is with you in it.

### Step 2: Identify the Three Storms You Are Facing

There are three storms operating at once in any major crisis:
- **Circumstantial storm:** The actual event—the diagnosis, the job loss, the relationship ending. You cannot control the wind, the sea, or the rain.
- **Emotional storm:** Your internal reaction—fear, terror, anxiety. Your emotions have been riveted up because of something big, deep, and devastating that you cannot control.
- **Theological storm:** Your spiritual crisis—questioning whether God cares, whether He is faithful, or whether He has forgotten you. When circumstances are out of control and emotions are crazy, you begin to question what you believe.

### Step 3: Shift Your Fear from the Circumstance to God

When you know who you are dealing with, you will be less afraid of the circumstance and appropriately reverent of God. Jesus asked, "Why are you afraid? Do you still have no faith?" (Mark 4:40). Your fear ought to be directed toward God, not the problem. If you could talk to your circumstance, it cannot help you. No one you knew could help you. You didn't have money to buy your way out. But God spoke to it and changed it suddenly. Shift your focus from the size of the storm to the sovereignty of God.

### Step 4: Remember That Jesus Feels Your Pain and Can Fix It

Jesus is fully human and fully divine—the hypostatic union. Because He is human, He can feel exactly what you feel. Because He is God, He can do something about it. Hebrews 4:15 says we have a High Priest who is able to sympathize with our weaknesses. You are not dealing with a distant God who does not understand your pain. You are dealing with One who has been tempted in every way, just as you are, yet without sin.

### Step 5: Keep Living and Trust That God Is at Work

Trials, as inconvenient and painful as they are, are a journey of discovery of who you are dealing with. Too many people have Jesus in a manger somewhere—they only know Him as a baby in Bethlehem—but they don't know who He is today. If you have a problem and you don't have an answer, keep living. God wants to take you to a place of understanding in Him that you have never been before. Don't let the problem distract you from the promise.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Disciples in the Storm (Mark 4:35-41)

Jesus and His disciples crossed the Sea of Galilee by boat. Jesus was exhausted and fell asleep in the stern. Suddenly, a fierce gale of wind blew down on the lake, and the boat began to fill with water. The disciples—many of whom were professional fishermen—were terrified. They woke Jesus and cried, "Teacher, don't you care that we are perishing?" Jesus stood up, spoke to the wind, and said, "Peace! Be still!" The wind ceased, and there was a great calm. Then Jesus asked them, "Why are you afraid? Do you still have no faith?" The disciples were filled with fear and asked one another, "Who is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?"

### Example 2: The Hospital Waiting Room

Tony Evans shares the scenario of sitting in a hospital waiting room, scared about a doctor's report. You cannot control the diagnosis. You cannot control the medical outcome. You cannot control the finances associated with treatment. But you can control where you place your fear. The circumstance is producing a helpless and hopeless feeling, but the same Jesus who calmed the Sea of Galilee feels your pain and can speak peace to your storm. Whether you are scared about a doctor's report, financial struggles, or a relationship direction—whatever it is—you can't control it. But when you know who you're dealing with, you will be less afraid of that and more scared of Him in the right way.

### Example 3: The Journey of Discovery

Trials are a journey of discovery. When you go through a storm, you learn something about God you never knew before. The disciples learned that Jesus had authority over nature. They went from being afraid of the waves to being in awe of the Man who commands them. Your pain is not just a punishment—it is a path to a deeper relationship with Christ. Hebrews 4:15 reminds us that our High Priest has been tempted in every way, just as we are, yet did not sin. He knows what you are going through.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Recognize that being in God's will does not exempt you from storms
- ✅ Identify all three storms—circumstantial, emotional, and theological—so you can address each one
- ✅ Shift your fear from the circumstance to God, because He is the One who can change your situation
- ✅ Remember that Jesus is fully human and fully divine—He feels your pain and can fix it
- ✅ Speak to your storm in faith, just as Jesus spoke to the wind and waves
- ✅ Keep living and keep trusting when you do not have an answer yet
- ✅ View trials as a journey of discovery that leads to a deeper understanding of who God is
- ✅ Don't let your circumstances become bigger than your knowledge of God

## Keep In Mind

- What feels overwhelmingly big to you is a small thing in the hands of God
- You can be in the will of God, on the boat with Jesus, and still face a storm
- Your circumstances are out of your control, but they are not out of God's control
- The emotional storm is often more damaging than the circumstantial storm
- The theological storm—questioning God's care—is a normal response to pain, but it should lead you back to Him, not away from Him
- Jesus knows exactly how you feel because He has been tempted in every way you have
- If you don't have an answer, keep living—God is still working
- Trials are a journey of discovery of who you are dealing with

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon provides spiritual encouragement and pastoral guidance; it is not a substitute for professional medical, psychological, legal, or financial advice
- When facing serious crises such as life-threatening illness, severe depression, or suicidal thoughts, seek immediate help from qualified professionals and trusted community
- Do not ignore your emotions—acknowledge them, but do not let them drive your decisions
- Be careful not to minimize someone else's pain by saying "it's just a storm"—every storm feels big to the person in it
- Always align your understanding of God's promises with the full counsel of Scripture

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Blaming God for the storm because you believe being in His will should protect you from difficulty
  **Solution:** Remember that Jesus sent the disciples into the boat and still allowed a storm to arise—obedience does not guarantee comfort

- **Problem:** Focusing only on the circumstantial storm and ignoring the emotional and theological storms
  **Solution:** Address all three storms—the circumstance, your emotional response, and your doubts about God's care

- **Problem:** Letting fear of the circumstance overwhelm your faith in God
  **Solution:** Shift your fear to the right object—God Himself. When you know who you are dealing with, the circumstance shrinks

- **Problem:** Questioning whether God cares because the situation looks hopeless
  **Solution:** Remember that Jesus calmed the storm while the disciples panicked—He is present and powerful even when He appears silent

- **Problem:** Isolating yourself during the storm instead of seeking community
  **Solution:** Share your burden with spiritually mature believers who can help you maintain perspective and faith

- **Problem:** Treating Jesus as a distant figure rather than a personal Savior who feels your pain
  **Solution:** Remember the hypostatic union—Jesus is fully God and fully human. He sympathizes with your weaknesses (Hebrews 4:15)

- **Problem:** Demanding that God fix the situation immediately on your terms
  **Solution:** Trust that God is working even when you cannot see it, and keep living in faith until the answer comes

- **Problem:** Forgetting that every storm is an opportunity to discover more about God
  **Solution:** View trials as a journey of discovery that leads to deeper intimacy with Christ
