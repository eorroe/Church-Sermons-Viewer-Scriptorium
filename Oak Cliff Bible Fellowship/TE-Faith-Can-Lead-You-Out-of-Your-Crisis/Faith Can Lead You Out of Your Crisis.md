# Faith Can Lead You Out of Your Crisis

## Overview

The sermon uses the story of Lazarus in John 11 to illustrate how faith operates when Jesus seems delayed and death appears final. This sermon teaches that faith is an action, not a feeling, and must often be exercised before evidence appears. It explains how God can use crises to display His glory, even when His timing seems delayed. It emphasizes marrying honest emotion with sound theology, acting on God's Word even when it doesn't make sense, and understanding that while Jesus gives life, He also calls His people to help unbind one another through community.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When facing a life-and-death crisis or serious medical situation
- When you have prayed but God's answer has not come as quickly as you hoped
- When you feel disappointed with God and are struggling to pray
- When you understand doctrine but have not experienced the power behind it
- When you sense God calling you to take action that seems irrational or risky
- When you have received breakthrough but still feel bound by habits, emotions, or circumstances

## Steps

### Step 1: Send Word to Jesus

Bring your crisis directly to God in prayer. Like Martha and Mary, acknowledge that the situation is serious and that only He can resolve it. Be honest about the weight of what you are facing.

### Step 2: Ask God What Glory He Wants from Your Crisis

In the midst of pain, ask God what He intends to accomplish through your situation. Recognize that God may allow difficult circumstances that are not preferable to you but are for the purpose of displaying His glory and making His Son known. The sermon distinguishes between God allowing a situation and God actively causing harm.

### Step 3: Hold Your Emotions and Theology Together

When you feel disappointed or confused, do not abandon what you believe. Express your pain honestly, but immediately anchor yourself in doctrinal truth. Express your emotions honestly to God, then anchor your heart by declaring what you believe based on Scripture.

### Step 4: Move from Doctrine to Relationship

Know that doctrine is meant to lead you into a personal relationship with Jesus Christ, not replace Him with mere information about Him. Study the Word so that you can recognize Him when He calls you, and so that your knowledge becomes experience.

### Step 5: Respond When Jesus Calls You

Even when you are too hurt to seek Him, wait for His call. When He bids you come, get up quickly. Do not stay away because of pride or disappointment.

### Step 6: Take the Step of Faith

Identify the specific obstacle God has called you to address—the "stone" that must be removed before you can see His power. This instruction is discerned through prayer, Scripture, and the counsel of mature believers. Take action to remove it by obeying His Word, and allow others to help carry the weight, even when the situation looks hopeless, smells of decay, or makes no logical sense. Faith is acting like God is telling the truth even when it is not yet so.

### Step 7: Let Jesus Intercede for You

Trust that Jesus intercedes for you before the Father based on His finished work on the cross, and He invites you to join Him in prayer.

### Step 8: Step Out in the Freedom He Has Given You

When you experience resurrection power, do not remain bound. Accept help from other believers to help remove the "grave clothes"—the habits, wounds, and emotional bindings that still hold you even after you have been made alive, and participate in the work of loosing and freeing others in the body of Christ.

## Examples

### Example 1: A Serious Medical Crisis

A family member receives a terminal diagnosis. Instead of accepting the prognosis as final, the family prays fervently, asks God what glory He will receive through the situation, and continues to stand on God's promises even when emotions scream that it is too late. They choose to believe God for healing while also preparing their hearts for whatever God allows.

### Example 2: A Delayed Breakthrough

You have been waiting for a door to open—a job, a relationship, or a financial miracle—and it has not happened despite your faith. You feel that God is distant or late. You cry out honestly to Him, reaffirm your trust in His character, and continue to act on the specific instruction He has given you, choosing to move the stone in faith rather than allowing fear or frustration to paralyze you.

### Example 3: Bound but Alive

You have been saved and know the doctrines of the Christian faith, yet you remain trapped in an addiction, a harmful relationship pattern, or an emotional wound. You realize that you have life but lack liberty. You allow mature believers to speak truth, pray, and walk with you until the grave clothes are removed.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Be honest with God about your pain, disappointment, and confusion
- ✅ Anchor every emotion in the truth of Scripture
- ✅ Ask God what glory He wants to receive from your crisis
- ✅ Act on the specific instruction God has given you in Scripture, even when your emotions contradict it.
- ✅ Stay connected to a community of believers where people can help unbind you from what you cannot free yourself from...
- ✅ Rely on Jesus' intercession instead of trying to approach God based on your own merit.
- ✅ Keep praying as long as you have not heard a clear "no"
- ✅ Serve others in their crises as part of your own healing

## Keep In Mind

- Faith is not a feeling; it is an action based on what God has said.
- God's timing is never accidental. Delays may be tied to a greater kingdom purpose.
- Martha and Mary were not condemned for their disappointment; Jesus welcomed their honest grief.
- Biblical doctrine without personal experience leaves you heavy with information but light on power.
- Personal experience without biblical doctrine leaves you unstable and easily deceived.
- The shortest verse in the Bible, "Jesus wept," shows that He deeply sympathizes with your pain.
- Having eternal life does not automatically mean you have freedom in every area of life.
- The church is a family for the purpose of unbinding and setting one another free.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon is intended for spiritual encouragement and personal reflection, not as a replacement for professional medical, psychological, or legal advice.
- If you or someone you know is in immediate physical danger, call emergency services rather than relying solely on prayer.
- Acting in faith does not mean ignoring legitimate medical treatment or professional care; stewardship of your health is also wise.
- Church community support should complement, not replace, licensed counseling when needed.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Putting sight before faith by waiting for evidence before acting on God's Word.
  **Solution:** Remind yourself that faith acts on what it cannot yet see. Remove the stone before you see the resurrection.

- **Problem:** Abandoning honest emotion and pretending you are not hurting.
  **Solution:** Express your pain to God honestly, then immediately realign your heart with theological truth.

- **Problem:** Knowing doctrine but never experiencing the living Christ behind it.
  **Solution:** Apply every passage of Scripture to your relationship with Jesus Christ, not merely as abstract doctrine.

- **Problem:** Trying to face a crisis alone without church community.
  **Solution:** Stay embedded in a local body of believers so that others can help unbind you when you cannot free yourself.

- **Problem:** Assuming God's delay means He does not care or has abandoned you.
  **Solution:** Trust that delays often serve a larger kingdom purpose. Continue praying and asking God what He is doing.

- **Problem:** Believing that receiving eternal life means all struggles and bondages automatically disappear.
  **Solution:** Recognize that receiving eternal life does not automatically remove every struggle; you may still need mature believers to help you walk in the complete freedom Christ died to provide.
