# Do You Find It Hard To Love Others

## Overview

This sermon teaches that genuine love for others is evidence that God is alive and operating in a believer's life. It explains that love is not merely an emotion but a demonstrated action rooted in sacrifice, and that operating in love is essential for experiencing fellowship with God and having prayers answered.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you struggle to love others in your spiritual family or community
- When you feel distant from God or sense that your prayers are not being answered
- When you want to understand the biblical definition of love beyond emotional feelings
- When you need to move from merely receiving blessings to becoming a conduit of God's love to others

## Steps

### Step 1: Love God First

Place God at the front of your life, not as an afterthought. Make Him first in your decision-making and daily walk.

### Step 2: Recognize Love as Action, Not Just Words

Understand that biblical love is demonstrable through deeds and truth, not merely verbal articulation. Do not merely say you love—demonstrate it through your actions.

### Step 3: Identify Needs Around You

Look for needs you can see in the lives of others, particularly within the household of faith. Train yourself to notice distressful situations like orphans and widows in need.

### Step 4: Feel the Need Compassionately

Allow God to open your heart through the Holy Spirit to truly feel the need you see. Do not close your heart when God has opened it to someone else's situation.

### Step 5: Address the Need According to Your Capacity

Act on the need to the extent you are able. Lay down your life for the brethren by sacrificing your time, resources, or comfort. If you see a brother or sister in need and have the world's goods to help, do not close your heart—address it.

### Step 6: Prioritize the Household of Faith

While doing good to all men as you have opportunity, give priority to expressing love within your spiritual family first. This is your primary training ground for love.

### Step 7: Live Out the Two Great Commandments Together

Treat the first commandment (love God with all your heart, soul, mind, and strength) and the second commandment (love your neighbor as yourself) as two hinges on a door that must swing together. Neither is complete without the other.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Good Samaritan

The sermon references the Good Samaritan from Luke 10 as the model for love: he saw the need, felt the need, and addressed the need to the extent he was able. This threefold pattern—seeing, feeling, and addressing—defines what it means to be a neighbor and demonstrates love in action.

### Example 2: A Father Feeding Neighborhood Kids While His Own Starve

The sermon uses the illustration of a father who feeds all the neighborhood kids while his own children starve to show that prioritizing love broadly while neglecting your spiritual family is shirking responsibility to your own home. You cannot be a reservoir merely receiving blessing; you must be an aqueduct conveying it.

### Example 3: Couples Miserable Because They Are Far From God

The sermon notes that many couples are miserable because they are so far from God they cannot even love the person they are living with. This illustrates how lovelessness keeps Christians in darkness and prevents them from experiencing God's reality in their closest relationships.

### Example 4: A Loveless Christian's Prayers Blocked

The sermon teaches that a Christian who does not love operates in darkness where God will not show up. When you slam the door on a need God has opened your heart to, you are not just rejecting the person—you are shutting God out, which blocks answered prayer and keeps the Spirit from operating in your heart.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Let love be demonstrated through sacrifice, not just words—deed and truth, not merely word and tongue
- ✅ Allow the Holy Spirit to open your heart to needs around you rather than closing it
- ✅ Prioritize loving those in the household of faith while also doing good to all men as you have opportunity
- ✅ View yourself as an aqueduct of God's blessing, not merely a reservoir to receive it
- ✅ Practice love as a decision of the will to seek another's well-being, regardless of emotional attachment
- ✅ Remember that perfect love casts out fear and produces confidence before God
- ✅ Experience God by living a sermon, not just eating one

## Keep In Mind

- Eternal life is not merely a length of time but a quality of existence—knowing God (as Jesus defined in John 17). Many Christians have everlasting life (salvation) but do not experience eternal life (the reality of knowing God) because they abide in darkness through lovelessness.
- God is light and in Him is no darkness at all. The one who does not love lives in darkness, and since God is light, He will not operate in that dark realm.
- Abiding means where you hang out, loiter, and stay. If you abide in lovelessness, you are staying in darkness where God does not dwell.
- Love is a commandment and a law, not primarily an emotion. It is a decision of the will to compassionately and righteously seek the well-being of another.
- The Holy Spirit operates in the realm of the heart. He breeds confidence and demonstrates God's reality in the heart center of your soul.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon is intended for personal spiritual growth and church community application. Do not use its teachings to manipulate, coerce, or guilt-trip others into actions against their will or well-being.
- The call to sacrifice should never be used to justify enabling abuse, neglect, or harmful behavior. True love seeks the well-being of another righteously.
- Prioritizing the "household of faith" should not be interpreted as excluding or mistreating those outside the church. The instruction to do good to all men remains in effect.
- If you are in an abusive relationship or unsafe environment, seek help from appropriate authorities and trusted community resources, not solely through religious channels.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Thinking love is primarily an emotional feeling rather than a demonstrated action. **Solution:** Remember that biblical love is defined by the laying down of life—seeing a need, feeling it, and addressing it to the extent you are able.
- **Problem:** Closing your heart to a need God has opened you to see. **Solution:** Recognize that slamming the door on a legitimate need is shutting God out, not just refusing a person. Allow the Holy Spirit to keep your heart tender.
- **Problem:** Coming to church only to receive—"Feed me"—without being willing to do anything for others. **Solution:** Understand that God saved you to become part of a spiritual family where you practice love, not merely a reservoir to receive blessing. Become an aqueduct that conveys God's love to others.
- **Problem:** Believing you can love God while hating or ignoring your brother or sister. **Solution:** If you say you love God and hate your brother, you are a liar. You cannot truly know or experience God while operating in lovelessness.
- **Problem:** Operating in darkness through lovelessness and wondering why God feels distant or prayers seem unanswered. **Solution:** Move out of darkness into light by operating in love. God is light and will not dwell in darkness. Love is the hinge that opens the door to fellowship and answered prayer.
