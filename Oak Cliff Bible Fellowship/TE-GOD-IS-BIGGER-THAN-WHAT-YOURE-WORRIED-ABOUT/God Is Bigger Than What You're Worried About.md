# God Is Bigger Than What You're Worried About

## Overview

This sermon teaches you how to distinguish between legitimate concern and destructive worry, and how to transform worry into an opportunity for faith. Through examining nature, scripture, and practical examples, you will learn to recognize when worry is taking control and replace it with prayer, thanksgiving, and trust in God's provision. The core message is that worry is a call to faith—an invitation to pray and to see God as bigger than any circumstance you face.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel worry taking over a legitimate concern
- When anxiety about the future, present struggles, or past troubles is controlling your thoughts and actions
- When you need to reframe your perspective from self-reliance to God-reliance
- When you are tempted to question God's capacity, ability, or intentionality to meet your needs
- When you want to learn practical steps to replace worry with faith and prayer

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize the Difference Between Concern and Worry

Understand that concern is having a real issue and seeking a way to resolve it. Worry is when that concern has taken over and is controlling you. The moment worry seeps in, treat it as a signal that you need to shift from problem-solving to faith-practicing. Do not allow concern to degenerate into worry. When you notice the transition, pause and acknowledge that worry has become a controlling force in your life.

### Step 2: Shift Your Focus to God's Provision in Nature

Look at nature to reset your perspective. Consider the birds: they do not sow, reap, or gather into barns, yet your heavenly Father feeds them. If God cares for birds, how much more does He care for you? Consider the lilies of the field: they neither toil nor spin, yet Solomon was not arrayed like one of them. God provides for creation effortlessly, and you are worth far more than the birds or the lilies. When worry returns, redirect your attention to these examples of God's faithful provision.

### Step 3: Stop Elevating the Natural Over the Supernatural

Worry tells you that you are your own god and that you must make things happen yourself. This is a sin because it elevates man over God and places you in a divided, torn state. God gets insulted when you question His capacity, ability, and intentionality to cover the needs of His people. Stop living like those who do not trust God—those who overwork themselves and mismanage their priorities because they believe they must make everything happen themselves. Remember that you have a Father who is also your Master.

### Step 4: Seek First the Kingdom and Righteousness of God

Make God's rule and standards your priority. The kingdom is divine rule—acknowledge that God is in charge. Righteousness is the divine standard—live to please Him based on His revealed Word. When you put God first, everything else falls into proper order. Matthew 6:33 promises that when you seek first His kingdom and His righteousness, all these things will be added to you. Do not let God be somewhere down the line; bring Him to the front of every concern.

### Step 5: Live One Day at a Time

Do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will take care of itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own. Most people are crucified between two thieves: yesterday and tomorrow. Today is the tomorrow you worried about yesterday, and when you get to tomorrow, you will worry about the next tomorrow. Break this cycle by focusing on today alone. Lamentations 3:22 and 23 remind you that God's mercies are new every morning—He gives you today's mercy today, not tomorrow's mercy today.

### Step 6: Convert Worry Into Immediate Prayer

Whenever worry shows up, treat it as a formal invitation to pray. Philippians 4:6 and 7 commands: be anxious for nothing, but with prayer and supplication with thanksgiving, let your request be made known unto God. This is not a morning-only practice. When worry raises its ugly head, God is saying, "You and I need to have a conversation right now." On the spot, turn your worry into a prayer. Come with thanksgiving in the midst of your request, and the God of peace will meet you in that space.

### Step 7: Replace "But" Statements With God-Centered Faith

Monitor your speech and thought patterns. People often say, "I trust God, but..." followed by a complaint or doubt. Flip this pattern. Instead of "I don't know how I'mma pay these bills, BUT I know he's able," try saying, "I don't know how I'mma make it, BUT MY GOD SAID HE WOULD MEET ALL MY NEEDS according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus." Replace "but" with a declaration of God's faithfulness. Stop reacting to your circumstances and start reacting to Him.

### Step 8: Cast Your Cares on the Lord

Psalm 55:22 says, "Cast your cares on the Lord, and he will sustain you." When you cast your cares, you transfer the weight from your shoulders to His. He will sustain you in that place. This is an active choice—you hand over the worry, the uncertainty, and the fear, and you trust that He will hold what you cannot carry alone. Do not hold onto what He has invited you to release.

## Examples

### Example 1: Financial Uncertainty

You lose your job or face a sudden financial shortfall. Concern says, "I need to find a solution." Worry says, "I will never make it; this is the end." Apply the steps: recognize worry taking over, look at how God feeds the birds, remember that your heavenly Father knows you need these things, and seek first His kingdom by trusting Him with the outcome. Cast your financial cares on Him, pray immediately with thanksgiving, and replace "I don't know how I'll pay my bills, BUT God will provide" with "I don't know how I'll pay my bills, BUT MY GOD SAID HE WOULD MEET ALL MY NEEDS." Live today with the mercies He has given you today.

### Example 2: Health Crisis

A doctor delivers unsettling news, and anxiety about the future begins to dominate your thoughts. Concern motivates you to follow medical advice and seek treatment. Worry paralyzes you with fear of what might happen. Apply the steps: recognize that worry has taken over concern, recall that God is your healer and sustainer, and bring the worry immediately into prayer. Thank Him for the doctors, the treatment, and His presence. Declare that you are reacting to Him, not to the diagnosis. Trust Him with tomorrow's mercy today, and focus on living this day with the strength He provides now.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Distinguish clearly between concern (action-oriented) and worry (control-oriented)
- ✅ Look to nature as a reminder of God's faithful provision
- ✅ Treat every instance of worry as an automatic invitation to pray
- ✅ Bring thanksgiving into your prayers even before the need is resolved
- ✅ Seek God's kingdom and righteousness as your first priority above all other pursuits
- ✅ Live one day at a time, trusting God for today's mercies only
- ✅ Replace "I trust God, but..." statements with "I don't know how, BUT God said..."
- ❌ Do not allow concern to degenerate into worry without recognizing the shift
- ❌ Do not elevate natural solutions over supernatural trust
- ❌ Do not question God's capacity, ability, or intentionality to meet your needs
- ❌ Do not live crucified between yesterday and tomorrow
- ❌ Do not act like the heathen who believe they must make everything happen themselves
- ❌ Do not try to carry your cares alone when God has invited you to cast them on Him

## Keep In Mind

- God feeds the birds and clothes the lilies; you are worth far more to Him than they are
- When you question God's ability to provide, you are essentially calling yourself God

- Your heavenly Father knows what you need before you ask
- God's mercies are new every morning—He does not give you tomorrow's mercy today

- When worry shows up, God is inviting you into a conversation with Him right now
- Come to prayer with thanksgiving in the midst of your request

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon addresses spiritual and emotional well-being, not medical, financial, or legal advice
- If you are experiencing severe anxiety, depression, or mental health challenges, seek professional counseling alongside spiritual guidance
- Financial and health decisions should be made with wise counsel, not solely through prayer without practical action
- Concern that motivates seeking solutions is healthy; do not use this sermon to avoid responsible action
- God's peace does not eliminate the need for medical treatment, financial planning, or legal protections
- Community support, therapy, and professional care are complementary to faith, not replacements for it

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Confusing healthy concern with sinful worry and suppressing legitimate problem-solving
  **Solution:** Recognize that concern leads to action while worry leads to paralysis. Honor concern by seeking solutions, but set a boundary before it becomes worry, and pray immediately when worry appears.

- **Problem:** Trying to stop worrying by sheer willpower, which leads to frustration
  **Solution:** You cannot stop worrying by trying harder. Instead, replace worry with a specific action: pray on the spot with thanksgiving. The act of praying redirects your focus and invites God's peace.

- **Problem:** Worrying about tomorrow and missing today's mercies
  **Solution:** Remind yourself that today has enough trouble of its own. God gives mercies for today, not for tomorrow. Focus on receiving and walking in today's provision rather than borrowing tomorrow's anxiety.

- **Problem:** Using "I trust God, but..." to justify ongoing fear and doubt
  **Solution:** Catch yourself when you say "but" after a statement of faith. Flip the sentence. Put God's promise first and your uncertainty second, but attach God's truth to it: "I don't know how, BUT GOD SAID..."

- **Problem:** Feeling guilty for being tempted to worry and hiding it instead of bringing it to God
  **Solution:** Worry is not a sign of weak faith; it is an invitation to deeper faith. Do not hide it. Bring it immediately to God in prayer with thanksgiving, knowing that He meets you in that space.

- **Problem:** Thinking that seeking God's kingdom means neglecting practical responsibilities
  **Solution:** Seeking first the kingdom means ordering your priorities so that God's rule and standards lead, not that you abandon your duties. God honors your work when He is first in your heart.
