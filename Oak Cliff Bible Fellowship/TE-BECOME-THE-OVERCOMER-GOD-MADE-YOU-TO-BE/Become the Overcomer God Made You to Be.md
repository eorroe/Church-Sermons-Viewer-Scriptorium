# Become the Overcomer God Made You to Be

## Overview

This sermon by Tony Evans explains the critical difference between having the victory that belongs to you because of what Jesus accomplished on the cross, which is yours whether or not you feel like you are winning, and actually obeying Christ in the choices you make each day when you face temptation, fear, or pressure. An "overcomer" is a believer who positions themselves in Christ's finished victory and actively obeys His commands when facing temptation or opposition. You are an overcomer by virtue of your union with Christ, and you overcome as you walk in that identity. It teaches that believers must write down at least three specific statements they currently believe about who Jesus is—for example, "Jesus is gentle," "Jesus died for my sins," or "Jesus is powerful"—and write them as complete sentences so they can compare them directly to Scripture. When they read Revelation 1:12–18 three times, write down five distinct descriptions of Jesus found in those verses—such as "His eyes are like a flame of fire" and "He holds all authority in heaven and on earth"—and review this list each morning and each evening for one week, reading each description aloud so they can recall it without looking, they can experience daily obedience and freedom from temptation and fear. Before temptation, fear, or pressure arrives, they should select one explicit teaching of Jesus from the commands listed at the end of Step 5 and write it on a note card. When the moment comes, they should read the card and perform the concrete action that expresses that teaching in their current situation—for example, if the teaching is "love your neighbor as yourself" (Mark 12:31), they should do the kind act that communicates love in this moment. Victory requires conscious, daily choices: when they can name a specific sin or failure and recognize it as wrong, verbally say to God: "Jesus' sacrifice covers this specific sin, and I receive His forgiveness for it"; this week, tell one person a specific way Jesus has been real in their life—for example, how they came to trust Him, how He changed a specific habit or attitude, or how He helped them in a particular situation; when a specific command of Jesus—such as forgiving someone who wronged them (Matthew 6:14–15) or refusing to retaliate (Matthew 5:39)—conflicts with their comfort, safety, or social approval, choose to obey that command even when it costs them time, money, comfort, social approval, or personal safety. When they make these choices, they submit to Christ's authority by obeying a specific command of Jesus in their current situation, even when it costs them, so they can obey God rather than yield to temptation or despair. Victory is not automatic—they must actively choose to obey Christ each time they face temptation or fear.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel overwhelmed by circumstances or sin that seem too big to overcome
- When you attend church but find that your daily choices do not reflect what you believe, and you feel distant from God during the week
- When you need to understand the difference between having the victory that belongs to you because of what Jesus accomplished on the cross, which is yours whether or not you feel like you are winning, and actually obeying Christ in the choices you make each day when you face temptation, fear, or pressure
- When you recognize you may be seeing Jesus only as gentle or as a historical figure, without also seeing Him as the risen Lord who holds all authority in heaven and on earth (Matthew 28:18) and who is with you now and has given you the ability to obey Him, even in the situation you are facing
- When you want to move from keeping your faith private to sharing with at least one person this week what Christ has done in your life
- When you are willing to obey a specific command of Jesus this week, even if it costs you comfort, safety, or social approval

## Steps

### Step 1: Adjust Your View of Jesus

Expand your understanding of Jesus to include the description in Revelation 1:12–18—His eyes like a flame of fire, feet like burnished bronze, and voice like many waters—so that you see Him as both gentle and as the risen Lord with full authority. When John saw this Jesus, he fell at His feet like a dead man. John's reaction shows the awe that the risen Lord deserves. This is the Jesus who is now in charge, who holds all authority in heaven and on earth (Matthew 28:18), who is currently reigning as Lord over your situation by exercising His authority and working to accomplish His purposes in your life right now. For example, "His eyes like a flame of fire" means He sees and judges all hypocrisy, so you must speak and act with integrity even when no one is watching.

Write down at least three specific statements you currently believe about who Jesus is—for example, "Jesus is gentle," "Jesus died for my sins," or "Jesus is powerful"—and write them as complete sentences so you can compare them directly to Scripture. List what you currently believe about Jesus, then compare that list to the explicit descriptions of His authority and glory in Revelation 1:12–18 and Matthew 28:18. Compare each statement on your list to what Revelation 1:12–18 and Matthew 28:18 say about Jesus. For any belief that does not match Scripture or that omits essential attributes of Jesus' character, such as His authority or glory, write the biblical description in its place. Replace each inaccurate belief with the specific description from Scripture, and rehearse those descriptions aloud every morning, every evening, and whenever you notice fear or discouragement during the day for at least 60 seconds. Write down the time of day you read them and whether you noticed any fear or discouragement, so you can see how often you are returning to these truths. Read Revelation 1 at a pace that allows you to write one sentence of observation for each major description of Jesus, pausing after each verse or natural section to complete that observation before continuing. When you finish, you should have written at least one observation for each major description of Jesus in Revelation 1:12–18. Read Revelation 1 slowly—reading each phrase carefully rather than skimming—write down what it says about Jesus' appearance and authority, and each time you are afraid or discouraged, read aloud the descriptions you wrote about Jesus' authority and presence, reminding yourself that this same Jesus is with you in your current difficulty. When you choose to trust and obey the Jesus described in Revelation 1—with all authority and glory—rather than a Jesus you have mentally reduced to something less than what Scripture says, you may find that you are able to obey Him in situations where you previously would have failed. Record the date, the situation you faced, the command you obeyed, and what happened as a result, including how you felt afterward. Over time, review these records to see patterns of obedience that you could not have produced on your own strength. You will experience His presence with you in difficulty by taking three specific actions: (1) pray aloud for five minutes, (2) read one passage of Scripture, and (3) speak with one believer. These are the tangible means through which Christ has promised to be present. If you complete all three, you have obeyed the command to seek Him, regardless of how you feel.

### Step 2: Apply the Cross to Your Current Struggles

Do not treat the cross as merely a historical event that benefits you only in eternity. Applying the cross means reminding yourself of Jesus' victory, praying, and choosing to obey a specific command of Christ in the situation you are facing. When you face a specific temptation or fear, name the temptation, fear, or pressure in one clear sentence—for example, "I am afraid to speak up in the meeting tomorrow" or "I am tempted to lie about why I missed the deadline." Then say: "Jesus defeated the root of this problem—sin and the devil's authority—on the cross. Because Jesus defeated sin and the devil's authority on the cross, you are no longer obligated to obey the devil's temptations. The devil's primary weapon is deception; when you recognize a thought as contrary to Scripture, you can reject it and choose obedience." Verbally reject the choice your struggle is pressuring you toward by saying, "I will not do what my struggle is telling me to do," and then select a concrete action that obeys a command of Jesus you have studied and understand, such as forgiving someone who wronged you (Matthew 6:14–15), speaking the truth in love (Ephesians 4:15), or refusing to retaliate (Matthew 5:39), rather than yielding to the temptation to sin, the impulse to despair, or the pressure to disobey God that your struggle presents. Jesus said in Matthew 28:18, "All authority has been given to me in heaven and on earth." Jesus has not come to remove every difficult circumstance immediately. He has come to exercise His authority over those circumstances and over how you respond to them, calling you to obey Him within them.

Communion is a regular reminder that Jesus' sacrifice is not only a past event but a present source of power that enables you to obey God rather than yielding to temptation, because the same power that defeated sin and death on the cross is the power available to you now when you pray and choose to obey God's command in your current situation. The cross provides the basis for your victory, but you must personally apply its truth to your situation by reminding yourself of Jesus' finished work and choosing to obey Him. The cross is not only a past event securing your eternity—it is the foundation for your ability to obey God and overcome temptation today. When you face a current struggle, remind yourself that Jesus defeated its root cause on the cross, then choose the action that obeys God in that specific moment. Applying the cross means reminding yourself before God in prayer that the same power that defeated sin and death on the cross is the power available to you now when you pray and choose to obey God's command in your current situation, and by refusing to obey your struggle's demands by choosing instead to obey a specific command of Jesus. Without applying the cross in this way, the cross does not automatically protect you from temptation or fear unless you actively apply it by reminding yourself of Jesus' victory and choosing to obey God.

### Step 3: Acknowledge Christ's Victory

Jesus removed the devil's legal right to control you on the cross. A legal right in this context means the devil's claim or authority to demand that you obey him; Jesus canceled that claim through His death and resurrection. Because Jesus removed the devil's legal right to control you on the cross, the devil's primary weapon now is deception—getting you to believe things about yourself, God, or your situation that contradict what the Bible says in its plain meaning, according to sound interpretive principles, or what a faithful pastor or teacher can show you from the text. Test every thought against that standard. When you notice a thought that contradicts a clear promise or description in Scripture—such as "God has abandoned me" or "I am defeated"—write the thought down, then look up a Scripture verse that contradicts it and write the verse reference next to the thought. For example, if you write "God has abandoned me," write "I am more than a conqueror in Christ (Romans 8:37)" beside it. Speak the Scripture verse out loud each time the thought returns, and write down how many times you had to speak it today. You are "more than a conqueror" in Christ (Romans 8:37), and Jesus has already overcome the world (John 16:33). Your standing before God is already victorious because of what Christ accomplished—your victory is a reality grounded in what Jesus did, even if your current circumstances or emotions do not yet reflect that reality. Obedience does not require confidence; it requires choosing to obey God's command even when you are afraid, uncertain, or do not feel like it. If you are waiting for fear to subside before obeying, you may never act. When you are afraid, obey the specific command of Jesus anyway. If the fear is so severe that you cannot safely act, speak with a pastor or counselor for help. Do not wait until you feel confident or successful before obeying God. Obey Him now, even while you feel afraid or uncertain, because your victory is based on what Jesus has already done, not on how you feel.

Submit to Christ's authority by obeying a specific command of Jesus in the situation you are facing, even if it costs you time, money, comfort, social approval, or personal safety. When you feel tempted to yield to sin or despair, say aloud: "Jesus has already defeated [name your specific struggle] on the cross, and I will not act as though it has power over me. I choose to obey God in this situation." Be as specific as possible—for example, "Jesus has already defeated my fear of losing my job on the cross." If you are aware of a specific command of Jesus that applies to your current situation, obey that command now. If you do not know which command applies, pray: "Lord, show me Your command for this situation," and spend time in prayer and Scripture before deciding. If the answer is not clear, ask a pastor or mature believer for help. Act on the conviction that Jesus has already won by refusing to sin, refusing to despair, and obeying God's command in your current situation.

### Step 4: Speak the Word of Your Testimony

The fullness of victory Christ has already won includes both the positional reality that Christ has won and the practical freedom to speak openly about your faith and obey Him publicly. If you have not shared your faith with another person recently, that may be a sign that fear is keeping you from the fullness of victory Christ has already won. Confess your fear to God, ask Him to help you trust Him, and take one step this week to share what Jesus has done in your life. If you use Christian language—such as saying "God is good" or "I trust the Lord"—but your actions contradict those words, recognize that your words are empty without obedience. This week, choose one command of Jesus and obey it before you speak about your faith. If you have not verbally shared your faith with another person in the past month, or if you have avoided praying in public for fear of judgment, recognize that these patterns may be keeping you from the fullness of the victory Christ has already won.

Revelation 12:11 declares that they overcame "by the word of their testimony." Testimony is not private belief—it is telling at least one other person what Jesus has done in your life, either in conversation, in writing, or in some form of public declaration. Tell one person a specific way Jesus has been real in your life this week—for example, how you came to trust Him, how He changed a specific habit or attitude, or how He helped you in a particular situation. If you became a believer within the past 12 months, share one specific event that led you to trust Christ and one specific thing God has provided for you this week—such as peace in a difficult situation, provision for a need, or strength to obey. According to Philippians 2:9–11, God the Father has given Jesus supreme authority over all things. This means that when you tell another person what Jesus has done, you represent the authority of Christ who has been given all authority in heaven and on earth (Matthew 28:18). Your spoken testimony is not a private opinion; it is a declaration made under the authority of the risen Lord. When you tell another person what Christ has done in your life, you publicly acknowledge Jesus as the one who saved you and who now leads your life. This is what it means to glorify the Father: to acknowledge Jesus publicly as the one who saved you and who now rules your choices. The Father is glorified when you tell others what Christ has done in your life, because that declaration publicly honors Jesus as Lord. Do not refuse to speak about Jesus when given a natural opportunity, avoid praying when others can see you, or live as though your faith has no public expression. However, use wisdom by asking three questions before sharing: (1) Will this conversation put me or others in physical danger? (2) Is this a good time and place? (3) Is the listener open to hearing about faith? If the answer to (1) is yes, do not share. If the answer to (2) or (3) is no, wait for a better opportunity.

### Step 5: Love Christ More Than Life Itself

They overcame "because they did not love their life, even when faced with death." They overcame because they were willing to put obedience to Christ above their own desire for safety, comfort, or even life itself. This is not following Christ only when it is easy, comfortable, and requires no sacrifice of time, money, safety, or reputation. Paul modeled this when he said, "For me to live is Christ, and to die is gain" (Philippians 1:21). Paul's statement that "to die is gain" reflects his deep conviction that being with Christ after death is far better than earthly life. This conviction fueled his willingness to suffer and preach the gospel despite beatings, imprisonment, and rejection, showing that hope in Christ's presence after death can free you from fear of sacrifice. Do not seek suffering or wish for death out of despair; if you are struggling with hopelessness, speak with a trusted pastor or counselor, and continue to obey Christ within your current difficulties.

Select the one command that addresses the specific situation you are facing—for example, if you are holding a grudge, choose "forgive as you have been forgiven" (Matthew 6:14–15). Choose the command that requires sacrifice, not the one that is easiest. To determine which command requires the most sacrifice, compare each option against your desire for comfort, safety, approval, or convenience. Rank each candidate command by what it would cost you: (1) time, (2) money, (3) comfort, (4) social approval, (5) safety. The command that requires the highest cost across these categories is the one requiring the most sacrifice. Obey that command as soon as you are able, within the next few days if possible. If the command is "forgive as you have been forgiven" (Matthew 6:14–15), take these three actions: (1) pray, asking God to forgive your own debts as you forgive this person, (2) release your claim to revenge or retribution, and (3) if it is safe for you and the other person, and if doing so will not cause further harm, communicate your forgiveness to the person face-to-face. If it is not safe, write your forgiveness in a letter you do not send, or speak it aloud to God alone. Do not wait until you feel like forgiving; obey the command now. The cost Jesus described in Luke 9:23 is the cost of choosing His command over your own desires—putting what He asks above your desire for comfort, safety, approval, or life itself. If obeying Christ requires you to lose something you value, that loss is part of the cost of following Him. When a command of Jesus and your comfort, safety, or reputation conflict, obey the command first. Do not delay obedience until you feel comfortable or until you are certain your reputation will not suffer. Obedience comes first; comfort and reputation come second. Each morning, identify one decision you will make today. Before deciding, ask: "Does this choice obey a specific command of Jesus?" If yes, choose it. If no, choose the option that most closely aligns with a command of Jesus, even if it is not a perfect match. To determine which option most closely aligns with a command of Jesus, compare each option against the specific commands listed in this document. Choose the option that does not directly violate any clear command and that most directly reflects Christ's character of truthfulness, love, or purity as demonstrated in Scripture. Record your decision and the command it obeyed. If any man would be Christ's disciple, he must deny himself, take up his cross daily, and follow Him (Luke 9:23). You must choose to obey Christ in a way that prioritizes His command over your desire to avoid discomfort, danger, or embarrassment. For example, if Christ commands you to forgive someone, you forgive them even if it costs you pride or social standing. You must choose Christ above your own comfort, safety, and reputation, so that you obey Him even when it costs you something.

For each thought that contradicts a clear promise or teaching in Scripture, write the thought on one side of a page and the Scripture verse that contradicts it on the other. Speak the Scripture verse aloud each time the thought returns, and write down how many times you had to speak it. Do this each evening, or at least five days each week, for one month.
- You are "more than a conqueror" in Christ (Romans 8:37) and Jesus has already overcome the world (John 16:33)
- Your standing before God is already victorious because of what Christ accomplished—your victory is a reality grounded in what Jesus did, even if your current circumstances or emotions do not yet reflect that reality. Do not wait until you feel confident or successful before obeying God. Obey Him now, even while you feel afraid or uncertain, because your victory is based on what Jesus has already done, not on how you feel.
- Some difficulties—such as chronic illness, ongoing temptation, or relational brokenness—may last for a long time. Some biblical passages, such as 2 Corinthians 12:7–10, describe God using prolonged difficulties to develop dependence on Him rather than self-reliance. This is one faithful way to interpret suffering, though the Bible does not explain every specific reason for every prolonged hardship. Continue to obey Christ within these difficulties, and do not assume that prolonged struggle means God has abandoned you or that your faith is weak. Growing in Christ means repeatedly obeying Christ, studying Scripture, praying, and receiving accountability from other believers, which gradually reduces the power of sin and temptation in your life over time.
- To overcome means to obey a specific command of Jesus when you face a temptation or circumstance that opposes that command. For example, if you face the temptation to lie, overcome by speaking the truth (Ephesians 4:15). If you face the pressure to retaliate, overcome by refusing to retaliate (Matthew 5:39). God has given you explicit commands in Scripture. When a situation or temptation arises, obey the specific command that applies to that situation. Do not wait for the situation to force your response; choose the response Christ has already commanded.
- No problem you face is beyond the reach of Jesus' victory over sin and death, though not every problem is removed immediately; some remain as part of the process of growing in Christ. In every problem, Jesus has given you the ability to obey Him and experience His presence, even if the problem itself remains. Your circumstances do not determine who you are in Christ. Even when circumstances are severe, your identity is "more than a conqueror through Christ" (Romans 8:37). Obey Christ within your circumstances, and do not let your situation dictate your identity or your obedience.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use this teaching to minimize genuine suffering or struggles—acknowledge the difficulty while remembering that Jesus has already defeated sin and death, and that your current suffering does not have the final authority over your identity or eternity
- Avoid the distortion that every problem is simply a lack of faith. Overcoming is a process of repeating these actions: (1) obey a specific command of Jesus each day, (2) study Scripture regularly, (3) pray regularly, and (4) meet regularly with other believers for encouragement and accountability. Track these actions in a journal. Over weeks and months, journaling these actions often reveals a decrease in the frequency and intensity of the sin you are fighting, though some struggles may persist as part of ongoing growth. Continue obeying Christ regardless of measurable change, because your obedience is not contingent on visible progress.
- Do not seek suffering or wish for death out of despair; if you are struggling with hopelessness, speak with a trusted pastor or counselor, and continue to obey Christ within your current difficulties.
- This is not casual Christianity—commitment to Christ may involve real rejection, misunderstanding, or suffering because of your faith, and that is part of the cost
- The enemy's defeat on the cross is real, but deception still operates; remain watchful. Each morning, identify one decision you will make today. Before deciding, ask: "Does this choice obey a specific command of Jesus?" If yes, choose it. If no, choose the option that most closely aligns with a command of Jesus, even if it is not a perfect match. To determine which option most closely aligns with a command of Jesus, compare each option against the specific commands listed in this document. Choose the option that does not directly violate any clear command and that most directly reflects Christ's character of truthfulness, love, or purity as demonstrated in Scripture. Record your decision and the command it obeyed.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Seeing Jesus only as gentle, harmless, or a historical figure, while ignoring His authority and power as the risen Lord, which can leave you without a full understanding of His authority, which may make it harder to trust Him when facing severe temptation or fear.
  **Solution:** Write down at least three specific statements you currently believe about who Jesus is—for example, "Jesus is gentle," "Jesus died for my sins," or "Jesus is powerful"—and write them as complete sentences so you can compare them directly to Scripture. Write down your current description of Jesus. If your description includes only kindness, gentleness, or historical importance—and does not include His authority, power, or current lordship—your understanding is incomplete. Add to your description the attributes listed in Revelation 1:12–18 and Matthew 28:18 until your description includes both kindness and authority. Read Revelation 1 at a pace that allows you to write one sentence of observation for each major description of Jesus, pausing after each verse or natural section to complete that observation before continuing. When you finish, you should have written at least one observation for each major description of Jesus in Revelation 1:12–18. Read Revelation 1 slowly—reading each phrase carefully rather than skimming—write down what it says about Jesus' appearance and authority, and each time you are afraid or discouraged, read aloud the descriptions you wrote about Jesus' authority and presence, reminding yourself that this same Jesus is with you in your current difficulty. Replace each inaccurate belief with the specific description from Scripture, and rehearse those descriptions aloud every morning, every evening, and whenever you notice fear or discouragement during the day for at least 60 seconds. Write down the time of day you read them and whether you noticed any fear or discouragement, so you can see how often you are returning to these truths. Do not limit your understanding of Jesus to His kindness and ignore His authority as the risen Lord who holds all power. Reject the idea that Jesus is only a historical teacher or a gentle friend who would never call you to difficult obedience. If your description of Jesus includes only kindness and excludes authority, you are holding an incomplete view. Correct your description by writing down the full picture of Jesus from Revelation 1:12–18 and Matthew 28:18. When you face a command that requires sacrifice, obey it—this is how you reject the idea that Jesus is only a gentle friend. Let this be the Jesus you obey by choosing His commands over your own comfort, safety, and social approval each day. Each morning, select one command of Jesus that you will obey today. If that command conflicts with your comfort, safety, or social approval, obey it anyway. At the end of the day, write down whether you obeyed the command and what it cost you.

- **Problem:** Treating the cross as only a historical event that benefits you in eternity but has no power for current struggles.
  **Solution:** Applying the cross means reminding yourself of Jesus' victory, praying, and choosing to obey a specific command of Christ in the situation you are facing. When you face a specific temptation or fear, name the temptation, fear, or pressure in one clear sentence—for example, "I am afraid to speak up in the meeting tomorrow" or "I am tempted to lie about why I missed the deadline." Then say: "Jesus defeated the root of this problem—sin and the devil's authority—on the cross. Because Jesus defeated sin and the devil's authority on the cross, you are no longer obligated to obey the devil's temptations. The devil's primary weapon is deception; when you recognize a thought as contrary to Scripture, you can reject it and choose obedience." Verbally reject the choice your struggle is pressuring you toward by saying, "I will not do what my struggle is telling me to do," and then select a concrete action that obeys a command of Jesus you have studied and understand, such as forgiving someone who wronged you (Matthew 6:14–15), speaking the truth in love (Ephesians 4:15), or refusing to retaliate (Matthew 5:39), rather than yielding to the temptation to sin, the impulse to despair, or the pressure to disobey God that your struggle presents. Remember that Jesus said, "All authority has been given to me in heaven and on earth" (Matthew 28:18), and Jesus has not come to remove every difficult circumstance immediately. He has come to exercise His authority over those circumstances and over how you respond to them, calling you to obey Him within them.

- **Problem:** Avoiding verbal testimony about your faith for an extended period, especially out of fear of what others will think.
  **Solution:** Tell one person a specific way Jesus has been real in your life this week—for example, how you came to trust Him, how He changed a specific habit or attitude, or how He helped you in a particular situation. If you became a believer within the past 12 months, share one specific event that led you to trust Christ and one specific thing God has provided for you this week—such as peace in a difficult situation, provision for a need, or strength to obey. Overcome by "the word of your testimony"—verbally declare to at least one other person what Christ has done. Testimony glorifies the Father by exalting Jesus.

- **Problem:** Putting your own comfort, safety, or desires above obedience to Christ, choosing to keep yourself safe or comfortable instead of obeying a clear command of Jesus.
  **Solution:** This week, choose one explicit command of Jesus that conflicts with something you value—such as your comfort, your money, your social approval, or your safety—and obey that command. Record the command, the cost, and the outcome. Select the one command that addresses the specific situation you are facing—for example, if you are holding a grudge, choose "forgive as you have been forgiven" (Matthew 6:14–15). Choose the command that requires sacrifice, not the one that is easiest. To determine which command requires the most sacrifice, compare each option against your desire for comfort, safety, approval, or convenience. Rank each candidate command by what it would cost you: (1) time, (2) money, (3) comfort, (4) social approval, (5) safety. The command that requires the highest cost across these categories is the one requiring the most sacrifice. Obey that command as soon as you are able, within the next few days if possible. If the command is "forgive as you have been forgiven" (Matthew 6:14–15), take these three actions: (1) pray, asking God to forgive your own debts as you forgive this person, (2) release your claim to revenge or retribution, and (3) if it is safe for you and the other person, and if doing so will not cause further harm, communicate your forgiveness to the person face-to-face. If it is not safe, write your forgiveness in a letter you do not send, or speak it aloud to God alone. Do not wait until you feel like forgiving; obey the command now. The cost Jesus described in Luke 9:23 is the cost of choosing His command over your own desires—putting what He asks above your desire for comfort, safety, approval, or life itself. If obeying Christ requires you to lose something you value, that loss is part of the cost of following Him. When a command of Jesus and your comfort, safety, or reputation conflict, obey the command first. Do not delay obedience until you feel comfortable or until you are certain your reputation will not suffer. Obedience comes first; comfort and reputation come second. Choose to obey Christ even when it costs you time, money, comfort, social approval, or personal safety; practice this by choosing one specific, explicit teaching of Jesus that applies to your situation—such as "forgive as you have been forgiven" (Matthew 6:14–15), "love your neighbor as yourself" (Mark 12:31), or "do not fear those who can kill the body" (Matthew 10:28)—and obey it today even when obeying costs you time, money, comfort, social approval, or personal safety—the same kind of cost Jesus described when He called His followers to deny themselves and take up their cross (Luke 9:23). Practice putting Christ's commands above your own desires by choosing to forgive when you want to retaliate, choosing to speak openly when you want to hide your faith, and choosing to obey Jesus when it conflicts with your comfort. Over time, these choices replace your own priorities with Christ's commands by choosing one explicit teaching of Jesus each day and obeying it, even when it conflicts with your desires. Choose the option that does not directly violate any clear command and that most directly reflects Christ's character of truthfulness, love, or purity as demonstrated in Scripture.
