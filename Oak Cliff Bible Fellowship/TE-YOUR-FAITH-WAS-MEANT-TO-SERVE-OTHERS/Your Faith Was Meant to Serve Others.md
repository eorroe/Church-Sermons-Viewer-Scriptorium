# Your Faith Was Meant to Serve Others

## Overview

This sermon, rooted in Galatians 6:1-5, confronts a critical misalignment in the modern church: far too many churches have become hospices—places that make people comfortable—instead of hospitals—places that make people better. Tony Evans teaches that the Church of Jesus Christ exists to restore broken people through the collective work of spiritually mature believers. The core message is that faith is not meant to be a spectator sport or a weekly event, but a call to actively restore those trapped in sin and bear one another's burdens, thereby fulfilling the law of Christ—love in action.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel disconnected from the purpose of the church and need to understand your role in the body of Christ
- When someone you know is caught in a sin, addiction, or stronghold they cannot escape on their own
- When the weight of life's burdens feels too heavy to bear and you need to know the church is meant to help
- When you are tempted to believe you are "too important" or "too busy" to serve others
- When you want to understand what it truly means to fulfill the law of Christ in everyday life
- When you need a biblical framework for helping others without enabling irresponsibility
- When you are preparing for a season where you will need someone to intervene on your behalf

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize the Church's True Purpose

Understand that the Church of Jesus Christ is in the restoration business. God's normal way of working is through His people for the improvement of His people. The church is not a hospice designed to make people comfortable, nor is it a spiritual event center where people come for a weekly show. It is a hospital—a place where broken, trapped, and hurting people can come to be healed, and where spiritually mature believers are called to be the doctors, nurses, and technicians of that healing environment. Accept this as your identity and the identity of your church community.

### Step 2: Identify Who Needs Restoration

Look around your church community and your personal relationships for people who are "caught in a trespass." This refers to someone stuck in a sin, addiction, stronghold, or set of circumstances that is holding them hostage and from which they cannot break away on their own. These are people who have been "tore up from the floor up"—broken by life and sin. Recognize that they cannot fix themselves and that they need the body of Christ to come alongside them. Do not ignore them or pass by on the pretext that it is not your concern.

### Step 3: Examine Your Own Spiritual Condition

Before you can restore someone else, you must examine your own work and spiritual condition. Paul warns in Galatians 6:3 that "if anyone thinks he is something when he is nothing, he deceives himself." Pride, self-importance, and the mentality of being "too good to serve" will disqualify you from being used by God to restore others. You must be operating according to the Word of God, which means you are operating by the Spirit. Only a person walking in the truth, in gentleness, and in humility is qualified to restore another believer. God will check your ministry report and your kindness report, so examine yourself honestly.

### Step 4: Restore the One Caught in Sin with Gentleness

When you have identified someone caught in a trespass and confirmed your own spiritual readiness, approach them with the specific assignment from Galatians 6:1: "restore such a one." The Greek word for restore means to reset a broken bone or to re-stitch a torn net—it is the work of mending something that has become frayed. Approach them with the spirit of gentleness: do not hurt the person more, do not embarrass or shame them, do not make them feel more trapped. Like a surgeon using a scalpel with precision, speak the truth in love so that the problem is addressed without causing additional damage. Your goal is to unhook them from the thing that has trapped them, not to condemn them further.

### Step 5: Bear One Another's Burdens

In addition to restoring those caught in sin, Galatians 6:2 commands: "Bear one another's burdens and thereby fulfill the law of Christ." A burden is not a sin—it is a weight, a load too heavy to carry on your own. This includes financial needs, emotional pressure, accumulated losses, and life circumstances that have become weighty. Step in and help carry that load. Be a spotter for someone who is under weight. The law of Christ is the law of love, and love is demonstrated by relieving and releasing others from their burdens, not merely by saying the words "I love you." Show up, serve, and carry the weight alongside them.

### Step 6: Fulfill the Law of Christ Through Action

Fulfilling the law of Christ means moving beyond words to tangible, biblical action. James 2 calls this the royal law—the law of love. Do not merely say "I love God" and "I love you"—love is not manifested until it is demonstrated. Every time you relieve someone's burden or release someone from a trap, you are fulfilling the law of Christ. God measures how much you love Him by how much you love and serve other members of His family. If you only come to church concerned about your own blessing, your own miracle, and your own needs, you are not fulfilling the law of Christ. You are talking a good game, but not walking a good game.

### Step 7: Help Without Enabling Irresponsibility

Bear the burden, but do not take the whole load. Galatians 6:5 says, "Each one will bear his own load." You are called to help lift the burden, not to carry the entire responsibility for an able-bodied person. Do not enable someone to avoid the work, effort, or accountability that is rightly theirs. For example, the Bible commands us to feed the poor, but it also says that a person who is able-bodied and refuses to work should not eat indefinitely. Help people stand up, but do not allow them to use the sympathy of the church to avoid personal responsibility. Love lifts—but love also calls people to rise.

### Step 8: Interpose Yourself on Behalf of Others

Understand the principle of interposition: when you intervene on behalf of another believer who is heading toward destruction, God responds. The story of Exodus 4 illustrates this—God was about to take Moses' life because Moses had refused to circumcise his son, but Zipporah interposed herself, acted on Moses' behalf, and God's wrath turned away. When you step in the middle of someone's downward spiral, you are interposing yourself, and God sees that act of love. You are saving a life—physically, spiritually, or both—because Satan wants to destroy them. Your intervention matters eternally.

### Step 9: Build a "Credit Report" of Kindness

Hezekiah's story in 2 Kings 20 demonstrates the power of a life lived in service to others. When Hezekiah was dying, he prayed and asked God to remember the good he had done, the people he had helped, and the kindness he had shown. God changed His mind. One day you will face a situation where you need God to change His mind, reverse your circumstances, or deal a death blow to something coming against you. God will check your kindness report. If you have spent your life intervening for others, serving the hurting, encouraging the downcast, and walking beside those who had nowhere else to turn, you will have a credit report with a high score. Invest in others now so that you have something to present to God when you need Him to intervene for you.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Dog Wrapped Around the Pole

A dog wrapped around a pole will pull and tug, tightening the leash around itself, unable to understand that the way to freedom is not more pulling but a change of direction. Many people caught in sin, addiction, or destructive patterns are exactly like this dog—they are trapped, and every effort they make on their own only tightens the trap. They need someone else to come alongside, re-steer them, and guide them back in the right direction so they can be released. As a spiritually mature believer, you are that person. Do not wait for them to figure it out—go to them, speak truth in gentleness, and help them find the way out.

### Example 2: The Jaws of Life

When a person is trapped in a car accident, the jaws of life are brought in to pry them free—but the rescuers must work with extreme care because the person is already injured and fragile. If they work too aggressively, they cause more damage. This is exactly the posture you must take when restoring someone caught in sin. You must be careful, gentle, and intentional. Your goal is to release them without doing additional harm. Restoring someone does not mean condoning their sin or covering it up—it means addressing the truth of their situation with a heart that seeks healing, not humiliation.

### Example 3: Zipporah's Interposition

In Exodus 4, God was about to take Moses' life because Moses had neglected to circumcise his son. Zipporah did not stand by and watch—she interposed herself, took action, and circumcised her son before God reached Moses. Because of her intervention, God's wrath turned away from Moses. This demonstrates that God responds when His people intervene on behalf of others. If you see a fellow believer heading toward destruction—spiritually, relationally, or physically—step in. Your intervention can save a life and turn away judgment.

### Example 4: Hezekiah's Credit Report

When Hezekiah was faced with his own death, he did not appeal to God based on his own righteousness alone. He appealed to God based on his track record: "Remember the good I did. Remember the people I helped. Remember the kindness I showed." God changed His mind. Build a life marked by service, kindness, and intervention on behalf of others. When the day comes that you need God to reverse your situation, intercede for you, or deal a death blow to something coming against you, you will have a kindness report to present. You will have a high credit score with God because you invested in others.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Recognize the church as a hospital, not a hospice—commit to being part of a community that heals rather than merely comforts
- ✅ Restore those caught in sin with gentleness—speak truth carefully so you do not cause additional harm
- ✅ Bear one another's burdens—step in and help carry the weight when life becomes too heavy for someone else
- ✅ Examine your own spiritual condition before attempting to restore another—pride disqualifies you from being used by God
- ✅ Help with the burden without taking the whole load—enable people to rise, but do not carry their full responsibility for them
- ✅ Interpose yourself on behalf of others—step in when you see a fellow believer heading toward destruction
- ✅ Fulfill the law of Christ through action, not words—demonstrate love through tangible service and care
- ✅ Build a "credit report" of kindness by consistently serving, encouraging, and intervening for others

## Keep In Mind

- The Greek word for "restore" means to reset a broken bone or re-stitch a torn net—it is active, intentional, and skilled mending work, not passive comfort.
- The "you" in Galatians 6:1 is plural in the original Greek—Paul is addressing the collective body of believers, not a single individual acting alone.
- A burden is not a sin. Verse 1 addresses sin (a trespass); verse 2 addresses weight (a burden). Do not confuse the two when helping others.
- God's normal way of working is through His people—do not wait for God to intervene directly when He has called you to be the means of His intervention.
- You will need a spotter one day. Be available to serve now so that when the load becomes too heavy for you, the body of Christ will be there for you as well.
- Your ministry report and kindness report will be checked by God. Invest in others now for the sake of your own future.
- The law of Christ is the law of love—love is only demonstrated through action, never through words alone.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not enable irresponsible behavior: when helping someone with a burden, ensure you are lifting the weight, not carrying the entire load for an able-bodied person who refuses to take personal responsibility. The Bible is clear that a person who refuses to work should not expect ongoing provision.
- Exercise wisdom when restoring someone caught in sin: involve spiritually mature leadership or trusted members of the body when the situation involves abuse, addiction, or legal consequences. Do not attempt to handle dangerous or illegal situations entirely on your own.
- Guard your own spiritual health: you cannot effectively restore or carry burdens for others if you are not yourself operating in the Spirit and grounded in the Word. Prioritize your own walk with God before ministering to others.
- Do not restore others out of pride or a sense of superiority: if your motive is to look spiritually mature rather than to genuinely help, you have already disqualified yourself from the assignment.
- Avoid gossip or public exposure when restoring someone: gentleness requires discretion. Address the person privately and with care, not in a way that shames or humiliates them before others.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** The church becomes a hospice instead of a hospital—prioritizing comfort over genuine spiritual healing and restoration.
  **Solution:** Reframe the purpose of your church community around restoration. Ask yourself and your leaders: are we making people comfortable, or are we making people better? Shift the focus from event-centered programming to people-centered ministry where healing and growth actually occur.

- **Problem:** Believers come to church only for their own benefit—sermons, songs, and personal blessing—with no intention of serving or caring for others.
  **Solution:** Examine your motives for attending church. If you only show up to be helped but never to help, you do not understand the nature of the church. Ask God to give you a servant's heart and look for one person each week whom you can encourage, serve, or support.

- **Problem:** Attempting to restore someone caught in sin without gentleness, causing additional harm, shame, or further entrenchment in their struggle.
  **Solution:** Before speaking into someone's life, check your heart. Are you coming from a place of love and care, or frustration and judgment? Restore "in the spirit of gentleness"—with the precision of a surgeon, not the aggression of an attacker. If necessary, wait, pray, and ask the Holy Spirit to give you the right words.

- **Problem:** Taking on the full load of another person's responsibility, enabling them to avoid the work, effort, or accountability that is theirs.
  **Solution:** Help lift the burden, but do not lift the load. Encourage people to take responsibility for what they can carry themselves. Love supports; it does not carry people who are able to carry themselves. Set healthy boundaries while still showing compassion.

- **Problem:** Pride causes you to believe you are "too important," "too busy," or "too spiritual" to serve others in practical ways.
  **Solution:** Remember that if anyone thinks he is something when he is nothing, he deceives himself. God knows you are not "all that and a bag of chips," and life will eventually remind you. Humble yourself now. Serve where you are, with what you have, while you can.

- **Problem:** Believing God will always intervene directly without involving His people, leading to a passive faith that never acts on behalf of others.
  **Solution:** God's normal way of working is through His people. Do not wait for a direct miracle when God has called you to be the means of restoration. Step up, step in, and be the hands and feet of Jesus for someone who is trapped, hurting, or burdened.
