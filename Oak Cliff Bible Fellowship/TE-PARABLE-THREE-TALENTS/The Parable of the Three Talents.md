# The Parable of the Three Talents

The Parable of the Three Talents

## Overview

This sermon teaches that God has given every believer time, talents, and treasures to steward for His kingdom. At the Judgment Seat of Christ, believers will give an account of how they used what God entrusted to them. The parable illustrates that God evaluates how you viewed and used what He gave you, not your salvation itself. Jesus calls believers to be "all in" with their commitment, not "half in."

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When evaluating how you are using the resources God has entrusted to you
- When feeling fear or reluctance to serve God with your gifts
- When considering your accountability to God at the Judgment Seat of Christ

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify and acknowledge what God has entrusted to you (time, talents, treasures)

Examine your life to recognize the specific gifts, abilities, time, and material resources God has given you. Acknowledge that these are not your own but are entrusted to you for kingdom purposes. Write down or prayerfully consider what you have been given, no matter how small it may seem.

### Step 2: Invest those resources for God's kingdom rather than hiding them out of fear

Actively use your time, talents, and treasures to advance God's kingdom. Do not fall into the "handkerchief mentality" of keeping gifts hidden. Step out in faith to serve, give, and use your abilities, knowing that God rewards faithfulness. Avoid making excuses or calling God an exacting master as justification for inaction.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Faithful Servants

The first servant doubled what was given and was rewarded with authority over 10 cities. The second servant increased what was given by 50% and was given authority over 5 cities. Both servants invested what they were given and were commended for their faithfulness.

### Example 2: The Lazy Servant

The third servant buried his one talent out of fear, calling God an exacting master, and was called worthless. This represents Christians who go through the motions but have nothing to show for their faith. His fear led him to inaction, and he lost what he had been given.

## Best Practices

- Do regularly assess how you are using your time, talents, and treasures
- Do serve faithfully with whatever God has given you
- Do not bury your gifts out of fear of failure or judgment
- Do not compare your gifts or results with others
- Do not use the "I have nothing to offer" excuse

## Keep In Mind

- The Judgment Seat of Christ is about reward, not salvation
- God evaluates how you viewed and used what He gave you
- Faithfulness with little leads to greater responsibility
- Going through the motions without fruit is unacceptable to God

## Security & Safety Notes

- This teaching addresses stewardship and accountability, not physical security
- Do not take risks that violate biblical principles or harm others in the name of service
- Seek wise counsel when making major decisions about how to invest your resources

## Common Pitfalls

- **The handkerchief mentality**: Keeping gifts hidden rather than investing them for God's kingdom. Solution: Step out in faith and use what God has given you.
- **Fear of failure**: Allowing fear to paralyze you from serving. Solution: Remember that God values faithfulness, not perfection.
- **Comparing yourself to others**: Measuring your success against other believers. Solution: Focus on your own stewardship and what God has specifically entrusted to you.
- **Going through the motions**: Having religious activity without real Kingdom impact. Solution: Evaluate the fruit of your labor and ensure you are truly invested in God's purposes.
