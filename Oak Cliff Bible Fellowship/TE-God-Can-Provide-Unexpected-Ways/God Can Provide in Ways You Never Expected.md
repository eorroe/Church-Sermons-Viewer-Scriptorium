# God Can Provide in Ways You Never Expected

## Overview

This sermon teaches that God alone is your source, not the resources He uses. Pastor Tony Evans emphasizes that when you make God your only source, He can provide through unexpected and even unlikely means. Using the story of Elijah and the widow at Zarephath, he shows that obedience comes before provision, and God often asks you to give something you need in order to receive His blessing. The core message is that your eyes should be on God, not just on the resources operating today.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are facing a need and don't know where your next provision will come from
- When you feel afraid to obey God because His instructions don't make logical sense
- When you find yourself bouncing from one resource to another, hoping something will work out

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify God as Your Only Source

Stop treating your job, bank account, church, or relationships as your source. Recognize that everything else is merely a resource or vehicle that God uses. Make God your source by acknowledging that He is the one taking care of you and providing for you.

### Step 2: Stop Limiting God to What You Can See

Too many Christians live only in the five senses of the visible and physical. When you operate this way, you never get to see what an encounter with God feels like. Don't let your physical circumstances dictate your faith. Trust that God is working even when you cannot see it.

### Step 3: Obey God Before You See the Provision

We often want God to provide first and then obey. But God's order is the opposite: obedience comes first, then provision. When God tells you to do something that doesn't make sense, do it anyway. The widow at Zarephath obeyed the prophet even though she had no bread. Her faith overcame her fear.

### Step 4: Give When You Have Little

God will regularly require a response of faith in the very area of your need. If you need money, God may ask you to give. If you need food, God may ask you to share. The principle is: give and it will be given to you — pressed down, shaken together, and running over (Luke 6:38). When you give, you become a bigger container to receive more.

### Step 5: Have Spiritual People in Your Life

You need somebody in your life who is spiritual enough to know what God is doing when you can't see it. When you don't feel like going the way God is leading, you need someone who will move you toward God. Don't try to walk this journey alone.

## Examples

### Example 1: Elijah and the Widow at Zarephath

God commanded Elijah to go to Zarephath, a Gentile city in Sidon that was the center of Baal worship — the last place anyone would expect to find God's provision. There, a widow who could not even take care of herself was commanded by God to feed Elijah. She had only a handful of flour and a little oil. Yet when she obeyed, God multiplied her provision so that her flour and oil did not run out until the rain came again. Her household ate for many days.

### Example 2: Pastor Tony Evans at the Gas Station

On a freezing day, Tony Evans stopped at a gas station where a homeless man asked him for a few dollars to get something to eat. After vetting the situation, he decided to give the man $100. But he only had $100 bills and the store could not break them. Evans prayed for the man and gave him the bill, trusting God. The store clerk initially said he had no change, but after a moment, he was able to break the bill. Evans realized God allowed that situation to happen so he would give more than he originally intended — a demonstration that God provides in unexpected ways.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Make God your only source and let Him choose what resource to use
- ✅ Obey God even when His instructions don't make logical sense
- ✅ Give generously in the area where you have need
- ✅ Surround yourself with spiritually mature people who can guide you
- ✅ Keep your eyes on God, not just on the resources operating today
- ❌ Don't bounce from resource to resource hoping something will work
- ❌ Don't expect to see provision without first obeying
- ❌ Don't limit yourself to only what your five senses can perceive
- ❌ Don't try to operate in your own strength without spiritual counsel

## Keep In Mind

- God has a myriad of ways to create encounters for you — stay open to the unexpected
- It is more blessed to give than to receive; the bigger your giving, the bigger your capacity to receive
- When a resource runs out, you can say "Okay God, what's next?" because He is your source
- Praise God for the job, the bank, and the church, but remember: they are not your source
- God is never inconsistent with Himself, but anything He created He can use to accomplish His purpose

## Security & Safety Notes

- Don't operate on feelings alone — feelings are unreliable; operate on faith and the Word of God
- Don't be afraid when God asks you to do something you don't understand — He says "Do not be afraid"
- Don't give to everyone who asks without vetting — use wisdom, but when God clearly speaks, obey
- Don't isolate yourself — you need spiritually mature people to help you discern God's direction

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Bouncing from one resource to another, hoping something will work out
  **Solution:** Stop treating resources as sources. Make God your only source and let Him lead you to the right resource.

- **Problem:** Wanting provision before obeying God
  **Solution:** Reverse your order. Obedience comes first. The widow obeyed before she saw the miracle of the flour and oil.

- **Problem:** Being afraid to do what God says because it doesn't make sense
  **Solution:** Remember that God can use the unlikely, the unclean, and the unexpected. Faith overcomes fear.

- **Problem:** Limiting yourself to only what your five senses can perceive
  **Solution:** Live in the spiritual realm, not just the physical. God is a spirit and He moves in ways you cannot see.

- **Problem:** Operating without spiritual counsel
  **Solution:** Find someone spiritual enough to move you toward God when you can't see what He is doing.
