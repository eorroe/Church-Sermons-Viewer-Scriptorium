# Dont Give Up Praying Until You See God Move

## Overview

This sermon teaches the power of persistent, Scripture-based prayer and why believers must never give up until they see God move. Drawing from James 5:13-16 and the story of Elijah in 1 Kings 17-18, it explains that God responds to prayer that is anchored in His Word, prayed with faith, and continued until completion. It also emphasizes that healing and restoration come through community, confession, and anointing, not isolation.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are going through suffering, pain, or difficulty
- When you feel weary or tired of praying with no visible breakthrough
- When you need to learn how to pray effectively based on God's promises
- When you are facing physical, emotional, financial, or relational hardship
- When you feel like giving up on prayer and need encouragement to persist

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify Your Condition and Respond With Prayer or Praise

Determine whether you are currently in pain or in a place of cheer. According to James 5:13, if you are suffering, hurting, or going through difficulty, you must pray. If you are not in pain and are cheerful, you must praise the Lord. Praise is never silent — it is always expressed physically and verbally. The goal is to communicate with God continuously throughout your day, whether through prayer in pain or praise in joy.

### Step 2: Pray the Psalms That Match Your Situation

Use the Psalms as your prayer language when specific situations arise. When you are struggling or discouraged, pray Psalm 42. When you are repentant and need a clean heart, pray Psalm 51. When you are afraid, pray Psalm 34. These prayers point God back to His own Word, and God has exalted His Word above His name.

### Step 3: Open Your Bible and Anchor Your Prayer in God's Word

Pray with your Bible open. Show God His own promises and hold Him to what He has said. God only has three answers: yes, no, or wait. If God has not said no and has not yet said yes, you are in a season of waiting — and you should pray until you receive a clear answer. Pray with Scripture in hand, saying: "God, You said this. I believe this. So I'm praying this."

### Step 4: If You Are Weary and Your Prayers Feel Stuck, Seek the Church

If you are sick, weary, or tired — to the point where your own prayers are not getting through — call on the elders of the church (James 5:14). You are not meant to be a lone-ranger Christian. The local church exists so that when you cannot keep going, others can stand with you and help carry your burden until breakthrough comes.

### Step 5: Receive the Anointing and Prayer of the Elders

When you come to the elders, they should pray over you and anoint you with oil in the name of the Lord (James 5:14-15). This represents the church ministering to you at the exact point of your need. The prayer offered in faith will restore you — bringing you back to God's original intent for your life.

### Step 6: Confess Any Sin That May Be Contributing to Your Condition

If you have committed sins that may have opened the door to your sickness or weariness, confess them to one another so that you may be healed (James 5:16). Not all sickness is caused by sin, but sin can keep you sick. Confess only to someone who will pray for you, not someone who will merely talk about you. Confession and prayer go together for deliverance and healing.

### Step 7: Pray Effectual, Energized, Focused Prayer

Offer prayers that are energized and God-centered. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous person accomplishes much (James 5:16). This is not casual prayer — it is serious, focused, and intentional. Like Elijah, who was a man with a nature just like ours, your prayers can accomplish extraordinary things when they are offered in faith and with fervency.

### Step 8: Persist in Prayer Until You See the Answer

Do not give up after one or two prayers. Elijah's servant had to look toward the sea seven times before he saw even a small cloud — the size of a man's fist. Sometimes God will give you just a glimpse, an appetizer, before He gives you the full meal. Keep praying. Keep looking. Keep standing in faith until you see the breakthrough come.

## Examples

### Example 1: Elijah Shutting and Opening the Heavens

Elijah prayed earnestly that it would not rain, and it did not rain for three and a half years (James 5:17). He then prayed again, and the sky poured rain and the earth produced its fruit (James 5:18). Elijah did not make this up — he prayed based on what God had already spoken. When God told him it would not rain, he prayed that. When God told him it would rain, he prayed that. His prayers were effective because they were rooted in God's Word.

### Example 2: The Paralytic Lowered Through the Roof

In Mark 2:1-2, a paralyzed man could not get to Jesus on his own. He had four friends who carried him, climbed onto the roof, and lowered him through the tiles directly in front of Jesus. When Jesus saw their faith — not just the man's faith — He healed him. This illustrates why you must be part of a local church: when you cannot carry yourself any further, others can carry you to the presence of Jesus.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Pray with your Bible open and anchor every prayer in a specific Scripture
- ✅ Match your prayers to your situation using the Psalms as a guide
- ✅ Be part of a local church so you have spiritual support when you cannot pray on your own
- ✅ Confess sin to a trusted believer who will pray with you, not gossip about you
- ✅ Persist in prayer until you see a clear yes, no, or tangible sign of movement from God
- ✅ Offer prayers that are focused, energized, and centered on God's will
- ✅ Expect God to restore you — emotionally, circumstantially, or physically

## Keep In Mind

- God only has three answers to prayer: yes, no, or wait. If you are waiting, keep praying.
- Not every physical sickness will result in perfect physical healing on earth, but God will always remove your weariness as you trust Him.
- God may give you a small sign — a "fist-sized cloud" — before the full storm of blessing arrives. Do not despise the small beginnings.
- You are either in pain (and should pray) or in praise (and should bless the Lord). There is no neutral spiritual state.
- The prayer of faith does not mean ignoring doctors or medicine — it means bringing every situation to God alongside wise counsel and community.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Only confess sin to a trusted, mature believer who is committed to praying for you — not to someone who may gossip or misuse what you share.
- Be cautious of anyone who promises guaranteed physical healing in exchange for money, giving, or manipulation.
- Biblical prayer and anointing with oil are acts of faith, not replacements for medical care. Seek both spiritual and medical help when appropriate.
- Do not use this teaching to shame or pressure someone who is suffering. Suffering and unanswered prayer are not always signs of weak faith.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Praying casually or without focus, expecting God to move without engaging His Word.
  **Solution:** Pray with your Bible open and anchor your prayers in specific promises God has already made.
- **Problem:** Giving up after a short time because the answer has not yet appeared.
  **Solution:** Commit to praying until completion — until God gives you a clear yes, no, or sign of movement.
- **Problem:** Trying to carry every burden alone as a "lone ranger" Christian.
  **Solution:** Join a local church and build relationships with spiritual leaders who can pray for you and anoint you when you are too weary to continue.
- **Problem:** Confessing sin to the wrong person and creating more harm than healing.
  **Solution:** Only confess to someone who will genuinely pray for you and keep your confidence.
- **Problem:** Assuming that every sickness or hardship is a direct result of personal sin.
  **Solution:** While sin can contribute to sickness (1 Corinthians 11), not all suffering or illness is caused by unconfessed sin. Approach each situation with humility and wisdom.
