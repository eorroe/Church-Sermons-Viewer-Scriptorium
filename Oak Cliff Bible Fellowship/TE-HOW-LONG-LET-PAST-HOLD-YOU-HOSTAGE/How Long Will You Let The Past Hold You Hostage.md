# How Long Will You Let The Past Hold You Hostage?

## Overview

This sermon teaches that unforgiveness functions as a chain that holds people hostage to past hurts, destroying their present and ruining their future. Pastor Tony Evans explains that forgiveness is first and foremost a decision—not an emotion—and explores the two categories of forgiveness: unilateral (granted even when the offender hasn't asked) and transactional (granted with repentance and confession). The message emphasizes that unforgiveness blocks God's grace, mercy, and miracles in a believer's life, and that Christians must forgive others to the same degree they expect God to forgive them.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When past hurts continue to affect your present peace and daily life
- When you feel stuck in unforgiveness toward someone who has not apologized or repented
- When you want to understand the biblical difference between legal and relational forgiveness
- When unforgiveness seems to be blocking your spiritual growth or preventing miracles
- When you struggle with self-forgiveness and regret over past decisions
- When you need to break free from the cycle of retaliation and bitterness

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize That Unforgiveness Holds You Hostage

Unforgiveness functions as a chain that holds you hostage to what happened yesterday, destroying your today and ruining your tomorrow. It can keep you hostage day after day, week after week, month after month, and year after year. Many relationships are in crisis because one party was unable or unwilling to forgive. The racial crisis in America continues to plague us because of unresolved forgiveness. Acknowledge that your refusal to forgive is keeping you trapped.

### Step 2: Understand That Forgiveness Is a Decision, Not an Emotion

Forgiveness is first and foremost a decision, not an emotion. It does not ignore how you feel, but it is not determined by your feelings. Emotions are fluid and fluctuate, so if forgiveness were based on feelings, you could be forgiving one moment and unforgiving the next about the same issue. You must decide to forgive regardless of where your emotions are at any given time. Your emotions will catch up to your decision.

### Step 3: Know the Biblical Definition of Forgiveness

Forgiveness is the decision to relieve a person from an obligation or a debt incurred by a sin or infraction against you. The word "debt" in the Lord's Prayer refers to a bill incurred by sin. When you sin against God, you incur a debt that must be paid. Forgiveness means clearing your record and canceling the bill. It does not mean excusing, ignoring, justifying, or pretending that the offense did not happen. You must acknowledge the truth of what occurred while still choosing to cancel the debt.

### Step 4: Distinguish Between Legal and Relational Forgiveness

**Legal Forgiveness (Justification):** This is eternal and settled when you accept Jesus Christ. God declares you legally not guilty for your sins because Jesus paid the penalty on the cross. This is a permanent legal relationship with God that every believer receives at salvation.

**Relational Forgiveness:** This is conditional and deals with your ongoing fellowship with God. When you sin as a Christian, you interrupt personal fellowship with the Father. 1 John 1:9 says that if you confess your sins, God is faithful and just to forgive you and cleanse you from all unrighteousness. Relational forgiveness restores your intimate fellowship with God on earth. A Christian can be on their way to heaven but refuse to deal with the mess in their life and live out of fellowship with God.

### Step 5: Practice Both Unilateral and Transactional Forgiveness

**Unilateral Forgiveness:** Forgiveness you grant even when the person hasn't asked for it. You do this when the issue is small, when the person refuses to repent, when they cannot repent because they have died, or when you don't know where they are. Unilateral forgiveness sets you free so you are not held hostage by something that can never be fixed.

**Transactional Forgiveness:** Forgiveness granted when a person repents of what they did, confesses their sin, and makes restitution. Repentance is the decision to turn from the sin and make it right. Visible acts of repentance confirm true confession. Transactional forgiveness opens the door for restoration of the relationship and reconciliation.

### Step 6: Measure Forgiveness by Your Actions, Not Your Feelings

How do you know when forgiveness has occurred? It is not based on feelings. Forgiveness occurs when you are no longer seeking retaliation against the person who offended you. As long as you reach back into history to bring up past offenses to cause pain today, you have not forgiven. Forgiveness means you are not crediting the offense to their account or putting a charge on their bill.

### Step 7: Understand What Biblical Forgetting Means

The Bible's concept of forgetting does not mean forgetting the information. It means forgetting the pain that was caused by the information. You may remember what happened, but it no longer has the same destructive impact on your life. Joseph remembered what his brothers did to him, but he forgot the impact it had on him. Even though you may remember the person or situation, when you forgive it no longer destroys your day, week, or life because you made a decision to forgive.

### Step 8: Forgive Yourself

Some of the forgiveness we need is not toward another person but toward ourselves. We live in regret thinking "if I hadn't" done certain things. But when you repent, God cancels your debt. You must decide to let it go and not go back there in your mind. Every time the devil brings it up, you must inform him that it has already been dealt with on the cross. Learn from what you did, but do not continue going back there in your mind, thoughts, and lifestyle.

### Step 9: Apply the Lord's Prayer Principle

The Lord's Prayer says "forgive us our debts as we forgive our debtors." This means God will forgive you to the same degree that you forgive others. A refusal to forgive the bill somebody owes you will cause God not to forgive the bill you owe Him. Your bill to God is probably bigger than the bill somebody else owes you. If you don't forgive others, you will not be able to get rid of your "mountain"—whether that's addiction, fear, anger, or a broken relationship. Unforgiveness will block God's miracles in your life.

## Examples

### Example 1: John and Sally's Duck

John killed his grandfather's duck with a slingshot and buried it in the woods. His sister Sally saw him and used this information to hold John hostage by threatening to reveal his secret whenever she wanted something. Grandfather eventually told John that he saw what happened but didn't say anything because he wanted to see how long John would let Sally hold him hostage. This illustrates how past mistakes can hold us hostage when we don't deal with them through forgiveness.

### Example 2: Joseph Forgives His Brothers

Joseph's brothers sold him into slavery, but years later Joseph told them "you meant it for evil, but God meant it for good." Joseph never forgot what happened, but he forgot the impact it had on him. This shows that biblical forgiveness doesn't require forgetting the facts, but it does require canceling the debt and no longer allowing the past to control your present.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Recognize that unforgiveness is a choice that keeps you trapped in the past
- ✅ Make forgiveness a decision regardless of your fluctuating emotions
- ✅ Practice unilateral forgiveness to set yourself free when the other person refuses or cannot repent
- ✅ Practice transactional forgiveness when there is genuine repentance to restore relationships
- ✅ Measure forgiveness by whether you are seeking retaliation, not by how you feel
- ✅ Remember that forgiveness cancels a debt without excusing the offense
- ✅ Forgive yourself by letting go of regret and informing the devil that your sin is cancelled
- ✅ Apply the Lord's Prayer principle: forgive others to receive forgiveness from God

## Keep In Mind

- Unforgiveness blocks God's grace, mercy, and miracles in your life
- You may still remember the offense after forgiving, but it will no longer have power over you
- Legal forgiveness through Christ is eternal, but relational forgiveness must be maintained through confession
- Forgiveness is for your freedom, not necessarily for the other person's benefit
- The person who hurts you most by not forgiving is you
- A false definition of forgiveness will lead to a false conclusion

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon deals with spiritual and emotional topics. If you are struggling with deep trauma or abuse, seek pastoral counsel in addition to personal forgiveness work.
- Forgiveness does not mean remaining in an abusive relationship or situation.
- Transactional forgiveness requires genuine repentance and should be applied with wisdom and discernment.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Waiting to forgive until you feel like it
  **Solution:** Forgiveness is a decision, not an emotion. Make the decision first and let your feelings catch up.
- **Problem:** Defining forgiveness as forgetting what happened
  **Solution:** Biblical forgiveness means canceling the debt and forgetting the pain, not necessarily forgetting the facts.
- **Problem:** Believing forgiveness means excusing the offense
  **Solution:** Forgiveness deals with the debt, not the reality of what happened. Acknowledge the truth and still choose to cancel the debt.
- **Problem:** Only forgiving when the other person apologizes
  **Solution:** Practice unilateral forgiveness to set yourself free when the other person refuses to repent or cannot repent.
- **Problem:** Thinking God's legal forgiveness eliminates the need for relational forgiveness
  **Solution:** Legal forgiveness secures your salvation, but relational forgiveness maintains your fellowship with God. Confess your sins to maintain intimate fellowship.
