# Stop Delaying What God Called You to Do

## Overview

This sermon confronts the tendency to keep God on hold while pursuing worldly security and comfort. Using three encounters with Jesus in Luke 9:57-62, Pastor Tony Evans exposes the danger of being an "audit Christian" — someone who consumes spiritual information through sermons and Bible study without ever applying it to daily decisions. He teaches that while salvation is free by grace, following Christ as a disciple requires sacrificing comfort, timing, and personal ambition. God requires your full commitment now, not delayed until your circumstances feel safe, and not motivated by what you can gain from Him.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need to stop delaying your commitment to Christ until circumstances feel safer
- When working with the temptation to follow Jesus only for blessings or prosperity
- When the user asks about double-mindedness or holding back from full discipleship
- When you attend church regularly but struggle to apply what you learn to daily decisions
- When you are wrestling with whether God should be first in your life, schedule, or finances

## Steps

### Step 1: Evaluate Your Motivation for Following Jesus

Ask yourself honestly whether you are following Christ for who He is or for what He can provide. If your commitment is tied to blessings, prosperity, or comfortable circumstances, recognize that this is a conditional, self-serving faith. Jesus wants to know if you will follow Him when the going gets tough, not only when He is on a "roll" of miracles and popularity. Examine whether you would still follow if there were no raise, no promotion, and no visible blessing. Write down what you would lose if you followed Jesus fully right now, and ask God to reveal whether those things are holding you back.

### Step 2: Stop Auditing Your Christian Life

An "audit Christian" is someone who listens to sermons, attends Sunday school, and consumes Christian teaching but never applies it. This produces no spiritual growth because there is no investment of obedience. Confess this pattern to God and choose to live by the Word, letting it instruct your decision-making rather than simply enjoying the experience of learning. After each sermon, pick one specific point and apply it to a real decision or relationship before the week ends.

### Step 3: Reject Delayed Commitment

Identify the specific areas where you have delayed following Jesus until you could shore up worldly security first — whether that is waiting for a raise, a marriage, the children to leave home, or an inheritance. Jesus calls you to follow Him now, not when your circumstances feel safe. The kingdom of God cannot wait on your timeline. Write down what you are waiting for, and ask God to show you whether that wait is wisdom or disobedience. Take one concrete step this week that demonstrates you are prioritizing God over that waiting period.

### Step 4: Eliminate Double-Mindedness

If you want both Jesus and the world, you are double-minded — wanting the approval of Christ and the approval or comfort of your old life at the same time. Like the man who wanted to say goodbye to his family before following Christ, you may be keeping relationships, habits, or loyalties that would pull you back from full obedience. Choose to put your hand to the plow and look forward, not backward. You cannot go straight while looking back. Name one relationship, habit, or loyalty that competes with God, and take a concrete step this week to reduce its influence over your decisions.

### Step 5: Commit to Follow Through Good Times and Bad

Salvation is free and comes by faith alone in the finished work of Christ, but experiencing God in everyday life requires a price. Decide now that you will follow Jesus whether there is a raise or a loss, whether you are popular or alone, whether you feel blessed or broken. Do not follow Jesus only because of the blessings or good feelings He provides; follow Him because He is Lord. When the next difficult circumstance comes, choose to pray and obey rather than retreat or complain.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Teacher of the Law (Luke 9:57-58)

A scribe eagerly declared, "I will follow you wherever you go." His words sounded spiritual, but Jesus tested his motivation by pointing to the reality of ministry — "Foxes have holes, birds have nests, but the Son of Man has nowhere to lay his head." This man was attracted to Jesus' popularity and miracles, not to a life of sacrifice. The example warns against following Jesus only when He is blessing you and reminds you that true commitment means following even when there is no comfort or reward.

### Example 2: The Man Who Wanted to Bury His Father (Luke 9:59-60)

Another man asked to delay following Christ until after his father's funeral so he could secure his inheritance. Jesus' response — "Let the dead bury their own dead" — reveals that the father had not yet died. The man was using a reasonable-sounding excuse to postpone his commitment while he pursued physical security. The example exposes how we delay God to shore up our own safety nets first and teaches that the kingdom cannot wait on worldly priorities.

### Example 3: The Man Who Wanted to Say Goodbye (Luke 9:61-62)

A third man asked to first go home and say goodbye to his people. Jesus replied, "No one who puts his hand to the plow and looks back is fit for the kingdom of God." Looking back means double-mindedness — wanting both Christ and the approval or comfort of your old life. The example illustrates that following Jesus requires singular focus, not divided loyalty, and that emotional attachments to past relationships, habits, or identities can make you unfit for the kingdom.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Give to give, not to get. When your motivation is service and ministry rather than personal gain, your motives are pure and God will meet you.
- ✅ Put God first in your choices, schedule, finances, and relationships before you shore up your own security.
- ✅ Apply what you learn from church to your daily decision-making instead of treating Christianity as an intellectual exercise.
- ✅ Commit to follow Jesus during the bad times, not only when you are experiencing blessings.
- ✅ Recognize that discipleship costs something, and be willing to pay that price now rather than later.

## Keep In Mind

- Salvation is free and comes by faith alone in the finished work of Christ. This sermon is not about earning salvation; it is about the cost of discipleship and experiencing God fully on earth.
- An "audit Christian" attends church for information but has no intention of applying it. There is no spiritual growth for attendance without investment.
- The physical world — money, relationships, security, comfort — will compete with your spiritual priorities. You must consciously choose God's kingdom over these things every day.
- Delaying commitment to God until your circumstances feel safe is not wisdom; it is disobedience wrapped in a reasonable excuse.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not confuse financial or material security with spiritual security. Accumulating what the world offers will not fix what only God can fix.
- Do not use your family or responsibilities as an excuse to delay obeying God. There will always be seemingly reasonable reasons to wait.
- Guard against prosperity theology that presents Jesus as a means to personal gain. This misrepresents Christ and creates a fragile, conditional faith.
- Remember that you do not give to God to get. Giving to get turns God into a cosmic slot machine and promotes greed, selfishness, and pride. You give to give, and then you get.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You treat church as an information-gathering exercise without applying the Word to your life.
  **Solution:** Choose one sermon point each week and intentionally live it out in a specific decision or relationship before the week ends.
- **Problem:** You delay following God until you feel secure in your career, finances, or relationships.
  **Solution:** Commit to follow Jesus now, trusting Him with the future. The kingdom cannot wait for your circumstances to stabilize.
- **Problem:** You want both Christ and the world, creating double-mindedness that keeps you from moving forward.
  **Solution:** Name one relationship, habit, or loyalty that competes with God, and take a concrete step this week to reduce its influence over your decisions.
- **Problem:** You follow Jesus only because of what He can do for you, not because of who He is.
  **Solution:** Repent of conditional faith and surrender your desire for blessings. Pursue God for Himself, and let Him bless you as a byproduct, not a goal.
