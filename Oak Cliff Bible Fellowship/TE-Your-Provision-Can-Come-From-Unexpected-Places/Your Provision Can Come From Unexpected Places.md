# Your Provision Can Come From Unexpected Places | Tony Evans Sermon

## Overview

This sermon demonstrates that God alone is the singular source of all provision, while jobs, money, and other resources are merely temporary mechanisms He uses. Through the story of Elijah and the widow of Zarephath in 1 Kings 17, it shows that when familiar resources dry up, God can and will provide through unexpected, even culturally "unclean," channels. He does this to reveal His power to others and to remind believers that He alone is their source.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you experience anxiety or panic due to a sudden loss of income, employment, or financial resource
- When you have made a resource such as a bank account, career, or relationship your primary source of security instead of God
- When you are walking through a drought season where provision seems impossible
- When you need to remember that God is not restricted to conventional or expected means of provision
- When you are experiencing fear but are being called to act in faith despite uncertain outcomes

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify the difference between a resource and the Source

Audit your current sources of security and recognize that there is only one true source of provision: God. Jobs, banks, income, and other resources are merely mechanisms He uses, not sources in themselves. When you designate any resource as your primary source of security, you create an idol that competes with God for your trust and allegiance.

### Step 2: Release your grip on resources as security

Understand that no resource should ever dictate your decisions, emotions, or identity. God can change, redirect, or remove resources suddenly because the resource was never the source—it was only a temporary tool He used.

### Step 3: Expect the unexpected when God moves

When God is preparing to provide, His methods often will not align with human logic or conventional expectations. Do not attempt to force His provision into human frameworks of understanding. He often acts in ways that differ from your expectations and may include supernatural elements so that you will recognize Him as the source.

### Step 4: Make spiritual decisions before resource-based ones

When a need arises, do not immediately turn to your financial or practical resources. Seek God's guidance and make spiritual decisions first. If you default to your resources before seeking God, that reveals what you truly believe is your source of provision. Faith involves risk, and fear often accompanies it—but yield to faith, not to fear.

### Step 5: Trust that God can use any channel, even an unlikely one

Ravens were unclean birds under Jewish law, yet God commanded them to feed Elijah. A widow in a pagan land had nothing, yet God used her to sustain a prophet. Do not limit God by your assumptions about what constitutes acceptable or practical provision channels. He can use people, institutions, or methods that seem unqualified or unconventional by worldly standards.

### Step 6: Stay open to new resources when old ones dry up

When God provides through a specific channel and then that provision ceases, it is not because He has abandoned you. It is because He is transitioning you to a new provision channel. He will create discomfort in your current situation so that you will follow Him to the next place of provision. Arise and go where He sends you.

### Step 7: Remember that God provides for more than just you

When God meets your need, He is also providing for someone else through your obedience. He wants to work through you so that you can be a blessing to another. Like the widow of Zarephath, your obedience to God's word can result in provision that not only meets your needs but also sustains others.

### Step 8: Pass the test of the preacher's word

God is seeking those who will believe and act on His word even when His instructions do not align with practical human reasoning. The widow of Zarephath was the only one among many widows in Israel who believed the prophet's word, and because she acted on it, she received the miracle while others did not.

## Examples

### Example 1: Elijah and the Ravens

Elijah was told to hide by the brook Cherith where he would drink from the water and be fed by ravens—unclean birds that were prohibited by God's own law. God used an unlikely and ritually "unclean" mechanism to provide for His prophet, demonstrating that He is not restricted by human definitions of acceptable or clean provision channels.

### Example 2: The Widow of Zarephath

During a three-and-a-half-year drought, God sent Elijah to a Gentile widow in a pagan region who had only a handful of flour and a little oil left for her final meal. When she gave Elijah what little she had in obedience to God's word, her jar of oil and bowl of flour never ran out until the drought ended. She received supernatural provision because she chose to act on faith rather than fear.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Regularly ask: "What is my source?" so you do not unconsciously elevate a resource above God
- ✅ When a provision channel ceases, pause and ask God what He is transitioning you toward rather than panicking
- ✅ Make spiritual decisions first, even when they feel risky or impractical
- ✅ Remember Deuteronomy 8:3 and allow seasons of hunger or lack to remind you that God alone is your provider
- ✅ Give generously so that God can work through you to bless others
- ✅ Do not limit God by your expectations of how or from where provision should come

## Keep In Mind

- Fear and faith often coexist. The presence of fear does not mean you lack faith—yielding to fear is what disqualifies it. The widow of Zarephath was terrified, but she acted on faith anyway.
- God is not limited by "unclean" or unexpected channels. Ravens, pagan kings, Gentile widows, and sinners have all been used as mechanisms for His provision.
- When God provides, His methods will not always follow logical patterns or be verifiable by human reasoning alone. He does this intentionally so that you will know He is the source.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not confuse the temporary safety provided by a resource with the eternal security found in God alone. A full bank account, a stable job, or a strong network are channels, not sources.
- Be cautious of the subtle idolatry of placing your identity or security in what God has given you rather than in God Himself.
- If a resource begins to dictate your decisions, you have crossed the line from mechanism to idol.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Treating a job, savings account, or person as your source of provision instead of God
  **Solution:** Consciously remind yourself that all resources are mechanisms and that God alone is the singular source. Release your grip on them as security.
- **Problem:** Refusing to act on God's word because it does not make practical sense
  **Solution:** Remember that faith involves risk. By human logic, the widow's meal should have ended in starvation, but it became supernatural because she obeyed God's word.
- **Problem:** Being trapped by fear when God shifts your provision channel
  **Solution:** Recognize that the cessation of a current provision is often God's signal that He is moving you to a new provision channel. Do not yield to fear; move when He says move.
- **Problem:** Limiting God to clean, expected, or conventional channels
  **Solution:** Trust that God can use anything—including ravens, Gentiles, and unexpected people—to provide for you when you keep Him as your source.
