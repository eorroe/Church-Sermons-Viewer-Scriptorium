# What You Plant Today Determines Your Harvest Tomorrow

## Overview
Dr. Tony Evans teaches the law of sowing and reaping, a spiritual principle of cause and effect that governs every believer's life. He explains that there are two fields available to Christians—the flesh and the spirit—and sowing into the flesh leads to decay while sowing into the spirit leads to abundant, eternal life experiences with God.

## When to Follow This Sermon
Follow this sermon when you are facing a situation you cannot break free from on your own, when you need to understand why certain patterns keep recurring in your life, or when you want to align your daily choices with God's will to experience His reality. It is especially relevant when you feel trapped by persistent sin or when you desire a deeper, more authentic relationship with God.

## Steps

### Step 1
Acknowledge the law of causality: every action you take produces a corresponding result. What you plant determines what you harvest, and you cannot expect a different harvest than the seed you sowed.

### Step 2
Identify the two fields available to you—the flesh and the spirit. The flesh represents your unredeemed humanity, driven by desires independent of God. The spirit represents alignment with God's will and the life He offers.

### Step 3
Examine what you are currently sowing. Ask yourself whether your choices, thoughts, and actions are feeding the flesh or the spirit. Be honest about where you are investing your time and energy.

### Step 4
Choose to sow into the spirit. Prioritize spiritual input, surround yourself with mature believers who can help you when you are trapped, and align your desires with God's will rather than your own independent cravings.

### Step 5
Expect the harvest from your sowing. If you sow into the flesh, you will reap corruption and decay. If you sow into the spirit, you will reap eternal life and a growing experience with God.

## Examples

### Example 1
A believer struggles with persistent anger. They have tried to stop being angry on their own but keep snapping at loved ones. By acknowledging the law of sowing and reaping, they realize they have been feeding their flesh by replaying hurtful thoughts and refusing to forgive. When they begin sowing into the spirit through prayer, scriptural meditation, and accountability with a spiritual mentor, they experience a gradual transformation where anger loses its grip and peace takes root.

### Example 2
A Christian feels distant from God and longs for a more real experience of Him. They recognize that while they attend church occasionally, they rarely engage with spiritual food throughout the week. By sowing into the spirit—daily reading the Word, worship, and seeking God's will in decisions—they begin to sense God's presence more tangibly, experiencing the "real life" Jesus described in John 17.

## Best Practices
- ✅ Accept that you cannot sow one thing and harvest another; your harvest always matches your seed.
- ✅ Seek out spiritual mentors and teachers who can help you when you are caught in patterns you cannot break alone.
- ✅ Value spiritual input highly, supporting ministries and relationships that teach you how to walk in the spirit.
- ❌ Do not deceive yourself into thinking you can ignore God's principles and still experience His best.
- ❌ Do not attempt to overcome fleshly patterns through willpower alone; you need spiritual sowing and community support.
- ❌ Do not expect a harvest before you have planted; consistency in sowing is required.

## Keep In Mind
- The flesh and the spirit are both present within you, much like two different radio frequencies on the same device. They are distinct but operate in the same vicinity. Your soul was saved progressively at conversion, not instantly, which means ongoing transformation is part of the process. The sin nature was shut down at salvation, but its products—like cars produced by a closed factory—still run on the highways of your soul. You must actively choose which field to sow into each day.
- Do not use the principle of sowing and reaping to judge or condemn others who are struggling. The focus is on personal responsibility and growth, not on shaming people for their harvest. Protect your heart by applying these truths to yourself first before offering correction to others.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Problem:** Assuming that because you are saved, you no longer have to deal with the flesh. **Solution:** Remember that your spirit was saved instantly, but your soul is being saved progressively. Ongoing sowing into the spirit is necessary for transformation.
- **Problem:** Sowing into the flesh because a desire feels good in the moment, ignoring the long-term consequence. **Solution:** Before acting on a desire independent of God, ask yourself what harvest it will produce. The short-term pleasure often leads to long-term decay.
- **Problem:** Trying to unhook yourself from a sinful pattern without spiritual help. **Solution:** Follow Galatians 6:1 and allow spiritual believers to come alongside you. You cannot free yourself alone when you are trapped.
