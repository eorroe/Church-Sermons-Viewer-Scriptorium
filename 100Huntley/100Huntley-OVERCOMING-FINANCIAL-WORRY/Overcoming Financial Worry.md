# Overcoming Financial Worry

## Overview

This sermon, delivered by Dr. David Jeremiah in an interview with Moira Brown, addresses the crippling fear of financial loss that believers face. Using Psalm 37 as a foundational text, Dr. Jeremiah presents seven practical, actionable principles for overcoming financial anxiety through faith in God. The sermon emphasizes that worry about money is a fear of the future, and that believers can face financial uncertainty by anchoring their trust in God's faithfulness rather than their circumstances.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are experiencing fear, anxiety, or stress related to financial loss or uncertainty
- When you want to replace worry with active, faith-driven trust in God's provision — choosing to act and give even when afraid
- When the fear of financial disaster is preventing you from acting and keeping you from the life God is calling you to live as a believer
- When you need practical Biblical principles to navigate economic hardship, recession, or significant financial downturn

## Steps

### Step 1: Trust in the Lord

Trusting God is a decision, not an emotion. You do not need to wait until you feel afraid to trust Him — choose to trust Him now. As Dr. Jeremiah explains, just as coming to Christ is a decision, so is trusting God. Isaiah 12:2 declares, "I will trust in God and not be afraid." David wrote in Psalm 56:3, "Whenever I am afraid, I will trust in God." Make the deliberate choice to place your confidence in Him before fear takes hold.

### Step 2: Do Good

When financial fear prevents you from acting, do not stop doing the good things God has called you to do. Continue giving, serving, and honoring the Lord with your resources. Dr. Jeremiah's father, a pastor, demonstrated this by taking on a new missionary work during financial difficulty — saying, "It's kind of like in your face, enemy, we're going to do this." Generosity weakens fear's power over you. Generous actions are linked to improved mental health and greater longevity.

### Step 3: Dwell on God's Faithfulness

When you are overwhelmed by thoughts of financial loss, shift your focus from what you have lost to what God has proven faithful to provide. David consistently returned to what he knew for sure: God is good. Do not replay your losses in your mind; instead, recall God's faithfulness throughout your life and in the lives of others. Fill your thoughts with these testimonies of His faithfulness.

### Step 4: Delight Yourself in the Lord

In the midst of financial loss, choose to worship. Go to church, lift your voice in praise, and delight in the Lord. Dr. Jeremiah notes that during past serious recessions, the church experienced some of its best worship — because adversity removes the false security of material wealth and forces us back to the center of who God is. This is a sacrifice of praise, acknowledging that material things are temporary but spiritual things are eternal.

### Step 5: Dedicate Your Life to the Lord

When financial loss occurs, use it as an opportunity to renew your commitment to God. "Renew" your life by returning to the core truths of your identity in Christ — that you are loved, forgiven, and called by God. Tell the Lord, "I'm still here. You've given me breath, life, and hope." God is faithful to complete the work He began in you (Philippians 1:6). Do not quit on Him, for He will not quit on you.

### Step 6: Release Your Worry to the Lord

Worry is essentially fear of the future — dwelling on future difficulties that have not yet happened. The Bible commands us not to live this way. Live one day at a time. As Jesus teaches in Matthew 6:34, 'Sufficient unto the day are the evils thereof.' And as Psalm 118:24 reminds us, 'This is the day the Lord has made; rejoice and be glad in it.'. Trust God hour by hour, even minute by minute. Release your future anxieties to Him and focus on what He is doing in the present moment.

### Step 7: Discipline Yourself to Wait on the Lord

Waiting is one of the hardest disciplines for a believer, but it is also one of the most purifying. Waiting teaches patience and reminds you that while you live within time, Almighty God exists outside of time. He is with you in your situation and walks through it alongside you so that your trust in Him will deepen. Your earthly losses are a reminder of the one eternal gift — eternal life through Jesus Christ — that once received can never be lost.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Pastor Who Took on a Missionary

Dr. Jeremiah shares the example of his own father, a pastor who, during a season of financial trouble when money was not coming in, chose to take on a new missionary. Rather than retreating and worrying, he moved forward in generosity, declaring it was "in your face, enemy — we're going to do this." This act of faith in the midst of financial fear changed the spiritual tone of his household and modeled trust over fear for his family.

### Example 2: Worship in Times of Recession

Dr. Jeremiah reflects on past serious recessions and observes that the church experienced some of its most heartfelt worship during those times. When material security fades, believers naturally turn back to the reality of who God is and how they rely on Him. The adversity produced more heartfelt worship than times of prosperity ever could.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Make trusting God a daily decision, not an emotional reaction to circumstances
- ✅ Continue doing good — give, serve, and invest in God's work even when finances feel uncertain
- ✅ Keep a written journal or record of God's past faithfulness to review when fear arises
- ✅ Deliberately choose worship and praise when you feel most afraid
- ✅ Use financial hardship as a catalyst to recommit your life to Christ
- ✅ Live in the present moment, trusting God with tomorrow
- ✅ Embrace seasons of waiting as opportunities for spiritual growth
- ❌ Don't use faith as an excuse to ignore real financial problems
- ❌ Don't make impulsive financial decisions without wise counsel

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon provides spiritual principles for dealing with financial worry, not financial advice or professional counsel.
- Trusting God does not eliminate the need for wise stewardship, budgeting, or seeking godly financial guidance.
- If you are facing a financial crisis, also seek practical help from trusted advisors, church leaders, or community resources.
- Stop and prayerfully consider your specific circumstances before applying these principles.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Avoid making impulsive financial decisions based solely on emotional or spiritual motivations — seek practical wisdom and counsel alongside prayerful discernment.
- Generosity should never compromise your ability to meet essential needs for yourself or your family.
- These principles are meant for spiritual formation, not as a substitute for professional financial planning or emergency assistance.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Waiting on God becomes passive resignation instead of active trust.
  **Solution:** Waiting on the Lord is active — continue doing good, worshiping, and walking in obedience while you wait.
- **Problem:** Using faith as an excuse to ignore real financial problems.
  **Solution:** Trust God AND take wise, practical steps. Faith and action are not opposites.
- **Problem:** Dwelling on past losses instead of God's faithfulness.
  **Solution:** Intentionally shift your thoughts to God's track record of provision and care.
- **Problem:** Trying to manufacture an immediate emotional high or expect instant financial relief.
  **Solution:** Trusting God is a decision you make daily, not a one-time emotional event.
