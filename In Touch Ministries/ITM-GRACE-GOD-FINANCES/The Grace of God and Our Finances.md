# The Grace of God and Our Finances

## Overview

This sermon teaches the biblical relationship between God's grace and personal finances. Dr. Charles Stanley explains the eight dangers of desiring to get rich, reveals God's plan to grace our finances through the principle of sowing and reaping, and shows that God's ultimate purpose is to make us generous people rather than simply prosperous. The message centers on 2 Corinthians 9 and 1 Timothy 6, calling listeners to become cheerful, sacrificial givers who view their resources as channels of blessing rather than reservoirs of security.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need biblical guidance on finances, stewardship, and giving
- When struggling with the temptation to prioritize wealth and material security over God
- When seeking to understand God's purpose for your financial resources
- When learning how to give cheerfully, sacrificially, and by faith
- When feeling stuck in a "reservoir" mindset of hoarding rather than a "channel" mindset of generosity

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize the Eight Dangers of Desiring to Get Rich

1 Timothy 6:9-10 lists eight consequences for those whose driving desire in life is to get rich. These dangers apply to anyone whose primary motivation is accumulating wealth:

1. Temptation to fall into sin
2. Falling into a trap
3. Being mastered by foolish and harmful desires that eat away at character
4. Engaging in harmful desires that damage moral integrity
5. Plunging into moral ruin
6. Ultimate destruction
7. Wandering from the faith
8. Many griefs piercing the soul

Key insight: Wealth itself is not sinful, but when wealth becomes your god and is out of sync with God's Word and His way, problems follow. There are godly, generous wealthy people, and the Bible does not take pleasure in keeping people poor. The issue is balance and priority.

### Step 2: Understand That God Desires to Grace Your Finances

God's plan is to bless, favor, and show grace toward your finances. From 2 Corinthians 9:6-15 and Proverbs 3:9-10:

- God loves a cheerful giver
- God is able to make all grace abound to you
- God promises all sufficiency in everything and abundance for every good deed
- When you honor the Lord from your wealth, your barns will be filled with plenty and your vats will overflow

God is not limited in His favor. He desires to grace His children financially when they abide by His principles.

### Step 3: Practice the Principle of Sowing and Reaping

Man's part in God's financial plan involves two elements: action and attitude.

**Action:** He who sows sparingly shall also reap sparingly; he who sows bountifully shall also reap bountifully (2 Corinthians 9:6). This principle applies to every area of life—relationships, work, love, and finances. To have friends, you must give friendship away. To be loved, you must give love away. To get ahead, you must be diligent.

**Attitude:** Give not grudgingly or under compulsion, but cheerfully (2 Corinthians 9:7). God loves a cheerful giver and is ready to grace those who give with the right heart.

### Step 4: Give as Jesus Gave—Sacrificially and by Faith

To know when you are truly practicing grace-giving, examine your heart against these five markers from 2 Corinthians 8:

1. **Give in spite of your circumstances.** Do not check to see how much you will have left. Give even when it requires trust.
2. **Give enthusiastically.** Be excited and joyful about the privilege of giving.
3. **Give sacrificially.** Follow Jesus' example—He gave Himself away completely.
4. **Give willingly and generously.** Want to give; do not give out of obligation.
5. **Give by faith.** Trust God even when you cannot see your way clear.

Grace-giving is not about dollars and cents; it is about an attitude that reflects the character of Christ.

### Step 5: Shift From Reservoir to Channel

Most people operate with a reservoir mindset: "God, this is mine. Bless my small reservoir." But God wants to turn your reservoir into a channel wide enough for Him to pour through. As long as you see your finances as yours, you limit what God can do. When you give generously, you become a channel through which God blesses others and, in turn, blesses you.

### Step 6: Understand God's Purpose for Your Generosity

God's primary purpose in gracing you financially is not to make you prosperous—it is to make you generous. Prosperity is the backlash or byproduct of a generous heart. When you give generously with the right heart, God enriches you to enable further generosity, creating a cycle of blessing.

### Step 7: Experience the Six Results of Grace-Giving

When you practice grace-giving with a generous spirit, these six results follow:

1. **God will enrich you as you enrich others.** (2 Corinthians 9:11)
2. **Other people's needs will be met.** You become the channel through which God provides.
3. **You will become more like Jesus.** Giving conforms you to the character of Christ.
4. **You will motivate other people to give.** Your testimony inspires generosity in others.
5. **It will unite God's people.** Generosity creates harmony and eliminates conflict that stems from selfishness.
6. **It will glorify God.** When you practice grace-giving, God gets exalted and receives the glory.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Farmer and the Principle of Sowing

A farmer in biblical times understood that planting a small amount of seed yielded a small harvest, while planting bountifully yielded a bountiful harvest. This same principle applies to every occupation and every area of life. Whether in your work, relationships, or finances, the amount you invest in blessing others determines the measure of blessing you receive.

### Example 2: The Employer and Employee Relationship

An employer who hires you expects you to do a good job and will reward diligent work. Likewise, an employee has the responsibility to work diligently. This reciprocal relationship demonstrates that the sowing and reaping principle extends beyond money into every area of life, eradicating laziness, slothfulness, stinginess, and greediness.

### Example 3: The Family United by Generosity

In a family where everyone is generous, there is a wonderful sense of oneness and harmony. "You can have that, take some more." But when one or more people are stingy, conflict arises: "Don't step on that, that's mine." Generosity unites; selfishness divides.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Give cheerfully, not grudgingly or under compulsion
- ✅ Give sacrificially, even when it requires faith
- ✅ Give first, before your own needs feel fully secure
- ✅ View yourself as a channel, not a reservoir
- ✅ Practice grace-giving in every area of life—time, love, friendship, and money
- ✅ Give by faith, trusting God to multiply what you release
- ✅ Focus on becoming a generous person rather than seeking personal prosperity

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon provides spiritual guidance on finances based on biblical principles from 1 Timothy 6, 2 Corinthians 8-9, and Proverbs 3.
- Apply these teachings with discernment and in alignment with your personal circumstances and convictions.
- Generosity should never be practiced under coercion or manipulation.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Giving grudgingly or under compulsion
  **Solution:** Cultivate a cheerful heart by remembering that God loves a cheerful giver and that grace-giving reflects the character of Christ. Focus on the privilege of giving, not the obligation.

- **Problem:** Viewing wealth as a reservoir for personal security
  **Solution:** Shift your perspective to see yourself as a channel through which God flows blessings to others. Release your grip on personal security and trust God to meet your needs as you bless others.

- **Problem:** Giving only to receive personal prosperity
  **Solution:** Focus on becoming a generous person rather than seeking wealth. Prosperity follows generosity as a byproduct, not the other way around. Check your motives regularly.

- **Problem:** Believing God's blessings are limited to finances
  **Solution:** Understand that God enriches every area of your life—relationships, joy, peace, and purpose—when you practice grace-giving. Do not narrow God's work to dollars and cents.

- **Problem:** Thinking grace-giving only applies when you have abundance
  **Solution:** Grace-giving begins in spite of your circumstances. The widow's mite demonstrates that the heart behind the gift matters more than the amount. Give what you have, where you are.
