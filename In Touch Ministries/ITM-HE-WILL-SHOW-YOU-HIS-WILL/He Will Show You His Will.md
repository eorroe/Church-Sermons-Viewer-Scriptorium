# He Will Show You His Will

## Overview

This sermon teaches that God has a specific will and purpose for every person's life, and that believers can know God's will through Scripture, prayer, relationship with Him, circumstances, godly counsel, and the witness of the Holy Spirit, and this sermon explains how to apply each of these methods in your life. The sermon explains each of these methods and provides practical steps for applying them. Dr. Charles Stanley explains that understanding God's will is not complicated but requires a lifestyle of regular engagement with God through His Word and a heart surrendered to His leading.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need to understand if a significant life choice - such as a career change, relationship decision, ministry opportunity, or major relocation - aligns with God's will
- When seeking direction for career, relationships, or ministry
- When feeling uncertain about God's purpose for your life
- When wanting to grow in discerning God's voice and leading

## Steps

### Step 1: Read and Study the Word of God Regularly

Make reading and studying the Bible a daily habit in your life. The Word of God is described as a lamp to your feet and a light to your path (Psalm 119:105). As you regularly read, study, and memorize Scripture, the Holy Spirit uses the Word to shape your thinking and develop your ability to recognize God's principles in everyday situations. When discerning God's will becomes a regular practice through daily time in the Word and prayer, the Holy Spirit guides your decisions in both major and minor situations because your mind has been renewed by the Holy Spirit through daily engagement with God's Word. This renewed mind recognizes His leading as consistent with Scripture and produces a steady inner calm—a peaceful confidence that persists even when external circumstances are uncertain—rather than confusion or anxiety. Test this calm against the seven verification questions in this sermon to confirm it is the Spirit's witness and not merely your own wishful thinking.

### Step 2: Humble Yourself in Prayer Before Seeking Direction

Approach God with humility and a surrendered heart when seeking direction. Prayer involves both telling God your thoughts and feelings and then waiting quietly before Him in His Word, allowing the Holy Spirit to speak to your heart through Scripture and a sense of His peace that surpasses understanding and is not dependent on your circumstances. This quiet time should be undistracted and unhurried, giving God space to speak. Kneeling before God physically demonstrates your willingness to humble yourself, though the posture of your heart matters more than the position of your body. If you cannot kneel due to physical limitations, a humble heart before God is what He values. Do not come to God with pride, as pride can prevent you from hearing what He has to say. Ask God specifically for wisdom and direction in the area where you need guidance.

### Step 3: Focus on Your Relationship with God Rather Than Just Seeking Information

God is not a system that dispenses answers on demand; He is your Father who desires an intimate, growing relationship with you. Instead of only bringing your specific decision to God, focus on deepening your relationship with Him. Ask Him to show you how to love Him, how to express your devotion, and how to walk in His ways. As your relationship with God grows richer and deeper through obedience and fellowship, His will for your life becomes clearer.

### Step 4: Observe Open and Closed Doors in Your Circumstances

God reveals His will through observable circumstances, including the opening and closing of opportunities such as job offers, relationships, ministry roles, or physical limitations that become clear. Always interpret circumstances in light of Scripture and the witness of the Holy Spirit, because not every closed door is from God and not every open door is God's will. Test every door against Scripture and the witness of the Holy Spirit before assuming it is God's direction for your life. When God closes a door, He is protecting you from a path that would be harmful or contrary to His purpose for your life. When He opens a door, He is extending an invitation. Do not be paralyzed by fear when you have genuinely surrendered your will to God. If you make a decision that turns out to be wrong despite sincere obedience, God will rescue you from further error by closing doors that would lead you deeper into wrong choices, bringing circumstances that redirect your path, or using His Word and the counsel of mature believers to bring you back to His will. Look for these specific signs of rescue: new doors closing that would have led to more harm, strong conviction when you read specific Scripture passages, or wise counsel from believers that redirects your thinking. Trust that God's reasoning and timing are far greater than your limited understanding, and submit your plans to His sovereign wisdom.

### Step 5: Seek Godly Counsel from Mature Believers

Allow God to use other believers in your life to confirm or clarify His direction for you through their counsel, wisdom, and occasionally through specific words they speak that align with what God is already showing you in His Word and through prayer. In Dr. Stanley's experience, this is a frequent way God confirms His will through ordinary conversations. Godly counsel frequently comes through ordinary conversations where the other person does not know the specific issue you are seeking guidance for, yet God uses their words to speak directly to your situation. In Dr. Stanley's experience, this is a common way God confirms His will, though it is not the only way God speaks. Verify any such word against Scripture and the inner witness of the Holy Spirit. God will sovereignly use a trusted believer - one who demonstrates Christlike character, knows God's Word, and walks in fellowship with Him - to say exactly what you need to hear at the moment you need to hear it, confirming His direction in your life. Such a word will always align with Scripture and the Spirit's witness, and you can verify it by asking the seven questions in this sermon. A trusted believer is someone you know to be prayerful, humble, and grounded in the Bible. Listen carefully to wisdom from mature believers - those who demonstrate Christlike character, know the Word of God deeply, and walk in close fellowship with Him - because God regularly speaks through their lives and words when you are seeking His will. Test their counsel against Scripture.

### Step 6: Apply Seven Verification Questions to Confirm God's Will

Before moving forward with any major decision, ask yourself these seven questions:
1. Is this consistent with the Word of God?
2. Is this a wise decision?
3. Can I confidently ask God to enable me to do this?
4. Do I have the witness of the Holy Spirit - a quiet, settled sense of peace and affirmation on the inside that is distinct from emotional excitement, wishful thinking, or anxiety? The Holy Spirit's witness will always align with Scripture and will not contradict God's character. Test this by comparing your peace against specific Bible passages: if your feeling of peace disappears when you read clear Scripture that contradicts the decision, it is not the Spirit's true witness.
5. Does this choice align with my identity as a child of God - my growing Christlike character, my spiritual gifts, and the values that define my life in Christ? Ask yourself if this choice builds up your walk with God or diminishes it. Specifically, ask if this choice helps you love God more, love others better, and grow in Christlikeness. A choice that contradicts who God is making you to be is not His will for your life.
6. Does this choice fit within the general trajectory of God's plan for my life, as I understand it from Scripture and His past faithfulness? This is not about knowing every future detail, but about confirming that this step is consistent with the direction God has already established in your life through His Word, His providence, and the witness of the Spirit.
7. Will this honor God?

### Step 7: Trust God to Rescue You from Wrong Decisions

God will rescue you from wrong decisions by closing doors that would lead you further into error, bringing circumstances that redirect your path, or using His Word and the counsel of mature believers to bring you back to His will. Look for these specific signs of rescue: new closed doors that block further error, strong conviction when you read specific Scripture passages, or wise counsel from mature believers that redirects your thinking. If you sincerely sought God and still made a wrong turn, He will not abandon you. Remain sensitive to the Holy Spirit's leading by staying in the Word and prayer, and be willing to adjust your course when circumstances, clear Scripture, or the inner conviction of the Spirit indicates you are moving away from God's will. The Spirit's conviction brings godly sorrow that leads to repentance and change, not despair or condemnation. If you are uncertain whether an inner feeling is from the Spirit, test it against Scripture and the seven verification questions. Daily obedience to the Spirit - expressed through loving God, loving others, sharing the gospel, and living with integrity - keeps your heart tender to His leading and makes you more receptive to His guidance in future decisions.

## Examples

### Example 1: Mission Field Calling That Violates Family

A woman came to Dr. Stanley believing God had called her to the mission field, but she planned to leave her husband and children to do so. This decision clearly violated God's Word regarding the priority of family commitments, as Scripture calls believers to love, provide for, and prioritize their household. No call from God can contradict His clear commands in Scripture. Even if God had called her to missions years earlier, He would never lead her to abandon her family. This example demonstrates that God's will never contradicts His Word. When a decision conflicts with clear biblical principles, it is not from God regardless of how strongly someone may feel about it.

### Example 2: Young Believer Discerning a Calling to Ministry

Dr. Charles Stanley shares that at age 12, after trusting Jesus Christ as his Savior, he immediately sensed that God had something specific for his life. Two years later, at age 14, he knew with absolute certainty that God wanted him to preach the gospel, and that certainty came through the inner witness of the Holy Spirit combined with the consistent affirmation of Scripture and godly mentors in his life. This certainty did not come from emotions or circumstances alone, but from the Spirit's witness aligned with God's Word. He never doubted this calling, and it has been the only thing he ever thought about doing. This example demonstrates that God can make His will clear even at a young age, and that an early, Spirit-given conviction about God's purpose can be trusted and followed throughout a lifetime of ministry.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Make reading and meditating on the Bible a daily habit so the Holy Spirit can use Scripture to shape your thinking and help you recognize God's principles. Meditate on passages that speak to your current situation and ask the Holy Spirit to illuminate their meaning for your life.
- ✅ Spend time in both speaking to God and listening to Him during prayer, including quiet meditation on His Word so He can speak to your heart. This means setting aside undistracted time to wait on the Lord rather than treating prayer as a monologue.
- ✅ Focus on deepening your relationship with God rather than only seeking specific answers
- ✅ Watch for open and closed doors as God's guidance in your circumstances
- ✅ Seek counsel from mature believers who know and walk in God's Word
- ✅ Apply all seven verification questions before making major decisions
- ✅ Trust God to redirect you if you make a wrong decision with a surrendered heart
- ❌ Do not treat God as a system that dispenses answers on demand
- ❌ Do not rely on emotional excitement, anxiety, or fleeting feelings alone as confirmation of God's will, because these states can be misleading, especially when they conflict with Scripture. Wait for the settled witness of the Holy Spirit that produces peace consistent with God's Word.
- ❌ Do not neglect reading doctrine and understanding who God is
- ❌ Do not make major decisions without comparing them against Scripture

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon provides spiritual guidance on discerning God's will, but it does not replace the need for personal relationship with God through Jesus Christ, regular prayer, and diligent study of Scripture.
- Stop and seek God personally through prayer and the Word if you feel uncertain about any guidance or direction in your life.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon provides spiritual principles for discerning God's will based on the teachings of Dr. Charles Stanley. It is intended for personal spiritual growth and reflection, not as a substitute for pastoral counseling, professional advice, or medical care.
- Spiritual discernment requires a personal relationship with God through faith in Jesus Christ, the indwelling presence of the Holy Spirit, and regular engagement with Scripture. These are the non-negotiable foundations for hearing God's voice.
- Do not make major life decisions based solely on this sermon or any single teaching. Seek God personally through prayer, Bible study, and obedience, and consult with mature believers who know you and the Bible.
- Always measure any perceived direction against the clear teachings of the Bible. If a perceived direction contradicts Scripture, it is not from God regardless of how strongly you feel about it or what circumstances seem to indicate.
- If you are struggling with mental health issues, addiction, or crisis situations, seek professional help immediately. God's will for your life includes caring for your physical and mental well-being.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Treating God as an information center and only coming to Him with specific questions instead of pursuing relationship.
  **Solution:** Shift your focus to spending quality time with God in prayer and the Word, asking Him to reveal Himself to you rather than just giving you answers.

- **Problem:** Confusing emotional excitement, anxiety, or wishful thinking with the settled witness of the Holy Spirit, which produces a steady, calm confidence that is consistent with Scripture and God's character, not just a temporary feeling. Test every impression against the seven verification questions in this sermon to avoid this pitfall. If your feelings shift with every new circumstance, they are likely not the Spirit's witness.
  **Solution:** Wait for a quiet, settled assurance from the Holy Spirit that is grounded in Scripture and consistent with God's character, and test it against the seven verification questions outlined in this sermon, rather than relying on fleeting emotions or manufactured feelings of peace that lack biblical foundation. A manufactured peace will not hold up when tested against God's Word, but the Spirit's witness will only grow stronger.

- **Problem:** Seeking God's will for a specific decision without first establishing the habit of walking in God's ways daily.
  **Solution:** Develop a consistent lifestyle of reading Scripture, praying, and walking in daily obedience to the Spirit - expressed through loving God, loving others, sharing the gospel, and living with integrity in your work and relationships - so that discerning God's will in major decisions becomes a natural outflow of your walk with Him rather than a frantic last-minute search for answers. Start small: obey God in the small decisions today so you can trust Him with the big decisions tomorrow.
