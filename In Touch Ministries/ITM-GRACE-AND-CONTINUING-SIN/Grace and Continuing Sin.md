# Grace and Continuing Sin

## Overview

This sermon addresses the struggle of repeating sin and the fear that God's forgiveness has limits. Dr. Stanley teaches that you cannot out-sin the grace of God because forgiveness is rooted in the cross of Jesus Christ, not in how many times you confess. The message explains that grace is God's undeserved favor poured out superabundantly through the blood of Jesus, and it does not give license to sin. Instead, true understanding of grace motivates thanksgiving, worship, and obedience.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you have confessed the same sin repeatedly and wonder if God is still listening
- When Satan whispers that you have exhausted God's patience and forgiveness
- When you are living under guilt instead of the reign of grace
- When you want to understand the true purpose of God's law and the cross
- When someone asks whether grace means they can keep sinning freely
- When you need to be reminded that Jesus paid your sin debt in full at Calvary

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize the Reality of Repeating Sin

Admit that every person struggles with the same sin returning over and over. Do not be deceived into thinking you are the only one. When you confess a sin and then commit it again, Satan will accuse you and ask how many times you think God will forgive you. This is a lie. All believers wrestle with this, and your honesty about it is the starting point for resting in grace.

### Step 2: Choose to Run to the Cross

When the guilt and weight of sin return, you have only two options. You can walk away and carry the burden, or you can run to the cross and remind God of His promise. Confess and repent, but do not trust in your feelings or your track record. Trust in what Jesus promised on the basis of His shed blood, not on the basis of how sincere you feel today.

### Step 3: Understand That Grace Is God's Provision Alone

Confession alone does not deal with sin. Repentance alone does not deal with sin. Only the blood of Jesus Christ at Calvary can forgive sin. Not even God can simply wipe away guilt without the payment being made, because He is faithful to His own law. Grace is God's provision for man's sin, and it is expressed only through the cross. Any teaching that omits the cross is not biblical.

### Step 4: Rest in the Truth That Grace Superabounds

Romans 5:20-21 states that where sin increased, grace superabounded all the more. This means no matter how much sin you have committed, no matter how many times, grace is always greater. You cannot out-sin the grace of God. If your sin weighs 2 pounds, grace weighs 100. Grace is infinite, matchless, and always ahead of your sin.

### Step 5: Do Not Take Grace as a License to Sin

Grace does not promote sin, and it does not motivate you to want to sin. God hates sin, and He expresses His love through divine discipline and chastisement when you stray. If you think grace gives you permission to sin freely, examine yourself because you may not be saved. True grace produces a desire to obey and walk in holiness.

### Step 6: Let Grace Motivate Thanksgiving and Obedience

When you truly grasp how much you have been forgiven, the natural response is thanksgiving, praise, worship, and a desire to be obedient. Grace does not leave you proud or complacent. It drives you to your face before Almighty God, thankful for the cross, and eager to live for Him. Focus your heart on God, fasten your soul to Him, and let Him keep you holy.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Woman Caught in Adultery

The Pharisees threw a woman caught in adultery at Jesus' feet and quoted the law that demanded she be stoned. Jesus did not say, "Guilty, shameful adulterer." He said, "Don't do that anymore." This shows that Jesus came not to condemn but to forgive and restore. Grace does not ignore sin; it calls us to a new life while removing its penalty through the cross.

### Example 2: Zacchaeus the Tax Collector

Zacchaeus was despised as a traitor who collected taxes for Rome from his own people. Jesus looked past his reputation and said, "Zacchaeus, come on down. I'm going home with you today." The religious leaders were furious, but Jesus loved the sinner. Grace reaches the most despised and welcomes them into relationship, not because they deserve it, but because grace is undeserved.

### Example 3: Peter's Question About Forgiveness

Peter asked Jesus how many times he should forgive his brother, suggesting seven times. Jesus answered, "Seventy times seven." He was not setting a limit of 490. He was saying there is no end to forgiveness. If God expects you to forgive without limitation, then God Himself will never limit His forgiveness toward you. You cannot out-sin the grace of God.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Confess and repent, but place your trust in the blood of Jesus, not in your own sincerity
- ✅ When Satan accuses you, run to the cross and remind God of His promise
- ✅ Understand that the law was given to expose sin, not to save you
- ✅ Let the reality of grace produce thanksgiving, worship, and obedience
- ✅ Examine yourself if you think grace gives you license to sin freely
- ✅ Root yourself in truth so you can distinguish biblical teaching from error

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace personal Bible study, prayer, or accountability within a local church.
- Stop and ask for clarification if any teaching conflicts with sound doctrine or causes confusion about the cross.
- God's grace does not eliminate the reality of divine discipline and chastisement for willful sin.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon addresses spiritual and theological matters and is not a substitute for professional mental health care.
- If you or someone you know is struggling with suicidal thoughts, seek immediate professional help and contact emergency services.
- Grace covers all sin, but continued unrepentant sin can bring destructive consequences in this life.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You confess the same sin repeatedly and Satan tells you God is no longer listening.
  **Solution:** Remember that forgiveness is based on the cross, not on how many times you have prayed. Run to the cross and remind God of His promise.

- **Problem:** You believe the law can save you or make you acceptable to God.
  **Solution:** The law was given to expose sin and drive you to Christ. Only the blood of Jesus can forgive sin and make you right with God.

- **Problem:** You think grace gives you license to sin freely.
  **Solution:** Grace does not promote sin. If you have that attitude, examine your salvation. True grace produces a desire to obey and walk in holiness.

- **Problem:** You carry guilt and shame instead of resting in God's forgiveness.
  **Solution:** The moment you received Christ, your sin debt was paid in full. Live under the reign of grace, not the reign of guilt.

- **Problem:** You take pride in your moral behavior and think you do not need God.
  **Solution:** Pride is what keeps people from Christ. Your flesh is unimproved and sinful. You need the cross regardless of how good you think you are.
