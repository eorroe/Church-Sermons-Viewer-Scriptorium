# Building Wise Friendships

## Overview

Dr. Charles Stanley teaches that genuine, loyal, and devoted friendships are among life's greatest assets but must be intentionally built rather than casually found. He explains that wise friendships are founded on mutual concern for one another, a desire to meet others' needs rather than being needy oneself, sacrificial love, openness and transparency, and governance by biblical principles. He illustrates this with examples from Scripture—Abraham, Moses, David, Daniel, and Jesus—and concludes by presenting friendship with Jesus Christ as the most important and eternal friendship a person can have.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel you lack deep, genuine friendships and want to understand why
- When you want to build wiser, more lasting relationships with others
- When you are preparing for marriage and need to evaluate relational health
- When you feel lonely or isolated and want to take practical steps toward connection
- When you desire to grow spiritually through accountable, godly friendships

## Steps

### Step 1: Evaluate the depth of your current friendships

Ask yourself how many of your friends are truly loyal, devoted, loving, genuine, and true. Recognize that casual friendships are common but do not provide the anchoring support that genuine friendships offer. Distinguish between many shallow connections and a few deep, lasting ones.

### Step 2: Commit to building rather than expecting friendships to happen

Understand that real, genuine friendships are not found—they are built. Accept that they require time, effort, and energy. Resolve to invest intentionally in relationships rather than waiting for them to develop passively.

### Step 3: Base friendships on mutual concern, not personal neediness

Ensure your desire for friendship is motivated by wanting to contribute to the other person's life, not by what you can get from them. Allow God to be your source of sufficiency so you are free to give rather than cling out of need.

### Step 4: Be willing to risk rejection and pain

Recognize that hurt, misunderstanding, and rejection are inevitable in any meaningful relationship. Commit to enduring these difficulties because the reward of a genuine, anchored friendship is worth the pain.

### Step 5: Love sacrificially

Practice giving without expecting anything in return. Be willing to lay down your own wants and wishes to please and support the other person. Do not insist on having your way even when you believe you are right.

### Step 6: Be open and transparent

Share your heart honestly, including your feelings, thoughts, and struggles. Allow genuine friends to confront you in love for your growth. Do not shut people out out of fear of rejection or further hurt.

### Step 7: Let Scripture govern the relationship

Decide from the beginning that the principles of God's Word will guide what you say, how you react, and how you conduct yourself. Build the friendship on a foundation of godly responsibility and genuine concern for the other person's spiritual growth.

### Step 8: Prioritize friendship with Jesus Christ above all others

Understand that your relationship with Jesus is the most important friendship you will ever have. Trust Him as your Savior so you can experience an eternal, unbreakable friendship that satisfies every gap in your heart.

## Examples

### Example 1: Biblical Examples of Wise Friendships

Abraham was called God's friend. Moses relied on Aaron and Joshua as intimate companions. David shared a covenant bond with Jonathan and had many loyal friends who would have died for him. Daniel's friends Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego risked their lives for him. Jesus had twelve disciples and three intimate friends—Peter, James, and John. The apostle Paul maintained deep friendships with fellow believers like Timothy and Luke.

### Example 2: The Danger of Neediness in Relationships

Dr. Stanley warns that building a friendship based on emotional, physical, financial, or material neediness leads to instability. He uses marriage as an example: no human being can fully meet all of another person's needs—only Jesus can. Neediness drives people away and causes relationships to fail.

### Example 3: Transparency and Risk

Dr. Stanley shares that he once tried to build friendships by opening his heart completely, only to find the other person never reciprocated. He explains that people who shut others out shut themselves into loneliness, and that real connection requires risking hurt and rejection.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Distinguish between casual friendships and genuine, devoted friendships
- ✅ Base relationships on mutual concern and a desire to serve the other person
- ✅ Allow God to meet your needs so you can give freely to others
- ✅ Be willing to risk rejection, pain, and misunderstanding
- ✅ Love sacrificially, putting the other person's needs above your own
- ✅ Be open, honest, and transparent about your heart and struggles
- ✅ Let biblical principles govern every aspect of the relationship
- ✅ Invest time and effort consistently, knowing true friendships take years to build
- ✅ Prioritize your friendship with Jesus Christ as the foundation for all other relationships
- ❌ Do not base friendships on what others can do for you rather than how you can serve them
- ❌ Do not avoid vulnerability out of fear; isolation only deepens loneliness

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace personal Bible study, prayer, or counsel from mature believers when making decisions about relationships.
- Stop and seek the Lord's guidance through prayer if you are unsure about the health or direction of a particular friendship.
- Wisdom in choosing friends: Be discerning about who you allow into your inner circle, ensuring they share your values and commitment to Christ.
- Guard your heart: The Bible teaches that out of the heart flow the issues of life; protect your emotional and spiritual well-being by cultivating relationships that honor God.
- Avoid unhealthy attachments: Be cautious of relationships that lead you away from God's principles or that exploit your vulnerability.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Having many shallow, casual friendships and expecting them to provide deep support during hardship.
  **Solution:** Focus on cultivating a small number of genuine, loyal friendships that can anchor you when life gets difficult.
- **Problem:** Building friendships based on personal neediness, emotional dependency, or what others can do for you.
  **Solution:** Allow God to be your source of sufficiency, and orient your friendships toward serving and meeting the needs of others.
- **Problem:** Avoiding vulnerability out of fear of rejection, hurt, or further pain.
  **Solution:** Recognize that transparency and risk are necessary for genuine connection, and that the reward of true friendship outweighs the pain.
- **Problem:** Expecting friendships to develop quickly without investing time and effort.
  **Solution:** Commit to the slow, intentional process of building trust, openness, and shared life over time.
