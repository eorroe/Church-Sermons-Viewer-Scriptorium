# Humility, The Obstacle

## Overview

This sermon teaches that pride is the primary obstacle to genuine humility in the life of a believer. Dr. Charles Stanley explains that pride is the most difficult sin to detect, the hardest to confess, and is the root cause of all other sins. Drawing from passages in 1 Peter 5, Proverbs 6, Proverbs 8, Isaiah 14, 1 John 2, and 2 Corinthians 12, the message makes clear that "God is opposed to the proud, but gives grace to the humble." The sermon calls every believer to humble themselves under the mighty hand of God and provides practical steps for identifying and removing pride from one's life.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When struggling with pride or self-exaltation in your personal life
- When feeling distant from God or sensing spiritual opposition
- When preparing for ministry or Christian leadership
- When going through seasons of success, giftedness, or recognition
- When seeking to grow in genuine humility and dependence on God
- When you sense that something is hindering your relationship with God

## Steps

### Step 1: Detect Pride in Your Life

Ask God to search your heart and reveal any evidence of pride. Be willing to honestly and openly examine yourself, asking God to show you pride in your possessions, appearance, relationships, position, or any other area. Knowing what is wrong is not enough—you must actively humble yourself under the mighty hand of God, recognizing His sovereignty and power over all things.

### Step 2: Ask a Trusted Friend to Confront You

Tell one or more of your closest, most trusted friends: "If you see any pride in me, I want you to show me. I want you to tell me." This requires genuine humility because most people will not tell you the truth for fear of your disfavor or rejection. True friendship means being willing to speak the truth in love, and true humility means being willing to receive it.

### Step 3: Recognize That God May Allow Hardship to Keep You Humble

If you have done all you can do and pride still lingers, understand that God may send or allow difficulty, hardship, pain, and suffering into your life to protect you from becoming prideful. As seen in Paul's thorn in the flesh, God knows exactly what is needed to keep you from exalting yourself, and He will not allow pride to continue indefinitely.

## Examples

### Example 1: Paul's Thorn in the Flesh

In 2 Corinthians 12:7, Paul speaks of a "thorn in the flesh, a messenger of Satan to torment me," which God allowed in his life specifically "to keep me from exalting myself." Paul had received surpassing revelations from God, and God knew that such spiritual prominence could easily lead to arrogance. Paul implored the Lord three times to remove it, but God's answer was: "My grace is sufficient for you, for power is perfected in weakness." Paul learned to boast in his weaknesses so that Christ's power would dwell in him. This demonstrates that God actively works—even through suffering—to protect His children from the sin of pride.

### Example 2: Satan's Fall Through Pride

In Isaiah 14:13-14, the passage describing Lucifer's fall is filled with the phrase "I will"—"I will ascend to heaven; I will raise my throne above the stars of God; I will make myself like the Most High God." Pride began with Satan in heaven, and it was pride that led to his being cast out. That same prideful spirit was passed to Adam and Eve in the Garden of Eden, when Satan told them they could be "like God, knowing good and evil." This shows that pride is the root of all rebellion against God, and every person is susceptible to it because it originates from the very being who first said "I will" above all else.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Ask God to search your heart and reveal any pride you cannot see in yourself
- ✅ Ask a trusted friend to honestly confront you if they observe pride in your life
- ✅ Humble yourself voluntarily under the mighty hand of God
- ✅ Cast all your anxieties on God, knowing He cares for you
- ✅ Remember that God gives grace to the humble
- ✅ Recognize that the more God has gifted you or blessed you with success, the more you must watch out for pride
- ❌ Don't assume that reading the Bible, praying, or confessing alone will make you humble
- ❌ Don't rely solely on your own assessment of your humility
- ❌ Don't respond defensively or with retaliation when someone confronts you about pride
- ❌ Don't think there is such a thing as "humble pride" or "prideful humility"

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon is a spiritual guide, not a replacement for personal self-examination, prayer, and the work of the Holy Spirit in your life.
- Pride is often invisible to the person who possesses it—what seems like confidence to you may appear as arrogance to others.
- Humility is not something you achieve through effort alone; it is a posture you choose before a sovereign, almighty God.
- God may allow pain, suffering, or hardship in your life not as punishment, but as protection from pride.
- No one is an expert on humility, and no one is a perfect example of it—all believers are subject to this sin.
- Only share your struggles with pride with people you genuinely trust and who have earned the right to speak into your life.
- If you feel unsafe or believe someone is using pride-confrontation as a weapon against you, step back and seek wise counsel.
- God's opposition to pride is an act of love and protection, not condemnation—He hates the sin, not the sinner.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing you are humble when pride is still present and unseen
  **Solution:** Ask a trusted, honest friend to evaluate you and be willing to receive their feedback without defensiveness
- **Problem:** Responding with retaliation when someone confronts you about pride
  **Solution:** Remember that true friends speak the truth in love, not to condemn you, but to help you grow
- **Problem:** Thinking that spiritual disciplines like Bible reading and prayer alone will remove pride
  **Solution:** True humility requires voluntarily submitting yourself under God's mighty hand—it is a choice, not a byproduct of religious activity
- **Problem:** Assuming pride only affects unbelievers or people in leadership
  **Solution:** Pride is the most common sin among believers and the root of all other sins; every person is subject to it regardless of their role or spiritual maturity
- **Problem:** Believing that God hates you when He opposes your pride
  **Solution:** God hates pride because He loves you and knows its destructive power—His opposition is an act of protection, not rejection
