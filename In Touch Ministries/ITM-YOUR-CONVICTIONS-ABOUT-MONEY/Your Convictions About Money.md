# Your Convictions About Money – Dr. Charles Stanley

## Overview

This sermon teaches believers to adopt God's perspective on money rather than the world's accumulation mindset. The world's accumulation mindset is the belief that the primary purpose of money is to gather as much as possible for personal security, pleasure, and future retirement, with no obligation to share. Dr. Stanley explains that God owns everything and calls Christians to be stewards who distribute rather than accumulate, emphasizing tithing as an act of obedience. The message warns that the love of money leads to destruction while faithful giving brings God's supernatural blessing, protection, and overflowing provision. Supernatural blessing means God providing beyond natural means; protection means God guarding your resources from unexpected loss; overflowing provision means receiving more than you need so you can freely give to others without anxiety.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need to understand what the Bible says about personal finances and money management
- When struggling with debt, financial anxiety, or the temptation to prioritize accumulation over generosity
- When considering whether to tithe or seeking to align your financial habits with biblical convictions
- When you want to build a lifestyle of generosity and trust in God's provision rather than self-reliance

## Steps

### Step 1: Acknowledge That God Owns Everything

Begin with the foundational truth from Psalm 24:1: "The earth is the Lord's, and all it contains, the world, and those who dwell in it." Recognize that everything you have—your income, possessions, and resources—belongs to God. You are not an owner but a steward, entrusted with what He has placed in your hands. This perspective shift is the starting point for every financial decision.

### Step 2: Identify Your Viewpoint — Accumulation or Distribution

Examine your belief system about money. The world's viewpoint is accumulation: "How much can I get? How much can I have? How much can I save for the future?" It is rooted in self-interest and promises no lasting security because money can be lost suddenly, relationships can break over finances, and self-reliance cannot protect against illness, job loss, or economic downturns.. God's viewpoint is distribution: "Give, give, give." Decide which worldview you are living by. Ask yourself honestly whether you are building a reservoir to keep everything or becoming a channel through which God's blessings flow to others. When you give generously, God uses your generosity to bless other people through your obedience, and you become the means by which His provision reaches others.

### Step 3: Commit to Tithing as an Act of Obedience

A tithe is ten percent of your income. Bring the whole tithe into the storehouse (your local church or to other ministries, missionaries, or individuals God specifically directs you to support through prayer and discernment). Malachi 3:10 commands: "Bring the whole tithe into the storehouse, that there may be food in My house. Test Me now in this, says the Lord of hosts, if I will not open for you the windows of heaven and pour out for you a blessing until it overflows." God's blessing will exceed your expectations and needs, resulting in surplus that enables you to give generously to others without anxiety. Tithing is not optional—it is obedience. It is not about the church's needs; it is about your submission to God.

### Step 4: Give Cheerfully and Trust God's Promise

Give because you love God and want to obey Him, not out of obligation or guilt. Trust that God will honor His Word: "Give, and it will be given to you. They will pour into your lap a good measure, pressed down, shaken together, and running over. For by your standard of measure it will be measured to you in return" (Luke 6:38). In Dr. Stanley's teaching, this means God provides for your needs—sometimes through increased resources, sometimes through unexpected opportunities, and always through His sustaining presence. The return includes both material provision for your needs and spiritual blessings such as peace, joy, and deeper trust in Him. You cannot out-give God. When you obey, He provides for your needs, often through unexpected sources or increased resources, and He guards what is necessary for your well-being, even when circumstances appear threatening..

### Step 5: Flee the Love of Money and Pursue Godliness

First Timothy 6:9–10 warns: "But those who want to get rich fall into temptation and a snare and many foolish and harmful desires which plunge men and women into ruin and destruction. For the love of money is a root of all sorts of evil." Instead of pursuing wealth, pursue "godliness, faith, love, perseverance and gentleness." In the context of finances, godliness means living according to God's standards; faith means trusting God for provision; love means caring for others' needs; perseverance means continuing to give even when it's difficult; gentleness means handling money with humility and without pride. Make the daily choice to say no to selfish gain and yes to a life of contentment, obedience, and trust in God's provision.

## Examples

### Example 1: Testing God Through the Tithe

Dr. Stanley shares from his own experience: from his first job earning four dollars a week, he gave God a dollar—not because he understood tithing, but because he was grateful. Over decades, he has seen God honor that obedience repeatedly. Dr. Stanley has personally witnessed many people in severe financial distress who began tithing in obedience, and he has never seen one person return to say the promise failed. This is not a theoretical guarantee; it is a promise God has repeatedly honored in the lives of those who trust and obey Him. Dr. Stanley teaches that faithful tithing is the starting point for getting out of debt because God promises to provide and protect when you obey, which creates the capacity to pay down what you owe. Even in deep debt, the starting point is obedience: bring the whole tithe, and watch God provide.

### Example 2: The Reservoir vs. the Channel

Dr. Stanley contrasts two images: a reservoir, which holds onto everything and never releases, and a channel, through which water flows freely to wherever it is needed. Those who hoard money solely for personal comfort, security, and future pleasure are like a stagnant reservoir—water that sits unused, grows stale, and never refreshes anyone. Setting aside savings for basic living expenses—such as food, shelter, transportation, and emergencies—is wise and responsible. However, continuing to accumulate wealth solely for personal comfort, luxury, or security while ignoring the needs of others and God's command to give becomes selfish accumulation. Those who give generously are like an open channel—God's blessings pass through them and reach others, and in the process, they are personally overflowing with provision, protection, and joy.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Give the whole tithe (10%) first, before paying other expenses—make it a priority, not an afterthought
- ✅ Give cheerfully and as an act of obedience to God, not as a transaction for personal gain
- ✅ Trust God to meet your needs when you obey, even if your circumstances say otherwise
- ✅ Teach your children about tithing early—line up ten pennies, set one aside for God, and let them choose the nine
- ✅ Read and meditate on God's Word about finances regularly so your convictions are shaped by Scripture, not the world
- ❌ Don't fall into the trap of thinking "I can't afford to tithe"—obedience comes before understanding
- ❌ Don't accumulate wealth for self-interest, believing it will bring security or happiness
- ❌ Don't love money—recognize that the desire for more can lead to temptation, ruin, and broken relationships

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace situation-specific financial advice from a qualified professional, pastoral counseling, or a careful review of your personal financial circumstances.
- Stop and pray for wisdom if you are unsure where or how much to give beyond the tithe, or if your circumstances make obedience feel impossible. Seek the Lord's direction through Scripture, prayer, and counsel from mature believers, and trust that He will guide your decisions.
- God's promises are personal and His ways are higher than yours—trust Him even when the math does not make sense.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Your financial decisions, including tithing, are between you and God. No one should pressure, manipulate, or shame you into giving.
- Be discerning about where you give. Ensure your giving goes to organizations or ministries that demonstrate financial transparency, biblical integrity, and a proven track record of using funds according to God's purposes, or to your local church where you can see the impact firsthand.
- If you are facing a financial crisis that threatens your ability to meet basic needs such as housing, food, or healthcare, seek wise counsel from a pastor or a financially responsible advisor who understands and applies biblical principles before making major decisions.
- Guard your heart against pride or self-righteousness because you give—generosity is about humility and dependence on God, not status.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing "I can't afford to tithe" and using debt or financial pressure as an excuse to disobey.
  **Solution:** Tithing is the first step toward financial freedom because God promises to provide and protect those who obey. As you trust Him with the tithe, He opens unexpected avenues of provision—such as new job opportunities, unexpected gifts, reduced expenses, or creative solutions—that give you the capacity to address your debts systematically. Dr. Stanley teaches that faithful tithing is the starting point for getting out of debt because God promises to provide and protect when you obey, which creates the capacity to pay down what you owe. Begin with the tithe and trust Him with the rest.

- **Problem:** Treating tithing as a church obligation rather than a personal act of obedience and worship.
  **Solution:** Reframe your giving as a response to God's ownership, not a duty to an organization. Ask, "Am I obeying God?" rather than "What will the church do with my money?"

- **Problem:** Falling into the love of money by constantly desiring more, which leads to temptation, broken relationships, and spiritual emptiness.
  **Solution:** Pursue godliness, faith, love, perseverance, and gentleness instead. In the context of finances, godliness means living according to God's standards; faith means trusting God for provision; love means caring for others' needs; perseverance means continuing to give even when it's difficult; gentleness means handling money with humility and without pride. Measure your wealth by your obedience and generosity, not your bank balance.

- **Problem:** Expecting immediate or material-only results from tithing, then becoming discouraged when blessings don't look like you expected.
  **Solution:** God's blessing includes peace, protection, provision, and joy—not always more money. Supernatural blessing means God providing beyond natural means; protection means God guarding your resources from unexpected loss; overflowing provision means receiving more than you need so you can freely give to others without anxiety. Trust His perfect timing and His definition of blessing, which includes peace in your heart, protection from harm, provision for your needs, joy in your circumstances, and the ability to bless others through your obedience.

- **Problem:** Thinking you are spiritually mature while neglecting the simple, clear command to tithe.
  **Solution:** If you can count to ten, you can tithe. Do not overcomplicate obedience. Return to the basics: God owns it all, and He asks for a tenth back.
