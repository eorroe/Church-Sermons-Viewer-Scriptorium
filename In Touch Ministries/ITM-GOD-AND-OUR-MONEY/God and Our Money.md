# God and Our Money

## Overview

This sermon by Dr. Charles Stanley addresses the spiritual dimension of personal finances, teaching that how we handle money is ultimately a matter of obedience to God rather than personal preference. Drawing from key passages in Proverbs 3, Malachi 3, 2 Corinthians 9, Psalm 50, 1 Chronicles 29, and Luke 6, Dr. Stanley makes the case that God owns everything, calls His people to tithe 10% of their income, and promises to bless and protect those who honor Him with their finances. The core message is that financial struggles often stem from disobedience to God's clear instructions about giving, and that trusting God with a tithe is an invitation to experience His faithfulness firsthand.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel anxious, guilty, or uncertain about your financial situation
- When you are questioning whether tithing is still relevant or required today
- When you are looking for biblical principles on money, stewardship, and giving
- When you want to teach your children about financial stewardship from a Christian perspective
- When you feel you have a "hole in your pocket" — earning but never seeming to have enough
- When facing a major financial decision such as buying a home and need to hear from God
- When you want to understand what the Bible actually says — not what culture says — about money

## Steps

### Step 1: Acknowledge That God Owns Everything

Recognize and confess that everything you have — your income, your job, your health, your home, your car — belongs to God. Psalm 50:12 states plainly: "The world is Mine, and all that is in it." You are not the owner of your resources; you are a steward, caretaker, or manager of what God has entrusted to you. Whatever income you earn comes from God's enabling, and it is His to direct.

### Step 2: Examine Your Heart About Your Finances

Before acting, honestly assess your feelings and attitudes toward your money. Ask yourself: Do I have a good feeling or a bad feeling when I think about my finances? Do I feel I have been treated fairly in my job? Am I making enough? Do I have a good excuse for not giving? God calls you to be honest with yourself about where your trust truly lies — in your own understanding or in Him.

### Step 3: Turn to God's Word — Not Man's Opinion

The critical question is not what people think about your money, but what God thinks. Read and study Proverbs 3:5-6: "Trust in the Lord with all your heart and do not lean on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge Him, and He will make your paths straight." Submit your financial decisions, attitudes, and plans to Scripture rather than cultural norms or personal reasoning.

### Step 4: Commit to Tithing — Giving 10% of Your Income

Make the decision to tithe — to set aside one-tenth of your income and give it to the Lord through your local church or wherever God leads. Malachi 3:10 commands: "Bring the whole tithe into the storehouse." This is not optional — it is obedience. God does not ask for 20% or 50%; He asks for 10%. Stack up ten pennies, set one aside for God, and keep nine. Envelope or automate your tithe so it is given first, not as an afterthought.

### Step 5: Give Cheerfully, Not Grudgingly

Your attitude in giving matters as much as the amount. 2 Corinthians 9:7 says: "Each one must do just as he has purposed in his heart, not grudgingly or under compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver." If giving feels like a burden or a loss, pray and ask God to change your heart. Giving should be an opportunity to partner with God, not a reluctant obligation.

### Step 6: Put God to the Test in Your Giving

Malachi 3:10 records God's invitation: "Test Me now in this, says the Lord of Hosts, if I will not open for you the windows of heaven and pour out for you a blessing until it overflows." God challenges you to try Him — to give faithfully and watch what He does. Trusting God with your finances begins with a step of obedience, not waiting until you feel secure enough to give.

### Step 7: Trust God for Protection Over What You Have

God promises to protect the finances of those who honor Him. Malachi 3:11 says: "I will rebuke the devourer for you, so that it will not destroy the fruits of your ground." Before making major purchases or financial decisions, listen for the Holy Spirit's inner warning or reservation. If you have a deep, uneasy feeling about a purchase you cannot afford — such as a home that stretches your budget — drop it. God desires to protect you from financial ruin and the stress of debt.

### Step 8: Teach Your Children About Tithing and Stewardship

Train your children early in the principle of tithing. Use physical pennies or coins to show them: stack ten, set one aside for God, keep nine. Talk openly about your finances in age-appropriate ways so they learn that everything belongs to God and that giving is an act of worship and obedience, not an optional extra.

### Step 9: Surrender Your Life to Christ if You Have Not Already

The sermon closes with a direct call to the unsaved: your greatest need is not financial — it is spiritual. You will one day give an account to God, and no amount of money can pay for your sin. Salvation comes through faith in Jesus Christ and His death on the cross. Accept Christ as your Savior today, and then bring your finances under His lordship as well.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Hole in Your Pocket (Haggai)

A person works hard, earns a good income, and yet money seems to disappear as quickly as it comes — a "hole in the pocket." According to Dr. Stanley, this is often a direct result of disobeying God in the area of tithing. The solution is not working harder or earning more; it is returning to obedience by tithing. When you honor God with the firstfruits of your income, He promises to protect and multiply what remains.

### Example 2: Buying a Home You Cannot Afford

You and your family fall in love with a home that is beyond your budget. You stretch to make the mortgage work and rationalize it away. The result: you never feel at rest in the home you worked so hard to get. Dr. Stanley teaches that God will not lead you into debt. If there is an inner reservation or unease about a financial decision, that is the Holy Spirit warning you. Honor that voice, drop the purchase, and trust that God has something better — something you can enjoy in peace.

### Example 3: Teaching a Child Through Ten Pennies

To teach a child the principle of tithing, line up ten pennies on a table. Ask the child which group they would choose: the one penny or the nine pennies. They will choose nine. Explain that God asks for one — the tithe — and promises to bless the nine. This simple, tangible lesson trains a child early to trust God with their money and to view giving as normal and expected.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Give the tithe (10%) first, before paying other expenses — make it a priority, not an afterthought
- ✅ Give cheerfully — examine your heart before giving and ask God to remove any resentment or reluctance
- ✅ Trust God's Word over your own financial fears or reasoning
- ✅ Teach your children about tithing early, using tangible, age-appropriate examples
- ✅ Listen to the Holy Spirit's inner warnings before making major financial decisions
- ✅ Study the specific passages mentioned: Proverbs 3:5-10, Malachi 3:8-12, 2 Corinthians 9:6-8, Luke 6:38
- ✅ Test God by tithing consistently and watching for His faithfulness over time
- ✅ Recognize that everything you have belongs to God — your job, health, home, and income are all from Him

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace personal Bible study, prayerful discernment, or wise financial counsel. Always seek God's specific direction for your unique situation through prayer and the Holy Spirit.
- Stop and ask for clarification if you are unsure what God is calling you to do in a specific financial decision. A vague sense of unease about a purchase should not be ignored.
- Tithing is a matter of the heart and obedience — it is not a formula for guaranteed material wealth. God blesses in many ways, not always financially.
- If you have never trusted Christ as your Savior, your greatest need is not financial advice — it is a relationship with Jesus. Address that first.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never give money in response to pressure, guilt, or manipulation — whether from a person, an organization, or even this sermon. Giving must always flow from a cheerful heart before God.
- Verify the legitimacy of any ministry or organization before giving financially beyond your local church. Make sure your giving supports work that honors God and aligns with biblical principles.
- Debt is not inherently sinful, but taking on debt you know you cannot afford — ignoring the Holy Spirit's warnings — is disobedience and can lead to serious financial harm.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Giving grudgingly or out of obligation, believing that any giving is enough
  **Solution:** Pray and ask God to soften your heart. 2 Corinthians 9:7 is clear: God loves a cheerful giver. The issue is not just the amount — it is the heart behind it.

- **Problem:** Believing "I can't afford to tithe" and using finances as an excuse
  **Solution:** Tithing is an act of trust, not a calculation of affordability. God challenges you to test Him — give the 10% and watch Him provide. The nine pennies left after tithing are blessed by God in ways the ten never would be.

- **Problem:** Leaning on your own understanding instead of trusting God with financial decisions
  **Solution:** Proverbs 3:5-6 calls you to trust in the Lord with all your heart and not lean on your own understanding. Before major purchases or financial moves, pause, pray, and ask God for clarity and peace.

- **Problem:** Thinking your money belongs to you to do with as you please
  **Solution:** Psalm 50:12 and 1 Chronicles 29:12 remind you that everything belongs to God. You are a steward, not an owner. Shift your mindset from "my money" to "God's money that He has entrusted to me."

- **Problem:** Assuming tithing is no longer relevant in modern times
  **Solution:** The principles of tithing predate the Law and are rooted in God's character. Tithing is not about legalism — it is about acknowledging God's ownership, trusting His provision, and participating in His work. The heart behind tithing is timeless.

- **Problem:** Making major financial decisions (like buying a home) that stretch far beyond your means, rationalizing that God will provide
  **Solution:** God will not lead you into reckless debt. If there is a deep inner reservation or unease about a purchase, that is the Holy Spirit warning you. Drop the decision, wait, and seek God's peace before moving forward.
