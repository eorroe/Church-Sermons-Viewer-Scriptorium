# Healing Our Hurts

## Overview

This sermon teaches how to deal with past hurts and break free from unforgiveness. Dr. Charles Stanley explains that unhandled hurts solidify in the mind like concrete and ultimately damage emotions, fellowship with God, and physical health. He provides clear, biblical steps for choosing forgiveness, even when the other person does not deserve it or is no longer available, and shows how the Holy Spirit enables believers to forgive through the power of Christ's own forgiveness.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When someone has hurt you and you cannot seem to move past it
- When you recognize bitterness, resentment, or hostility growing in your heart
- When your relationship with God feels distant or blocked
- When past pain keeps affecting your current relationships and emotions
- When you need a practical, biblical method to forgive someone who is unavailable or deceased

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize That Hurts Cannot Be Simply Forgotten

Acknowledge that the hurt happened. Do not deny it or suppress it. Hurts that are not dealt with properly will harm you in every aspect of life. What may have started as a small injury can solidify like concrete in your mind if left unhandled.

### Step 2: Understand the Damage of Unforgiveness

Unforgiveness harms you in three major areas:
- **Emotionally:** It freezes your capacity to love, be generous, and receive love from others.
- **Spiritually:** It erodes your fellowship with the Lord, making prayer and worship feel empty or forced.
- **Physically:** It can manifest as physical illness, chronic pain, or prolonged dependence on medication because the body eventually reacts to unresolved inner turmoil.

### Step 3: Choose to Forgive — No One Can Make You Unforgiving

No one else is responsible for your unforgiving spirit. Anger, malice, and resentment are choices you make in your emotional being. You must choose to lay down the debt that the other person owes you, regardless of how they respond or whether they deserve it.

### Step 4: Define What Forgiveness Is Not

Forgiveness is not:
- Justifying the other person's actions
- Forgetting what happened
- Tolerating or excusing the hurt
- Denying that the pain was real
- Assuming time alone will heal it

### Step 5: Define What Forgiveness Is

Forgiveness is deliberately and willfully laying aside the debt the other person owes you because of how they hurt you. You choose to no longer hold it against them. This does not guarantee the relationship will be perfectly restored, but it sets you free.

### Step 6: Use the Two-Chair Exercise for Unavailable or Deceased Offenders

If the person who hurt you is unavailable, distant, or deceased:
1. Set up two chairs.
2. Sit in one chair and place the other person in the second chair.
3. Express all your feelings, hurt, and anger honestly to the empty chair.
4. Declare forgiveness verbally: "Because the Lord Jesus Christ went to the cross and paid my sin-debt in full, and because He has continually forgiven me, I choose to forgive you for what you have done to me."
5. Thank God for enabling you to lay down that hurt.

### Step 7: Write and Burn a Letter (Alternative to the Two-Chair Exercise)

If you do not want to speak aloud:
1. Write a longhand letter expressing everything you feel.
2. Include your prayer of forgiveness in the letter.
3. Burn the letter.
4. With the burning of the letter, your anger, resentment, hostility, bitterness, and nurtured hurts are released.

### Step 8: Accept That the Other Person's Response Is Not Your Responsibility

You are not responsible for whether the other person changes, repents, or responds well. They will give an account to God for their own actions. Your responsibility is to forgive so that you can be free.

### Step 9: Trust the Holy Spirit for Ongoing Healing

When you trust Christ as your Savior, the Holy Spirit comes into your heart and enables you to forgive. He strengthens you, places a new love in your heart, and gives you a different perspective on the person who hurt you. Healing begins the moment you choose to forgive, and God will set you free from the harm those hurts could have caused.

## Examples

### Example 1: Forgiving an Abusive Parent

A person who was verbally, physically, or sexually abused by a parent may feel trapped by lifelong bitterness. They may try to say, "I love my mother" or "I love my father," while deep inside the hurt remains like a hidden cancer. The two-chair exercise allows them to honestly express their pain and then choose forgiveness based on Christ's forgiveness of them. The healing process begins even if the parent is deceased or unwilling to reconcile.

### Example 2: Letting Go of a Long-Held Grudge Against a Friend

A friend said something malicious years ago, and though you have tried to move on, the memory keeps resurfacing and coloring your current relationships. You write a letter detailing every wound, declare your forgiveness in the letter, and burn it. From that moment, the Holy Spirit begins to melt the frozen emotions and restore your capacity to trust and love again.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Face the hurt honestly instead of denying or suppressing it.
- ✅ Remember that forgiveness is a choice, not a feeling.
- ✅ Separate the act of forgiveness from the other person's response.
- ✅ Use the two-chair exercise or write-and-burn method for deep or long-held wounds.
- ✅ Thank God for the power and privilege of laying down the hurt.
- ✅ Allow the Holy Spirit to change your perspective over time.
- ❌ Do not justify or excuse what the other person did.
- ❌ Do not keep revisiting the hurt to feel righteous or justified.
- ❌ Do not expect the other person to change before you forgive.
- ❌ Do not confuse suppressing pain with healing.

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace professional counseling for severe trauma or abuse.
- Forgiveness does not always mean restoring the same level of relationship with the offender.
- Stop and ask for spiritual guidance if you are unsure whether you have truly dealt with a deep hurt.

## Security & Safety Notes

- The two-chair exercise and letter-burning are spiritual and emotional practices, not legal or therapeutic procedures.
- If you are in immediate danger or experiencing active abuse, seek professional help and contact appropriate authorities.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that time alone will heal a hurt without actively choosing forgiveness.
  **Solution:** Actively choose to lay down the debt through prayer and a concrete act of forgiveness.

- **Problem:** Thinking you must feel better before you can forgive.
  **Solution:** Forgiveness is a choice you make first; feelings often follow later as God heals you.

- **Problem:** Refusing to forgive because the other person has not apologized or changed.
  **Solution:** Your forgiveness is about your freedom, not their transformation.

- **Problem:** Using religion or good behavior to cover up unresolved resentment toward parents or authority figures.
  **Solution:** Honestly examine your heart and deal with hidden hurts through the two-chair exercise or journaling.

- **Problem:** letting physical symptoms become the only outlet for emotional pain.
  **Solution:** Address the root hurt spiritually and emotionally rather than relying solely on medication or distraction.
