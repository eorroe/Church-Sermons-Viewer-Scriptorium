# Solving Problems Through Prayer

## Overview

God has promised to be by your side and guide you in every situation. This sermon examines 2 Chronicles and the life of Jehoshaphat to show how God responds to the prayers of His people and always provides a way forward. It teaches that becoming a Christian does not mean the end of your problems, but it does mean you have a powerful asset in helping you solve them. Whatever problem you face, God is interested in it, He is greater than it, and He will get you through it if you seek Him first.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are facing a problem that seems insurmountable or impossible
- When you feel your problem is too small or you are too insignificant for God to care
- When you don't know what to do or where to turn in a difficult situation
- When prayer has become a last resort instead of a first response
- When you need a biblical pattern for responding to life's challenges

## Steps

### Step 1: Remember that God is interested in your problem

Believe that God is personally interested in everything that concerns you, no matter how small or large the problem may be. If you do not believe that God is interested, your prayers will not be effective because you will be speaking without faith. When you understand that God cares, you invite Him into the situation and set a direction for how you will deal with the issue.

### Step 2: Remember that God is greater than your problem

God is absolutely greater than any problem you face, regardless of how long it has been in your life or how impossible it seems. Nothing is too difficult for Him. When you focus on God's greatness, the problem begins to get smaller and smaller in your thinking. This is why Jehoshaphat's first response was to seek the Lord rather than to panic or rely on his own understanding.

### Step 3: Seek the Lord first

Make seeking the Lord your first response when a problem hits, not your last resort. Like Jehoshaphat, turn your attention to seek the LORD and proclaim a fast. When you seek Him first, your problems get stunted because God becomes larger and larger in your thinking. Don't wait until all other options fail before you pray. Instead, let the first word out of your mouth when trouble comes be "Father," inviting God with all of His supernatural power, love, and devotion into your situation.

### Step 4: Involve others when appropriate

God may want to involve other people in your prayers about your problems. Proclaim a fast and call upon others to seek the Lord with you, just as Jehoshaphat did with all of Judah. This strengthens the faith of those who pray and teaches them something about God as they watch Him work. When others see God answer prayer in your life, it builds their own faith for when they face problems.

### Step 5: Keep your prayer God-centered, not problem-centered

When you pray, focus on God's power, might, love, and what He has done in the past, rather than only on the details of your problem. Jehoshaphat's prayer was all about God: His power over all kingdoms, His past deliverance, and His willingness to act. The more you talk about God in prayer, the smaller the problem becomes because the Spirit of God resurrects the awareness and faith that often get buried under difficulty.

### Step 6: Trust that God will provide a solution

God will give you a solution to your problem. If He was willing to give His Son to die on the cross for your sins and forgive you, He will certainly give you an answer to your problems and enable you to solve them through His guidance and direction. God does not want to keep anything from you. He is not delaying because He doesn't care or because He doesn't intend to answer. Sometimes He delays because He is more interested in your becoming a godly person, learning to follow Him, trust Him, and walk in His ways than He is in simply solving your problem.

### Step 7: Step out in faith when God gives direction

God's solution usually requires an act of faith. When the prophet told Jehoshaphat that the battle was not his but God's, God gave them an unusual direction: assemble the choir, assemble the orchestra, array the army behind them, and march while singing praises to God. It took faith to march toward battle with singers instead of soldiers, but when they obeyed, God confused the enemy armies so thoroughly that they destroyed each other. When God tells you to do something that seems impossible, the victory is already won if you trust Him and obey.

### Step 8: Surrender to God's way and timing

Be willing for God to solve the problem His way and in His timing. You may not understand, you may not like the timing, but God knows exactly how to deal with your issues and problems. He loves you too much to give you what you want too quickly if you would miss what He wants to give you in return. God's solution is always the best one if you are willing to trust Him, because you don't know all the ins and outs, what happened before, or what the surroundings really are. You only know what you see and feel, and how limited we are.

## Examples

### Example 1: Jehoshaphat and the Battle of 2 Chronicles 20

Jehoshaphat was a good king, and God had blessed him and Judah. When he received news that three great armies were coming to destroy them, he was afraid, but his first response was to turn his attention to seek the LORD and proclaim a fast throughout all Judah. He called the people together to seek help from the LORD, and they gathered from the cities of Judah to fast and pray. Jehoshaphat stood in the assembly and prayed a God-centered prayer that acknowledged the LORD as ruler over all kingdoms, recalled God's past faithfulness, and admitted their powerlessness. God responded by giving them an unexpected battle plan: send the choir out first to sing and praise God while the army followed. As they marched and sang, God confused the enemy armies so that they turned on each other and destroyed themselves. When Judah arrived at the battle scene, all the enemy were already dead. This demonstrates every step of the pattern: seeking God first, involving others, keeping prayer God-centered, stepping out in faith, and surrendering to God's unusual solution.

### Example 2: Applying the Pattern to Modern Problems

When you face a major financial crisis, a health diagnosis, a broken relationship, or a career setback, the pattern works the same way. First, remind yourself that God is interested in your pain and heartache. Second, remind yourself that God is greater than this problem. Third, turn to God in prayer immediately rather than waiting until you have exhausted every other option. Fourth, ask trusted fellow believers to pray with you and for you. Fifth, frame your prayers around who God is and what He has done, not just on the details of your crisis. Sixth, expect that God has a solution, even if it is not what you expected. Seventh, when God gives you direction, step out in faith even if it seems counterintuitive. Eighth, surrender to God's timing and method, trusting that His solution is best even when you do not understand it.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Make seeking the Lord your first response when a problem arises, not your last resort
- ✅ Write down the eight sentences or pattern so you have a clear guide during difficult times
- ✅ Focus your prayers on God's character, power, and past faithfulness rather than only on your problem
- ✅ Involve other believers in prayer when the problem is large enough to affect others
- ✅ Trust that God's timing is perfect even when He delays the answer you want
- ✅ Step out in faith when God gives you direction, even if it seems impossible or unusual
- ✅ Surrender your will to God's way and timing

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace environment-specific validation, testing, or expert advice for practical problems. It is a spiritual framework for how to respond to difficulties through prayer and faith.
- Stop and ask God for clarity if you are unsure whether a direction is truly from Him or whether you should involve others in your prayer situation.
- The pattern taught here is for followers of Jesus Christ. The speaker clearly states that none of this will work for those who have not received Christ as Savior.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not share deeply personal or sensitive problem details with others unless you are certain they are trustworthy and godly.
- Be cautious about making major life decisions based solely on a subjective feeling that "God told me" without confirmation through Scripture, wise counsel, and circumstances aligned with God's Word.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Treating prayer as a last resort after trying every human solution first.
  **Solution:** Make prayer your first response, not your last. Turn to God immediately when trouble hits.
- **Problem:** Making prayers only problem-centered, constantly rehearsing the details and pain without focusing on God.
  **Solution:** Keep your prayers God-centered. Talk about His power, His love, and His past faithfulness. The more you focus on God, the smaller the problem becomes.
- **Problem:** Refusing to obey God's direction because it seems impossible or foolish.
  **Solution:** Trust that when God tells you to do something, the victory is already won. Obey in faith, just as Jehoshaphat's army marched behind the choir.
- **Problem:** Demanding that God solve the problem immediately and in the way you want.
  **Solution:** Surrender to God's way and timing. He loves you too much to give you what you want if you would miss what He wants to give you in return. Trust that His solution is always best.
- **Problem:** Believing that your problem is too small or that you are too insignificant for God to care.
  **Solution:** Remind yourself that God is personally interested in everything that concerns you. He is greater than your problem, and He will get you through it no matter what.
