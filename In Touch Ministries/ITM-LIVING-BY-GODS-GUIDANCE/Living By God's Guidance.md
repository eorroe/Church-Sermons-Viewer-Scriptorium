# Living By God's Guidance

## Overview

This sermon teaches that true stability, direction, and purpose in life come not from our feelings, emotions, or personal reasoning, but from a living, surrendered relationship with God through His Word and the indwelling Holy Spirit. Dr. Charles Stanley explains that every believer has been given the Holy Spirit as an internal compass, and that God's Word serves as the lamp to our feet and light to our path. The message challenges listeners to examine who or what is truly guiding their thinking, attitudes, desires, and daily decisions, and to commit to being led by God in every area of life through obedience, meditation on Scripture, and sensitivity to the Holy Spirit's voice.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel uncertain, unstable, or directionless in life
- When you are making a significant decision and need to discern God's will
- When you are facing opposition, persecution, or pressure at work or in relationships
- When you feel distant from God or sense that you have been living independently of Him
- When you want to grow in hearing and obeying the voice of the Holy Spirit
- When you are struggling with fear, anxiety, or the temptation to live by your emotions
- When you need encouragement to trust God's timing rather than rushing ahead on your own
- When you want to understand how to practically follow God's guidance day by day

## Steps

### Step 1: Meditate on the Word of God Daily

Make the Bible the primary source of your guidance and direction. Read it daily—even if it is just one verse or one chapter—and ask God to help you understand, remember, and apply what you have read. As Psalm 119:105 declares, "Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path." Meditating on God's Word means reading it slowly, thinking about it throughout the day, talking to God about it, and asking Him to embed it in your mind and heart. The Holy Spirit inside of you takes the Word and drives it to your mind, your heart, and your spirit to guide you throughout the day. Do not treat the Bible as something to open only when you are in trouble—make it a daily discipline.

### Step 2: Be Sensitive to the Leadership of the Holy Spirit

Recognize that the Holy Spirit lives within every believer and that one of His primary responsibilities is to guide you into all truth (John 16:13). The Spirit gives you direction, discernment, strength, energy, and protection every day. Cultivate a sensitivity to His voice by maintaining a surrendered heart. When the Spirit gives you a check—a clear inward sense that a direction is not right or that you should not do something—do exactly what He says. Do not argue with Him or demand an explanation. He is under no obligation to answer your "why." He is obligated to give you direction, guidance, and protection. Disobedience leads to suffering consequences.

### Step 3: Wait on God's Timing

Trust God's timing rather than rushing ahead on your own schedule. Isaiah 64:4 declares, "A God who acts in behalf of the one who waits for Him." Waiting on God is not passive—it is actively seeking Him through prayer, reading His Word, and surrendering your agenda. When you wake up, spend time in prayer and Scripture before launching into your day. Do not hurry off after reading a verse; ask God to help you remember it, apply it, and live by it. In a world of uncertainty, the one sure thing you have is today—and God's Spirit is with you right now to guide you through it.

### Step 4: Trust in the Lord with All Your Heart

Commit Proverbs 3:5-6 to memory and practice it daily: "Trust in the Lord with all your heart and do not lean on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge Him, and He will make your paths straight." Surrender your own wisdom, plans, and desires. Do not be wise in your own eyes. Trust God even when the path ahead is unclear, even when it seems illogical, and even when others are doing things differently. Acknowledge Him in every decision, every relationship, every financial choice, and every direction in life. He promises to make your paths straight.

### Step 5: Be Strong and Courageous in Obedience

Obedience to God often requires courage. When God tells you not to go somewhere, not to do something, or to take a direction that is difficult or unpopular, you must be strong enough to obey. Joshua 1:9 commands, "Have I not commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Do not tremble or be dismayed, for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go." Living by God's guidance means sometimes standing alone, going against the crowd, or choosing a harder path. God is with you in every step of obedience, and He will give you the strength you need.

### Step 6: Obey God Rather Than Men

When the world, your workplace, your friends, or even your family pressures you to go against what God has clearly shown you in His Word, choose to obey God. Acts 5:29 records the apostles' response to persecution: "We must obey God rather than men." The Holy Spirit within you is the voice of the Living God—follow His leading above all other influences. Do not yield to pressure from others when it contradicts the clear direction of Scripture and the witness of the Spirit in your heart.

### Step 7: Surrender Your Life to God and Trust Him with the Consequences

True guidance begins with surrender. Commit to leaving all consequences in God's hands and obeying Him regardless of the outcome. Ask yourself honestly: Am I willing to live by God's guidance and direction in my life, or am I just going to have it my way? If you are not willing to obey, you will not clearly hear His voice. God is committed to showing you what obedience is, but you must be committed to doing it.

## Examples

### Example 1: Israel in the Wilderness

The children of Israel were delivered from Egyptian bondage and should have reached the Promised Land in a few weeks. Instead, they wandered in the wilderness for 40 years because of their unbelief, fear, and disobedience. They had a visible guide—a cloud by day and a fire by night—yet they took their eyes off God, looked at the obstacles in front of them, and allowed their fear to overtake them. They backslid, missed the Promised Land, and died in the wilderness. Their story illustrates what happens when God's people choose to live by their feelings, fears, and own reasoning instead of by God's clear guidance and direction.

### Example 2: Joshua 1 – Strength and Courage to Follow God

When Joshua was commissioned to lead Israel into the Promised Land, God told him repeatedly to be strong and courageous, to meditate on the Word of God day and night, and to trust that God would be with him wherever he went. Dr. Stanley shares how his own mother gave him Joshua 1:9 to read before his first sermon, reminding him that God's presence and Word are the source of strength for every step of obedience. This passage teaches that God's guidance always comes with His empowering presence to do what He has called you to do.

### Example 3: Proverbs 3 – Trusting God Over Your Own Understanding

Proverbs 3:5-6 is presented as a guiding compass for every decision in life. The passage calls us to trust God with all our heart, to not lean on our own understanding, to acknowledge Him in all our ways, and promises that He will make our paths straight. Dr. Stanley illustrates this with the example of turning a key in a car ignition—you expect the engine to start. When you read and quote this passage daily, something turns on inside you: the awareness of the Holy Spirit who will guide you, enable you, empower you, and give you understanding for every circumstance of life.

### Example 4: Acts 5:29 – Obeying God in the Face of Opposition

Peter and the apostles faced persecution and threats from the religious authorities of their day. Their response was clear and unwavering: "We must obey God rather than men." This verse provides a biblical model for responding when your workplace, your culture, your friends, or even your family pressures you to compromise what you know God has called you to do. The Holy Spirit who lives in you is the same Spirit who empowered the apostles, and He will give you the courage and discernment to obey God in every circumstance.

### Example 5: Isaiah 64:4 and Waiting on God's Timing

Isaiah 64:4 reveals a heart of God that many miss: "A God who acts in behalf of the one who waits for Him." Dr. Stanley emphasizes that waiting on God is not passive inactivity—it is active dependence on Him through prayer and the Word. The Israelites' 40 years in the wilderness were not caused by God's absence but by their own impatience and disobedience. When we rush ahead without waiting for God's timing, we often step out of His will. When we wait on Him, He acts on our behalf in ways we cannot imagine.

### Example 6: Dr. Stanley's Personal Testimony of the Holy Spirit's Guidance

Dr. Stanley shares a personal example from his own week: he had a plan all laid out, but at the last minute, God gave him a "check in his spirit" that redirected his decision. This was not a matter of right or wrong, but of wise versus unwise. He illustrates that the Holy Spirit speaks to every believer personally—not audibly with human ears, but spiritually within the heart. When you are committed to being obedient, God is committed to showing you what obedience looks like in that specific moment.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Start every day in the Word of God, even if it is only one verse
- ✅ Meditate on Scripture throughout the day by thinking about it, talking to God about it, and asking Him to apply it
- ✅ Cultivate a heart committed to obedience so you can clearly hear the Holy Spirit's voice
- ✅ Trust God's timing and wait on Him rather than rushing ahead on your own
- ✅ Be strong and courageous when God calls you to a difficult or unpopular direction
- ✅ Obey God rather than yielding to pressure from people, culture, or circumstances
- ✅ Memorize key passages like Proverbs 3:5-6 and Joshua 1:9 and quote them daily
- ✅ Test every impression or direction against the Word of God
- ❌ Do not live by your feelings, emotions, or moods—they are unstable and unreliable guides
- ❌ Do not open the Bible only when you are in trouble; make it a daily discipline
- ❌ Do not argue with the Holy Spirit or demand explanations when He redirects you
- ❌ Do not yield to pressure from others when it contradicts what God has clearly shown you
- ❌ Do not believe that past mistakes or backsliding disqualify you from God's guidance and forgiveness
- ❌ Do not make decisions independently of God and then ask for His blessing afterward
- ❌ Do not assume that hearing God's voice is only for pastors or spiritual leaders—it is for every believer

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace personal Bible study, daily prayer, or wise counsel from mature believers. Discerning God's guidance is a spiritual practice that grows over time.
- Stop and ask for clarification from God or a trusted spiritual leader if you are unsure whether an impression or direction is truly from the Holy Spirit or from your own desires.
- The Holy Spirit never leads you in a direction that contradicts the clear teaching of Scripture. The more you know God's Word, the better you will be at recognizing His voice.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This message encourages personal spiritual responsibility and a growing relationship with God through Jesus Christ.
- If you have never trusted Jesus as your Savior, the sermon invites you to do so: confess your sins, acknowledge that Christ's death on the cross paid your sin debt in full, and accept Him by faith as your personal Savior.
- Those struggling with serious mental health concerns, addiction, or deep emotional pain should seek professional help alongside spiritual guidance and community support.
- Be cautious of any teaching or impression that contradicts the clear teaching of the Bible. God's Word is the final authority for all guidance and direction.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Living by feelings, emotions, or moods instead of by God's Word and Spirit
  **Solution:** Make a daily commitment to read the Bible and ask the Holy Spirit to guide your thinking and decisions before acting on how you feel.
- **Problem:** Closing the Bible and ignoring the Holy Spirit's voice until crisis strikes
  **Solution:** Establish a daily routine of Scripture meditation and prayer so that God's Word is already at work in your heart before you face difficult decisions.
- **Problem:** Disobeying a clear impression from the Holy Spirit and suffering unnecessary consequences
  **Solution:** When the Spirit gives you a check or redirects you, obey immediately—do not argue, delay, or demand an explanation. Trust that God's direction is for your protection and good.
- **Problem:** Relying on your own understanding instead of trusting God with all your heart
  **Solution:** Memorize and practice Proverbs 3:5-6. Before every significant decision, pause and acknowledge God, surrendering your own plans to His.
- **Problem:** Letting fear or the opinions of others prevent you from obeying God
  **Solution:** Remember Joshua 1:9—God is with you wherever you go. Be strong and courageous in obedience, trusting that His presence is enough.
- **Problem:** Yielding to worldly pressure and obeying people instead of God
  **Solution:** Stand firm on Acts 5:29: "We must obey God rather than men." The Holy Spirit within you is your guide and your defender.
- **Problem:** Believing that God does not speak to you personally or that you are not important enough to hear from Him
  **Solution:** Every believer has the Holy Spirit living within them (John 16:13). God is committed to guiding you. Ask, seek, and knock—He promises to answer (Matthew 7:7).
- **Problem:** Thinking that past mistakes or backsliding have permanently disqualified you from God's guidance
  **Solution:** God's forgiveness is complete and immediate when you confess and return to Him. Your past does not prevent God from leading you into His best for your future.
