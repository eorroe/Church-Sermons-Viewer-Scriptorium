# Our Rewards – Dr. Charles Stanley

## Overview

This sermon teaches that God faithfully rewards every act of service done by His children, whether done in this life or in eternity. Dr. Stanley anchors the message in Hebrews 6:10: "God is not unjust so as to forget your work and the love which you have shown toward His name, in having ministered and in still ministering to the saints." He clarifies a critical distinction: salvation is not a reward for good works—it is the free gift of God's grace received by faith. Rewards, however, are the result of our service and conduct after we are saved. The sermon outlines the nature of rewards (crowns, increased capacity to enjoy God's glory, and specific responsibilities in heaven), the method God uses to judge them (according to the light of truth we knew, the opportunities God gave us, and our response to those opportunities), and the fact that some rewards are received now while others are reserved for eternity. The message is a call to live with eternity in mind, avoiding the devil's trap of short-sighted thinking that wastes our time, talents, and opportunities.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel weary or discouraged in serving the Lord and need assurance that your labor is not in vain
- When you are confused about whether salvation can be earned by good works or whether rewards are the same as salvation
- When you want to understand what the Bible teaches about the judgment seat of Christ and how God evaluates a believer's life
- When you are tempted to serve for human applause, personal prestige, or self-promotion rather than for God's glory
- When you need motivation to invest your time, talents, and opportunities in eternal things rather than temporary pleasures or self-centered pursuits
- When you question whether small acts of service matter to God or whether only "big" ministry work counts
- When you want to understand the nature and variety of rewards God has prepared for those who serve Him faithfully

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand That Salvation and Rewards Are Separate

Clarify in your heart that salvation is not a reward for services rendered. It is the free gift of God given to all who trust in Jesus Christ as their personal Savior (Ephesians 2:8–9). Accept this by faith, and do not confuse entrance into heaven with earning rewards in heaven. Your acceptance before God is based solely on faith in the Lord Jesus Christ plus nothing. Once you are saved, God begins the process of rewarding your service, good conduct, and sacrificial acts done in His name. This distinction protects you from the burden of trying to earn salvation through works while also giving you hope that your service truly matters to God.

### Step 2: Rest in the Assurance That God Never Forgets Your Service

Memorize and believe Hebrews 6:10: "God is not unjust so as to forget your work and the love which you have shown toward His name, in having ministered and in still ministering to the saints." God's justice and righteousness are at stake in your rewards—He would not violate His own divine law by failing to reward those who serve Him. His omniscience means He knows every single act of service, every motive of the heart, and every sacrifice made, even those no one else ever sees. No amount of labor, no act of service, no good conduct will ever go unheeded or overlooked by God. When you feel unseen or unappreciated, return to this truth: God sees, God knows, and God remembers.

### Step 3: Serve With the Right Motive

Examine your motives for serving. God does not forget our actions, but He also does not forget our motives. Dr. Stanley teaches that whenever you and I serve the Lord or do anything in order to be seen of others, for our own glory, our own praise, our own promotion, or our own gain, Jesus said you have all the rewards you are going to get right there—and all the rewards you have is the praise of men (Matthew 6:1). This means you receive no eternal reward for service done in the wrong spirit. The right motive is love for God and genuine concern for people. When your motive is pure, God can reward you here and now, and He will reward you in the life to come. Serve as unto the Lord, not as unto men, because it is God working through you, doing His work through you for His glory and honor.

### Step 4: Recognize That Rewards Come Both Now and in Eternity

Reject the false assumption that all rewards are reserved for heaven. The Bible teaches that there are rewards that come now and rewards that come in the future. Rewards that come now can be either temporary and unfulfilling (like the praise of men when you serve to be seen) or genuine and lasting (such as the blessing, protection, and provision that come from walking obediently before God). Jesus said in Luke 14:14, "You will be blessed, since they do not have the means to repay you; for you will be repaid at the resurrection of the righteous." Live now with an eternal perspective, allowing your current obedience to shape your future reward.

### Step 5: Understand the Three Crowns and Their Significance

Study the specific rewards mentioned in Scripture so you know what God promises to those who serve faithfully. The incorruptible crown (1 Corinthians 9:24–27) is given to those whose heart's desire is to walk obediently before God and who, despite struggling and sometimes failing, keep pursuing a godly life. The crown of life (James 1:12) belongs to those who endure through troubles, trials, heartaches, difficulties, and persecutions without giving up or losing heart. The crown of righteousness (2 Timothy 4:8) belongs to those who love the appearing of Christ—those who want to walk godly and live godly in Christ Jesus. The crown of glory (1 Peter 5:4) belongs to those who faithfully feed the flock—pastors, Sunday school teachers, disciplers, and anyone who shares the Word of God with others. Each of these crowns represents not just honor but an increased capacity to serve and enjoy God for all eternity.

### Step 6: Live With the Judgment Seat of Christ in View

Know that you and I will all appear before the judgment seat of Christ (2 Corinthians 5:10), "that each one may be recompensed for his deeds in the body, according to what he has done, whether good or bad." This is not a judgment for sin—Christ has already paid that penalty. This is a judgment for rewards, where God examines our lives to reward us for the good things we have done. The "bad" here refers not to moral evil but to works that were good for nothing—things that amounted to nothing and had no value in the eyes of God, such as sermons or service done purely for self-promotion. Stand before God regularly with this reality in mind, asking Him to help you invest your life in things that will last.

### Step 7: Understand How God Will Judge Your Rewards

Know that God will judge you by three standards so that no two people are judged alike. First, He will judge according to the light of truth that you knew—you will not be held accountable for truth you were never given. Second, He will judge according to the opportunity God gave you—your spiritual gifts, your talents, your capacities, and the specific circumstances of your life. Third, He will judge according to your response to that opportunity—what you actually did with what you were given. Because of this, no one can compare their reward with anyone else's. The person who serves quietly in a Sunday school classroom and the person who preaches to thousands will both be rewarded, but according to the light they had and the opportunities they embraced.

### Step 8: Build Your Life Around Eternal, Not Temporal, Rewards

Resist the devil's trap of short-sighted thinking that focuses only on today and ignores eternity. The devil wants you to ask, "What suits me today?" because this life is brief—sixty-five, seventy-five, or a hundred years is nothing compared to eternity. Wisely invest your life in things that will survive the fire of God's judgment. When your life is tested by fire at the judgment seat of Christ, the gold, silver, and precious stones represent acts of service, godly conduct, and sacrificial love done with pure motives for God's glory. The wood, hay, and straw represent wasted time, unused talents, and selfish pursuits that amount to nothing in God's eyes. Ask yourself today: When I stand before the Lord, how much of my life will be gold, silver, and precious stone, and how much will be wood, hay, and straw?

### Step 9: Trust That God's Reward Is Far Beyond What You Can Imagine

Hold firmly to the truth that God is bringing His reward with Him (Revelation 22:12), and no one who has trusted Christ and served Him faithfully will be disappointed. The rewards of heaven will be absolutely overwhelming, far beyond anything you can currently understand or imagine. Even the person who comes to Christ late in life and has only a short season of service will enjoy heaven to the fullest capacity God has prepared for them. No one in heaven will be unhappy, and no one will wish they had more. But those who have lived faithfully, invested their talents, and served with pure motives will have a greater capacity to enjoy and manifest the glory of God for all eternity. Trust that the just and holy God will do the absolutely perfect, right thing by you when you stand before Him.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Reward for Ministering to the Saints

Consider the example of a Sunday school teacher or a small-group discipler who invests quietly in the lives of others, never seeking recognition or applause. According to Dr. Stanley's teaching, when you share the Word of God with another person, God says He is going to reward you. It does not matter whether you are a pastor preaching to thousands or a layperson mentoring one individual in their home—both are sharing the Word of God, and both will be rewarded by God. The person who faithfully teaches a class of three children for twenty years, praying for them, investing in them, and watching them grow in Christ, may have a greater reward than someone who preached to thousands but did so for personal glory. The key question is: What did you do with the opportunities, the spiritual gifts, and the truth that God gave you?

### Example 2: The Concert Analogy — Capacity to Enjoy Heaven

Dr. Stanley illustrates the nature of rewards with the analogy of a husband and wife at a concert. The wife loves classical music and is lifted into ecstasy by the performance. The husband, who grew up in the country and never heard a concert until he was 40 years old, sits there wishing it would end. They are both in the same place hearing the same music, but one has a great capacity to enjoy it and the other does not. So it will be in heaven. Some people will have a greater capacity to enjoy and manifest the glory of God because of how they lived their lives here—walking in obedience, studying His Word, serving faithfully, investing in others. Others will enjoy heaven to the fullest of their own capacity, but no one will be unhappy or regret the life they lived. This is not a motivation for comparison but a call to invest faithfully in the things that grow your capacity to know, love, and glorify God forever. Someone who serves God for 50 years will have a greater capacity than someone who serves for two, but both will enjoy heaven fully according to their capacity.

### Example 3: Gold, Silver, Precious Stones vs. Wood, Hay, and Straw

Imagine a believer who stands before the judgment seat of Christ after living a life of self-serving ambition. They spent their energy building a career for personal prestige, neglected their family, never served in the church, wasted their Sundays on selfish pursuits, and used their gifts only when it benefited them socially. Much of their life's work—the motivations behind it, the self-promotion, the time wasted on trivial things—will be like wood, hay, and straw: combustible, worthless, and burned up in the fire of God's judgment. But consider another believer who, though imperfect, made every effort to walk obediently, served faithfully in their church, invested in their children's faith, shared the gospel with neighbors, and gave generously even when it cost them something. Their life's work—motivated by love, marked by service, and directed toward God's glory—will be like gold, silver, and precious stones: enduring, valuable, and worthy of eternal reward. Dr. Stanley challenges every believer to honestly ask: If I died today and had to take my life as it is, how much of it would be gold and silver and precious stone, and how much would be wood, hay, and straw?

## Best Practices

- ✅ Rest in the assurance of Hebrews 6:10—God is not unjust; He will not forget your work or your love shown toward His name
- ✅ Serve others and minister to the saints as unto the Lord, remembering that God is working through you for His glory and honor
- ✅ Examine your motives regularly—serve out of love for God and genuine concern for people, not for human applause, self-promotion, or personal gain
- ✅ Invest in things that will last: your relationship with Christ, the Word of God, the lives of others, and the work of the gospel
- ✅ Recognize that every single act of service matters to God—no labor is too small, no sacrifice is unnoticed
- ✅ Live with eternity in view, making decisions today that you will be glad you made when you stand before Christ
- ✅ Study the crowns mentioned in Scripture and let them motivate you to walk obediently, endure faithfully, love Christ's appearing, and shepherd others
- ✅ Trust that God will judge you fairly according to the light you had, the opportunities He gave you, and how you responded
- ✅ Share the gospel and disciple others—every time you share the Word of God with someone, God will reward you
- ✅ Avoid the trap of short-sighted thinking that asks only "What suits me today?" and instead ask "What will matter for all eternity?"

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace personal Bible study, the guidance of the Holy Spirit, or the teaching of a committed local church. Each believer must prayerfully apply these truths to their own life under the Lordship of Christ.
- Stop and seek God's clarity if you are confused about whether your salvation is genuine. Salvation is by faith alone in Christ alone—it is not earned, maintained, or guaranteed by works. If you have trusted Jesus as your Savior, you are His child, and your rewards are separate from your salvation.
- Do not compare your rewards or your service to anyone else's. God will judge each person individually according to the light of truth they knew, the opportunities they had, and how they responded. Your race is yours alone to run.
- If you are facing seasons where you feel unable to serve actively—due to illness, caregiving, or other limitations—know that God sees your faithfulness in surrender, prayer, and trust, and these too are acts of service He rewards.
- The judgment seat of Christ is for believers only. If you have never trusted Jesus Christ as your personal Savior, this sermon's teaching on rewards does not apply to you—you need to be saved first, and you will face the Great White Throne judgment apart from Christ.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon teaches biblical doctrine about rewards and the Christian life. Do not use this teaching to manipulate, pressure, or shame others into serving or giving. Every person stands before God individually and must respond to Him freely.
- Beware of anyone who claims to know the exact nature, timing, or amount of your rewards. The Bible teaches that rewards are given by a just and holy God according to His own standards, not human measurement or prediction.
- Guard against pride. The teaching on rewards is not a ladder to climb over others but a call to humility—you are accountable to God for what He gave you, not for what someone else received.
- If you are a new believer or someone exploring Christianity, do not let the concept of rewards cause anxiety about your standing with God. Salvation is by grace through faith alone; rewards are the joyful overflow of a life lived for Him after you are already His child.
- Do not delay trusting Christ because you feel you need to "do more" first. The moment you place your faith in Jesus, you are saved—and from that moment on, God begins to work through you and reward your service.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that you must earn or maintain your salvation through good works and service.
  **Solution:** Salvation is a free gift of God's grace received by faith in Christ alone. It is not a reward for services rendered. Once you are saved by faith, God then rewards your service. Do not confuse the two. Your standing with God is based on what Christ has done, not on what you do.

- **Problem:** Serving God or doing good deeds primarily to be seen, praised, or promoted by other people.
  **Solution:** When you serve for human applause, Jesus said you have your reward in full—the praise of men—and there is no eternal reward for that service. Examine your motives honestly. Serve as unto the Lord, not as unto men. It is God working through you, not you working for your own glory.

- **Problem:** Feeling that your small, unseen acts of service do not matter to God.
  **Solution:** Dr. Stanley teaches that no act of service is too small. Whether you give someone a cold drink of water, help someone pick up something they dropped, or serve in a major ministry role, God will reward every single solitary act of service. He does not forget our work of love. Your faithfulness in the small things prepares you for greater things in eternity.

- **Problem:** Thinking that "just getting into heaven" is all that matters and that rewards are unimportant.
  **Solution:** Dr. Stanley challenges this directly: "That is not all that matters." Heaven will be far more glorious than most people imagine, and your capacity to enjoy and manifest God's glory for all eternity will be directly shaped by how you live today. Wise men and women live today with the future in mind. You cannot afford to waste your life, your time, your talents, or your testimony.

- **Problem:** Comparing your rewards, service, or spiritual progress to other believers and becoming discouraged or prideful.
  **Solution:** God judges each person individually according to the light of truth they had, the spiritual gifts and capacities He gave them, and the opportunities they had. No two people will be judged alike. Focus on being faithful with what God has given you, not on measuring yourself against someone else's race or calling.

- **Problem:** Falling into the devil's trap of short-sighted, self-centered thinking that wastes your life on temporary pleasures.
  **Solution:** The devil wants you to think only about today—"What suits me today?"—because 65, 75, or 100 years is nothing compared to eternity. Resist this trap by regularly asking: What am I doing now that will affect my reward for all eternity? Invest your time, talents, and opportunities in things that will last. Wise men and women make sure that when they die, their entrance into heaven is settled by faith in Christ, and they live today with the future in mind.
