# Before You Step Out of the Will of God

## Overview

This sermon, delivered by Dr. Charles Stanley, explains that God has a specific will, plan, and purpose for every individual's life, and that the Holy Spirit indwells believers to make that plan clear and provide the power to walk in it. Through biblical examples — Adam and Eve, the Israelites, Samson, David, Jonah, and Peter — the sermon demonstrates that stepping outside of God's will always carries inescapable consequences, regardless of who you are or what your circumstances may be. It closes with a clear call to confess sin, trust Jesus Christ as Savior, and choose daily to live surrendered to God's perfect will.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are facing a major decision and need clarity on God's will
- When you have been living in persistent disobedience or compromise and sense the cost of that choice
- When you feel the pull of temptation to take a path that contradicts what you know God has called you to
- When you want to understand the biblical consequences of ignoring God's plan for your life
- When you need assurance that God can forgive and restore you after you have strayed

## Steps

### Step 1: Acknowledge That God Has a Plan for Your Life

Understand that God has a specific purpose, plan, and will for your life individually. This is not borrowed from anyone else's life — it is uniquely yours. Recognize that His choice for you is the best, regardless of how your own desires may conflict with it.

### Step 2: Remember the Holy Spirit Lives Within You

If you are a believer, the Holy Spirit indwells you to make God's plan crystal clear and to enable you to follow it. Do not start your day without reading the Word of God and praying. Whatever you do, do it heartily "as for the Lord rather than for men" (Colossians 3:23), remembering that you are serving Jesus Christ and will give an account to Him.

### Step 3: Understand the Inescapable Law of Consequences

God's law of sowing and reaping applies to every person without partiality (Galatians 6:7; Colossians 3:25). "No partiality" means no one — regardless of occupation, status, or background — can escape the consequences of disobedience. "Be sure your sin will find you out" (Numbers 32:23). If you live outside the will of God, you will suffer the results; there are no exceptions.

### Step 4: Pause and Think Before Stepping Out

Before you step out of the will of God, think three times, not once. Pray. Turn to God. Do not allow fear, doubt, or the voice of others to override what you know God has called you to do. The Israelites refused to enter the Promised Land because they focused on the giants rather than God's promise, and an entire generation paid the price with forty years in the wilderness.

### Step 5: Learn From the Biblical Examples

Study the lives of those who stepped out of God's will:
- **Adam and Eve** had a perfect relationship with God and chose to listen to the wrong voice, losing their place in the Garden.
- **Samson** ignored his God-given calling and, in a moment of weakness, revealed his secret to Delilah, losing his strength and ending in prison.
- **David** chose to stay home when he should have been with his soldiers; one look at Bathsheba led to adultery and ruined his life.
- **Jonah** tried to flee from God's call to Nineveh and was swallowed by a great fish; even after being saved, his prejudice remained.
- **Peter** denied Jesus three times when a servant girl challenged him, forgetting the years of miracles he had witnessed.

Each of these examples shows that there is always a price for disobedience, and it is often far steeper than the cost of obedience.

### Step 6: Confess and Be Restored If You Have Sinned

If you have already stepped out of the will of God, confess your sin. God is faithful and just to forgive you and cleanse you from all unrighteousness (1 John 1:9). You cannot change the past, but you can change the direction of your life today. God is willing to pick you up where you are and lead you to where He wants you to be.

### Step 7: Respond to the Gospel if You Have Never Trusted Christ

If you have never trusted Jesus Christ as your personal Savior, respond today. You do not need to recount every sin — simply confess that you are a sinner and have disobeyed God, ask Him to forgive you, and choose to step into His will. "Whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved" (Romans 10:13).

## Examples

### Example 1: The Israelites Refusing to Enter Canaan

God had delivered Israel from Egypt, given them the Law, and prepared a land flowing with milk and honey for them. But when the twelve spies returned, ten of them focused on the giants in the land rather than God's promise. The people believed their report, refused to go in, and rebelled against God. As a result, an entire generation — everyone twenty years old and older — died in the wilderness over forty years. Their fear and doubt cost them the very blessing God had prepared for them.

### Example 2: Samson and Delilah

God gave Samson supernatural strength and a Nazirite vow that required him never to cut his hair. For years he defeated Israel's enemies. But Delilah persistently tempted him, and in a moment of weakness he revealed his secret. She had his hair cut, he lost his strength, was captured by the Philistines, blinded, and imprisoned. Even in his final act, God used him, but the cost of his compromise was losing his freedom, his sight, and his life.

### Example 3: David and Bathsheba

David, a man after God's own heart, chose to stay home from battle one night and saw Bathsheba bathing. Instead of turning away, he pursued an adulterous relationship with her and then arranged for her husband Uriah to be killed in battle. This one choice brought profound consequences to David's household and kingdom for the rest of his life, illustrating that no one is beyond the reach of temptation and its devastating cost.

### Example 4: Jonah Fleeing to Tarshish

God commanded Jonah to go to Nineveh and preach repentance. Because of his hatred for the Ninevites, Jonah boarded a ship headed in the opposite direction. God sent a violent storm, Jonah was thrown overboard, and he was swallowed by a great fish. Only when he was at the point of death did he cry out to God. After being delivered, he reluctantly went to Nineveh — yet even after the entire city repented, Jonah was angry that God had shown them mercy. His initial disobedience cost him physically and left a permanent mark on his heart.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Start every day with prayer and reading the Word of God so that you are anchored in His will before the day begins
- ✅ Work heartily in everything you do "as for the Lord rather than for men" (Colossians 3:23)
- ✅ Think and pray before making any major decision, especially when you sense you are being pulled away from what you know is right
- ✅ Confess your sin immediately when you realize you have stepped out of God's will — do not wait
- ✅ Trust that God's plan for your life is better than anything you could devise for yourself
- ✅ Remind yourself daily that you serve the Lord Jesus Christ and will give an account to Him
- ✅ If you have never trusted Christ as your Savior, do so today — confess your sin, ask for forgiveness, and receive Him by grace
- ❌ Do not allow fear, doubt, or the voice of others to override what you know God has called you to do
- ❌ Do not delay responding to the gospel — today is the day of salvation
- ❌ Do not assume that being successful in some areas of life means you are living in God's will
- ❌ Do not compare your walk with others; God's plan for your life is uniquely yours

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace personal Bible study, prayerful discernment, and wise counsel when seeking God's will for specific decisions in your life. Stop and seek clarification through prayer and Scripture if you are unsure whether a choice aligns with God's will.
- God's will is made known individually — you cannot look at another person's life and determine God's plan for yours.
- Forgiveness is available through Jesus Christ, but forgiveness does not always remove the earthly consequences of disobedience.
- Even after forgiveness, you may still carry reminders of past disobedience (as Jonah did with his time in the great fish).

## Security & Safety Notes

- The forgiveness offered through Jesus Christ is available to all who confess their sin and call upon His name — no one is beyond the reach of God's grace.
- Do not delay responding to the gospel. Today is the day of salvation.
- Confession of sin requires honesty before God — you cannot pretend or minimize disobedience and expect true restoration.
- Be cautious of those who may offer you a "better" path than the one God has called you to. Not every voice that sounds reasonable is speaking truth.
- Guard your heart against compromise in small things, because small compromises often lead to much larger departures from God's will.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that your own plan is better than God's plan because it seems more enjoyable or brings more pleasure.
  **Solution:** Remember that God's choice for your life is always the best, and the temporary pleasure of disobedience is never worth the eternal and often temporal cost.

- **Problem:** Comparing your life to others and wondering why God blesses them differently.
  **Solution:** God's ways are not partial. Each person stands before Him accountable for their own choices. Focus on being faithful in your own walk, not on the outcomes you see in others' lives.

- **Problem:** Allowing fear of the unknown or fear of difficulty to keep you from obeying God's call.
  **Solution:** The Israelites missed their inheritance because they focused on the giants rather than God's promise. Trust that God's omnipotence, omniscience, and omnipresence go with you into whatever He has called you to do.

- **Problem:** Thinking you can escape God's call or will by physically removing yourself from a situation.
  **Solution:** Jonah's flight to Tarshish demonstrates that you cannot outrun God. He will bring you to the point where you must face His will — the question is whether it will be in obedience or through painful correction.

- **Problem:** Believing that forgiveness means there will be no lasting consequences for past disobedience.
  **Solution:** While God forgives truly and completely, the earthly consequences of sin often remain. Choose today to walk in God's will so that you do not have to pay that price.

- **Problem:** Assuming that being successful in some areas of life means you are living in God's will.
  **Solution:** You can be successful in certain areas and still be living outside God's will. True fulfillment and blessing come only from a life surrendered to Him, not from worldly achievement alone.
