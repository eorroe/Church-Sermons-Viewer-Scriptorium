# When We Are Fearful

## Overview

Dr. Charles Stanley teaches that fear and anxiety are universal struggles rooted in negative internal thought patterns—mental habits such as catastrophic thinking, self-doubt, and replaying past failures—rather than external circumstances. He explains how childhood experiences, self-imposed standards, guilt, and "tape recorder" messages from the past create hidden fears that distort judgment, lead to procrastination, and hinder everyday functioning. Through the lens of Isaiah 41, Dr. Stanley provides a biblical framework for recognizing these fears, rejecting them in Jesus' name, and anchoring oneself in God's promises to find true peace and security.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel overwhelmed by fear, anxiety, or worry about the future
- When past experiences or negative self-talk are preventing you from pursuing opportunities
- When you need to refocus your attention from threatening circumstances to God's faithfulness

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize and Identify the Fear

Stop and honestly acknowledge that you are anxious or afraid. Then ask yourself what triggered this feeling—consider all possible sources such as a phone call, something you saw, a memory, a thought, or an interaction with another person. Identifying the root cause is essential because unresolved fears lurk beneath the surface and erupt when triggered by circumstances.

### Step 2: Ensure Your Relationship with Jesus Christ Is Right

If you have not accepted Jesus Christ as your personal Savior, you have every reason to live with fear and anxiety. This means acknowledging that you have sinned, asking Jesus Christ to forgive your sins, and trusting in His death and resurrection to save you. Receiving Him by faith in this way brings lasting security through the forgiveness of sins and the assurance of being kept by God's grace—not by your own performance. This foundational relationship with Christ is the primary source of lasting peace.

### Step 3: Identify and Tear Up the "Tape Recorder"

Every person carries an internal "tape recorder" filled with messages from childhood, parents, teachers, and life experiences—both good and harmful. Once you identify the anxiety, examine your thoughts to locate the specific message on that tape that is causing distress. Then actively reject that message by saying aloud: "I don't have to play that tape anymore." Realize that the next time it tries to play, you can say, "I tore that up. I'm not listening to that message."

### Step 4: Rebuke the Spirit of Fear

When anxiety hits suddenly like a cloud, exercise your authority as a child of God by verbally declaring your faith in Jesus Christ. Say aloud: "Father, I thank You that this is not from You because You have not given me a spirit of fear. I rebuke this in the name of Jesus Christ. I reject this feeling and refuse it from this moment on." Speaking these words aloud is an act of trusting God and resisting the enemy's attempt to make you fearful.

### Step 5: Reach for the Anchor in Isaiah 41

Memorize Isaiah 41:10 so you can recall it when fear arises: "Do not fear, for I am with you; do not anxiously look about you, for I am your God. I will strengthen you, surely I will help you, surely I will uphold you with My righteous right hand." When fear arises, recite this verse aloud and personally apply it to your situation by declaring that God is with you, that He is your God, and that He will strengthen and help you.

### Step 6: Refocus Your Attention on God

Anxiety grows when you anxiously look around at your circumstances. Instead, shift your focus from what you see to what God has said. Do this by recalling and meditating on God's promises in Scripture, such as Isaiah 41:10, and by choosing to trust that God is in control even when your circumstances feel threatening. As you practice this shift in focus, fears and anxieties will diminish because you are placing your trust in the living Word of God rather than in temporary, changing circumstances.

## Examples

### Example 1: Dr. Stanley's "Tape Recorder" from Childhood

Dr. Stanley shared that his mother's constant warnings—"Don't get hurt, don't play in the street, don't work too hard, don't fail God"—created an internal tape that played throughout his life, teaching him to avoid risk and operate out of fear. While her motives were loving, the messages left him struggling with anxiety until he learned to identify and reject those tapes. He illustrates how childhood programming can produce lifelong fear patterns that must be confronted and replaced with God's truth.

### Example 2: The Isaiah 41 Anchor

Dr. Stanley points to Isaiah 41:10 as an anchor text that every believer should know and claim when facing fear or anxiety. He breaks down the passage phrase by phrase: God is with you at every moment, He is your God (not your circumstances), He will strengthen you, help you, and uphold you with His righteous right hand. This anchor shifts your focus from terrifying unknowns to the faithful, omnipotent presence of God, causing anxiety to diminish.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Recognize and honestly name your fears instead of denying them
- ✅ Identify the specific trigger or "tape" causing the anxiety
- ✅ Ensure your relationship with Jesus Christ is personal and secure
- ✅ Memorize Scripture like Isaiah 41:10 to use as an anchor when fear arises
- ✅ Rebuke anxious thoughts in Jesus' name and refuse to accept them
- ❌ Don't deny that you're fearful or anxious—even pastors and mature Christians face fear
- ❌ Don't anxiously look around at circumstances, because the more you focus on them, the more fearful you become
- ❌ Don't listen to old tape recorder messages from the past or accept them as truth
- ❌ Don't rely on your own strength or performance to overcome anxiety—rest in God's grace

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon is provided for personal spiritual growth and reflection and does not constitute professional counseling, mental health treatment, or medical advice. Seek qualified professional help if you are experiencing severe or persistent anxiety—such as anxiety that significantly interferes with daily life, relationships, or work, or that continues for weeks despite self-help efforts.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trying to overcome fear without first identifying the root cause or "tape" behind it
  **Solution:** Pause when you feel anxious and ask, "What triggered this? What old message is playing?" Trace it to its specific source before trying to fix it.

- **Problem:** Focusing on circumstances instead of God, which only amplifies anxiety
  **Solution:** Immediately reach for your Scripture anchor—Isaiah 41:10—and verbally refocus your attention on God's presence and promises.

- **Problem:** Living in denial about fear because of pride, ministry role, or spiritual maturity
  **Solution:** Humbly acknowledge your fear; recognizing it is the first step to overcoming it. Even the apostle Paul and Dr. Stanley experienced anxiety.

- **Problem:** Listening to old "tape recorder" messages from childhood and accepting them as truth
  **Solution:** Identify the specific message, trace its origin, and actively reject it: "I don't have to play that tape anymore. I tear it up and refuse to listen."
