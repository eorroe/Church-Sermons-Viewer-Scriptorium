# Making Wise Decisions

## Overview

Dr. Charles Stanley delivers a practical, Scripture-based message on making decisions that honor God. Drawing from over 50 years of personal experience and passages such as Psalm 25:12 and Proverbs 3:5-6, Dr. Stanley presents a clear seven-step plan for seeking and receiving God's direction. The central promise is that God is willing to give you direction every single time you ask, though He may not answer in the way, timing, or manner you expect. The sermon closes with a call to the most important decision any person can make: accepting the Lord Jesus Christ as personal Savior and Lord.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When facing a major life decision and desire clarity from the Lord
- When you feel pressured by friends, family, or your own fears
- When you have prayed but have not yet received an answer
- When you want to ensure your decisions are rooted in Scripture rather than emotion or opinion
- When you are ready to surrender your own desires and seek God's will above all else

## Steps

### Step 1: Clear Your Heart of Any Known Sin
**Clear your heart of any known sin.** Ask the Lord to reveal anything that would keep you from hearing the truth. Confess and repent of sin, turning away from it. Sin introduces confusion in our minds and hearts and hinders our capacity to hear and receive from God, because sin and the wisdom and knowledge of God do not fit together. If you want to clear out all the static and noise that goes on in the mind when making decisions, you begin with cleaning your heart of any known sin.

### Step 2: Bring Your Desires to a Position of Neutrality
**Bring your desires to a position of neutrality.** Say "Lord, I want what you want" even if you do not fully mean it at first. Be willing to accept God's will over your own desires. True neutrality means being willing to say, "I really don't know what to do, but I want to know." When you bring your mind and heart toward neutrality, God will begin His work in your life. The first time you say this you may not fully mean it, but as you persist, God will shape your heart to truly desire His will.

### Step 3: Exercise Patience
**Exercise patience.** Wait upon God with confident expectation that He has heard you and will answer. Do not run ahead trying to solve problems yourself. God's pauses are part of His plan. Waiting upon God is not simply marking time; it is living in a confident expectation and anticipation of His actions on your behalf. It is refusing to run ahead of Him trying to solve your problems for yourself. God knows where the emergencies are in your life, where the answer must come now, and when you are not ready for the answer. His delays are purposeful, teaching you to trust Him when you do not have any evidence that He has heard anything.

### Step 4: Be Alert to Pressure
**Be alert to pressure.** Watch for external pressure from friends and internal pressure from your own fears. Trust God more than anyone else's advice. There are two types of pressure: external pressure from your friends, and internal pressure from your own fears of loss and similar concerns. Even your dearest friends may make a mistake because they love you and may want to please you and make you happy, but what you need is to know the will of God. You must be careful about those pressures and trust God above all others.

### Step 5: Persist in Prayer
**Persist in prayer.** Pray continuously without ceasing. Do not stop praying when you do not get an immediate answer. God teaches us to trust Him through delays. First Thessalonians 5:17 tells us to pray without ceasing. That means you do not stop praying because you do not have an answer. When God does not answer the way you expect, He is teaching you to trust Him when you have no evidence that He has heard anything. Prayer is the greatest gift God has ever given us apart from salvation.

### Step 6: Rest in God's Promise
**Rest in God's promise.** Trust that God will hear and answer your prayer. The Bible is full of wisdom and promises. Let God's Word filter your thinking and desires. The Bible is the most valuable resource we have in making decisions because it is full of wisdom and it is the mind of God in print. God has given us promise after promise that He will hear and answer our prayer if we trust Him, be patient, and wait upon Him.

### Step 7: Wait for His Peace
**Wait for His peace.** Let the peace of Christ rule in your heart. When you have His peace, you know you have the answer. This is God's assurance about the decision. The Greek word for this peace means to bind together — the peace of God binds you and Him together about this decision, and therefore you can trust Him. Colossians 3:15 says, "Let the peace of Christ rule in your hearts." When you have His peace, you know that you have the answer. That is God's grace, giving you absolute assurance about the decision.

## Examples

- A pastor preparing for Sunday worship waits days for a sermon, receives no direction, and then opens his Bible to Proverbs 3:5-6. God meets him in that moment and gives him a message he had never planned to preach.
- A person senses pressure from friends to choose one path, but instead waits on God and discovers that the internal pressure of fear was the real obstacle.
- Someone prays without ceasing for weeks, trusting that God's delay is purposeful, and eventually receives the peace of Christ that confirms the right direction.

## Best Practices

- Begin every decision-making process by inviting the Holy Spirit to search your heart for known sin.
- Be honest with God about your true desires, then surrender them to His sovereignty.
- Write down what waiting on God means: it is not passive mark time, but active, confident expectation that He will act.
- Surround yourself with people who will point you to God's Word, not simply agree with what you want to hear.
- Keep a record of God's promises in Scripture and return to them regularly as you wait for direction.
- Treat the peace of Christ as the final confirmation, not your own feelings or opinions.

## Keep In Mind

- God may not answer in the way, timing, or manner you expect, but He will answer.
- Sin clouds the mind and confuses judgment; a clean heart positions you to hear the truth.
- True neutrality of desire takes time and is often a process, not a single moment.
- God's delays are not denials. He uses waiting to prepare your heart for the answer.
- Pressure from others, even those closest to you, can pull you away from God's will.
- Persistence in prayer is not nagging God; it is trusting Him enough to keep coming before Him.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon is a spiritual teaching intended for personal edification and faith-based decision making.
- Do not use this sermon as a substitute for professional, medical, legal, or financial counsel where such expertise is required.
- If you or someone you know is in crisis or emergency, seek immediate help from qualified professionals or emergency services.
- Guard against using spiritual decision-making language to justify harmful choices or to pressure others into decisions that are not their own.

## Common Pitfalls

- Stopping prayer when an answer does not come quickly, rather than persisting without ceasing
- Allowing sin to remain unconfessed, which hinders the ability to hear from God clearly
- Seeking advice from people who will simply affirm your own desires instead of pointing you to God's will
- Running ahead of God and trying to solve the problem yourself instead of waiting in confident expectation
- Mistaking internal pressure, anxiety, or fear for God's direction
- Giving up on neutrality and demanding your own way rather than submitting to God's plan
- Forgetting that the peace of Christ is the true confirmation of God's will, not merely the absence of conflict
