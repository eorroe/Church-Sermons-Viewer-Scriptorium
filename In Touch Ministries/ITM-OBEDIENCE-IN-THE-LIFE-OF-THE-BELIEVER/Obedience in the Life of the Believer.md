# Obedience in the Life of the Believer

## Overview

This sermon teaches that obedience is a defining mark of the Christian life and that true obedience means doing what God says, how He says it, and when He says it. Dr. Charles Stanley explains that partial obedience is disobedience, that rebellion is the opposite of obedience, and that love for God is demonstrated through keeping His commandments. The message also addresses how the Holy Spirit convicts and enables believers to live obediently, and it calls listeners to surrender their lives to Christ as the starting point of a life of obedience.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need to understand what biblical obedience actually requires beyond surface-level compliance
- When wrestling with whether partial obedience or substituting other activities for God's specific call is acceptable
- When seeking to renew your daily commitment to live under Christ's authority and guidance
- When you want to understand the role of the Holy Spirit in convicting and empowering obedient living

## Steps

### Step 1: Define Obedience Clearly

Understand that obedience means doing what God says, how He says to do it, and when He says to do it. You cannot eliminate any one of these three elements. Partial obedience—such as giving 8 percent when God calls for 10 percent, or substituting Sunday school teaching for a missionary call—is still disobedience. Anything that imitates obedience without fulfilling God's specific instruction falls short.

### Step 2: Examine Your Motives for Obedience

Ask yourself why you obey. True obedience should flow from reverence for who God is—the holy, sovereign Creator who gave His Son to die for your sins—and from a genuine desire to please Him. Reflect on whether you love God enough to obey when it is inconvenient, unpopular, or costly. If you find yourself making excuses or delaying obedience, recognize this as a warning sign of misplaced priorities.

### Step 3: Respond to the Holy Spirit's Conviction

When the Holy Spirit convicts you of sin—whether through your conscience, Scripture, or a sermon like this one—confess it immediately, repent, and turn away from it. Do not delay by saying, "I'll deal with that later." The Spirit never sleeps and will continue to bring conviction until you respond. If you need to make restitution with someone you have wronged, do so as part of your obedience.

### Step 4: Start Each Day Surrendered to God's Guidance

Begin each morning by asking God to guide your thoughts, words, and actions. Instead of assuming you will naturally do what is right, intentionally ask Him to help you be sensitive to the people you meet and the situations you face. When temptation arises, remind yourself that obedience is the key and that God's commands are designed to protect you, not restrict you.

## Examples

### Example 1: Tithing as a Test of Full Obedience

A person may reason that giving 8 percent instead of the biblical 10 percent is acceptable because it is more than most people give. However, if God's instruction is to bring a full tithe, giving less than that is not obedience—it is partial obedience, which this sermon defines as disobedience. The issue is not the amount of money but the willingness to follow God's specific instruction completely.

### Example 2: Substituting Activity for Obedience

Someone may feel called by God to be a missionary but choose instead to make money and give it to the church, believing this is "as good as preaching." Dr. Stanley states that this is an act of absolute disobedience to God. You cannot pay God off or substitute your own good works for the specific call He has placed on your life. Teaching a thousand Sunday school classes does not replace obeying a direct call to preach the gospel.

### Example 3: The Marriage Analogy

If you tell your spouse you love them but continue in immoral behavior, your words contradict your actions. The sermon uses this illustration to show that claiming to love God while knowingly and unrepentantly disobeying Him is equally contradictory. True love for God produces genuine obedience, not merely verbal profession.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Start each morning by asking God to help you be obedient, rather than assuming you will naturally do what is right.
- ✅ Respond immediately to the Holy Spirit's conviction by confessing, repenting, and making restitution if needed.
- ✅ Test your actions against the three-part standard: does this match what God said, how He said it, and when He said to do it?
- ✅ Examine whether your obedience flows from reverence for God and a desire to please Him, not from guilt, habit, or social pressure.
- ❌ Do not delay obedience by saying, "I'll deal with that later"—this leads to generational patterns of unresolved sin and broken relationships.
- ❌ Do not substitute financial giving, religious activity, or moral behavior for the specific thing God has called you to do.
- ❌ Do not redefine God's commands to fit your preferences; if God says no, He means no, and His boundaries are for your protection.

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace personal Bible study, pastoral counseling, or community accountability. Use it as a teaching tool alongside regular engagement with Scripture and wise Christian mentors.
- Stop and ask for clarification if you are uncertain whether a specific decision is a direct command from God or a matter of personal conviction. Not every choice in life is a test of obedience, and applying this message to non-moral preferences can lead to legalism.
- If you are struggling with a pattern of repeated disobedience, seek out a trusted pastor or mature believer who can walk with you toward lasting change. You do not have to fight this battle alone.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon is a spiritual teaching resource. It is not a substitute for professional mental health care, crisis intervention, or legal advice.
- If you are in an unsafe situation—such as abuse, addiction, or imminent harm—contact appropriate authorities and professional help immediately. Spiritual obedience does not require you to remain in dangerous circumstances.
- Do not use this sermon to judge, condemn, or manipulate others. The message is directed at personal examination and response to God, not at policing the behavior of other people.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that partial obedience is acceptable because it is "better than nothing."
  **Solution:** Accept that partial obedience is still disobedience. Commit to following God's instructions completely rather than negotiating with Him about what parts you will keep.

- **Problem:** Using religious activity to avoid a specific call from God.
  **Solution:** Recognize that good works, generosity, or church involvement cannot replace direct obedience to a specific command. Ask God to reveal any area where you are substituting activity for obedience.

- **Problem:** Delaying confession and repentance, assuming you can deal with sin later.
  **Solution:** Respond to the Holy Spirit's conviction immediately. The longer you delay, the deeper the habit becomes and the more people you may hurt. Confession and repentance are always available, but they are meant to be timely.

- **Problem:** Relying on your own strength to obey rather than depending on the Holy Spirit.
  **Solution:** Remember that the Holy Spirit indwells every believer, convicts of sin, and enables you to do what is right. When you feel unable to obey, ask the Spirit for power and guidance rather than trying to change through sheer willpower.

- **Problem:** Assuming that because some people appear to "get away with" disobedience, there are no real consequences.
  **Solution:** Trust that God's justice is certain even when human consequences are not immediately visible. Disobedience always carries a penalty, whether seen now or accounted for in eternity. Choose obedience because it is right, not because it is easy or immediately rewarded.
