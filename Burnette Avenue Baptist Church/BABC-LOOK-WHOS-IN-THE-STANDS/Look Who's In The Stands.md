# Look Who's In The Stands

## Overview

This sermon, based on Hebrews 12:1-3, encourages believers to run the Christian race with patience by stripping off every weight and sin that hinders progress. It emphasizes keeping eyes fixed on Jesus, the author and finisher of faith, while recognizing the great cloud of witnesses — those who have run the race before us — who inspire and encourage us to finish well. The message reminds us that the race is not over, that we must not look back once we start, and that Jesus is the winner who helps us finish.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need to overcome distractions or obstacles slowing your spiritual progress
- When working through seasons of fatigue or discouragement in your faith journey
- When a believer asks about perseverance, endurance, or staying focused on Christ

## Steps

### Step 1: Strip Off Every Weight and Sin

Conduct a personal inventory by setting aside 15–30 minutes for prayer and reflection. Ask God to reveal specific sins (e.g., dishonesty, unkindness, impurity) and weights (e.g., excessive entertainment, unhealthy relationships, bad habits) that hinder your spiritual progress. Write these down, confess them to God, and take concrete steps to remove them—such as deleting triggering content, setting boundaries, or seeking accountability from a trusted believer. Hebrews 12:1 calls believers to lay aside every weight and the sin that so easily entangles.

### Step 2: Run the Race Set Before You

Identify the specific spiritual race God has placed before you by reflecting on your current circumstances, gifts, and opportunities. This might be raising your children in faith, serving in a particular ministry, or pursuing a specific calling. Make a daily commitment to run this race by setting aside time for spiritual disciplines and intentionally saying "no" to distractions that pull you away. Do not compare your race to someone else's. Focus on the course God has laid out for your life.

### Step 3: Keep Your Eyes on Jesus

Practice fixing your gaze on Jesus throughout the day by starting your morning with Scripture and prayer about His character and sacrifice. When challenges arise, pause and verbally remind yourself of Jesus' faithfulness and the cross. Memorize key verses like Hebrews 12:2–3 and recite them when your focus drifts. He is the example of perfect faith who endured the cross for the joy set before Him.

### Step 4: Look Around at the Great Cloud of Witnesses

Create a "cloud of witnesses" list by writing down 3–5 specific believers who have run the race well—such as a grandparent, a pastor, or a historical figure like Corrie ten Boom. Read their stories or biographies, and when you feel discouraged, recall how they persevered. Consider writing their testimonies in a journal to review regularly. Note: This step is for encouragement, not comparison. Their examples should point you to Jesus, not away from your own race.

### Step 5: Do Not Look Back

When memories of past failures or regrets arise, practice the "stop and redirect" method: acknowledge the thought, then immediately turn your focus to a specific Bible verse, a prayer of repentance, or a concrete action step for today. Do not dwell on past mistakes; instead, ask God to redeem them and use them for your growth. Looking back drains your focus and slows your pace. Keep your attention forward on the spiritual work God has called you to do.

### Step 6: Remember the Race Is Not Over

Remain engaged in your spiritual race by completing the specific tasks God has given you today. This might include loving your family, serving at church, sharing your faith, or pursuing a calling. Do not quit before you cross the finish line—persevere through difficulty by remembering that Jesus is with you. There is still ministry to complete, people to love, and race left to run. Do not grow weary or quit before you cross the finish line.

## Examples

### Example 1: Aunt Hattie and Frank Thomas

The sermon references faithful believers from the preacher's own congregation history, like Aunt Hattie and Frank Thomas, who ran their race well. Their lives serve as witnesses encouraging the current generation to keep going.

### Example 2: Rosa Parks and Martin Luther King Jr.

Historical figures like Rosa Parks and Martin Luther King Jr. are cited as examples of those who endured hardship and kept their eyes on a greater purpose. Their witness shows how perseverance through difficulty can advance God's kingdom. Just as they pressed forward in the civil rights movement with unwavering resolve, believers are called to run the spiritual race with fixed eyes on Jesus, enduring hardship as part of the journey toward the ultimate victory He has already secured.

### Example 3: Three Generations Present

The preacher notes that three generations are present in the congregation: those who can be seen in the sanctuary, the unborn future generations, and those who have passed on. This illustrates the great cloud of witnesses — both living and deceased — and reminds believers that their race impacts generations past, present, and future. The "present" generation refers to those spiritually present as witnesses and participants in the congregation's ongoing story of faith, not merely those physically seated in the room.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Regularly examine your life to identify weights and sins that hinder your race
- ✅ Keep your focus on Jesus daily through prayer and Scripture
- ✅ Learn from the examples of faithful believers who have run before you
- ✅ Press forward without looking back at past mistakes or failures
- ✅ Stay engaged in ministry while the race is still before you
- ❌ Do not compare your spiritual race to someone else's journey
- ❌ Do not look back at past failures and become discouraged
- ❌ Do not carry weights and sins that you know hinder your progress

## Keep In Mind

- The Christian race is not a sprint but a marathon requiring patience
- You are surrounded by a great cloud of witnesses even if you cannot see them
- Jesus has already won the victory; He is helping you finish
- Division within the church — whether from denominational splits, theological arguments, or personal conflicts — is temporary; the race continues forward
- Your finish matters to generations you may never meet

## Spiritual Application Guidance

- This sermon is meant for personal spiritual encouragement and discipleship
- Apply these principles with humility, remembering that growth comes through grace, not self-condemnation
- Seek accountability and community support when working through personal challenges
- Focus on healthy spiritual growth rather than legalism or striving in your own strength

## Common Challenges in the Christian Race

- **Challenge:** Looking back at past failures and becoming discouraged
  **Guidance:** Keep your eyes forward on Jesus and press toward the mark He has set before you
- **Challenge:** Comparing your race to someone else's journey
  **Guidance:** Run the race God has set before you with patience, trusting His unique plan for your life
- **Challenge:** Carrying weights and sins that hinder progress
  **Guidance:** Consciously strip them off through confession, repentance, and accountability with trusted believers
- **Challenge:** Losing focus during long seasons of difficulty
  **Guidance:** Recall the great cloud of witnesses and remember Jesus is with you, strengthening you
- **Challenge:** Quitting before the race is finished
  **Guidance:** Remember that Jesus is the author and finisher of your faith, and He will see you through to the end
