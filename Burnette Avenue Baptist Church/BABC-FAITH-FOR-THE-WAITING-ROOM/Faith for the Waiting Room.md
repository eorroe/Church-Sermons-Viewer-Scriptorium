# Faith for the Waiting Room

## Overview

This sermon explores the challenging but necessary experience of waiting on God, using Psalm 40 as its foundation. Rev Dr George Parks teaches that waiting is not passive inactivity but an active expression of trust that positions us for God's rescue, restoration, and inspiration. The message emphasizes that while waiting can be mentally frustrating and emotionally draining, it produces perseverance, deepens our reliance on God, and results in a transformed life that gives glory to Him.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are waiting on a doctor's report, test result, or medical diagnosis
- When you are waiting for a breakthrough in your career, education, or personal dreams
- When you feel stuck in a difficult situation with no clear way out
- When you are tempted to take matters into your own hands instead of trusting God's timing
- When you need encouragement that God has not forgotten you

## Steps

### Step 1: Wait Patiently on the Lord

Commit to waiting on God rather than rushing ahead with your own solutions. David declared, "I waited patiently for the Lord," and the original language emphasizes the persistence of his wait—not merely how long he waited, but who he waited on. Make God your only option and refuse to grasp at every opportunity that presents itself. Recognize that leaving God's waiting room early can usher you out of the will of God.

### Step 2: Trust That God Will Come Close

Believe that God responds to your faithfulness even when you cannot feel His presence. The sermon teaches that God is not nonchalant or aloof; when you are faithful in waiting, He will incline His ear to your cry. Trust that He knows how to interpret your deepest groanings when you lack the words to pray. His closeness is not determined by your emotions but by His unwavering love and faithfulness.

### Step 3: Receive God's Rescue and Restoration

Expect God to lift you out of the pit of destruction and set your feet upon a solid rock. The waiting room may feel like a miry bog—dangerous, dark, and difficult—but God is not repulsed by your condition. He will rescue you completely, not partially, and He will make your steps secure. This rescue is not for your vain glory but so that others may see what the Lord has done and put their trust in Him.

### Step 4: Allow God to Inspire You With a New Song

After God rescues you, give Him thanks for what He has already done. David declared that God put a new song in his mouth—a song of praise to our God. Stop only shouting about what God is about to do and learn to praise Him for what He has already accomplished. Look back at your life and remember His past faithfulness. God will put a melody in your mouth for the lyrics your heart has written through the waiting.

## Examples

### Example 1: David's Deliverance From the Pit

David likely wrote Psalm 40 while on the run from King Saul or during his son Absalom's rebellion. He described being trapped in a pit—a place of danger and near death—yet God drew him out and set him on a firm rock. This illustrates how God can rescue us from situations that seem inescapable and transform our pain into a testimony that encourages others to trust the Lord.

### Example 2: Horatio Spafford's "It Is Well With My Soul"

Horatio Spafford experienced devastating loss—financial ruin from the Chicago Fire and the death of his four daughters in a shipwreck. After receiving the telegram that his wife survived alone, he could have become bitter. Instead, he waited on God and wrote the hymn "It Is Well With My Soul." His story demonstrates that waiting on God produces a song of praise even in the midst of overwhelming sorrow.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Wait on God patiently and persistently, making Him your only option
- ✅ Trust that God is close to you even when you cannot sense His presence
- ✅ Look back at past deliverances to strengthen your faith for current waiting
- ✅ Praise God for what He has already done, not only what He will do
- ✅ Allow your testimony to point others to Christ rather than seeking personal glory
- ❌ Do not leave God's waiting room early by grasping at shortcuts or ungodly solutions
- ❌ Do not compare your waiting timeline with others' timelines
- ❌ Do not rely on human efforts alone when God is calling you to wait on Him
- ❌ Do not demand that God act according to your preferred method or timing

## Keep In Mind

- Waiting on God is an active trust, not passive resignation
- God's waiting room is not always comfortable, but it is necessary for your growth
- God may use your waiting to make you a better servant, spouse, parent, or leader
- God saves completely and thoroughly—He will not leave you half-rescued
- Your waiting is not in vain; it is producing something eternal in you
- There are some things that only God can do, and He will do them in His perfect timing

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not take every hand that is extended to you while waiting—not every offer of help is from God
- Remain in God's will even when painful or uncomfortable alternatives seem easier
- Guard your heart against bitterness and despair during prolonged waiting periods
- Share your struggles only with trusted, godly counsel who will point you back to Christ
- Remember that God is a better Father than any earthly father and He will interpret your tears correctly

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trying to escape God's waiting room by taking matters into your own hands
  **Solution:** Rest in the truth that God's timing is perfect and His ways are higher than yours. Refuse to settle for solutions that compromise your faith or take you outside of God's will.

- **Problem:** Believing that God has forgotten you or abandoned you during long seasons of waiting
  **Solution:** Recall David's testimony and your own history of God's faithfulness. God is never aloof or nonchalant toward His children; He responds to unwavering trust with divine intervention.

- **Problem:** Focusing only on future blessings and failing to praise God for past and present faithfulness
  **Solution:** Develop a habit of looking back at what God has already done in your life. Let gratitude for past deliverances fuel your trust for current and future waiting.

- **Problem:** Comparing your waiting season with someone else's apparent breakthrough
  **Solution:** Understand that God's timing is personal and purposeful. Your waiting is not an indication of God's absence but an invitation to deeper trust and dependence on Him alone.
