# The Secret

## Overview

This sermon teaches that genuine contentment in life is guaranteed not by the success of our resources but by the strength of our source. Drawing from Philippians 4:10-14, Rev. Dr. Charlie E. Dates explains that the "secret" Paul learned in prison was not a formula for getting more, but a revelation of finding sufficiency in Christ alone. The message confronts the lie that money, prestige, sex, or power can satisfy the human soul, and instead points believers to the faithfulness of God, the refining work of uncertainty, and the indwelling presence of Jesus Christ as the true foundation of lasting joy and contentment.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are struggling with dissatisfaction despite having material success
- When you are going through financial hardship, loss, or uncertainty
- When you feel pressure to pursue more money, status, or possessions to be happy
- When you have been through valleys, trauma, or loss and need encouragement that God is still faithful
- When you need clarity on the proper use of Philippians 4:13
- When you want to understand the difference between resources and the source
- When you are tempted to believe that positive thinking or "name it and claim it" theology is the key to a better life

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Lie of Secular Humanism and Pop-Culture Theology

Identify and reject the false promise that you can think yourself into better circumstances. The world offers books like "The Secret" and church cliches like "name it and claim it" or "blab it and grab it," but these place the burden of blessing on human effort rather than divine grace. Ten years after reading The Secret, the preacher notes, none of the readers were any richer or thinner than before. Judas had money, David had power, King Saul had prestige, and Nebuchadnezzar had authority—none of these resources satisfied them. Acknowledge that external resources alone cannot bring contentment.

### Step 2: Stop Confusing Resources with the Source

Make a clear distinction between what God provides and the means He uses to provide it. Your paycheck comes from your job, but your living comes from the Lord. Your entertainment may come from your verse, but your happiness comes from Jesus Christ. You may buy medicine at Walgreens or CVS, but your health is sustained by the Lord. Write down three areas where you have been relying on resources rather than the Source, and consciously redirect your trust to God alone. If you have the Source, resources will keep on coming.

### Step 3: Find Your Contentment in the Faithfulness of God

Look to Philippians 4:10 where Paul writes, "I rejoiced in the Lord greatly." Notice that this joy is not dependent on the Philippian church's gift, but on the reminder of God's faithfulness that the gift represented. Paul was in a Roman jail cell paying his own rent, yet he wrote with "the pen of joy." Practice rejoicing in the Lord for His faithfulness in your life: He woke you up this morning, He watches over you as you sleep, He supplied your needs when you did not know where your next provision was coming from. Testify that money is an imposter—God is the one who faithfully supplies all your needs.

### Step 4: Learn Contentment Through the Frequency of Uncertainty

Understand that contentment is learned through both plenty and poverty. Paul says in verse 11, "I have learned to be content in whatever circumstances I am." The word "learned" here means to learn by experience through time and trial. Like a college science class with lecture (Sunday morning Bible study) and lab (Friday life experiences), you need both. Accept that God may allow you to go through valleys—divorce, loss of a house, illness, the death of a loved one—before you are ready to hear His instruction. Do not despise the valley; it is in the valley that you learn the rock at the bottom will not let you out. Contentment is learned by walking through both abundance and need and discovering that neither can ultimately harm you.

### Step 5: Ground Your Contentment in the Indwelling Christ

Place your primary confidence in Philippians 4:13: "I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me." Understand this verse correctly: it is not a promise of omnipotence, but a promise of competence. Paul is not saying you can do anything you set your mind to; he is saying that because Christ lives in you, you can handle whatever comes your way. If Christ is not in you, you cannot do anything at all. Stay in Christ through prayer, Bible study, and obedience. The indwelling Christ gives you the buoyancy to ride the waves of life—the outside pressure cannot crush you because of the strength of the inside. Let this be your confession: "I don't look like what I've been through because there is Someone on the inside who gives me strength."

### Step 6: Recognize the Inward Name That Sustains You

Like the Steinway piano in the preacher's story, your value is not in your outward condition but in the name written on the inside. Even when the piano was damaged and companies offered to replace it with shiny new ones, the Steinway name on the inside frame made it worth more than the replacements. So it is with you: you may have been through lonely nights, dark days, mountains, and valleys, but the reason you made it is because there is a name on the inside of you—the Lord Jesus Christ. Call on that name. Testify that He has been faithful to you over mountains, through valleys, and across streams.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Steinway Piano

When Rev. Dates and his wife came to their church in 2011, they found an old eight-foot grand concert piano on the platform. It never worked right, so they moved it to the lower level during the week. When they called repair companies, every company offered to replace it with a new shiny piano, and some even offered cash on top. They called the Steinway factory, and two technicians discovered the piano only needed a new floorboard, some new hammers, some new harp strings, and refinishing. The preacher asked, "How much?" and when he heard "tens of thousands of dollars," he declined. The technician then pointed to the Steinway name inside the frame and said, "We only make five of these a year. Even in the shape it's in, it's worth more than those new pianos because of the name that's only inside of it." The application: You may look worn on the outside, but the name of Jesus on the inside makes you priceless.

### Example 2: Denise Mitchell and Alma McIntosh

Two older women in the preacher's church, Denise Mitchell and Alma McIntosh, both lost their husbands of more than 40 years within the last two years. He stood with them at the graveside and in the hospital room as disease ravaged their husbands' bodies. Yet every Sunday he sees them walk into the sanctuary and raise their hands in worship. They have been to the bottom, and at the bottom they learned there is a rock that will not let you out. Their testimony demonstrates that God is faithful even when you are down and out, and that you can learn contentment through the frequency of uncertainty.

### Example 3: Paul in the Roman Jail

Paul wrote Philippians from a Roman jail apartment where he had to pay his own rent. Most people in that situation would write a short letter saying, "Get me out of here." Instead, Paul wrote four chapters of joy, book-ending the epistle with the theme of joy and declaring, "I have learned the secret." His joy was not dependent on his circumstances—it was rooted in the Lord. This proves that genuine contentment is found in the faithfulness of God, not in favorable circumstances.

### Example 4: The College Science Class

Paul's argument about contentment works like an undergraduate science class with two parts: lecture and lab. Lecture happens on Monday and Wednesday in a big hall with a professor. Lab happens on Friday in a smaller setting with hands-on work. You could skip lecture, but when lab comes you will not be prepared. Christian living has both instruction and experience. Sunday morning Bible study is your lecture. The trials, valleys, and uncertainties of life are your lab. When lab shows up, you better have Philippians 4:13 and Philippians 4:19 ready. The curious part: in this text, experience sometimes precedes instruction. God may let you go through something first, and afterward your ears will be open to hear what He has to say.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Keep your Bible open during teaching and take copious notes, because lab (life trials) is coming and you will need the Word
- ✅ Recognize that plenty cannot buy you brains, a home, health, heaven, or a savior—only God can provide these
- ✅ Take both courses: the course of prosperity and the course of poverty, because neither alone teaches contentment
- ✅ Rejoice in the Lord greatly, letting your joy exceed all circumstances
- ✅ Testify about God's faithfulness publicly so that others may be encouraged
- ✅ Remember that God sometimes allows you to hit bottom so that your ears will be open to hear Him
- ✅ Abide in Christ daily through prayer and Bible study so that His strength flows into your weakness
- ✅ Distinguish between resources (what you use) and the Source (who provides it) in every area of life

## Keep In Mind

- Joy is not self-generated. In the original language, "I rejoiced" is a passive verb, meaning something rejoiced Paul—he did not make himself joyful. The Lord puts joy down in your heart.
- Paul's joy was "greatly" (adverb used only here for joy in this epistle), exceeding all other expressions of joy because it was rooted in God, not circumstances.
- The offering from the Philippian church reminded Paul of God's faithfulness, not just provided financial relief. The doors of the church had closed, but the joy was still lingering.
- The word "learned" in verse 11 means to learn by experience through time and trial. The word "learned" in verse 12 means to learn by instruction through revelation. Contentment requires both experience and instruction.
- God will sometimes let you go through trials without explaining them first, but He knows how to get your attention. When you hit bottom, your ears open to hear what He has to say.
- You do not need to despise the valley; the songwriter says, "I thank you for my mountains, I thank you for my valleys, I thank you for my problems, I thank you for all of the storms that I went through because if I hadn't gone through them I wouldn't know that you're a God who can solve them."
- Christ is so big that you can be in Him in Chicago while others are in Him in Louisville, in the United States while others are in Him around the world. Where you are geographically does not matter; where you are theologically matters.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not place your security in wealth, career, degrees, connections, or material possessions. The preacher states plainly: "My degrees did not get me here, my connections did not get me here, my career did not get me here, but You have saved me over some mountain, through some valleys, across some streams."
- Your security must be in the Lord Jesus Christ alone. If your security has been removed from Christ and placed in wealth, you are living implicitly as though you no longer need God.
- Contentment is not a private luxury; it is a public testimony. When you walk through the valley and come out the other side still worshiping, you testify to everyone that God is faithful.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Misreading Philippians 4:13 as a promise that you can do anything you set your mind to (omnipotence). The verse is not about omnipotence; it is about competence. Christ gives you the ability to handle whatever situation you are in, not the ability to do anything you imagine.
  **Solution:** Restate the verse correctly: "I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me." If Christ is not in you, you cannot do anything at all.

- **Problem:** Believing that more money, sex, prestige, or power will finally satisfy you. Biblical examples prove otherwise: Judas hanged himself despite his money, David sinned despite his power, King Saul spared the Amalekites despite his prestige, and Nebuchadnezzar was exiled despite his authority.
  **Solution:** Confess that none of these resources can fully satisfy you, and turn your heart back to the Source who alone can meet your deepest needs.

- **Problem:** Trying to manufacture your own joy through self-effort. Paul did not rejoice himself; something rejoiced him. Joy is a gift from the Lord, not a product of positive thinking.
  **Solution:** Stop striving to make yourself happy and instead ask the Lord to pour His joy into your heart. Abide in Christ, and His joy will flow into you.

- **Problem:** Despising the valley or resenting the difficult seasons of life. If you had not gone through your storms, you would not know that God is a God who can solve them.
  **Solution:** Thank God for your mountains and your valleys, for your problems and your storms. Accept that the experience of uncertainty is one of the classrooms where contentment is taught.

- **Problem:** Skipping the "lecture" (Sunday Bible study, corporate worship, discipleship) and expecting to pass the "lab" (life trials). If you do not show up for lecture, you will not be prepared for lab.
  **Solution:** Commit to regular Bible study and church attendance so that when trials come, you have the truth of Philippians 4:13, Philippians 4:19, and Psalm 23 stored in your heart.

- **Problem:** Confusing resources with the Source. You may think your paycheck, your job, your medicine, or your entertainment is what sustains you, but these are merely the means God uses.
  **Solution:** Make a daily practice of acknowledging God as the Source of every good thing. Thank Him for your provision, recognizing that your living, your happiness, and your health come from Him alone.
