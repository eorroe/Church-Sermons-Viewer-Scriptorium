# Thank You Lord

## Overview

This sermon teaches that gratitude is the defining mark of a believer's life, anchored in 1 Timothy 1:12-17. Rev. Dr. F. Bruce Williams explains that Paul begins his letter not with doctrine or instruction, but with thanksgiving—because a heart truly changed by grace cannot help but say "thank you." The message emphasizes that God chose, appointed, empowered, and entrusted every believer, even when we were undeserving, and calls us to live out that gratitude through service, sacrifice, and sharing our testimony.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When preparing for Thanksgiving or any season of gratitude
- When struggling with feelings of inadequacy or past mistakes
- When discerning your purpose or calling in service
- When feeling overwhelmed by a task, assignment, or life circumstance
- When tempted to compare yourself to others or feel spiritually inferior
- When you need encouragement that God equips those He calls
- When you have been saved but are unsure what to do with that salvation

## Steps

### Step 1: Start Every Day and Every Endeavor With Thanksgiving

Follow Paul's example by beginning with gratitude before you get into the details or demands of the day. When you wake up, the first thing you should do is thank God that you opened your eyes—not complain about what you have to do. Gratitude shifts your perspective from what you lack to what you have, and from your strength to God's provision.

### Step 2: Recognize That God Chose You Despite Your Past

Understand that your salvation is not based on your goodness, resume, or worthiness. Like Paul, you may have been headed in the wrong direction—sincere but sincerely wrong. God chose you not because of who you were, but in spite of who you were. Reflect on your life before Christ and acknowledge that without His grace, you would still be on that downward path.

### Step 3: Understand That You Have Been Appointed to Service

Salvation is not just for comfort or celebration; it is for service. Whatever your title, position, or role, remember that you are appointed to serve, not to be celebrated, recognized, or honored. Be careful that titles do not puff you up or distract you from the real purpose of your appointment. Your assignment is not about you—it is about who you serve.

### Step 4: Receive the Empowerment That Comes With Your Appointment

God never gives you an assignment without giving you the power to fulfill it. With the call comes divine capability. If God has appointed you to raise children, hold a job, love a difficult spouse, or endure hardship, He has already given you the strength to do it. Remind yourself: "I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me."

### Step 5: Live a Life That Puts Something on the Ledger

You can never pay God back for what He has done, but you can put something on the ledger. Respond to His grace with praise with your lips, joy with your dance, giving with your hands, and service with your life. When you see someone in need, forgive someone who wronged you, or speak up for the voiceless, you are putting something on the ledger—showing that His investment in you was not wasted.

### Step 6: Share Your Full Testimony so Others Can Be Saved

Do not stay in the shadows. Do not tell only the sanitized version of your story. Tell the whole truth about where you were and what God brought you from. Your transformed life is on display so that others who are still struggling will look at you and say, "If God did it for them, He can do it for me." Paul's worst-of-sinners story became the most powerful testimony of grace because it was honest and complete.

## Examples

### Example 1: Paul's Gratitude in the Midst of a Storm

The sermon recounts how Paul began his letter to Timothy with thanksgiving even though he had just been released from incarceration. This shows that gratitude is not dependent on circumstances. Just as Paul took bread and gave thanks while on a ship in the midst of a storm, you can thank God in the middle of your own storms. Thanksgiving releases the power of God to multiply what little you have and carry you through.

### Example 2: Paul's Testimony as a Living Display of God's Mercy

Paul declares himself the worst of sinners, yet God chose, appointed, empowered, and put him on display. The preacher explains that God did not choose Paul despite his past—He chose him because of his past, so that His mercy could be magnified for all to see. Your testimony works the same way: the more drastic your transformation, the more clearly God's power is displayed, and the more effective your witness becomes to those who are still lost.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Start each day by thanking God before complaining about your obligations
- ✅ Be honest about your past when sharing your testimony—include the "warts and ugliness"
- ✅ Remember that your appointment is for service, not celebrity or recognition
- ✅ Give credit to God for every success, never taking full credit yourself
- ✅ Look for opportunities to "put something on the ledger" daily through acts of love, justice, and service
- ✅ Praise God with your pen, your voice, your hands, and your life

## Keep In Mind

- God's choice of you is not based on your qualifications but on His grace
- You can never outgive or out-serve what God has already done for you
- The same God who saved Paul from blasphemy and violence can save anyone
- Your testimony is more powerful when it includes the truth about your former life
- Thanksgiving should be the first response, not the last resort
- God is eternal, immortal, invisible, and uniquely worthy of all glory—not your accomplishments, your country, or your church

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use your testimony to shame others or glorify past sin; always point to the transformation, not the transgression
- Be wise and prayerful about how openly you share personal details of your past, especially if it involves harm to others or legal consequences
- Remember that God's grace covers your past, but it does not give you license to continue in sin or to exploit your story for personal gain

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Feeling unqualified or unworthy because of your past
  **Solution:** Remember that God chose you precisely because of grace, not merit. Paul was the worst of sinners and still became the greatest apostle.
- **Problem:** Serving for recognition, titles, or praise rather than for Christ
  **Solution:** Regularly examine your motives. Ask God to keep you humble and focused on service, not status.
- **Problem:** Staying silent about your testimony because of shame
  **Solution:** Your story is not about you—it is about what God can do. Share it honestly so that others may come to faith.
- **Problem:** Thinking you can pay God back for His blessings
  **Solution:** You can never equal what He has done, but you can respond with gratitude. Shift your goal from repayment to a life that reflects His investment in you.
- **Problem:** Complaining instead of thanking when faced with difficulty
  **Solution:** Make a conscious habit of starting every difficult situation with "thank you, Lord." Gratitude changes the atmosphere and opens the door for God to work.
