# Sermon Title

## Overview

This sermon, based on Matthew 6:9, explores the meaning of addressing God as "Our Father" rather than "My Father." Through a vivid parable about a king's invitation to a party, Dr. Thomas illustrates that God's invitation is extended to all people by grace alone, not by merit, pedigree, or personal holiness. The sermon challenges listeners to reject judgmental pride, embrace the inclusive family of God, and remain grateful for the invitation they have received.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you find yourself comparing your spiritual standing to others and feeling superior
- When you have been tempted to judge, label, or exclude people based on their past, identity, or background
- When you have lost sight of the grace that invited you and have begun questioning how others got into the kingdom
- When you need to be reminded that salvation and adoption into God's family is a gift, not an achievement
- When you want to understand the communal nature of prayer and the body of Christ beyond your own circle

## Steps

### Step 1: Shift Your Prayer From "My Father" to "Our Father"

Examine the language you use when you pray and when you speak of God. If you find yourself thinking or saying "my Father" in a way that excludes or distances others, intentionally shift to "our Father." Recognize that the invitation to call God Father is extended to every person, regardless of their history, mistakes, ethnicity, or identity. Acknowledge that everyone in the room — every nationality, every background, every struggling soul — has been adopted as a son or daughter of the same King.

### Step 2: Release the Need to Be "Special" Above Others

Reflect on whether you have fallen into the trap of wanting to be the only special one in God's family. The desire to be uniquely favored at the expense of others is rooted in a scarcity mindset that dishonors the King's generosity. Let go of the idea that someone else's blessing, calling, or presence diminishes yours. Celebrate that others have been invited, adopted, and clothed in the same robes of righteousness. Gratitude for your own invitation should free you to welcome others, not exclude them.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Judgmental Church Member

A longtime, active church member notices new visitors attending the service who look, dress, or live very differently from the regular congregation. Instead of welcoming them, the member wonders how "people like that" got in and begins to gossip or withdraw. This mirrors the attitude in the sermon's parable, where the invited guest resented the presence of Methodists, Pentecostals, immigrants, and people with painful pasts. The corrective action is to remember that the doors are open by the King's invitation, not by human qualification.

### Example 2: The Self-Righteous Believer

A person who has overcome addiction, a difficult past, or moral failure begins to feel spiritually superior to others who are still struggling. They privately (or openly) judge those who have not yet "arrived" spiritually. This reflects the parable's guest who listed the reasons others should not be at the party. The sermon teaches that all who are present are there solely by the King's mercy, and anyone who forgets this and judges others will be the one escorted out — not the people they judged.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Practice gratitude daily for the fact that you were invited — you did not earn or deserve your place at the table
- ✅ Use inclusive language — "our Father" — to remind yourself and others that the family of God is expansive
- ✅ Extend a personal invitation to someone who may feel unwelcome or unqualified to attend church or enter a relationship with God
- ✅ Remain humble by remembering your own history of being a "sinner, a peasant, and an outcast" before the King called you
- ❌ Do not judge people based on their past, labels, ethnicity, sexual orientation, or socioeconomic status
- ❌ Do not act as though God speaks exclusively to you or that your spiritual experience is the only valid one
- ❌ Do not compare your invitation to others' invitations as though there is a limited supply of God's grace
- ❌ Do not call people by their old names or define them by their former mistakes when God has done a new thing in their lives

## Keep In Mind

- The invitation to God's family is entirely based on the King's generosity, not on your degrees, family lineage, church membership, or years of service
- When you forget how much God has saved you from, you begin to wonder how somebody else got saved — gratitude is the antidote to judgment
- God will always let you call Him Lord, but He withholds the intimate address of "our Father" until you bring your neighbor into the picture and recognize grace for all
- The kingdom of God is bigger than your denomination, your church, your ethnicity, your economic status, your musical preferences, and your theological traditions
- Even when you are disciplined or removed from fellowship, God leaves you with a "ring" — a standing invitation to return when you repent and change
- Being so busy praising and thanking God for what He has done in your own life leaves no time to snoop around in other people's business

## Security & Safety Notes

- The King's household does not tolerate arrogance and judgmental pride — these attitudes will result in being escorted from the party, not because God is cruel, but because a judgmental spirit destroys the family
- Your place in God's family is secured by adoption, not by your performance — you cannot lose your adoption by failing, but you can forfeit the joy and freedom of it by hardening your heart toward others
- God will let you suffer the consequences of your actions, but He will always leave a way back — repentance is always available
- The "ring" given to the adopted child is an act of mercy: it represents a standing invitation to return, no matter how far you have strayed or how badly you have behaved toward others in the family

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Thinking that your invitation makes you more special than others who have also been invited
  **Solution:** Remember that all who are in the party wear the same fine robes — your adoption is no more legitimate than anyone else's; your gratitude should overflow into welcome, not comparison

- **Problem:** Using your spiritual identity to justify looking down on people with different backgrounds, orientations, or pasts
  **Solution:** Recall that you were invited as a sinner, a foreigner, and an outcast — your standing is by grace alone, and grace leaves no room for contempt

- **Problem:** Becoming so focused on correcting or fixing other people that you neglect your own relationship with God
  **Solution:** Relax in God's presence, enjoy your own salvation, and trust Him with the outcome of others' lives — your job is to love and invite, not to reform

- **Problem:** Allowing personal preferences (music style, denomination, culture) to become barriers that keep others from feeling welcome in God's house
  **Solution:** Recognize that God is bigger than what you like, and the kingdom extends far beyond your comfort zone — say "our Father" and make room
