# When Its Not Looking Too Good

## Overview

This sermon teaches believers how to respond when life circumstances appear hopeless, using the story of Jairus in Luke 8:49 as the foundation. When Jairus was told his daughter had died and to leave Jesus alone, the messenger represented the lie that some situations are "too bad for Jesus." The core message is that no circumstance is beyond Jesus' power, and believers must refuse to let what they see poison what they say. Faith must be exercised through praise, spoken scripture, and persistence at the altar even when the outlook is grim.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When a medical diagnosis, financial crisis, or relationship breakdown makes you want to give up
- When political or social turmoil causes you to fear for the future
- When someone tells you to stop praying because the situation is already lost
- When you feel your back is against the wall and you don't know where your next provision will come from
- When you are tempted to let negative reports from doctors, news, or loved ones override God's promises

## Steps

### Step 1: Go Back to the Altar and Get Down on Your Knees

When circumstances deteriorate, return to the place of prayer and submission. The messenger came to Jairus while he was still with Jesus, telling him to stop bothering the master. Instead of accepting that word, Jairus continued to stand with Jesus. Do not abandon prayer when the situation worsens. Physical posture of humility before God opens the door for divine intervention. Kneel at the altar and present your case to God regardless of how bad it looks.

### Step 2: Refuse to Let What You See Poison What You Say

Whatever the doctor, the news, or your circumstances declare, speak the opposite. When you see a spot on an X-ray, say, "By His stripes I am healed." When you see layoffs happening around you, say, "My God shall supply all my needs according to His riches in glory." When your relationship seems broken, speak the word of reconciliation. Your confession must align with scripture, not with your current reality. Control your tongue because death and life are in the power of the tongue.

### Step 3: Comprehend the Multi-Dimensional Reality of Jesus' Personhood

Do not reduce Jesus to a mere teacher, rabbi, or miracle worker. He is all of those and more. He is the resurrection and the life. When you understand that Jesus is multi-dimensional, you know He can handle every dimension of your problem. He is not limited to what human logic says is possible. Spend time in the Word to renew your understanding of who Jesus truly is, because your view of Him determines your expectation of Him.

### Step 4: Be Completely Reassured by the Past

God has a track record. Read Luke chapter 7 and remember how Jesus raised the widow's only son from the dead. If He did it for someone else, He can do it for you. Look back over your own life and recall the times God came through when there was no natural way. Sit with people who have walked with God for decades and let their testimonies strengthen your faith. God is not a past-tense God; He is a present-tense God who does the same things today that He did yesterday.

### Step 5: Respond Under Pressure with "Fear Not, Have Faith, It Will Be Fixed"

When pressure mounts, your commanded response is faith, not fear. Jesus told Jairus, "Fear not. Only believe. She shall be made whole." This is not a suggestion; it is a command. You may want God to fix it first so you can more easily believe, but God's order is belief first, then manifestation. Your faith grows when you choose to trust before you see the result. Speak "Fear not" over your own situation and command your heart to believe.

### Step 6: Shout for Someone Else Before It Is Your Turn

Do not become bitter or resentful when God blesses someone else before He blesses you. Rejoice for their promotion, their healing, and their breakthrough because it confirms that God is still in the neighborhood. When you see someone else receive what you need, shout for them. Your gratitude for others' blessings releases God to move on your behalf. Envy blocks your own miracle, but celebration accelerates it.

### Step 7: Reject the Lie That Your Situation Is Too Bad for Jesus

Nothing is too bad for Jesus. Divorce, abuse, incarceration, sudden death, addiction, public shame, or decades of pain are not beyond His reach. He is the resurrection and the life. He specializes in what looks impossible. If you have been avoiding prayer, church, or transparency because your problem feels too dark, come back. Tell God about it. He already knows, and He is waiting for you to bring it to Him so He can turn it around.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Woman with the Issue of Blood

For twelve years this woman suffered from a condition that doctors could not fix, and she was ceremonially unclean. She pushed through a crowd, ignoring social and religious barriers, determined just to touch the hem of Jesus' garment. Her intentionality and persistence demonstrate the kind of faith required when you are desperate. She did not wait for an appointment or a better moment. She reached out, and virtue flowed from Jesus. Her healing shows that if you have something you need Jesus to fix, you can be in the crowd but you must be determined to touch Him. Do not let shame, doubt, or people's opinions stop you from getting to Jesus.

### Example 2: Jairus and His Daughter

Jairus was a ruler of the synagogue, an intelligent and financially stable man with prestige and power, yet none of that could save his dying daughter. He humbled himself, fell at Jesus' feet, and begged for help. In the middle of his request, a messenger arrived with the devastating news that his daughter had already died and he should stop troubling Jesus. That messenger represented the voice that tells you to give up when things get worse. But Jesus responded with "Fear not. Only believe. She shall be made whole." Jairus' situation teaches that position, education, and resources cannot fix everything, and that God's timing often includes delays where your faith is tested. The result was that his daughter was raised from the dead, proving that no situation is too late for God.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Go back to the altar regularly, not just when emergencies arise
- ✅ Speak scripture over your situation out loud
- ✅ Remember what God did for your grandparents and ancestors
- ✅ Celebrate other people's victories before your own comes
- ✅ Examine your heart for hidden sin and hypocrisy before judging others
- ✅ Be intentional about church attendance and prayer, not casual
- ✅ Tell God exactly what you are going through without sugarcoating it
- ❌ Do not let what you see in the news, at the doctor's office, or in your finances dictate your confession
- ❌ Do not avoid prayer because your situation feels too dark or embarrassing
- ❌ Do not resent people who receive their breakthrough before you
- ❌ Do not reduce Jesus to a religious figure who only handles certain types of problems
- ❌ Do not let other people's negative words become your reality
- ❌ Do not stop praying when circumstances get worse instead of better

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon was preached during a revival in Louisville, Kentucky, by Rev. Reginald Sharpe from Macon, Georgia, and reflects an urgent call to return to authentic faith and dependence on Jesus.
- The central scripture is Luke 8:49, but the preacher also references Luke chapter 7, where Jesus raised the widow's only son from the dead, to establish that God has always been a resurrection God.
- The phrase "It ain't looking too good" is used intentionally to connect with real, raw human experiences rather than polished religious language.
- Not every problem will be fixed instantly in this life, but the sermon emphasizes that God is still able and that your praise should not be contingent on your circumstances.
- Faith is not the absence of struggle; it is the decision to trust and speak God's truth in the middle of struggle.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon contains strong emotional appeals, altar calls, and direct confrontations with sin and hypocrisy that may be uncomfortable for listeners who are not ready for honest self-examination.
- The preacher directly addresses political and social issues, including the 2016 U.S. presidential election, LGBTQ+ violence in Orlando, racism, sexism, and church hypocrisy. Some listeners may find these references provocative or politically charged.
- The message challenges listeners to be vulnerable about their pain, which may bring up past trauma or grief. Those dealing with severe mental health issues should seek pastoral support alongside listening.
- The altar call invitation to "go back to the altar" and "get down on your knees" is a physical act of submission. Individuals with physical limitations should adapt the posture to what their body allows while maintaining the heart posture.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Waiting for God to fix the situation before you speak in faith
  **Solution:** Speak God's word first. Your faith grows when you believe before you see. Jesus told Jairus to fear not and have faith before his daughter was raised. Speak scripture over your life now, not after the breakthrough.
- **Problem:** Comparing your pain to others and concluding yours is not bad enough to need God
  **Solution:** God cares about every dimension of your suffering, from a broken nail to a broken marriage. Come to Him with everything. Do not minimize your pain or disqualify yourself from prayer.
- **Problem:** Letting a negative messenger or naysayer make you stop praying
  **Solution:** The moment someone tells you to "stop bothering Jesus" is exactly when you should cling to Jesus harder. Messengers often speak from a limited, human perspective. Their report is not God's final word.
- **Problem:** Forgetting what God did in the past and panicking in the present
  **Solution:** Keep a journal of past deliverances. Sit with older saints and listen to their stories. When fear rises, recall how God came through previously. Spiritual amnesia causes unnecessary panic.
- **Problem:** Harboring jealousy when others receive their blessing first
  **Solution:** Train yourself to shout for others. God is not limited, and someone else's miracle does not diminish your opportunity. Jealousy blocks your own breakthrough, but celebration releases it.
- **Problem:** Using your religiosity or spiritual knowledge to avoid honest pain
  **Solution:** The preacher explicitly says he is not talking to the "super duper saved" who quote hymns and scriptures while hiding real hurt. Authentic faith acknowledges the struggle and still chooses to trust. Do not use religion as a shield against vulnerability.
