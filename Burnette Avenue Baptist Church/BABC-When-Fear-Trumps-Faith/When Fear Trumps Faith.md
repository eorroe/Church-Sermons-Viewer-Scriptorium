# When Fear Trumps Faith

## Overview

This sermon, delivered by Rev. Jeremiah Wright at Burnett Avenue Baptist Church on Seniors Day 2016, explores Mark 4:35-41, where Jesus calms the storm on the Sea of Galilee. It teaches that fierce storms in life can cause fear to overrule faith, and when this happens, believers forget three critical things: what God said, where they are, and who Jesus is. The message offers hope by reminding listeners that Jesus is with them through every storm, and that Storms are time-limited trials meant to strengthen your faith, not lifelong sentences. They have a beginning and an end.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are facing a crisis — such as illness, job loss, or family breakdown — that threatens to overwhelm your trust in God.
- When you are navigating relationship or marital difficulties
- When you are dealing with conflict, disappointment, or spiritual discouragement within your church community.
- When you feel afraid and your faith is being challenged by circumstances

## Steps

### Step 1: Remember What God Said

When fear trumps faith, the first thing believers forget is what God promised. God has given promises throughout Scripture:
- Isaiah 43:2 — "When you pass through the rivers, they will not sweep over you; when you walk through the fire, you will not be burned."
- Isaiah 54:17 — "No weapon formed against you shall prosper."
- Psalm 30:5 — "Weeping may endure for a night, but joy comes in the morning."
- Hebrews 13:5 — "I will never leave you nor forsake you."

Do not let the fierceness of your storm make you forget the faithfulness of God's Word.

### Step 2: Remember Where You Are

The second thing believers forget when fear trumps faith is where they are. You are on a journey with Jesus. Like the disciples in the boat, you are not alone in the storm. Jesus is with you. Isaiah 43:2 reminds us that when we pass through the waters and go through the storms, God will be with us. A storm is a season you pass through with God, not a permanent place you remain.

### Step 3: Remember Who Jesus Is

The third thing believers forget when fear trumps faith is who Jesus is. He is not only your Master; He is the Master of the ocean, earth, and sky. All things were made by Him. He is the same Jesus who:
- He is the same Jesus foretold across 42 generations of Scripture, from Abraham to Christ, as part of God's redemptive plan.
- He delivered the man tormented by a legion of demons (Mark 5).
- He healed the sick and restored them to full physical and spiritual wholeness.
- Fed 5,000 people
- Walked the dusty roads of Galilee
- He stood with the oppressed in the segregated streets of Montgomery, Alabama.
- He identified with the suffering of enslaved people, a truth powerfully embodied in Harriet Tubman's rescue missions.
- He is still speaking today through Scripture, the Holy Spirit, and the preaching of His Word.

Don't forget who He is. He chose to save you before you were born, and that choice has not changed.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Disciples in the Storm

The disciples were doing exactly what Jesus asked them to do—crossing to the other side—when a huge storm came up unexpectedly. Even though Jesus was with them in the boat, fear caused them to forget His presence, His promises, and His power. Jesus calmed the storm with a simple command, demonstrating that He has authority over every circumstance.

### Example 2: Rev. Wright's Personal Storm

In 1980 — thirty-six years before this sermon — Rev. Wright faced a fierce storm when his teenage daughter was in a relationship with a 19-year-old man who was leading her away from the Lord. During his morning devotion, Matthew 14:28 — where Peter stepped out of the boat toward Jesus — gave Rev. Wright the faith to act, and that action became his breakthrough. Just as Jesus calmed the disciples' fear by His presence, Jesus calmed Rev. Wright's storm. His presence soothes fear and doubt.

### Example 3: No Trouble-Free Existence

Dr. William Augustus Jones taught that everyone under the sound of the preacher's voice is in one of three categories: in a storm, coming out of a storm, or heading into a storm and didn't even know it. There is no such thing as a trouble-free life, a trouble-free relationship, or a trouble-free church. Joy and pain are part of what it means to be in relationship and to follow Christ.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Remember what God said about your situation before fear paralyzes your thinking and drowns out God's promises.
- ✅ Remember that you are not alone—Jesus is with you in the storm
- ✅ Remember who Jesus is: Master of all creation and your personal Savior
- ✅ Declare God's promises aloud over your situation instead of rehearsing your fears.
- ✅ Share your testimony of how God brought you through previous storms
- ❌ Don't let fear make you forget God's promises
- ❌ Don't isolate yourself from the body of Christ during difficult times
- ❌ Don't judge your private struggles by another person's public victories.
- ❌ Don't assume that following Jesus means you will be storm-free

## Keep In Mind

- Storms don't last forever, but they do come unexpectedly
- Jesus doesn't always calm the storm; sometimes He calms you in the storm
- Being with Jesus doesn't mean you won't face storms—it means you won't face them alone
- The same Jesus who calmed the Sea of Galilee is the same Jesus who walks with you today
- Your testimony of how God brought you through can encourage someone else in their storm

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon contains references to social and racial injustice, including mentions of Ferguson, Flint, Trayvon Martin, and Emmett Till. These references are historical and contextual to the 2016 cultural moment.
- The speaker uses prophetic and confrontational language to critique political leaders and social injustice. This reflects the prophetic tradition of speaking truth to power.
- The sermon includes personal testimony about family struggles and ministry challenges.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Forgetting God's promises when the storm gets fierce
  **Solution:** Write down key Scripture promises before the storm comes so you can recall them when fear tries to take over
- **Problem:** Feeling alone and abandoned during difficult times
  **Solution:** Remember Isaiah 43:2—God is with you through the waters and the fire. You are on a journey with Jesus.
- **Problem:** Letting fear override faith when circumstances seem overwhelming
  **Solution:** Focus on who Jesus is rather than how big the storm appears. He is the Master of ocean, earth, and sky.
- **Problem:** Comparing your relationship or life to others who appear to have no trouble
  **Solution:** Remember that everyone is either in a storm, coming out of a storm, or heading into one. There is no such thing as a trouble-free existence.
