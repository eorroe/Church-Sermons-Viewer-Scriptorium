# What Is a Christian Couple Allowed to Do in Sex?

## Overview

This sermon teaches married couples how to discern what is sexually permissible within marriage by applying Hebrews 13:4 and 1 Corinthians 7:3–5. It provides a framework using these five tests: (1) whether the act brings glory to God (1 Corinthians 10:31), (2) whether it is reserved exclusively for the husband and wife, (3) whether both spouses give free and willing consent, (4) whether the act strengthens emotional oneness by deepening love and care for the spouse, spiritual oneness by drawing the couple closer to God together, and physical oneness by uniting their bodies in mutual pleasure and self-giving, as described in Genesis 2:24, and (5) whether it avoids introducing third parties. Following these five tests ensures that the marriage bed remains honoring to God and unifies both spouses.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When a married couple wants to understand the specific boundaries of marital intimacy established in Hebrews 13:4 and 1 Corinthians 7:3–5
- When a spouse is unsure whether a particular sexual practice is permissible within marriage
- When a couple seeks to honor God and each other in their physical relationship
- When counseling others on the biblical view of sex in marriage

## Steps

### Step 1: Honor God With Your Body

Apply 1 Corinthians 6:12–13 and 1 Corinthians 6:20 — sex is meant to glorify the Lord, not to be enslaved by sinful desires—the fallen, sinful nature within every person that opposes God's will (Galatians 5:17)—or used for sexual immorality. Before engaging in any sexual act, both spouses should ask whether the act brings glory to God rather than gratifying selfish desire.

### Step 2: Keep Sex Exclusive to Your Spouse

Apply 1 Corinthians 7:2 — sex is reserved exclusively for a husband and wife. Any sexual activity outside the marriage covenant, including adultery or any form of infidelity, is not permissible (Hebrews 13:4).

### Step 3: Practice Mutual, Freely Given Consent

Apply 1 Corinthians 7:3–4 — each spouse yields his or her body to the other in service, meaning neither person should be pressured, forced, or coerced into any sexual act they do not freely and willingly agree to. Both husband and wife must freely agree to any sexual activity; consent means a willing, unpressured "yes," not the absence of a "no."

### Step 4: Pursue Regular Sexual Intimacy

Apply 1 Corinthians 7:5 — couples should not deprive one another except by mutual consent for a specific, temporary, agreed-upon period—specifically, a voluntary fast from sexual intimacy devoted to prayer that both spouses agree upon in advance—with the explicit intention to return to regular intimacy, after which the couple resumes marital relations. Consistent physical intimacy protects against temptation and deepens the bond between spouses.

### Step 5: Evaluate Whether the Act Strengthens the Marriage Bond

Apply 1 Corinthians 7:5 — any sexual act within marriage should strengthen the couple's emotional oneness by deepening love and care for the spouse, spiritual oneness by drawing the couple closer to God together, and physical oneness by uniting their bodies in mutual pleasure and self-giving love as described in 1 Corinthians 7:3-5, and not create division or resentment. This reflects the "one flesh" union—a combined physical, emotional, and spiritual bond established in Genesis 2:24 and affirmed in Matthew 19:5.

### Step 6: Apply All Five Tests Together

If a sexual act satisfies all five tests—(1) it brings glory to God rather than profaning His name, (2) it is reserved exclusively for the husband and wife, (3) it is freely and willingly consented to by both spouses, (4) it strengthens emotional oneness by deepening love and care for the spouse, spiritual oneness by drawing the couple closer to God together, and physical oneness by uniting their bodies in mutual pleasure and self-giving love as described in 1 Corinthians 7:3-5, and (5) it does not introduce third parties—and both spouses freely and willingly agree, the act is not explicitly forbidden by Scripture. Couples should still seek wisdom from Scripture and from mature, spiritually grounded believers before acting.

### Step 7: Eliminate What Is Never Allowable

Know what Scripture explicitly forbids: "swapping," "swinging," or bringing in third parties (threesomes, foursomes, and group sex) constitutes adultery (Galatians 5:19; Ephesians 5:3; Colossians 3:5; 1 Thessalonians 4:3), and viewing pornography appeals to "the lust of the flesh and the lust of the eyes" (1 John 2:16) and is explicitly sinful and forbidden by God, regardless of whether a spouse approves.

## Examples

### Example 1: Discussing a New Sexual Practice

A couple is considering trying oral sex. They examine whether it (1) brings glory to God rather than gratifying selfish desire, (2) is reserved exclusively for their marriage, (3) is freely consented to by both spouses, (4) strengthens emotional oneness by deepening love and care for the spouse, spiritual oneness by drawing the couple closer to God together, and physical oneness by uniting their bodies in mutual pleasure and self-giving, and (5) does not introduce third parties. If both spouses freely agree and it passes all five tests, the act is not explicitly forbidden by Scripture. Couples should still seek wisdom from Scripture and from mature, spiritually grounded believers before acting.

### Example 2: A Spouse Feels Uncomfortable With a Request

If one spouse asks the other to try something the other finds uncomfortable or believes it is wrong according to Scripture, mutual consent (1 Corinthians 7:5) means the request should not be pursued. Neither spouse should be pressured.

## Best Practices

- Apply the five biblical tests—whether the act (1) brings glory to God, (2) is reserved exclusively for the husband and wife, (3) is freely consented to by both spouses, (4) strengthens emotional oneness by deepening love and care for the spouse, spiritual oneness by drawing the couple closer to God together, and physical oneness by uniting their bodies in mutual pleasure and self-giving, and (5) does not introduce third parties—to every sexual act within marriage
- Seek mutual consent before and during any sexual act
- View sex as an act of unity and mutual yielding, not personal gratification
- Communicate openly about desires and boundaries
- Do not engage in adultery, swinging, threesomes, or any sexual activity involving third parties, even with spousal approval
- Do not view or use pornography in any form within the marriage
- Do not pressure or coerce a spouse into any sexual act they are uncomfortable with

## Keep In Mind

- Scripture permits consensual sexual expression within marriage when it aligns with the five tests established in Hebrews 13:4 and 1 Corinthians 7:4–5
- Mutual consent applies equally to abstaining from sex and to deciding what is done sexually (1 Corinthians 7:5)
- Each couple must prayerfully apply these tests to their own marriage, seeking God's wisdom through Scripture and from mature, spiritually grounded believers

## Security & Safety Notes

- Neither spouse should be coerced into any sexual act they are not comfortable with; mutual consent is non-negotiable
- Any practice that involves adultery, pornography, or third parties is explicitly sinful regardless of consent
- Couples experiencing conflict over sexual boundaries should seek pastoral or professional Christian counsel

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** One spouse uses scripture or authority to pressure the other into an unwanted sexual act
  - **Solution:** Apply 1 Corinthians 7:3–4 — each spouse yields their body to the other in service, not by force; mutual consent must always be present

- **Problem:** A couple assumes anything not explicitly forbidden in the Bible is automatically permissible
  - **Solution:** Apply the five tests—whether the act (1) brings glory to God rather than profaning His name, (2) is reserved exclusively for the husband and wife, (3) is freely consented to by both spouses, (4) strengthens emotional oneness by deepening love and care for the spouse, spiritual oneness by drawing the couple closer to God together, and physical oneness by uniting their bodies in mutual pleasure and self-giving, and (5) does not introduce third parties—to every act before assuming it is acceptable

- **Problem:** A couple engages in adultery or brings in a third party, believing spousal consent makes it acceptable
  - **Solution:** Adultery and sexual immorality (Galatians 5:19; Ephesians 5:3; Colossians 3:5; 1 Thessalonians 4:3) are always sin, regardless of whether a spouse approves or participates
