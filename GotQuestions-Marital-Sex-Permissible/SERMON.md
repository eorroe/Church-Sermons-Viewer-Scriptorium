# What Is a Christian Couple Allowed to Do in Sex?

## Overview

This sermon teaches married couples how to discern what is sexually permissible within marriage by applying biblical principles from passages such as Hebrews 13:4 and 1 Corinthians 7:3–5. It provides a clear framework based on God-honoring motives, exclusivity, mutual consent, and unity, helping couples navigate their intimate life according to biblical principles of love, respect, and mutual consent. Following these principles ensures that the marriage bed remains honoring to God and deeply unifying for both spouses.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When a married couple wants to understand the biblical boundaries of marital intimacy
- When a spouse is unsure whether a particular sexual practice is permissible within marriage
- When a couple seeks to honor God and each other in their physical relationship
- When counseling others on the biblical view of sex in marriage

## Steps

### Step 1: Honor God With Your Body

Apply 1 Corinthians 6:12–13 and 1 Corinthians 6:20 — sex is meant to glorify the Lord, not to be controlled by passions or used for sexual immorality. Before engaging in any sexual act, both spouses should ask whether the act brings honor to God.

### Step 2: Keep Sex Exclusive to Your Spouse

Apply 1 Corinthians 7:2 — sex is reserved exclusively for a husband and wife. Any sexual activity outside the marriage covenant, including adultery or any form of infidelity, is not permissible (Hebrews 13:4).

### Step 3: Practice Mutual, Loving Consent

Apply 1 Corinthians 7:3–4 — each spouse lovingly yields his or her body to the other, meaning neither person should be pressured, forced, or coerced into any sexual act they do not freely and willingly agree to. Both husband and wife must freely agree to any sexual activity.

### Step 4: Pursue Regular Sexual Intimacy

Apply 1 Corinthians 7:5 — couples should not deprive one another except by mutual consent and for a temporary time—a specific, limited period such as a spiritual fast or season of focused prayer devoted to prayer — typically a few days to a few weeks, not months — then come together again. Consistent physical intimacy protects against temptation and deepens the bond between spouses.

### Step 5: Evaluate Whether the Act Unifies the Marriage

Apply 1 Corinthians 7:5 — any sexual act within marriage should strengthen the couple's emotional, spiritual, and physical oneness, not create division or resentment, solidifying the "one flesh" aspect of marriage (Genesis 2:24; Matthew 19:5).

### Step 6: Apply All Five Principles Together

If a sexual act satisfies all five principles—God-honoring, exclusive, loving, mutually agreed upon, and unifying—and both spouses freely and willingly agree, the Bible does not explicitly forbid it, though couples should seek wisdom from Scripture and godly counsel. Both spouses must be in full agreement for an act to be permissible.

### Step 7: Eliminate What Is Never Allowable

Know what Scripture explicitly forbids: "swapping," "swinging," or bringing in third parties (threesomes, foursomes, etc.) constitutes adultery (Galatians 5:19; Ephesians 5:3; Colossians 3:5; 1 Thessalonians 4:3), and viewing pornography appeals to "the lust of the flesh and the lust of the eyes" (1 John 2:16) and is always off-limits, regardless of whether a spouse approves.

## Examples

### Example 1: Discussing a New Sexual Practice

A couple is considering trying oral sex. They examine whether it honors God, is exclusive to their marriage, is loving and mutually consensual, strengthens their emotional, spiritual, and physical oneness, and is agreed upon by both. If both freely agree and it passes all five principles, the Bible does not explicitly forbid it, though couples should seek wisdom from Scripture and godly counsel.

### Example 2: A Spouse Feels Uncomfortable With a Request

If one spouse asks the other to try something the other finds uncomfortable or believes is wrong, mutual consent (1 Corinthians 7:5) means the request should not be pursued. Neither spouse should be pressured.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Apply the five biblical principles (God-honoring, exclusive, loving, mutually agreed upon, unifying) to every sexual act within marriage
- ✅ Seek mutual consent before and during any sexual act
- ✅ View sex as an act of unity and mutual yielding, not personal gratification
- ✅ Communicate openly and lovingly about desires and boundaries
- ❌ Do not engage in adultery, swinging, threesomes, or any sexual activity involving third parties, even with spousal approval
- ❌ Do not view or use pornography in any form within the marriage
- ❌ Do not pressure or coerce a spouse into any sexual act they are uncomfortable with

## Keep In Mind

- The Bible provides freedom for consensual sexual expression within the boundaries of the five principles (Hebrews 13:4; 1 Corinthians 7:4–5)
- Mutual consent applies equally to abstaining from sex and to deciding what is done sexually (1 Corinthians 7:5)
- Each couple must prayerfully apply these principles to their own marriage, seeking God's wisdom through Scripture

## Security & Safety Notes

- Neither spouse should be coerced into any sexual act they are not comfortable with; mutual consent is non-negotiable
- Any practice that involves adultery, pornography, or third parties is explicitly sinful regardless of consent
- Couples experiencing conflict over sexual boundaries should seek pastoral or professional Christian counsel

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** One spouse uses scripture or authority to pressure the other into an unwanted sexual act
  **Solution:** Apply 1 Corinthians 7:3–4 — each spouse yields their body lovingly to the other, not by force; mutual consent must always be present

- **Problem:** A couple assumes anything not explicitly forbidden in the Bible is automatically permissible
  **Solution:** Apply the five overarching principles (God-honoring, exclusive, loving, unifying, mutual) to every act before assuming it is acceptable

- **Problem:** A couple engages in adultery or brings in a third party, believing spousal consent makes it acceptable
  **Solution:** Adultery and sexual immorality (Galatians 5:19; Ephesians 5:3; Colossians 3:5; 1 Thessalonians 4:3) are always sin, regardless of whether a spouse approves or participates
