# Get Rid of These Sexual Sins

## Overview

This sermon by Donald Franz addresses the destructive nature of sexual sin—including fornication, adultery, pornography, and lust—and provides a biblical roadmap for repentance, purity, and freedom. It emphasizes that sexual sin is not a private matter: it wounds families, future generations, and the believer's God-given purpose. The message calls listeners to renounce past sin, break spiritual strongholds, and walk in holiness through intentional boundaries, accountability, and prayer.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you sense that sexual sin or temptation is damaging your marriage, family, or relationship with God.
- When you want to understand how sexual sin affects future generations and why it must be confronted.
- When you are ready to renounce past sexual sin, break soul ties, and pursue purity with intentional boundaries.

## Steps

### Step 1: Set Boundaries with the Opposite Sex

Establish clear, practical rules that protect your heart and your marriage. Examples include avoiding being alone in private settings with members of the opposite sex, such as closed offices, late-night meetings, or isolated locations; not providing transportation alone to members of the opposite sex unless another adult is present; and committing to transparency with your spouse about all relationships and interactions. Boundaries act as a spiritual hedge and remove opportunities for the enemy to exploit weakness.

### Step 2: Think About the Consequences

Before making any decision, ask yourself what the worst possible outcome could be. A brief moment of sexual sin can cost a lifetime of trust, family stability, financial peace, and eternal purpose. Consider how your actions affect your children, your spouse, and the church community.

### Step 3: Confess Temptation and Seek Help

Do not hide when you are being tempted. Tell your pastor, your church leader, and your spouse so they can pray with you and hold you accountable. Darkness loses its power when temptation is brought into the light. A mature marriage is one where spouses can confess struggles and pray together.

### Step 4: Make a Pact with Your Eyes

Lust begins with sight. Commit to not looking at what you should not see, just as Joseph refused to look at Potiphar's wife. Replace inappropriate focus with Scripture, such as Psalm 101:3: "I will not set before my eyes an unjust thing."

### Step 5: Change Your Thought Patterns

Your mind is the most powerful sexual organ. When an inappropriate image or thought enters your mind, do not dwell on it or nurture it in your mind. Replace it immediately with godly content. Guard your heart because sooner or later what you entertain in your mind will become desire, and desire will become action.

### Step 6: Ask for Help and Build Community

Isolation makes destruction easier. Find a trusted mentor, pastor, or Christian friend who can walk with you. You were not designed to fight this battle alone. The body of Christ exists to carry one another through temptation and healing.

### Step 7: Plan Your Escape and Flee

Do not try to overcome temptation through reasoning or debate. Run from it. Paul commands in 1 Corinthians 6:18 to flee sexual immorality. Joseph ran from Potiphar's wife. Solomon warns in Proverbs to keep your path far from the adulteress. Remove yourself from the situation immediately.

### Step 8: Watch and Pray

Stay spiritually alert through prayer. Ask God to protect you, and ask your wife and your church leaders to pray for you. Jesus said in Matthew 26:41 to watch and pray so you do not fall into temptation. Pride in your own strength will lead you straight into failure.

## Examples

### Example 1: Joseph and Potiphar's Wife

Joseph had a great dream and purpose from God, but Potiphar's wife used seduction to try to destroy that calling. She did not love Joseph; she was an instrument of the enemy. Joseph's response was to flee rather than reason with temptation. His decision preserved his destiny, while those who yield to similar pressure often lose everything.

### Example 2: David and Bathsheba

King David, described as a man after God's own heart, committed adultery and then tried to cover it up with lies and murder. The consequences were generational: multiple sons died, his family was shattered, and the sword never departed from his house. This shows that even the most anointed people can fall, and remaining in unrepentant sin—not the sin itself—defines the outcome.

## Best Practices

- Set non-negotiable boundaries before temptation arrives.
- Plan for success in your marriage and sexuality before problems occur.
- Guard your eyes and mind as your first line of defense.
- Confess temptation early rather than waiting until you have fallen.
- Build a network of accountability so you are never fighting alone.
- Flee immediately when you recognize a dangerous situation.
- Renew your mind daily with Scripture so godly thoughts replace harmful ones.
- Pray specifically for protection over your marriage, children, and sexual purity.

## Keep In Mind

- Most people experience sexual temptation, so you are not alone—but you are responsible for your choices.
- No one plans to fail in their marriage or sex life, yet many do because they never planned for success.
- A bad sexual decision can cause pain for the rest of your life.
- Sexual sin is never private; it affects your children, parents, spouse, and entire community.
- Iniquity—the root system of sin—can pass to future generations if it is not repented of and renounced.
- Making a mistake does not define you; staying in that mistake does.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never keep sexual sin hidden in darkness. Confession breaks its power and prevents generational iniquity.
- Do not entrust yourself to people who are unfaithful in their marriages; their betrayal pattern will likely extend to other areas.
- Beware of pride that says, "That will never happen to me." Pride is often the doorway into temptation.
- If you have been abused, trafficked, or forced into sexual sin, seek pastoral counseling and professional therapy from a licensed Christian counselor immediately—freedom requires both spiritual and practical support.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing sexual sin is a private matter that only affects you.
  **Solution:** Remember that your choices ripple into your family, children, and future generations. Sexual sin destroys more than the individual.
- **Problem:** Trying to reason with temptation instead of fleeing.
  **Solution:** Do not negotiate with the enemy. Run immediately. Joseph fled Potiphar's wife; do not stay to "handle it" or "win her for Christ."
- **Problem:** Waiting until you have fallen to ask for help.
  **Solution:** Confess temptation the moment it appears. It is easier to stop a spark than to extinguish a forest fire.
- **Problem:** Assuming you are exempt because of your position, age, or spirituality.
  **Solution:** No one is exempt—not pastors, not mature Christians, not long-time believers. The enemy studies your weaknesses and attacks at vulnerable moments.
