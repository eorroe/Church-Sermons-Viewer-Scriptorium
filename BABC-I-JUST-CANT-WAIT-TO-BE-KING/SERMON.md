# I Just Can't Wait To Be King

## Overview

**Description:** This sermon teaches believers to surrender their ambition to God by refusing to scheme, lie, cheat, or fabricate their way into a position He has not assigned to them, and instead to wait for His clearance, celebrate the blessings He gives others without turning them into a verdict about themselves, and submit every desire to King Jesus so that God exalts them in His own time.

The message is drawn from the preached Word: 1 Kings 1 (NIV), where Adonijah exalts himself, builds his own crowd, and throws his own feast while God has already chosen Solomon, and it follows the congregation's reading of 1 Corinthians 11:23-34. This sermon exists because the same pull that made Adonijah say "I will be king" still works in believers who want a good thing that God has not given them to carry. It provides a concrete, this-week set of actions for aligning ambition with God's will, waiting on God as disciplined devotion instead of manufacturing a season, and climbing the highest mountain of faith: celebrating someone else's blessing without comparison.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you have applied, auditioned, or competed for a position and were not hired or not chosen
- When you are tempted to exaggerate, manipulate, back-channel, or fabricate in order to get ahead
- When a crowd of people is applauding you but you have no peace from God about the move you are making
- When a peer, sibling, or colleague receives the blessing, promotion, or platform you prayed for
- When you have been waiting on God for a door to open and the waiting feels like doing nothing
- When your ambition and God's will feel misaligned and you need a prayer that resets what you want
- When you are sitting in a seat that carries a title you wanted but an assignment you were never anointed for

## Steps

### Step 1: Name the Good Desire You Are Reaching For and Ask Whether It Is Assigned to You

This week, write down the specific position, relationship, promotion, platform, or open door you want most. Under each one, answer three questions in writing: (1) Is this good in itself? (2) Has God assigned this to me, or am I reaching for a good thing He never assigned to my life? (3) If it is good but not God's best for me, am I willing to release it today? Adonijah wanted to be king, and that was not a bad thing — it simply was not God's assignment for him (1 Kings 1:5). Pray the sermon's prayer out loud: "Lord, any way you bless me, I'll be satisfied."

### Step 2: Stop Scheming — Remove Every Shortcut From Your Plan

Audit the last 90 days for any scheming, exaggeration, fabrication, or manipulation you used or planned to use to get ahead. Confess each item to God, then correct every record you can still correct — a résumé, an application, or a claim made in conversation. Set one concrete boundary for this week: no inflated claims on a résumé or application, no back-channel maneuvering, no negative talk about the person currently holding the position. Post Psalm 37:23 — "The steps of the good man are ordered by the Lord, and he delighteth in his way" — in a place you see every morning, such as your bathroom mirror or your phone's home screen, and each morning pray: "If it is God's best for my life, I don't have to scheme to have it; I just walk into my season."

### Step 3: Trade Human Support for God's Clearance

List the people currently backing your pursuit, as Joab and Abiathar backed Adonijah (1 Kings 1:7). Do not ask them to campaign, endorse, or advance you. Instead, spend this week waiting on the Lord in disciplined devotion: set aside 20 minutes every day for Scripture and prayer, and ask God to make His will unmistakable. Write down the specific confirmation you are asking God to give you before you move — an open door that cannot be forced, a peace that does not move, or a clear no — and do not move until you see it. Waiting is not passivity; waiting is disciplined devotion that says, "I can't do it all by myself; I need God to clear the pathway."

### Step 4: Confirm That Position and Purpose Are Aligned Before You Sit Down

For any seat you are pursuing, write one sentence that answers: What job did God design this seat to accomplish, and am I anointed for that assignment? Adonijah could have occupied the throne, but he was not anointed for the assignment that came with it; God wanted Solomon because God had a job only Solomon could do. Test the seat with these questions: Does it let me serve, or does it demand that others see me? Does it require self-exaltation, or does it require humility? Does it seek everyone's applause, or the applause of the One who sits high and looks low? If you cannot write that one sentence, the seat is not yours — get up with all deliberate speed.

### Step 5: Celebrate Someone Else's Blessing Without Interpreting It as a Commentary About You

Identify the specific person whose blessing, promotion, or upgrade has been bothering you. This week, do these three concrete actions: (1) send them a written note of genuine congratulations; (2) pray for them by name every day for seven days; (3) say out loud when comparison rises, "God's will is personal, and what God has for me is for me." Do not read their upgrade as a statement about what you are not. Adonijah wanted to be king, but God had another king in mind — Solomon — and the highest mountain of faith is learning to celebrate someone else's blessing without making it a verdict on you.

### Step 6: Refuse to Let Disappointment Become an Identity Crisis

When the "no" arrives, take out two columns on one page. In the left column write the fact: "I did not get this." In the right column write the truth: "What God has for me is for me, and nobody else can carry it the way I can." Then take one physical action that re-anchors your identity in Christ rather than in the rejection: serve one person, give away one possession, or worship for 20 minutes. You did everything right and still got the "no" — do not allow that disappointment to become an identity crisis.

### Step 7: Submit Your Ambition to King Jesus

Pray the sermon's surrender prayer every day this week, following the other King who said, "Not My will, but Thine be done" (Luke 22:42). Pray it specifically: "If You want me to have it, give it to me. If You don't want me to have it, take the desire away. If You want me to go through that door, open it so widely that I fall through it. If You don't want me to go through it, close it so tightly that I can't pry it open. If You want me to press go on that relationship, make it abundantly clear; if not, remove it." Then close the week with one act of humble service to someone who cannot repay you, because the way to exaltation is the way down, and God will exalt you in due time.

## Examples

### Example 1: Adonijah Exalts Himself (1 Kings 1:5-10)

Adonijah, whose mother was Haggith, put himself forward and said, "I will be king." He got chariots and horses ready with 50 men to run ahead of him, conferred with Joab and Abiathar, sacrificed at the stone of Zoheleth near En Rogel, and invited his brothers and the royal officials of Judah to his own feast. What he did not do was ask God. This is the sermon's warning picture: a self-made procession, a self-made crowd, and a self-made feast that God never authorized.

### Example 2: Support Without God's Approval (1 Kings 1:7-8, 11-14)

Joab and Abiathar gave Adonijah their support, but Zadok the priest, Benaiah, Nathan the prophet, Shimei and Rei, David's special guard, and Solomon did not join him. Adonijah had real human endorsement and still ended up on the outskirts of the nation of Israel he was born into, because other people's support without God's approval always leads to failure. Human applause can be loud while God's arms are folded.

### Example 3: Solomon and the Shotgun Coronation (1 Kings 1:38-40)

Nathan and Bathsheba went to David and appealed to the oath he had sworn: "Surely Solomon your son shall be king after me, and he will sit on my throne." Solomon was crowned the third king of Israel — a coronation arranged by God's oath to David and God's prophet Nathan, not by a campaign. Solomon's season arrived because God had chosen him, not because he built momentum for himself.

### Example 4: David — When God Hires You

For 40 years David served as Israel's second king. God chose David when nobody else thought he had what it took and when nobody was looking at him with king potential. As the preacher's childhood pastor, Rev. J. K. L. Alexander, said: "When God hires you, can't nobody fire you; but if God fires you, won't nobody hire you." If God gives a blessing to you, nobody can take that blessing away.

### Example 5: The Blessing That Landed on the Other Side of the Street

You come to church every Sunday, you pay your tithes, you show up for Bible study, and your prayer still has no answer — while the neighbors who never attend anyone's church have, from your perspective, every blessing lined up on their side of the street. That comparison is the contaminated territory the sermon names. God's will is not universal; it is personal. What God has for you will wear differently on you: a different peace, a different joy, a different confidence, because it was designed for you.

### Example 6: The Colleague Who Received the Promotion You Wanted

You applied, you prepared, you were rejected, and someone else was celebrated. The response this sermon teaches is not silent resentment but active celebration: congratulate them in writing, pray for them by name for a week, and discipline your tongue from critical commentary. Their blessing is not a commentary on your worth, and your season is not cancelled by theirs.

### Example 7: Adonijah Points to Another King

Adonijah said, "I will be king," but there is another King who said, "Not My will, but Thine be done." Adonijah threw a feast; the other King took bread and said, "This is My body which is broken for you." Adonijah sought a crown; the other King received a crown of thorns. Adonijah rode in chariots; the other King rode on a donkey. Adonijah tried to find his way up; the other King came down from heaven, humbled Himself to death on the cross, and rose Sunday morning with all power in His hands. Submit your ambition to Him and He will exalt you in due time.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Write down the exact position, relationship, or blessing you want and ask God out loud whether it is assigned to you before you pursue it
- ✅ Let the steps of the righteous be ordered by the Lord and walk into your season instead of manufacturing one
- ✅ Confess and remove every scheme, exaggeration, fabrication, and manipulation you have used to get ahead
- ✅ Submit your progress to God for clearance and ask Him to open or close the door unmistakably
- ✅ Match the position to the assignment: identify the job the seat carries and confirm your anointing for it
- ✅ Remember that God chose David when nobody was looking, and that a blessing God gives cannot be taken away
- ✅ Celebrate the other person's blessing out loud, in writing, and in prayer
- ✅ Pray daily, by name, for the person you are most tempted to envy
- ✅ Declare "God's will is personal" whenever you catch yourself comparing
- ✅ Treat waiting as active devotion: fixed prayer times, daily Scripture, and service while you wait
- ✅ Take the low road — humble yourself under God's hand and let Him exalt you in due time
- ✅ Write the fact and the truth side by side after every rejection
- ❌ Do not scheme, lie, connive, cheat, or fabricate to obtain God's best for your life
- ❌ Do not organize your own coronation — your own procession, your own feast, your own crowd — before God speaks
- ❌ Do not treat human applause as clearance from God
- ❌ Do not sit in a place you have not been anointed to fill
- ❌ Do not interpret someone else's upgrade as a statement about what you are not
- ❌ Do not slide into the contaminated territory of comparison
- ❌ Do not allow disappointment to harden into an identity crisis
- ❌ Do not conclude that because someone else was blessed, God must have forgotten you

## Keep In Mind

- Achieving God's best for your life does not require your scheming; it requires your obedience
- Adonijah's problem was not ambition; his ambition was not aligned with God's will
- Adonijah wanted a good thing that was simply not his — good is not always good for you
- Other people can pat you on the back while God is saying, "That is not My will for your life"
- If God opens a door for you, nobody else can stop you from walking through it; if God gives a blessing to you, nobody can take that blessing away
- You can want a good thing that God has never assigned to your life — that is the danger the sermon names: ambition that is not aligned with God's will
- The highest mountain of faith is learning to celebrate someone else's blessing without interpreting it as a commentary about you
- Once you read other people's blessings as being about you, comparison takes over and God's will starts to look universal when it is personal
- What God has for you is for you — nobody else can have it, and if someone else has a similar blessing, they cannot carry out that blessing the way you do it
- A blessing God designed for you wears differently: the peace is different, the joy is different, the confidence is different
- "I don't want nothing that ain't for me" is a position of strength, not resignation
- Waiting is not passivity; waiting is disciplined devotion that says, "I need God to open the door, clear the pathway, and point the direction"
- The way to exaltation is the way down: serve, submit, and let God exalt you in due time

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not take on debt, quit a job, or relocate to fund a position God has not yet confirmed
- Keep confidences: do not broadcast the details of a job opening, a hiring process, or a private conversation to people who will campaign for you
- Protect private information you learn while waiting, including compensation, personnel matters, and the plans of others
- Do not compromise conviction, honesty, or integrity to keep the support of your crowd
- Delay public announcements until God confirms the door, so that the applause of people cannot drive the decision
- Tell one trusted believer or mentor who will ask you, each week, whether God has actually spoken
- Guard your inputs: for 30 days, mute or unfollow social media accounts and relationships that fuel comparison and resentment
- Do not vent your disappointment to people who will fuel it; share it with one accountable believer, pastor, or counselor
- Never speak negatively about the person who received the blessing you wanted, in person or online
- If waiting has produced despair, chronic anxiety, or loss of sleep, seek pastoral or professional help — waiting well includes caring for your body and mind
- Do not quit a current responsibility out of frustration; keep serving in it faithfully until God confirms your next placement
- Keep your private prayers private rather than broadcasting disappointment publicly while you wait

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You have a crowd cheering for you, like Joab and Abiathar, but no peace from God.
  **Solution:** Stop collecting endorsements. Ask God for one unmistakable confirmation, write it down, and wait until you see it.
- **Problem:** You inflate your story, résumé, or gifting so that you will be chosen.
  **Solution:** Confess it, correct the record this week, and let the steps of the righteous be ordered by the Lord.
- **Problem:** You want the seat because of the title.
  **Solution:** Write out the assignment the seat carries. If you cannot serve others from it, get up with all deliberate speed.
- **Problem:** You read your rejection as proof that God has forgotten you.
  **Solution:** Remember God chose David when nobody was looking at him. Ask God to make His personal will for you clear again.
- **Problem:** You are chasing a good thing that is not yours.
  **Solution:** Pray "any way you bless me, I'll be satisfied," and release what God has not given you to carry.
- **Problem:** Comparison slides into bitterness when a peer is blessed.
  **Solution:** Celebrate them out loud, pray for them by name for seven days, and declare that God's will is personal.
- **Problem:** Waiting turns into passivity and nothing gets prayed.
  **Solution:** Keep fixed daily times of prayer and Scripture, and serve where you are until God clears the pathway.
- **Problem:** A "no" becomes "I am a nobody."
  **Solution:** Write the fact and the truth side by side, and let God define you by His assignment rather than by the rejection.
- **Problem:** You want a blessing because it looks good on someone else.
  **Solution:** Ask God for what He designed for you and pray, "Any way You bless me, I'll be satisfied."
- **Problem:** You are exalting yourself the way Adonijah did.
  **Solution:** Follow the other King: humble yourself, serve, submit your ambition to Jesus, and let God exalt you in due time.
- **Problem:** You sit in a position for the title while the assignment goes unfilled.
  **Solution:** Get up with all deliberate speed and ask God to place you where your purpose and position are aligned.
