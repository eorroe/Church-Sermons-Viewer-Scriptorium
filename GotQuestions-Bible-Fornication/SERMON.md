# What Does the Bible Say About Fornication?

## Overview

This sermon teaches what the Bible defines as fornication and why it is considered sexual immorality. It explains that fornication—from the Greek term *porneia*—covers any sexual activity outside of marriage. The sermon further instructs believers to flee all sexual sin, recognizing the body as the temple of the Holy Spirit, and warns that those who persist in fornication will not inherit the kingdom of heaven.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When studying or teaching on the biblical definition of sexual immorality
- When helping someone understand what the Bible says about fornication and sexual purity
- When preparing for or defending the biblical view of marriage and the marriage bed
- When addressing spiritual unfaithfulness or the symbolic use of fornication in Scripture

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand What the Bible Defines as Fornication

Fornication is the biblical term for any sexual activity outside the marriage covenant between one man and one woman. The word comes from the Greek term *porneia* (the root of the English word "pornography") and is a general term for sexual immorality. It includes adultery—a married person engaging in sexual relations with someone other than their spouse—as well as any sexual relations before or outside of marriage. For example, in 1 Corinthians 5:1, fornication describes a man sleeping with his father's wife.

### Step 2: Recognize the Biblical Condemnation of Fornication

Fornication is condemned throughout Scripture. In Romans 1:29, the apostle Paul lists fornication among horrendous sins. Jesus mentions fornication in a list of corrupting sins that come from within a person's heart (Matthew 15:19; Mark 7:21). The sin of fornication violates the seventh commandment (Exodus 20:14), which was intended to safeguard the integrity of the family and the marriage union. Hebrews 13:4 states: "Marriage is to be honored by all and the marriage bed kept undefiled, because God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterers" (CSB). Those who persistently indulge in fornication will not inherit the kingdom of heaven (1 Corinthians 6:9).

### Step 3: Abstain from All Forms of Sexual Immorality

Abstaining from fornication was one of four conditions required of Gentiles to be accepted into the early church at the Jerusalem conference (Acts 15:29). Believers are instructed to run from every kind of sexual sin: "Let there be no sexual immorality, impurity, or greed among you. Such sins have no place among God's people" (Ephesians 5:3, NLT). This instruction is reinforced in 1 Corinthians 7:2 and 1 Thessalonians 4:3.

### Step 4: Understand the Unique Nature of Sexual Sin

According to 1 Corinthians 6:18, sexual sin is unique in that it is a sin against one's own body. Believers are members of the body of Christ (1 Corinthians 6:12–17), and an immoral sexual union violates the believer's mystical "one flesh" union with Jesus Christ (1 Corinthians 6:15). A Christian's body is also the temple of the Holy Spirit—a holy place that belongs to Jesus Christ (1 Corinthians 6:19). Christians have been redeemed by God for good and righteous works and not for sin (Ephesians 2:10).

### Step 5: Recognize the Figurative Use of Fornication

In both the Old and New Testaments, Scripture uses fornication figuratively to describe the corruption of God's people through idolatry and unfaithfulness. Both Israel and the church are depicted as the Lord's wife (Israel in the Old Testament) and the Bride of Christ (the Church in the New Testament). When God's people engage in idolatry and unfaithfulness, God calls this sin "fornication" (Jeremiah 2:20–36; Ezekiel 16:15–43; Revelation 2:14, 20–22; 17:1–18; 18:2–9).

## Examples

### Example 1: The Corinthian Church's Tolerance of Fornication

In 1 Corinthians 5:1, the apostle Paul addresses a case of fornication in the Corinthian church where a man was sleeping with his father's wife. Paul's reaction makes clear that even the church at large is not to tolerate sexual immorality.

### Example 2: Israel as God's Unfaithful Bride

In Ezekiel 16:15–43, God describes Jerusalem as His wife who committed spiritual fornication through idolatry and reliance on foreign nations rather than on Him. This illustrates the biblical pattern of using fornication as a symbol for abandoning God.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Study the biblical definition of fornication so you can accurately recognize and address sexual immorality
- ✅ Flee from every kind of sexual sin rather than attempting to manage or control it
- ✅ Honor the marriage bed and keep it undefiled in all circumstances
- ✅ Remember that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit and belongs to Christ alone
- ✅ Recognize spiritual unfaithfulness as a form of fornication against God

## Keep In Mind

- Fornication is a broad biblical term covering all sexual immorality, not just one specific act
- God designed sex for marriage, and marriage is intended to be a holy, honored institution
- Sexual sin is uniquely described as sinning against one's own body
- God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterers
- Those who persistently practice fornication will not inherit the kingdom of heaven

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not engage in sexual activity outside marriage, view pornography, or use your body for any sexual purpose outside the one-flesh union with your spouse, as your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit
- You have been redeemed by God for good and righteous works, not for sin

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Dismissing fornication as only referring to certain acts while excusing others
  **Solution:** Understand that fornication is a broad term for any sexual immorality outside of marriage, as defined by Scripture
- **Problem:** Believing sexual sin is "just like any other sin" without unique consequences
  **Solution:** Recognize that sexual sin is specifically a sin against one's own body and against Christ Himself
- **Problem:** Ignoring the figurative use of fornication to describe spiritual unfaithfulness
  **Solution:** Read passages such as Ezekiel 16, Jeremiah 2, and Revelation 17–18 to understand how God views idolatry and apostasy as spiritual fornication
