# Is Masturbation Ever Not a Sin in Marriage? (GotQuestions)

## Overview

This sermon examines two specific scenarios where married Christians question whether masturbation constitutes sin: when spouses are separated for at least one month with mutual consent between husband and wife, and when married couples engage in mutual masturbation as part of their physical intimacy together. Rather than trying to find exceptions to God's design for sexual expression, it calls believers to search the Scriptures, pray, and follow the leading of the Holy Spirit—always communicating openly with one's spouse and being fully convinced before acting, meaning you have prayed, studied Scripture, sought counsel from mature believers grounded in God's Word—those walking with Christ for at least 2 years, accountable to church leadership, and demonstrating the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22-23)—and your conscience is clear before God that the activity honors Him—grounded in the clear commands of 1 Corinthians 7:3–5 (fulfill each other's marital duty), Romans 8:12 (do not live according to the flesh—the fallen, sinful nature within you (Galatians 5:17)—and Romans 14:5 and 14:23 (be fully convinced in your own mind; anything not done in faith is sin).

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When a married Christian is separated from their spouse for at least one month and is questioning whether masturbation is permissible
- When a married couple is exploring mutual masturbation as part of their physical intimacy and wants biblical clarity
- When a believer feels the temptation to seek loopholes in otherwise solid biblical standards regarding sexual purity
- When a Christian needs to discern an action through the lens of Romans 14:5 and Romans 14:23. "Fully convinced" means you have prayed, studied Scripture, sought counsel from mature believers grounded in God's Word, and your conscience is clear before God that the activity honors Him. "From faith" means you act without doubt that the action is right.
- When a believer struggles with sexual temptation and wants to understand what Scripture commands regarding fleeing temptation versus managing it

## Steps

### Step 1: Determine if Sexual Release Is Absolutely Necessary

Before considering any action, honestly evaluate whether sexual release is required because you cannot resolve temptation through prayer, Scripture, accountability, or other means the Bible provides. The Bible does not tell believers to find ways to make temptation less powerful; it commands them to flee from it (1 Corinthians 6:18; 10:13; 2 Timothy 2:22). Trust that the power of the indwelling Holy Spirit (1 John 4:4) enables you to resist temptation without relying on physical release as a substitute for God's strength.

### Step 2: Apply the Principle of Spousal Consent and Mutual Permission

If spouses are separated for at least one month due to military deployment, work assignment, or other circumstances where spouses will be physically apart for a period lasting at least one month, mutual consent and agreement between husband and wife are essential. The principle of 1 Corinthians 7:4—that a spouse's body is not solely their own—must be honored. Any consideration of masturbation in this context requires open, honest communication about motives and feelings, and explicit agreement between both husband and wife.

### Step 3: Eliminate Clearly Sinful Elements

Regardless of the scenario, ensure that pornography and lustful thoughts toward anyone other than one's spouse are entirely absent. The focus must be solely on one's spouse, not on sexual fantasy or anyone other than the spouse. If either pornography or lustful thoughts about anyone other than one's spouse is present, the act cannot be considered in good faith before God, as these are explicitly sinful (Matthew 5:28; 1 John 2:16).

### Step 4: Assess Whether the Act Reflects Selflessness or Self-Centeredness

For couples considering mutual masturbation within marriage, evaluate the biblical focus of sex: it is about giving one's body to the spouse (1 Corinthians 7:4), not focusing on oneself. Masturbation, even when done together, is directed toward self-gratification rather than the self-giving love—giving one's body to serve the spouse (1 Corinthians 7:4)—that should characterize marital intimacy. Ask honestly: does this act give your body to your spouse for their good, as 1 Corinthians 7:4 teaches, or does it primarily serve your own personal sexual gratification?

### Step 5: Search the Scriptures, Pray, and Follow the Holy Spirit

Rather than seeking loopholes, intentionally search the Scriptures related to the situation, pray for wisdom, and seek the leading of the Holy Spirit. Romans 14:5 calls believers to be "fully convinced" in their own minds before they act, and Romans 14:23 states that "everything that does not come from faith is sin." If any doubt remains about whether the action honors God, refrain from it entirely until you are fully convinced before God through Scripture and prayer that it is right.

### Step 6: Fulfill and Do Not Deprive Your Spouse (With Mutual Consent)

Honor the clear biblical commands regarding marital duties. The husband should fulfill his marital duty to his wife, and the wife to her husband (1 Corinthians 7:3). Do not deprive each other except by mutual consent and for a time, so that you devote yourselves to prayer; then come together again (1 Corinthians 7:5). Consistent, mutual fulfillment in marriage, as designed by God in Genesis 2:24 and affirmed in Hebrews 13:4, is the standard for sexual expression.

### Step 7: Walk in the Spirit, Not in the Fallen Sinful Nature (Romans 8:12; Galatians 5:16-17)

Remember the overarching command: "We have an obligation—but it is not to the flesh, to live according to it" (Romans 8:12). Believers are called to live by the Spirit and not gratify the desires of the flesh—the fallen, sinful nature (Galatians 5:16-17). Any consideration of masturbation must be weighed against this fundamental call to Spirit-led living.

## Examples

### Example 1: Military Spouse Separated from Their Partner

A husband is deployed overseas for six months and struggles with persistent sexual temptation—repeated, unwanted lustful thoughts or urges that you cannot overcome through prayer and accountability that he cannot resolve through other means. He discusses the matter with his wife, and they mutually agree to permit masturbation as a means of relieving tension, with no pornography or lustful thoughts toward anyone other than one's spouse involved. Before acting, he honestly assesses whether sexual release is necessary or whether the person flees temptation through other means such as prayer, Scripture, or accountability. He prays, searches Scripture, and asks whether he is fully convinced before God. Ultimately, the decision rests between him, his wife, and God—but it must still align with the clear teachings of Scripture regarding sexual purity and the one-flesh union of marriage.

### Example 2: Married Couple Exploring Mutual Masturbation

A husband and wife, in enjoying their time together, consider adding mutual masturbation to their physical intimacy. They ask: does this act focus on my own gratification, or does it give my body to my spouse for their good (1 Corinthians 7:4)? They study 1 Corinthians 7:4, pray together, and consider whether they are fully convinced it honors God and each other. They recognize that what a married couple does by mutual consent is between the couple and God, but they must seek God's revealed will through Scripture—not simply follow their own feelings or desires.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Carefully assess whether sexual release is necessary before considering any action
- ✅ Obtain explicit, freely given mutual consent from one's spouse before any departure from established biblical standards
- ✅ Eliminate all pornography and lustful thoughts toward others from any consideration
- ✅ Search Scripture, pray, and seek the Holy Spirit's leading before acting
- ✅ Be fully convinced in your own mind—through prayer, Scripture study, and counsel from mature believers—before proceeding (Romans 14:5)
- ✅ Ensure every action comes from faith in God's revealed will, not from doubt or uncertainty about whether it honors Him (Romans 14:23)
- ✅ Communicate openly and regularly with your spouse about sexual needs and boundaries
- ✅ Fulfill your marital duty to your spouse regularly and generously (1 Corinthians 7:3)
- ✅ Live according to the Spirit, not according to the flesh—the fallen, sinful nature within you (Galatians 5:17) (Romans 8:12)
- ❌ Do not search for exceptions to otherwise clear biblical standards in order to justify a behavior you desire
- ❌ Do not assume you are incapable of resisting sexual temptation without physical release; the indwelling Holy Spirit (1 John 4:4) empowers you to flee temptation as the Bible commands (1 Corinthians 6:18; 2 Timothy 2:22)
- ❌ Do not neglect the power of the indwelling Holy Spirit (1 John 4:4) as your source of strength to flee temptation rather than accommodate it
- ❌ Do not make decisions about sexual behavior from doubt or without being fully convinced before God through Scripture and prayer
- ❌ Do not deprive your spouse of marital intimacy without their explicit consent and a specific, temporary, godly purpose: specifically, a voluntarily agreed-upon period of prayer focused on seeking God together

## Keep In Mind

- The Bible's answer to sexual temptation is to flee from it (1 Corinthians 6:18; 2 Timothy 2:22), not to manage or lessen its power by seeking physical release (1 Corinthians 6:18; 10:13; 2 Timothy 2:22).
- The indwelling Holy Spirit (1 John 4:4) empowers believers to resist temptation by fleeing it and praying, without relying on physical release as a substitute for God's strength.
- The biblical focus of sex in marriage is the other person—each spouse giving their body to the other (1 Corinthians 7:4)—not personal self-gratification.
- Romans 14:5 and 14:23 establish that believers must be fully convinced in their own minds, and everything not done in faith is sin—if any doubt exists about whether the action honors God, refrain entirely.
- Married couples have clear, non-negotiable commands: fulfill each other's marital duties sexually (1 Corinthians 7:3), do not deprive each other except by mutual consent for a specific, temporary, prayer-focused purpose (1 Corinthians 7:5), and do not live according to the flesh (Romans 8:12).
- What a married couple does by mutual consent is between the couple and God, but they must seek God's revealed will through Scripture, not simply follow their own feelings or cultural norms.

## Security & Safety Notes

- No personal information, private details, or confidential marital matters are required to engage with the biblical principles in this sermon; all guidance comes from Scripture alone.
- Decisions about marital intimacy are private between a husband and wife and should remain between the husband, wife, and God; do not share specifics of your intimate life publicly or with anyone outside the marriage without both spouses' explicit agreement.
- If struggling with sexual addiction, pornography, or compulsive behavior, seek counsel from a trusted pastor or Christian counselor with a proven track record of biblical counseling in a safe, confidential setting.
- This sermon addresses general biblical principles, not medical, psychological, or therapeutic advice; consult qualified professionals for those needs.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Seeking loopholes in biblical standards to justify masturbation in marriage
  **Solution:** Instead of justifying, search the Scriptures, pray, and ask whether you are fully convinced before God. Romans 14:23 makes clear that anything not done in faith is sin.
- **Problem:** Believing you are incapable of resisting sexual temptation without physical release
  **Solution:** Rest in the power of the indwelling Holy Spirit (1 John 4:4) and remember the Bible commands you to flee temptation, not accommodate it (1 Corinthians 6:18; 10:13; 2 Timothy 2:22).
- **Problem:** Using masturbation, even with spousal consent, as a replacement for genuine marital intimacy
  **Solution:** Fulfill your marital duty to your spouse (1 Corinthians 7:3–5) and ensure that physical intimacy remains centered on giving yourself to your partner, not on self-gratification.
- **Problem:** Acting on sexual questions without being fully convinced before God
  **Solution:** Apply Romans 14:5 and Romans 14:23: be fully convinced in your own mind, and ensure the action comes from faith — but always within the boundaries of God's design for marriage. If you cannot honestly say you are fully convinced, do not proceed.
- **Problem:** Neglecting open communication with your spouse about sexual boundaries and needs
  **Solution:** Make honest, ongoing communication with your spouse a priority. The clarity and peace that come from mutual understanding and freely given consent between spouses is itself a safeguard against sin.
