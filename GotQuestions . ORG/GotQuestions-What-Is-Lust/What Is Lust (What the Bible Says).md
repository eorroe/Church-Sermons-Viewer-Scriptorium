# What Is Lust? (What the Bible Says)

## Overview

This sermon teaches the biblical meaning of lust. It contrasts lust with the Christian call to holy living—living in obedience to God's commands, set apart from sin (1 Peter 1:15-16)—and shows how lust—rooted in selfish desire for something that is not yours (Exodus 20:17) and in greed for what belongs to another—stands in direct opposition to selfless love for God and others and to sanctification, the process of becoming more like Christ. It guides believers through practical, scripture-backed steps to either armor themselves against the temptation of lust, or to confess it and seek God's intervention for deliverance.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need to understand what lust is from a biblical perspective and how it differs from worldly definitions
- When studying or teaching on sexual sin, purity, or holy living
- When preparing to combat temptation or seeking deliverance from patterns of lustful thinking
- When a believer wants to live above reproach and conform their thoughts and actions to Scripture

## Steps

### Step 1: Define Lust Biblically

Study and understand what the Bible says about lust. Merriam-Webster defines lust as intense or unrestrained sexual desire, but Scripture defines lust more specifically as intense or unrestrained sexual craving directed toward self-gratification rather than God's design (Matthew 5:27-28). Review Exodus 20:14, 17 and Matthew 5:28. Notice that Jesus teaches even looking at someone with lust is committing adultery in the heart. Job 31:11-12 describes lust as a shameful sin that consumes everything.

### Step 2: Recognize the Heart of Lust

Understand that lust focuses on pleasing yourself at the expense of others and leads to unwholesome actions: adultery, fornication, or using another person for self-gratification—to fulfill selfish desire with no regard for the harm it causes to others or to your relationship with God. Lust is about wanting something that is not yours—including another person's body or another person's spouse—and seeking to possess it for your own selfish gratification. Reflect honestly: have your thoughts, motives, or actions been driven by self-gratification—seeking your own pleasure—rather than by love for God and love for others as yourself (Mark 12:30-31)?

### Step 3: Compare Lust to the Christian Calling

Contrast lust with the call of the Christian faith, which is about selfless love for God and others and holy living that reflects Christ's character—as described in Romans 6:19, 12:1-2; 1 Corinthians 6:19-20; Ephesians 4:24; Colossians 3:12; 1 Thessalonians 4:3-8; and 1 Peter 1:15-16. The goal of every believer is to become more like Jesus each day (2 Corinthians 3:18), putting off the old way of life in which sinful desires controlled your behavior, and conforming thoughts and actions to the standard of Scripture. Lust is in direct opposition to this ideal.

### Step 4: Ready Yourself Against Lust

If lust has not yet taken control of your heart and mind—if you have not yet acted on lustful thoughts or allowed them to become a pattern—do not be complacent. Prepare yourself through a life lived above reproach. Build spiritual habits now: daily prayer, memorizing Scripture, specifically Matthew 5:28 and 1 Corinthians 10:13, entering into accountability relationships with mature believers, and guarding what you see and think—so that you are equipped to resist temptation when it arises.

### Step 5: Confess and Seek God's Intervention

If you currently struggle with lust, it is time to confess your sins to God and ask for His intervention. Confess the specific sin of lust to God, acknowledge the full extent to which it has gripped your heart and mind, and ask God specifically to intervene in your life to break its power. 1 Thessalonians 4:7-8 makes clear that God has called us to be holy, not to live impure lives. Rejecting God's call to holiness is rejecting God Himself, who gives the Holy Spirit to empower you to walk in holiness (1 Thessalonians 4:8). Turn to Him for forgiveness, strength, and transformation so that holiness becomes the mark of your life.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Person Who Has Not Yet Yielded to Lust

A believer who guards their thoughts against lust and lives with integrity, keeping their actions and thoughts aligned with God's Word so that they live above reproach before Him (1 Timothy 3:2), uses this teaching to reinforce their commitment to purity. They study Matthew 5:28 to understand that heart purity matters to God. They establish specific boundaries around media consumption: specifically, installing content filters on all devices and avoiding content that triggers lustful thoughts including media depicting sexual activity outside marriage, pursue accountability relationships with mature believers who will ask hard questions, and walk closely with God through daily Scripture reading and prayer so they are rooted in God's Word and strong in their faith when temptation comes.

### Example 2: The Person Struggling with Lustful Thoughts or Actions

A believer who recognizes that lust has taken root in their life—appearing as a persistent pattern of lustful thoughts or actions—follows Step 5: they confess before God, acknowledge the sin, and ask for God's intervention. They turn to passages like 1 Thessalonians 4:7-8 to remind themselves that God has called them to holiness, not to continue in impure living. They seek accountability with a Christian who is grounded in Scripture, accountable to a local church, and has demonstrated trustworthiness—specifically, the ability to keep confidences—and wisdom in applying Scripture to real-life situations, and rely on the Holy Spirit for the power to break free and live differently.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Study what Scripture explicitly says about lust before relying on personal feelings or cultural definitions
- ✅ Compare your desires against the standard of holy living outlined in the New Testament
- ✅ Memorize and meditate on key verses: Matthew 5:28, Job 31:11-12, 1 Thessalonians 4:7-8
- ✅ Pursue a life lived above reproach before you are tested by temptation
- ✅ Confess lust openly and sincerely to God and seek His intervention
- ✅ Surround yourself with accountable, mature believers who can help you stay on the path of holiness
- ❌ Do not assume that lust is only physical action—Jesus taught that lust in the heart is sin
- ❌ Do not compare yourself to others or assume you are safe because you have not acted on lustful thoughts
- ❌ Do not try to overcome lust in your own strength; rely on God's Holy Spirit for transformation
- ❌ Do not remain in unconfessed sin—refusing to live by God's call to holiness is a rejection of the God who gives His Spirit

## Keep In Mind

- Lust is not merely a physical act; even lustful thoughts are sin in God's eyes (Matthew 5:28)
- No Christian attains perfect sinlessness on this earth, but every Christian is called to pursue holiness as a lifelong process (1 Peter 1:15-16).
- God has called you to holiness and gives His Holy Spirit to empower you to walk in it
- A life lived above reproach before temptation strikes is your best preparation for when it does
- Confession of specific sins to God (1 John 1:9) and dependence on God's intervention through the Holy Spirit are the path to freedom from lust's controlling power

## Security & Safety Notes

- If you are struggling with repeated, compulsive patterns of sexual sin or addiction, consider reaching out to a trusted pastor, Christian counselor, or support group for professional and spiritual guidance
- Maintain appropriate boundaries in relationships and digital life to avoid unnecessary temptation
- Guard your heart by filtering content, media, and environments that feed lustful desires

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Minimizing lust as "just thoughts" or "not a big deal"
  **Solution:** Remember that Jesus taught lust in the heart is equivalent to adultery before God (Matthew 5:28). Take every thought captive (2 Corinthians 10:5) and bring it before God in confession.
- **Problem:** Relying on willpower alone to overcome lust
  **Solution:** Lust is a spiritual battle that cannot be won by human effort alone. Submit to God, draw near to Him in prayer and Scripture, and rely on the Holy Spirit for transformation (James 4:7-8).
- **Problem:** Waiting until lust has full grip before taking action
  **Solution:** Ready yourself now through a life lived above reproach—meaning your actions and character are so consistently aligned with God's Word that no one can bring a legitimate charge against you (1 Timothy 3:2). Do not wait until temptation is present to build the spiritual disciplines that protect you.
- **Problem:** Carrying unconfessed lust silently, thinking no one knows
  **Solution:** Sin committed in secret—including lustful thoughts, lustful looking at someone with intent, or sexual actions done in private where no one else sees—still grieves God (Hebrews 4:13), who sees all things (Hebrews 4:13). Come clean before Him. Confession brings freedom and opens the door to God's intervention in your life.
