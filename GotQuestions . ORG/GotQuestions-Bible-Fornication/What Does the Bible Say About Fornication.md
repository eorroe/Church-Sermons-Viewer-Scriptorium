# What Does the Bible Say About Fornication?

## Overview

This sermon teaches what the Bible defines as fornication and why it is considered sexual immorality. It explains that fornication—from the Greek term *porneia*—covers any sexual activity outside of marriage. The sermon further instructs believers to flee all sexual sin, recognizing the body as the temple of the Holy Spirit, and warns that those who continue in fornication without repentance will not inherit the kingdom of heaven.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When studying or teaching on the biblical definition of sexual immorality
- When helping someone understand what the Bible says about fornication and sexual purity
- When preparing for or defending the biblical view of marriage and the marriage bed
- When addressing spiritual unfaithfulness or the symbolic use of fornication in Scripture

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand What the Bible Defines as Fornication

Fornication is the biblical term for any sexual activity outside the marriage covenant between one man and one woman. The word comes from the Greek term *porneia* (the root of the English word "pornography") and is a general term for sexual immorality. It includes adultery—a married person engaging in sexual relations with someone other than their spouse—as well as any sexual relations before or outside of marriage. 1 Corinthians 5:1 records an example: a man in the Corinthian church was sleeping with his father's wife, and Paul commanded the church to remove this man from fellowship.

### Step 2: Recognize the Biblical Condemnation of Fornication

Fornication is condemned throughout Scripture. In Romans 1:29, the apostle Paul lists fornication among the sins of those who have rejected God and followed idolatry. Jesus mentions fornication in a list of corrupting sins that proceed from the heart of an unredeemed person (Matthew 15:19; Mark 7:21). The sin of fornication violates the seventh commandment (Exodus 20:14), which was intended to safeguard the integrity of the family unit and the marriage union between one man and one woman. Hebrews 13:4 states: "Marriage is to be honored by all and the marriage bed kept undefiled, because God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterers" (Christian Standard Bible). Those who persistently indulge in fornication without repentance and without faith in Christ will not inherit the kingdom of heaven (1 Corinthians 6:9).

### Step 3: Abstain from All Forms of Sexual Immorality

Abstaining from fornication was one of four conditions required of Gentiles to be accepted into the early church at the Jerusalem conference (Acts 15:29)—one of four conditions—abstaining from fornication, abstaining from idolatry, abstaining from murder, and abstaining from idolatry, murder, and other sins condemned in Scripture. Believers are instructed to run from every kind of sexual sin outside the marriage covenant: "Let there be no sexual immorality, impurity, or greed among you. Such sins have no place among God's people" (Ephesians 5:3, New Living Translation). This instruction is reinforced in 1 Corinthians 7:2 and 1 Thessalonians 4:3.

### Step 4: Understand the Unique Nature of Sexual Sin

According to 1 Corinthians 6:18, sexual sin is unique in that it is a sin directly against one's own body, which is the temple of the Holy Spirit. Believers are members of the body of Christ (1 Corinthians 6:12–17), and an immoral sexual union outside of marriage violates the believer's spiritual union with Jesus Christ, to whom the believer belongs as a member of His body (1 Corinthians 6:15). A Christian's body is also the temple of the Holy Spirit—a holy place that belongs to Jesus Christ (1 Corinthians 6:19-20). Christians have been redeemed by God through faith in Christ for good and righteous works (Ephesians 2:8-10), not for continued sin.

### Step 5: Recognize the Figurative Use of Fornication

In both the Old and New Testaments, Scripture uses fornication figuratively to describe the corruption of God's people through idolatry and unfaithfulness. In the Old Testament, Israel is depicted as the Lord's wife in Isaiah 54:5-6 and Jeremiah 2:2. In the New Testament, the church is depicted as the Bride of Christ in Revelation 19:7-9 and Ephesians 5:25-32. When God's people engage in idolatry and unfaithfulness to Him, God calls this sin "fornication" or "sexual immorality" in a figurative sense (Jeremiah 2:20–36; Ezekiel 16:15–43; Revelation 2:14, 20–22; 17:1–18; 18:2–9).

## Examples

### Example 1: The Corinthian Church's Tolerance of Fornication

In 1 Corinthians 5:1, the apostle Paul addresses a case of fornication in the Corinthian church where a man was sleeping with his father's wife. Paul's reaction—commanding the church to remove the man from fellowship—makes clear that even the church at large is not to tolerate sexual immorality.

### Example 2: Israel as God's Unfaithful Bride

In Ezekiel 16:15–43, God describes Jerusalem as His wife who committed spiritual fornication through idolatry and reliance on foreign nations rather than on Him. This illustrates the biblical pattern of using fornication as a symbol for abandoning God and pursuing false gods and false teaching.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Study the biblical definition of fornication in 1 Corinthians 6:9, Galatians 5:19, and related passages so you can accurately recognize and address all forms of sexual immorality
- ✅ Flee from every kind of sexual sin rather than attempting to manage or control it
- ✅ Honor the marriage bed and keep it undefiled in all circumstances
- ✅ Remember that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit and belongs to Christ alone
- ✅ Recognize spiritual unfaithfulness as a form of fornication against God

## Keep In Mind

- Fornication is a broad biblical term covering all sexual activity outside the marriage covenant between one man and one woman, including premarital sex, adultery, and any other sexual act outside marriage
- God designed sex for the marriage covenant between one man and one woman (Genesis 2:24), and marriage is intended to be a holy, honored institution established by God (Hebrews 13:4)
- Sexual sin is uniquely described as sinning against one's own body
- God will judge the sexually immoral and adulterers
- Those who continue in fornication without turning from it and without faith in Christ will not inherit the kingdom of heaven (1 Corinthians 6:9).

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not engage in sexual activity outside the marriage covenant between one man and one woman, view pornography, or use your body for any sexual purpose outside the one-flesh union with your spouse (Genesis 2:24; 1 Corinthians 6:15-20), as your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit
- You have been redeemed by God for good and righteous works, not for sin

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Dismissing fornication as referring only to adultery—the act of a married person engaging in sexual relations with someone other than their spouse—while excusing premarital sex or lustful thoughts about someone who is not your spouse as not really being sexual immorality
  **Solution:** Understand that fornication is a broad term for any sexual immorality outside of marriage, as defined by Scripture
- **Problem:** Believing sexual sin is just like any other sin without recognizing its unique consequences: it is a sin against your own body (1 Corinthians 6:18), against the one-flesh union with your spouse, and against Christ Himself (1 Corinthians 6:15)
  **Solution:** Recognize that sexual sin is specifically a sin against one's own body and against Christ Himself
- **Problem:** Ignoring the figurative use of fornication to describe spiritual unfaithfulness
  **Solution:** Read passages: Ezekiel 16 (Jerusalem as an unfaithful wife), Jeremiah 2:20-36 (Israel's unfaithfulness to God), and Revelation 17-18 (Babylon as the unfaithful bride) to understand how God views idolatry and apostasy as spiritual fornication
