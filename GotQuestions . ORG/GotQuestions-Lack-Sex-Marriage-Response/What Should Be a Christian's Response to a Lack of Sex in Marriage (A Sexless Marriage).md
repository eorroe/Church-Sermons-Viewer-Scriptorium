# What Should Be a Christian's Response to a Lack of Sex in Marriage (A Sexless Marriage)?

## Overview

This sermon provides a biblical framework for responding to a sexless marriage. Physical intimacy is part of God's design for marriage (1 Corinthians 7:2-4); both spouses bear responsibility to meet each other's emotional needs such as love and affection, physical needs such as sexual intimacy, and spiritual needs such as shared prayer and Bible study. The sermon guides Christians through examining personal contributions to the problem, praying for wisdom and grace, maintaining love that reflects Christ's love for the church (Ephesians 5:25) and direct, honest communication about needs and feelings—meaning each spouse speaks truthfully about what they need and feel without blame or defensiveness—and seeking pastoral or biblical counseling when needed. The ultimate goal is to trust God to heal the marriage through both spouses submitting to Scripture—practicing the love described in 1 Corinthians 13, showing patience as a fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22), and extending forgiveness as Christ forgave (Colossians 3:13).

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you or your spouse is experiencing a lack of physical intimacy in marriage
- When you need biblical guidance for addressing marital sexual concerns
- When you want to understand how to respond with biblical grace—the Christ-like love described in Ephesians 5:25-33—and truth (God's Word and direct, honest communication about needs and feelings) to a sexless marriage
- When you are seeking to rebuild intimacy through prayer, communication, and biblical obedience

## Steps

### Step 1: Pray for Wisdom, Mercy, and Grace

Begin by praying for divine wisdom, mercy, and grace to help in your present difficulty (James 1:5; Hebrews 4:16). Ask God to reveal anything you are doing that contributes to the problem (Psalm 139:23), and be willing to respond to what He shows you. This is the essential first step before attempting to address the issue.

### Step 2: Examine Yourself and Confess Sin

If the deprived spouse discovers that he or she has contributed to the cause of the lack of sex in the marriage, the sin should be confessed to God and to the spouse, and steps should be taken to change the behavior (Proverbs 28:13). Take an honest look at your own actions and attitudes—including harshness, neglect of your spouse's needs, pride that prevents repentance, or failure to listen to your spouse's concerns—that wounded your partner or created distance.

### Step 3: Maintain Unconditional Love and Prayer

Continue praying daily for grace to love as Christ loved the church (Ephesians 5:25) and trust God to work according to His will and timing (James 1:2-4). Waiting for change while continuing to love your spouse tests your trust in God's promises and His sovereignty. All the while, keep the lines of communication open with your partner and never neglect God's commands concerning the marital relationship found in Ephesians 5:22-33 (Ephesians 5:22-33). Be patient, knowing that healing requires time.

### Step 4: If Your Wife Is Withholding Intimacy

If the wife is withholding intimacy, the husband should consider whether he is being obedient to God's command to love his wife as Christ loves the church (Ephesians 5:25-33) and whether he is living with her with gentleness and consideration for her feelings (1 Peter 3:7, ESV). This is particularly important if she expresses feelings of inferiority or rejection. Use 1 Corinthians 13 as a checklist to evaluate whether your treatment of your wife matches the Bible's description of love. The Christ-like love described in 1 Corinthians 13 and commanded in Colossians 3:19 will keep you from bitterness and harshness (Colossians 3:19). Practice honest communication—speaking truthfully about your feelings and needs—and extend forgiveness as Christ forgave you (Colossians 3:13); avoid blaming your spouse without examining your own contribution (Matthew 7:3-5).

### Step 5: If Your Husband Is Withholding Intimacy

If the husband is withholding intimacy, the wife should examine whether she is fulfilling her responsibility before God to love, respect, and submit to her husband as described in Ephesians 5:22-24—meaning she respects his leadership and honors him as her husband (Ephesians 5:22-24). If he feels neglected, inferior, or dominated in the marriage relationship, he withdraws from intimacy out of hurt or frustration. Be mindful of the warning: "See to it that no one falls short of the grace of God and that no bitter root grows up to cause trouble and defile many" (Hebrews 12:15).

### Step 6: Seek Understanding and Counseling

If a spouse is withholding intimacy without a clear, identifiable reason, there is a deeper problem stemming from past emotional or physical experiences—including sexual abuse, physical abuse, emotional abuse, unresolved emotional trauma from those experiences, or unprocessed emotional grief from loss—that requires professional Christian counseling. In this case, professional Christian counseling can help the issue be identified, brought into the open, and addressed with biblical principles. Both parties should set aside uninterrupted time to discuss the issue directly, each taking responsibility for their own contributions without deflecting blame onto the other. A pastor or biblical counselor trained in marriage counseling can facilitate these conversations and provide biblical guidance.

### Step 7: Commit to Spiritual Disciplines and Trust

Healing requires a period of 3 to 6 months depending on the nature and depth of the wounds involved. In the meantime, forgiveness is an immediate command (Matthew 6:14-15). Commit to daily Bible reading and prayer, even if only for 5 minutes each day, to obedience to God's Word and to fulfilling your marital duties to each other (1 Corinthians 7:3-5). Patiently trust the Lord to work in both your hearts and heal whatever is causing the lack of sex in the marriage.

## Examples

### Example 1: Husband Reflects on His Treatment

A husband whose wife has been withholding intimacy reviews Ephesians 5:25-33 and 1 Peter 3:7, recognizing that his harshness or lack of understanding contributed to her withdrawal. He confesses his shortcomings, begins praying daily for wisdom, and commits to loving her as Christ loves the church. Honest communication and demonstrated change open the door for restored intimacy.

### Example 2: Couple Seeks Pastoral Guidance

A couple experiencing a sexless marriage for a period of 3 to 6 months takes time apart to pray and reflect individually, then meets with their pastor to discuss the issue without blame. Through counseling, they uncover past wounds that had been suppressed and begin working through them together, committing to daily Bible study and mutual forgiveness.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Pray for wisdom, mercy, and grace before addressing the issue (James 1:5; Hebrews 4:16)
- ✅ Ask God to reveal any personal contribution to the problem (Psalm 139:23)
- ✅ Confess sin to God and your spouse (Proverbs 28:13)
- ✅ Keep communication lines open with your partner (Ephesians 5:22-33)
- ✅ Use 1 Corinthians 13 as a checklist for loving behavior
- ✅ Seek pastoral or biblical counseling when communication has broken down or when there is unresolved hurt from past trauma or abuse.
- ✅ Commit to daily time in the Word and prayer
- ✅ Practice immediate forgiveness (Matthew 6:14-15)

## Keep In Mind

- Sexual intimacy within marriage is part of God's design for marriage, and spouses have a responsibility to meet each other's needs (1 Corinthians 7:2-4) — this includes emotional intimacy through love and affection, relational intimacy through shared activities and conversation, and physical intimacy through sexual relations.
- A lack of sex should only come by mutual consent for spiritual pursuits and for a specific, temporary period: specifically, an agreed-upon fast for prayer that both spouses consent to — not as a prolonged pattern of withholding intimacy as punishment or control (1 Corinthians 7:5)
- Healing requires a period of 3 to 6 months depending on the depth of the issues involved
- This is ultimately a test of faith (James 1:2-4)

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always consult with a qualified pastor or biblical counselor trained in marriage issues for ongoing conflict, betrayal, or abuse
- If there is abuse or danger in the relationship, seek appropriate professional and legal help immediately

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Blaming your spouse without examining your own contribution
  **Solution:** First ask God to reveal anything you are doing that contributes to the problem (Psalm 139:23) and be willing to respond to what He shows you

- **Problem:** Forcing intimacy or demanding change on your own timeline
  **Solution:** Healing cannot be forced; trust God to work according to His sovereign will and timing and commit to patience and prayer (James 1:2-4)

- **Problem:** Allowing bitterness to grow toward your spouse
  **Solution:** Practice the Christ-like love described in 1 Corinthians 13 and extend forgiveness daily (Colossians 3:13), remembering that this kind of love keeps you from bitterness and harshness (Colossians 3:19)

- **Problem:** Withholding communication or avoiding difficult conversations
  **Solution:** Keep the lines of communication open and consider a pastor or biblical counselor trained in marriage facilitation to guide healthy dialogue

- **Problem:** Neglecting spiritual disciplines during the struggle
  **Solution:** Commit to time in Scripture daily, prayer, and obedience to God's commands and to fulfilling your marital duties to each other (1 Corinthians 7:3-5), trusting the Lord to work in both hearts
