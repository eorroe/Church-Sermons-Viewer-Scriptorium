# Am I really a Christian: A Challenge for all Christians.

## Overview

This sermon is a self-examination challenge for Christians based on 14 short phrases from an early Christian writer from around 300 AD. Each phrase describes what a Christian truly is, serving as a mirror for honest self-reflection and spiritual growth. The speaker walks through each characteristic, urging believers to evaluate whether their lives align with these ancient benchmarks of genuine Christian faith.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need a thorough self-examination of your Christian walk
- When working through spiritual dryness or questioning your faith
- When preparing for confession, renewal, or recommitment to Christ

## Steps

### Step 1: Be Merciful to All

Practice mercy toward everyone, not just those you like or who are easy to love. Follow Christ's example of patience, understanding, gentleness, and forgiveness. Extend the same mercy to others that you hope to receive from God.

### Step 2: Do Not Get Angry for an Injury

Refuse to let anger dominate your response to every little annoyance or injury. Recognize that quick irritation is a symptom of something deeper in your soul. Take every moment of frustration as an opportunity to choose patience, remembering that baptism calls us to put the old self to death and rise as something new.

### Step 3: Give Aid to the Poor

See and respond to the needs of others around you. Like the rich man and Lazarus, do not let self-absorption blind you to the neighbor right next to you who needs help. Aid extends beyond material poverty to include emotional, spiritual, and relational poverty.

### Step 4: Suffer With the Afflicted

Rather than rushing to fix every problem, first see the person carrying the burden. Compassion means suffering alongside someone before trying to solve their struggles. Be present in their pain, and work through solutions together rather than imposing your own answers.

### Step 5: Participate in the Sorrows of Others as Though They Were Your Own

Imagine yourself witnessing another person's struggles and being called to help carry their cross. Fly to their aid just as you would if you were standing in the crowd on the day of Christ's crucifixion. Bear one another's burdens and fulfill the law of Christ.

### Step 6: Do Not Shut Your Door on the Unhappy

Be the person whose home and heart remain open to those who are struggling. Whether it is providing a meal, a ride, or simply a listening ear, let people leave feeling more special, more loved, and a little happier. Ask yourself honestly whether your door is mostly open or mostly closed.

### Step 7: Do Not Insult Anymore

Eliminate all forms of insult, mockery, criticism, and harsh judgment from your speech and your thoughts. This includes not only outward words but inward thoughts and private remarks, because God hears all. Follow Christ's example: when mocked and slandered, he returned no insult and answered gently.

### Step 8: Serve God Day and Night

Examine what or who you truly serve throughout your day. You serve what you love most. Jesus said that unless you love him more than everything else, you are not worthy of him. Give God the first and best place in your heart, and serve him through your work, your family, and your everyday life.

### Step 9: Continuously Meditate on His Ways

Fill your mind throughout the day with the ways of Christ. Being a Christian means following and imitating Christ, and that requires knowing him through Scripture and choosing to follow him each day. The more you know and believe in Jesus, the more you will naturally fall in love with him.

### Step 10: Be Poor in the Eyes of the World but Rich in the Eyes of God

Accept that the world may see you as a fool for following Christ, but know that you are rich in God's eyes. When vilified, bless; when persecuted, endure; when slandered, answer gently. Respond to the world's rejection with goodness, and you will store up treasures in heaven that far outweigh anything on earth.

### Step 11: Keep Your Soul Simple and Upright

Regularly examine the condition of your soul. A simple soul contains only goodness, love, and truth. An upright soul stands firmly for those same three things. If your soul feels like a dark, tangled mess, bring it to God and let him untangle it, so that you may know the peace and life he intends for you.

### Step 12: Keep Your Conscience Faithful and Pure

Guard your conscience by listening to its voice and obeying it. A pure conscience echoes only the voice of God, while a faithful conscience listens and acts accordingly. The more you ignore your conscience, the weaker it becomes and the more room you give to other voices. Ask yourself: who is my conscience faithful to, and am I faithful to it?

### Step 13: Rest Your Spirit in God and Place All Your Hopes in Christ

In moments of anxiety, stress, or desolation, practice releasing your burdens into God's hands. Hold your worries in your heart, then let them go, handing them over to Jesus. Say, "only you know all things, but let your will, not my will, be done," and then rest in him. This takes great trust, but it is the path to true peace.

### Step 14: Prefer the Treasures of Heaven to Those of Earth

Choose the eternal treasures of heaven over the temporary riches of this world. Where your treasure is, there your heart will be. You cannot serve both God and the world at the same time. Abandon the things of this world so that you may be filled with the eternal riches God offers to those who seek him first.

## Examples

### Example 1: Daily Work and Family Life

A Christian works a demanding job and raises a family, yet still serves God day and night by treating coworkers with mercy, refusing to insult others even when frustrated, and making time to meditate on Scripture each morning. At home, the Christian keeps the door open to neighbors in need and teaches children to do the same, demonstrating that serving God is not limited to formal church work.

### Example 2: Responding to Betrayal

When a close friend betrays a Christian's trust, the Christian refuses to respond with anger or insult. Instead, the Christian mourns the pain alongside the friend, listens without immediately trying to fix the situation, and chooses to extend mercy even when it is undeserved. Throughout the process, the Christian places hope in Christ rather than in the outcome, remembering that heavenly treasure is far more valuable than earthly reputation.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Examine your conscience regularly and honestly
- ✅ Practice mercy toward all people, even those who are difficult to love
- ✅ Serve God through your ordinary daily actions, not only through formal ministry
- ✅ Keep your heart and home open to those who are struggling
- ✅ Respond to criticism and betrayal with gentleness, not retaliation
- ❌ Do not assume that outward religiosity replaces inward character
- ❌ Do not ignore the poor and afflicted in your everyday life
- ❌ Do not trust in worldly success or possessions as the source of your security
- ❌ Do not allow anger, insult, or judgment to become habits of the heart

## Keep In Mind

- Spiritual self-reflection is deeply personal. Be honest with yourself, but also extend grace to yourself as you grow.
- Do not use this sermon as a weapon to judge others. These questions are for personal examination, not condemnation.
- If this self-examination brings up heavy emotions or spiritual distress, seek guidance from a trusted pastor or spiritual mentor.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Reading through the 14 phrases and feeling condemned rather than convicted
  **Solution:** Let these phrases guide you toward growth, not despair. Return to God's mercy and remember that transformation is a process, not a single moment.
- **Problem:** Trying to live out all 14 characteristics at once through sheer willpower
  **Solution:** Focus on one or two areas at a time, asking God to transform you from the inside out rather than relying on your own effort alone.
- **Problem:** Using these standards to judge other Christians instead of examining yourself
  **Solution:** These phrases are a mirror for your own heart, not a measuring stick for others. Keep the focus on your own walk with Christ.
