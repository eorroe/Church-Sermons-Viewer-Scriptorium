# Prayer life Stink? A short guide on how to talk with God, not to God.

## Overview

This sermon addresses the common problem of prayer becoming dry, repetitive, or transactional. It teaches step-by-step practices to transform prayer from a chore into a genuine two-way conversation with God, focusing on three essential ingredients: Presence (awareness of God), Heart (honest emotional and spiritual sharing), and Trust (radical reliance on God's love and sovereignty). "Radical trust" means entrusting your entire life to God, even for your next breath. "Honest heart-to-heart connection" means sharing your true thoughts and feelings without filtering or performing. The goal is to move from talking at God to talking with God through these three ingredients.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When your prayer life feels stale, repetitive, or like a broken record (repeating the same phrases without meaning them)
- When you treat God like a vending machine or feel prayer is an obligation or chore
- When you want to move from talking at God to talking with God
- When you struggle to feel connected or present during prayer

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare Your Space and Posture

Find a quiet space where you can be alone, with no louder sounds than a quiet fan or distant traffic (approximately 30-40 decibels). If kneeling causes pain or is physically difficult for you, sit comfortably in a chair instead. Kneel or sit with palms open and facing upward, arms uncrossed, body relaxed (shoulders dropped, jaw unclenched) and ready to receive (open to God's presence, guidance, or words).

### Step 2: Enter the Inner Room

Mute your phone, close all applications and tabs that are not needed for this exercise, and keep only the SERMON.md file you are reading open if you are reading digitally. Mentally set aside all distracting thoughts by silently naming each thought in your mind that pulls your attention away from God (for example, silently thinking the word "work" when you notice worry about work) and then gently returning your focus to God. You do not need to name every thought that arises—only the ones that distract you. If you have a private room, physically close the door. If not, find a space where you can be undisturbed and use headphones or white noise if needed.

Then use a simple de-stressing technique: notice the thoughts in your mind and the stress in your body. If you find it easy to create mental images, imagine these thoughts and stress as red smoke collecting in the center of your chest. If you do not create mental images easily, simply notice the thoughts and stress without engaging them, and imagine them dissolving with each exhale. Take deep breaths—inhale slowly through your nose for a count of four, hold for a count of four, then exhale slowly through your mouth for a count of six. Then imagine your surroundings fading away until only your heart remains in a quiet space free of external distractions.

### Step 3: Invite God Into the Room

Acknowledge that God is present with you in this moment, even though you cannot see Him. This awareness comes through faith and trust, not physical sensation. Calm your mind by focusing on your breath or a simple phrase like "God is here" until your breathing is steady and your mind feels less cluttered. It's just your heart and His, and nothing else.

### Step 4: Sit in Silence

When starting, sit in silence for 2-3 minutes, and gradually extend by 2-3 minutes each session up to a maximum of 15 minutes. Do not exceed 15 minutes in a single session until you have practiced regularly for at least two weeks. The goal is to become comfortable with simply being in God's presence without words.

### Step 5: Confession and Seeking Forgiveness

Start by saying something like "Dear Lord, I'm sorry for..." in your own words. Then reflect on specific actions, thoughts, or relationships where you have been selfish, dishonest, unloving, or acted against what you know is right according to your conscience and God's teachings. Examples include lying, holding grudges, neglecting someone in need, or choosing your own desires over what is good. Ask yourself: Where have I fallen short? What is blocking my connection to God? Ask Him to reveal these things to you during this prayer time and in the days ahead through prayer, scripture, or a consistent inner knowing about a particular issue that stays with you. As you confess, remember that God is not angry with you—He is like the father in the prodigal son story who runs out to meet you.

### Step 6: Pray for Others

We all experience pain, loss, difficulty, or hardship. Pray with an open heart: include the sick, the dying, those who have lost hope, and anyone else you specifically think of who is in need. Pray for the spiritual condition of people everywhere and for good to prevail over evil in our world and in our own hearts. It's not about the exact words you use but whether you sincerely intend what you pray and trust that He can act.

### Step 7: Pray for Yourself

Tell Jesus your fears honestly and directly, without fancy language. Tell Him what you need, what you want, your sorrows and joys. Pour it all out—include everything that is on your heart, from the smallest worry to the biggest joy. Needs are things essential for your spiritual health and relationship with God, such as forgiveness, guidance, and love. Wants are desires that are good but not essential for your spiritual health, such as a specific job, possession, or human relationship. Remember Jesus is more concerned about your spiritual welfare than your material welfare. Seek first Him, and then He will provide for your material provisions and daily needs.

### Step 8: Surrender Your Wants

After you have expressed your needs and desires and feel that you have said all you need to say, you may use words like: "Lord, you know what I truly need better than I do, and that is what I truly want." Then for any specific desires, you may say: "Lord, if this desire is from You, then let it be done. If not, please remove it from my heart. Either way, I trust You and I am held in Your love and grace." These are suggested prayers, not required scripts—use your own words if you prefer.

### Step 9: Give Thanks

Say thank you in your own specific words, naming the things you are grateful for (for example, "Thank You for my family," "Thank You for Your patience with me"). You don't need fancy language—just speak honestly and personally, as you would to someone you love. Many people close their prayer by saying something like "Lord, I know You can do all things," but you may use your own closing words if you prefer.

## Examples

### Example 1: De-stressing Before Prayer

You're feeling overwhelmed with work and anxiety. You enter your quiet space with minimal background noise, sit with palms open and facing upward, and focus on the stress in your chest. If you visualize easily, picture the red smoke of stress and breathe it out. If you do not visualize easily, simply notice the stress and breathe deeply—inhaling for a count of four, holding for four, exhaling for six—until your mind feels less cluttered and you sense the empty space of just your heart.

### Example 2: Praying When You Don't Know What to Say

You sit in silence, aware of God's presence, and simply rest in being with Him. No words are needed. This is the deepest form of prayer.

### Example 3: Surrendering a Specific Desire

You've been asking God for a specific job. After praying for it and feeling ready to surrender, you say "Lord, if this job is from You, let it be done. If not, please remove it from my heart. Either way, I trust You and I am held in Your love and grace."

## Best Practices

- ✅ Have radical trust in God, entrusting your entire life into His hands
- ✅ Focus on the presence of God rather than feelings
- ✅ Prepare physically and spiritually before prayer with posture and de-stressing
- ✅ Be honest and open about your fears, sorrows, and joys
- ✅ Mean what you pray and trust that God can act
- ❌ Don't treat God like a vending machine (put something in, get something out)
- ❌ Don't repeat the same prayers over and over without heart
- ❌ Don't worry about saying the right words or sounding fancy
- ❌ Don't make prayer a chore or obligation

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon provides guidance on prayer practice but does not replace your personal relationship with God or the need for ongoing spiritual growth.
- Stop and ask God for clarification through prayer and meditation on scripture, or ask trusted spiritual mentors for guidance, if you're unsure about what you should pray for or how to recognize God's voice. Trusted spiritual mentors are people with demonstrated faith, wisdom, and experience who know you personally and can provide biblical guidance. God's voice is typically recognized through: (1) consistent inner alignment with scripture—thoughts that match what the Bible teaches; (2) a deep sense of peace about a decision that persists over time; or (3) wise counsel from mature believers who have walked with God for many years and live out their faith consistently.
- Prayer is a personal relationship, not a formula. Make it your own.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Prayer becomes dry, stale, or repetitive.
  **Solution:** Focus on honest inner sharing rather than reciting memorized words. Enter the inner room and sit in God's presence rather than repeating phrases without meaning them.

- **Problem:** Treating God like a vending machine or expecting instant results.
  **Solution:** Remember that love and faith are what God works with, not transactions. Surrender your desires and trust in His will.

- **Problem:** Prayer feels like an annoying chore or obligation.
  **Solution:** Ask yourself: Do I love God? Do I believe He is a living person who hears me and loves me? If the answer is yes, then spending time with Him is the best use of your time. Shift from obligation to relationship by spending time simply being with Him.
