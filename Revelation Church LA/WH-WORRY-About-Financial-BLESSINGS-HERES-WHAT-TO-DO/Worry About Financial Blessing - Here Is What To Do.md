# Sermon Title

You WORRY About Your Financial BLESSINGS, chosen one: HERE'S WHAT TO DO NOW

## Overview

This sermon confronts the dangerous trap of becoming too comfortable managing poverty and teaches the spiritual principle that whatever you manage, you keep — and whatever you mismanage, you lose. It calls the believer to examine whether they have become faithful to a state of lack, living within limitations, and settling for survival instead of stepping into the full financial blessings God has prepared. The message challenges listeners to reject the "good management of poverty" mindset and reposition themselves for increase by being deliberately unfaithful to lack and faithful to abundance.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel stuck in a cycle of lack and have convinced yourself it is your lot
- When you find yourself living within tight limitations and no longer dreaming beyond your current means
- When you sense that you have become too skilled at surviving poverty rather than stepping into provision
- When worry about financial blessings is consuming you and you need a biblical reset on what stewardship really means
- When the Holy Spirit is calling you to re-evaluate your relationship with money, work, and God's abundance

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize the State You Are Managing

Examine your current financial situation honestly. Ask yourself whether you have become so accustomed to lack that it feels normal. Being faithful in poverty can keep you trapped in it — because whatever you manage well, you keep. If your default operating mode is limitation and survival, you are managing poverty, and that management is exactly what keeps it in place.

### Step 2: Reverse the Spiritual Principle for Your Situation

Understand the biblical principle: "He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much, and he that is unjust in the least is unjust also in much." Reverse it for your own life. To move out of lack, you must become unfaithful to poverty. Stop settling for the identity of a person who survives on little. Let go of the comfort of managing scarcity and begin to expect — and prepare for — more.

### Step 3: Stop Limiting Your Own Thinking

Poverty thrives in a limited mind. When you manage poverty well, you stop dreaming big. You begin thinking with artificial boundaries that were never placed there by God. Renounce the lie that your current financial state is permanent. Break the habit of living "beyond your means" in a way that leaves your account perpetually in the negative — not by reckless spending, but by refusing to keep accepting less than God's best for your life.

### Step 4: Choose Unfaithfulness to Lack and Faithfulness to Blessing

Make a conscious decision to be unfaithful to every mindset, habit, and circumstance that reinforces financial lack. Simultaneously, choose to be faithful to the vision God has placed in your heart for increase. Train your heart to handle more by practicing gratitude, wisdom, and generosity with what you currently have — so that when increase comes, you are ready for it.

### Step 5: Act on the Word with Expectation

Put what you have learned into action by adjusting your habits, conversations, and decisions. Speak life over your finances. Do not live as though lack is permanent. Surround yourself with truth, not the false comfort of "making do." Step into the identity of someone being promoted — because in God's kingdom, promotion comes to those who are ready to receive it.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Person Comfortable in Lack

A believer has survived on a minimal income for years and has become very good at budgeting down to the last penny. They take pride in their ability to "make it work" with almost nothing. While this seems like wisdom, it has actually kept them from pursuing greater opportunities. They no longer apply for promotions or consider new ventures because they have normalized limitation. Following this sermon, they must recognize that their good management of poverty is actually the very thing keeping them in it — and choose to step out in faith toward increase.

### Example 2: The Identity of the Survivor

Someone tells themselves, "I'm just not a wealthy person," or "People like me don't get ahead." They have accepted a false identity rooted in lack. They speak, think, and act from that identity, and their finances reflect it. The sermon calls them to reject that identity and to see themselves as a chosen one blessed by God — called to steward abundance, not merely to survive scarcity.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Regularly audit your mindset around money — ask God to reveal where you have accepted less than His best
- ✅ Practice faithfulness with whatever you currently have so you are prepared for greater responsibility
- ✅ Speak scripture over your finances rather than repeating limiting words
- ✅ Keep an open hand — generosity positions you to receive more
- ✅ Surround yourself with people who challenge your limitations rather than affirming them
- ❌ Do not become comfortable in a state of lack, even if you have learned to survive it well
- ❌ Do not let fear dictate your financial decisions or stop you from stepping into new opportunities
- ❌ Do not confuse "fake it till you make it" with genuine faith — one is deception, the other is trust in God
- ❌ Do not ignore wisdom and responsibility while claiming blessing — stewardship matters

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace sound financial planning, budgeting, or professional advice in your specific context.
- Stop and ask God for clarity if you are unsure whether your current season is temporary testing or a pattern you need to break.
- This message is not an endorsement of reckless behavior or ignoring real obligations — it is a call to break mental strongholds over your finances.
- Every believer's journey is different. Apply these principles with wisdom and accountability.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not make drastic financial decisions out of emotional reaction to a sermon. Seek wise counsel and pray through major moves.
- Be cautious of anyone using this message to manipulate you into financial schemes or pressure you into giving beyond your means.
- Guard your heart against guilt — this message is about freedom and increase, not shame over your current situation.
- Protect your personal financial information when discussing money with others. Transparency in community does not require oversharing sensitive details.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Misinterpreting "be unfaithful to poverty" as permission to be reckless or irresponsible with money.
  **Solution:** Being unfaithful to poverty means rejecting a poverty mindset, not abandoning wisdom. Steward what you have well while expecting and preparing for more.
- **Problem:** Using "managing poverty well" as a badge of pride and refusing to grow or change.
  **Solution:** Recognize that pride in surviving lack is not the same as walking in God's abundance. Humility will lead you to receive more.
- **Problem:** Hearing the message but waiting passively for blessing without changing habits, decisions, or actions.
  **Solution:** Faith without works is dead. Pair your renewed mindset with concrete steps — update your resume, seek new opportunities, adjust your spending, and pray boldly.
- **Problem:** Comparing your financial journey to others and feeling condemned or discouraged.
  **Solution:** Your season is yours alone. Focus on your own faithfulness and the specific call God has placed on your life, not on the pace or path of someone else.
