# Protect Your Inner Peace

## Overview

This sermon challenges Christians to guard their inner peace without becoming self-focused or indifferent to the needs of others. Joyce Meyer teaches that while it is important to set boundaries and protect your heart, true Christlikeness requires being interruptible—like Jesus, who stopped what He was doing to help people in need. The message uses the parable of the Good Samaritan to show that love is active, sacrificial, and can be inconvenient, and it calls believers to bear fruit by reaching out rather than simply sitting in church pews.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When others' requests place excessive demands on you and you need boundaries that protect your peace without isolating you from others
- When you perceive that your faith has become routine without intentional spiritual purpose or focused only on your own comfort and preferences
- When you habitually struggle with unforgiveness and want to walk in freedom
- When God is prompting you to serve someone outside your usual comfort zone
- When you want to evaluate whether you are genuinely practicing what you say you believe

## Steps

### Step 1: Guard Your Heart Without Hardening It

Protect your inner peace by setting healthy boundaries, but do not let those boundaries become a "Do Not Disturb" sign that keeps you from loving people. Jesus was occupied with tasks and had scheduled commitments, yet He never passed by on the other side of the road when someone needed help. Practice saying yes to what God asks of you, even when it conflicts with your planned activities.

### Step 2: Choose to Be Interruptible Like Jesus

Make a decision to be interruptible. Joyce Meyer shares personal stories—such as buying a coffee press for a stranger in a Starbucks—to show that God places divine appointments in ordinary daily encounters. When you sense the prompting to help, stop, observe, and act. Active love pursues opportunities to help, but indifference can find an excuse in any situation.

### Step 3: Bear Fruit Through Service, Not Attendance

Examine the fruit you are producing. Are you only receiving teaching, or are you applying what you hear in tangible ways? Jesus said, "I want you to go and bear fruit." Ask God every morning to bring a person across your path that you can help, and be willing to complete the action whether it requires little effort or great sacrifice of your time.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Good Samaritan

A man was beaten and left half dead on the road. A priest and a Levite—religious men who studied the religious law—passed by on the other side. But a Samaritan, someone looked down upon by society, stopped. He bandaged the man's wounds, put him on his own animal, took him to an inn, and paid for his full care. This is the standard Jesus set for neighborly love: observe, stop, sacrifice, and complete the action.

### Example 2: Joyce Meyer and the Coffee Press

While waiting in a Starbucks, Joyce Meyer felt God prompt her to buy a $150 coffee press for a young woman with multiple tattoos and colorful hair—someone she would have normally crossed the street to avoid. The woman began to cry, and later told her, "You are the kindest person in the world." The woman also shared that she had been angry at God since her mother died of cancer. A simple, interruptible act of kindness opened a door for the gospel.

### Example 3: The Doctor's Office Test

At one time, Joyce Meyer prayed that God would make an elderly man in the doctor's office stop talking so she could read her Bible. She sensed God asking her, "If that was Billy Graham, would you listen to him?" She realized she was being selective and choosing based on personal preference. True love does not weigh whether a person is beneficial or entertaining to you; it simply listens and cares because that is what Christ would do.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Ask God every morning: "God, would you bring a person across my path today that I can help?"
- ✅ Be prompt to forgive and willing to forgive even when it is difficult
- ✅ Observe tangible needs around you instead of staying focused only on your personal agenda
- ✅ Practice generosity without expecting repayment or praise from others
- ✅ Examine your underlying intentions to ensure you are not helping others only to get a personal benefit in return
- ❌ Do not wear a "Do Not Disturb" sign on your heart
- ❌ Do not pass by on the other side when someone is hurting
- ❌ Do not make excuses when God calls you to act

## Keep In Mind

- Attending church services does not make you a Christian; being a Christian means obeying Christ
- Jesus was interruptible—He stopped what He was doing to heal, cast out demons, and show compassion
- Indifference is a sin that hinders your relationship with God and fellow believers of the contemporary Christian community: "I don't know and I don't care"
- Active love pursues opportunities whenever they arise; excuses are the evidence of a hardened heart
- Forgiveness gives the devil fewer opportunities for influence in your life than any other action

## Security & Safety Notes

- Exercise discernment and seek specific guidance through prayer when reaching out to strangers, but do not let fear of others' opinions or legitimate physical danger keep you from loving people
- Refrain from performing generosity publicly for attention; Jesus taught that your left hand should not know what your right hand is doing
- Establish clear limits in relationships so you do not lose your capacity to serve, but do not use boundaries as an excuse to avoid persons who differ from you in appearance or background
- Forgiveness does not mean staying in unsafe or abusive situations; it means freeing your own heart from bitterness

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Prioritizing activity over God's commands, and your schedule is elevated above caring for others
  **Solution:** Ask God to interrupt your day and practice saying yes to divine appointments
- **Problem:** You exclusively help persons who can benefit you or share your appearance or social status
  **Solution:** Remember the Good Samaritan; true love crosses cultural, social, and personal boundaries
- **Problem:** Unforgiveness damages your peace and gives the enemy a point of influence
  **Solution:** Forgive promptly, even when it is emotionally costly, and do not continue to dwell on offenses
- **Problem:** You attend church but bear little fruit
  **Solution:** Shift from receiving teaching without serving to putting your faith into practical service; ask God daily who you can help
- **Problem:** You want public praise for your good deeds
   **Solution:** Do acts of kindness in secret when you can do so safely, and examine your underlying intentions to make sure your reason for acting is love, not public praise
