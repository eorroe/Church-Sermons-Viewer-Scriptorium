# God Has a Plan for Your Breakthrough!

## Overview

This sermon is a call to authentic spiritual maturity rather than temporary emotional excitement. It teaches that God knows exactly where you are, has a purpose for your situation, and will bring you through difficult times at the right time. The message emphasizes that experience—not just talent or religious activity—is what produces lasting character, fruitfulness, and the ability to help others. This sermon exists to help believers stay in revival long after the church meeting ends by embracing what God wants to teach them through their current circumstances.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel invisible or forgotten by God
- When you are going through a painful, unfair, or confusing situation
- When you feel your spiritual life is stuck or you are not growing
- When you are struggling with jealousy, bitterness, or self-pity
- When you want to be used by God but feel unqualified or overlooked
- When you are a young believer who wants to grow in wisdom and godliness
- When you are an older believer who wants to pass on what you have learned
- When you feel tempted to compare your ministry or life circumstances to other believers
- When you need encouragement to keep a good attitude through trials and unfair treatment

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize that God knows exactly where you are and has a purpose for your current situation

God knows exactly where you are right now. You may feel as though God has forgotten you or cannot see you, but God sees you and knows what God wants to develop in your life. God never does evil to people, but sometimes God allows you to remain in difficult situations longer than you would choose—and God has a specific purpose in allowing that. Instead of begging God to remove you immediately, begin to trust that God is working in your life even while you are still going through that situation.

### Step 2: Stop comparing your spiritual maturity to other believers and focus on your own personal revival

It is more important that you experience a personal revival in your own heart than that you simply attend a corporate revival meeting. Powerful spiritual experiences can happen in a church meeting that change a person permanently, but when the atmosphere, lights, speakers, and music are gone, you still need to be happy, joyful, stable, and peaceful. Do not measure your spiritual life by how other believers appear to be doing; measure your spiritual life by what God is doing inside your own heart.

### Step 3: Value your own life experience as a teacher of wisdom and godly understanding

The longer you live, the more experience you accumulate, and the wiser you should become. People live life forward but understand it backward—many specific experiences that seem terrible now will, in 20 years, be recognized as the very experiences that brought you closer to God and changed you the most. Do not despise the hard experiences God brings you through; those experiences are building something in you that cannot be built in any other way. A person who has walked through pain, learned from it, and grown through it has something authentic and powerful to share with others.

### Step 4: Ask older, experienced believers for one piece of wisdom that can help you live better

Young believers today have tremendous talent, zeal, and ability, but they lack the wisdom that comes from experience. If you are a younger believer, find a mature Christian believer who is 30 years older than you and ask one simple, specific question: "Can you tell me one thing about life that will help me live mine better?" If that believer walks with God, add this question: "Can you tell me one spiritual principle that will help me live better for Jesus?" When older believers share wisdom from years of walking with God and younger believers share the gifts God has given them, the devil has reason to fear—because real revival follows when wisdom and gifts are combined.

### Step 5: Trust God's timing instead of rushing to get what you want

Do not be in such a hurry about getting what you want and going where you are going. God's timing is not our timing. If God does not advance you in ministry right now, trust God—it may not be time yet, or you may not be ready. Many people have a gift that could advance them in ministry, but they do not have enough character to keep them there. Take time to learn what God wants you to learn at your current level before you move up.

God spoke to the preacher about the preacher's ministry calling, but the preacher spent 5 years teaching a home Bible study to 20 to 30 people, unpaid, while facing the preacher's own desperate financial needs, before God advanced the preacher's ministry.

### Step 6: Maintain a good attitude through trials and unfair treatment

How you go through something is just as important as getting through it. When somebody does something unfair to you, you will be tempted to be upset. There was a time when the preacher's television program time was canceled even though the preacher had a contract. It was devastating. But one night in church, God told the preacher to put that television station in the offering basket—to give the television station to God. Shortly after, the television station called back and offered the preacher a daily program. The lesson is clear: if you keep a good attitude, the devil cannot steal from you. Haters will hate, judges will judge, and critics will criticize—let them. What people think of you is not nearly as important as you think it is. Your reputation in heaven matters far more than your reputation on earth.

### Step 7: Allow God to work through your past hurts to help others

God may not take you out of a painful situation, but God will take you through it. What you go through can become the very tool God uses to help millions of people. Jesus learned obedience through what Jesus suffered, and Jesus's completed experience equipped Jesus to become the author of our salvation. You cannot know God through a preacher, a book, or a recording—you have to hold on to God in your midnight hour when it is nobody but you and God. That is when you get the victory that stays with you. Whatever you have survived, God can use it to bring healing and hope to someone else.

### Step 8: Stop blaming, feeling sorry for yourself, and being bitter; learn to forgive

Along the way, you will have to learn to stop blaming, stop feeling sorry for yourself, and stop being bitter. You must learn to truly forgive. The preacher shares that the preacher was abused as a child by the preacher's father and mother, and relatives who refused to help. The preacher prayed for God to get the preacher out, but God did not remove the preacher from the situation—God took the preacher through it. Years later, when the preacher's parents were elderly and not well, God asked the preacher to take care of the preacher's parents. After much wrestling with God, the preacher and the preacher's husband brought the preacher's parents into the preacher's city, put the preacher's parents in a nice house, provided transportation, and eventually paid for nursing home care for 15 years. During that time, the preacher's father, at age 80, apologized for the first time ever, and the preacher led the preacher's father to Christ. The preacher realized the preacher was not buying a house—the preacher was buying a soul.

### Step 9: Show fruit through your actions, not just leaves through Christian advertisements

A fig tree with leaves but no fruit is worthless. Many Christians are the same way—they have bumper stickers, crosses around their necks, Bibles they carry to work, underlined Bibles, and church attendance, but no real fruit. Leaves are the advertisement; fruit is the reality. You can have all the external signs of Christianity and still not know God. Do not just have leaves—have fruit. If someone at work treats you poorly and you have grown past gossiping about that person and even started praying for that person, that is good. But if you then find out that person's car is in the shop and God puts it on your heart to drive that person to work every morning even though it is out of your way, now you have fruit under your leaves. You cannot argue with real spiritual fruit, and the church needs more of it.

### Step 10: Embrace what God wants to teach you while in difficult situations

God knows where you are and what God wants to do in your life. The situation may be very difficult, but God is still good. Instead of only praying "get me out of this," pray a new way: "God, I know you will bring me out of this at the right time, but while I am in it, let me get what you want me to have. Let me learn what you want me to learn." When you pass the specific trials God is testing you through in your current season, you move to the next level of spiritual growth and ministry responsibility God has prepared for you. If you do not pass those trials, you will simply take them over and over. Embrace the lesson so you do not have to repeat the pain.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Fur Coat and the Television Station

The preacher was believing God for a fur coat because the preacher saw other ministers' wives wearing fur coats and thought that wearing a fur coat was the mark of spiritual success. The preacher's neighbor—a Christian girl who attended the preacher's Bible study—showed up at the preacher's door with the exact fur coat the preacher had been believing for. Inside, the preacher was filled with jealousy, rage, and judgment, thinking the preacher's neighbor was not as spiritual as the preacher was and did not deserve the fur coat. A few years later, the preacher's young pastor came by excited to tell the preacher about a large honorarium and new ministry partners. Again, the preacher was not truly happy for the preacher's young pastor. After crying out to God, the preacher made a decision to tithe and give offerings from then on, even if the preacher never saw a penny in return. After that day, the preacher's financial situation turned around. The preacher learned that the Christian life is not just about giving—it is about the attitude you carry in every part of your life, and trusting God so much that you do not want anything God does not want you to have, now or ever.

### Example 2: Caring for the Parents Who Abused Her

The preacher was sexually abused by the preacher's father for years. The preacher's mother knew but was afraid to act, and relatives the preacher turned to for help refused to get involved. The preacher was born again at age 9 and developed a "go-through spirit" rather than a give-up spirit. Years later, when the preacher's parents were elderly and not well, God asked the preacher to take care of the preacher's parents. After much wrestling with God, the preacher and the preacher's husband brought the preacher's parents into the preacher's city, put the preacher's parents in a nice house, provided transportation, and eventually paid for nursing home care for 15 years. During that time, the preacher's father, at age 80, apologized for the first time ever, and the preacher led the preacher's father to Christ. The preacher realized the preacher was not buying a house—the preacher was buying a soul. What the preacher went through is now helping millions of people around the globe.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Trust God's timing even when you do not understand it
- ✅ Ask older, experienced believers for wisdom rather than assuming you know best
- ✅ Keep a good attitude when people treat you unfairly
- ✅ Forgive those who have hurt you, even when it costs you something
- ✅ Look for real spiritual fruit in your life, not just religious advertisements or outward appearances
- ✅ Embrace the lessons God is teaching you in hard places
- ✅ Hold on to God in your midnight hour when nobody else is around
- ✅ Ask God to give you what you can handle, not just what you want
- ✅ Let your past hurts become the source of helping others
- ✅ Value experience and character over talent and early promotion

- ❌ Do not compare your spiritual maturity or ministry success to other believers
- ❌ Do not promote a believer to a position of influence if that believer has talent but lacks spiritual maturity
- ❌ Do not rely on religious activity—underlining Bible verses, bumper stickers, church attendance—as proof of genuine faith
- ❌ Do not blame other people, feel sorry for yourself, or hold onto bitterness
- ❌ Do not rush God's timing or demand that God remove you from every hard situation
- ❌ Do not let what other people think of you ruin your day or derail your God-given calling
- ❌ Do not seek to be used by God while refusing to be changed by God

## Keep In Mind

- God knows exactly where you are, even when you feel invisible or forgotten
- God never does evil to people, but God sometimes allows difficult circumstances to shape and mature people
- The world is desperate for hope, and your personal testimony can be that hope for other people
- You can have a revival inside you that does not depend on the church meeting, the music, or the preacher
- What other people think of you is not nearly as important as you think it is
- Your reputation in heaven matters far more than your reputation on earth
- God loves you, and that is what truly matters
- Painful, unjust experiences can be worked out for your good if you go through them with the right attitude
- Experience is more valuable than formal education when it comes to living a godly life
- You do not really know anything until you recognize that you do not know everything

## Security & Safety Notes

- Beware of confusing emotional excitement, crowd energy, or loud worship music with genuine spiritual anointing from God
- Do not let a person's talent or public ministry platform convince you that person has spiritual maturity—these are not the same thing
- Do not promote a new convert into a position of influence before that new convert has been tested and equipped with experience and godly character
- Guard your own heart against jealousy when other people receive what you want—this is a specific test God is running in your own life
- Do not put your trust in religious activity as a substitute for a genuinely transformed heart

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You attend every church meeting, sing every song, and feel emotionally moved, but nothing in your life actually changes after the church meeting ends.
  **Solution:** Focus on having a personal revival inside your own heart, not just experiencing a corporate revival meeting. Ask God to change you, not just to give you an emotional experience.

- **Problem:** You are young and full of zeal, but you dismiss older believers as outdated or irrelevant because those older believers do not understand your world.
  **Solution:** Humble yourself and ask one experienced believer for one piece of wisdom. That older believer's years of walking with God are more valuable than your technical skill or enthusiasm.

- **Problem:** You have been through a painful situation for a long time and you are angry at God for not getting you out of it.
  **Solution:** Shift your prayer from "God, get me out of this" to "God, while I am in this, teach me what you want me to learn." Trust that God will bring you out at the right time.

- **Problem:** You hold onto bitterness and blame toward people who have deeply hurt you, and it is poisoning your present life.
  **Solution:** Forgiveness is not for the person who hurt you—it is for you. God may even ask you to bless those who hurt you, as God did in the preacher's life with the preacher's parents. The freedom you gain is worth the cost.

- **Problem:** You have all the outward signs of Christianity—a Bible, church attendance, religious talk—but your life produces no real fruit.
  **Solution:** Let a person who does not know Christ look at your life. If that person can only point to your "leaves" (religious advertisements), you need to ask God to grow fruit in you. Serve a person who cannot repay you.

- **Problem:** You compare your ministry, calling, or life circumstances to other believers and become discouraged or jealous when those other believers are promoted and you are not.
  **Solution:** God's timing is perfect. If you are not ready, promotion would destroy you. Trust that God is preparing you in the season you are in right now.

- **Problem:** You let other people's opinions of you steal your joy and sidetrack your God-given purpose.
  **Solution:** Remember that haters will hate, judges will judge, and critics will criticize whether you are right or wrong. Your identity is in what God says about you, not what other people say. Do not let one more day of your life be stolen by another person's opinion.
