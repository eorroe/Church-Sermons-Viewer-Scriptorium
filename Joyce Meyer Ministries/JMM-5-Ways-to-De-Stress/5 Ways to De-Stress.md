# 5 Ways to De-Stress

## Overview

This sermon teaches practical, biblical strategies for managing stress by addressing its root causes rather than just its symptoms. Joyce Meyer explains that real stress comes from what is going on inside us, not from external circumstances, and presents actionable ways to experience the peace that Jesus promised. The message combines foundational spiritual principles—such as receiving forgiveness, trusting God, and processing emotions—with thirteen specific stress-relief practices that can be implemented immediately.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel overwhelmed by busyness, responsibility, or too many demands on your time
- When guilt, condemnation, or self-condemnation are weighing you down
- When you are stressed about your past, your future, your children, how people treat you, or circumstances beyond your reach that you cannot control or change
- When you feel like you are carrying your burdens alone instead of trusting God
- When you want practical, daily habits to reduce stress and experience more peace
- When you are stuck in comparison, overcommitment, or choice overload

## Steps

### Step 1: Come unto Jesus and Receive His Peace

Acknowledge that you are laboring and heavy laden, and come to Jesus for rest. Accept that the Prince of Peace lives inside you if you are a Christian, and that peace is defined as a quiet heart no matter what your circumstances look like on the surface. Realize that peace is a skill you can learn, just as Joyce Meyer learned to be more peaceful even with increasing responsibility. If she can learn to be peaceful, you can learn to be peaceful too.

### Step 2: Take Jesus' Yoke Upon You and Learn of Him

Take Jesus' yoke upon you by staying connected to Him, remaining close in every situation, and watching how He would handle your circumstances. Learn to handle situations the way Jesus would handle them rather than just asking Him to change your circumstances. Instead of only praying for God to remove your stressors, pray "God, teach me your ways" and ask Him to show you how to handle situations the way He would. Study how Jesus lived so you can follow His example.

### Step 3: Receive God's Forgiveness and Let Go of Condemnation

Confess your sin to God and receive His forgiveness through faith in Jesus Christ. Understand that God has forgiven your sin and your guilt—Psalm 32:5 says, "You instantly forgave me the guilt and the iniquity of my sin." If you have confessed and received Christ, God has removed your sin, and there is nothing left to feel guilty about. Accept that you are an imperfect human being, and that is exactly why Jesus came. Stop living under a burden of condemnation, and choose to have a right attitude toward yourself because everywhere you go, there you are—if you cannot get along with yourself, everything else will be rough.

### Step 4: Trust Yourself and Everything to God

Identify the things in your life that you cannot control—your past, your future, your children, how people treat you, and circumstances beyond your reach. Give all of these things to God and leave them there (1 Peter 2:23). Trust that what you receive is up to God, and if someone mistreats you, do not waste your life hating them—trust God to take care of it. Trust is the answer to all frustration, and you must learn to trust God with every little thing.

### Step 5: Process Your Emotions Through Prayer Instead of Exploding

When you feel sad, distressed, or overwhelmed, tell God exactly how you feel, just as Jesus did in Gethsemane when He said, "My soul is very sad and deeply grieved." Do not suppress your emotions—process them through prayer. Understand that prayer does not always mean God will remove what is bothering you; many times He strengthens you and enables you to endure it with a good attitude. Slip away quietly to pray, and let an angel minister to you in your spirit, just as one came to strengthen Jesus.

### Step 6: Practice Shrug Therapy (Don't Sweat the Small Stuff)

When something annoying or inconvenient happens that you cannot change or control, shrug it off instead of reacting emotionally. Say to yourself, "It is what it is," or "I'm not impressed." Do not waste energy getting upset over something that cannot be undone. Cast your care upon God once and for all, because God cannot and will not take care of you until you cast your care on Him (1 Peter 5:7). Shrug therapy means you choose not to take on burdens that are not yours to carry. Note that this is for minor, unchangeable irritations—not for situations that require you to take action, speak up, or address sin or injustice.

### Step 7: Know When to Be Quiet

When you are under pressure or being falsely accused, do not feel compelled to defend yourself or convince others that you are a good person. Jesus made no reply when the chief priests and elders accused Him, and He remained silent before Pilate and Herod. For 5 hours and 58 minutes on the cross, He said only 41 words in Greek—about one minute of speaking. Keep your mouth shut when the enemy is trying to provoke you, because opening your mouth can give him an entrance into your life. It is not always necessary or wise to speak up in every situation.

### Step 8: Forgive Quickly

Decide to forgive people immediately and completely, even for serious offenses. Remember that Jesus forgave His executioners from the cross, saying, "Father, forgive them for they know not what they do." If Jesus could forgive that, you can get over the day-to-day offenses that come your way. Being easily offended or touchy is a choice, and holding onto offense will keep you stressed no matter how many external stressors you remove. Real stress comes from what is going on inside you, not from what is happening around you.

### Step 9: Stay in Your Comfort Zone and Respect Your Limits

Recognize your own strengths and limits by honestly assessing where you consistently perform well and where you struggle, and do not compare yourself with other people who have different gifts and callings. Stop trying to do activities you are lousy at just because everyone else is doing them. It is not spiritual to overextend yourself into areas where you have no strength or calling. When you force yourself into situations outside your comfort zone, you will be stressed, miserable, and feel bad about yourself for not being good at it. You are designed for a specific purpose, and staying in the zone God has for you is where you will thrive.

### Step 10: Eliminate Unfruitful Commitments

Take a careful look at your schedule and your life, and eliminate everything that is not bearing good fruit—meaning activities that do not produce positive spiritual, relational, or personal growth in your life or in the lives of others. Remove yourself from committees, groups, or activities that argue without making decisions. Stop spending time gossiping or listening to gossip. Stop listening to people complain about problems they have no intention of solving. Stop wasting time watching meaningless television or flipping through channels for hours without purpose. Cut out anything that does not add value to your life or the lives of others.

### Step 11: Exercise Regularly

Make exercise a regular part of your life because it is one of the best stress relievers in the world. Physical activity increases endorphins, which are the feel-good hormones in your body, making you happier and helping you worry less. Exercise also improves your sleep, which counteracts stress, and sweating removes toxic poisons from your system. It lowers blood pressure, resting heart rate, and cholesterol while improving your overall mood. Start with something sustainable, such as a daily four-mile walk or a home workout routine, and be consistent.

### Step 12: Take Time to Relax and Do Things You Enjoy on Purpose

Do not wait until you collapse to rest. Schedule mini vacations and breaks throughout your day and your week, even if they are only 10 minutes long. Enjoy simple pleasures such as sitting quietly with a cup of coffee in the afternoon, getting a pedicure once a month, looking at beautiful things, or getting outside into nature and breathing deeply. Relax on purpose. Take regular breaks from work, and remember that there will always be more work to do—God did not provide life through Christ so you could work all the time.

### Step 13: Avoid Choice Overload by Being Decisive

Simplify your life by reducing the number of choices you have to make. Too many options—such as 48,750 items in an average grocery store or 228 kinds of cereal—create stress because they make it hard to decide. Stop going back and forth on decisions, and make a choice and move forward. Do not live in fear of missing God, because if you miss Him, He will lead you back. Be decisive, simplify your wardrobe, your schedule, and your commitments, and free your mind from the exhaustion of constant decision-making.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Spilled Coffee

Joyce Meyer arrived in the morning to find that her new automatic coffee pot had overflowed all over the counter because she forgot to put the pot all the way under the spout. She looked at the mess, felt nothing, and simply shrugged. This is shrug therapy in action: something went wrong, it could not be undone, and getting upset would not have fixed it. She cleaned it up without a full-on emotional reaction because she had trained herself not to waste energy on things that are out of her control.

### Example 2: The Tuna Casserole and Comfort Zones

Joyce Meyer decided to try cooking and bought expensive spices and cookbooks, only to make a tuna casserole that turned out like glue. She laughed at herself, returned the spices, and went back to her comfort zone of teaching and ministry where she thrives. She emphasizes that staying in your comfort zone does not mean you never do anything difficult; it means you recognize your limits and respect them rather than stressing yourself out by trying to be good at activities you were not designed to do.

### Example 3: Mary and Martha

Joyce Meyer references the story of Mary and Martha. Martha was stressed and overwhelmed with work while Mary sat at Jesus' feet, choosing what was better. Many people are so addicted to work and busyness that they miss the really important areas of life, such as relationships with their children, their spouse, and God. You cannot build a relationship with your kids overnight because you are their parent—relationships require time and presence.

### Example 4: Jesus in Gethsemane and on the Cross

Jesus experienced more stress than any person who ever lived. In Gethsemane, He was deeply grieved and told His disciples how sad He was, then He slipped away to pray. An angel came and strengthened Him. During His 5 hours and 58 minutes on the cross, He spoke only 41 words in Greek, which took about one minute to say. For nearly all of that excruciating time, He was completely silent. He also forgived His executioners immediately. These are the ultimate examples of processing emotions through prayer, knowing when to be quiet, and forgiving quickly.

### Example 5: Walking and Exercise

Joyce Meyer shares that she walks four miles every day and works out at home with a trainer and family members. She says her cholesterol is the lowest it has been in 15 years and that the energy she has gained from walking shocks her. Exercise has physically transformed her health and given her more stamina for her responsibilities. This demonstrates that taking care of your body is not optional—it is a practical, God-honoring way to manage stress.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Come to Jesus daily and abide in Him as your source of peace
- ✅ Receive God's forgiveness through confession and faith, then stop living under condemnation
- ✅ Give God everything you cannot control—your past, your future, your kids, and your circumstances
- ✅ Pray honestly about how you feel instead of suppressing your emotions
- ✅ Practice shrug therapy for minor irritations you cannot change, but take action on situations that require it
- ✅ Stay silent when someone is trying to provoke you or when speaking would only make matters worse
- ✅ Forgive quickly and completely, even before the other person asks for it
- ✅ Assess your actual strengths and limits honestly, and do not compare yourself with others
- ✅ Take inventory of your schedule and cut out activities that are not bearing good fruit
- ✅ Exercise regularly to increase endorphins, improve sleep, and lower stress physically
- ✅ Schedule mini breaks and real relaxation time on purpose, even if it is only 10 minutes
- ✅ Be decisive to avoid the stress of choice overload
- ✅ Make better preparations the night before to reduce morning rushing
- ✅ Learn to say no and let others help you instead of doing everything yourself

## Keep In Mind

- Stress comes from what is inside you, not from your external circumstances
- God does not want to change everything around you; He wants to change you
- "Follow me" means watch how Jesus lives and learn to handle situations His way, not just ask Him to fix situations for you
- The fruit of the Spirit does not pop out of you when you are rushing
- There will always be more work to do, so you might as well learn not to work all the time
- You will never be happy in your later years if you do not invest time in relationships now
- Laughter is a great stress reliever and is probably better than anxiety medicine
- God gives you wisdom and grace to make changes, but you have to actually make those changes
- Your comfort zone is where your strength is—stay there and serve God from that place
- If you miss God in a decision, He will lead you back

## Security & Safety Notes

- Protect your peace by guarding your mind and mouth. When you are under pressure, your words can give the enemy an entrance into your life, so be careful what you say.
- Avoid gossip and complaining, because listening to other people's unresolved problems without action can drain your emotional and spiritual energy.
- Set boundaries with people who are manipulative or toxic, because staying around negativity will keep you stressed and offended.
- Forgive quickly as a protection for your own heart, not just for the other person's benefit. Unforgiveness is an open door for stress, bitterness, and illness.
- Do not neglect your physical health through lack of sleep, exercise, or rest, because your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit and neglecting it opens the door to illness and burnout.
- Trust God with your finances, your future, and your family, because worrying about these things without praying and surrendering them to God will destroy your peace.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trying to manage stress while feeling guilty, condemned, and defeated inside
  **Solution:** Start by receiving God's forgiveness and letting go of condemnation. No matter what external stress-relief strategies you use, guilt will always push you over the edge. Confess your sin, receive God's forgiveness, and put on the robe of righteousness.

- **Problem:** Trying to control everything and everyone around you
  **Solution:** Practice shrug therapy. Cast your care upon God, because God cannot take care of you until you release the burden. Let go of people, circumstances, and outcomes that are not yours to control.

- **Problem:** Exploding emotionally when pressure builds up
  **Solution:** Process your emotions through prayer before you react. Tell God how you feel, let Him strengthen you, and learn to be quiet when you feel provoked. Do not let the fruit of the Spirit be squeezed out of you by rushing and pressure.

- **Problem:** Overcommitting and staying too busy to have relationships
  **Solution:** Eliminate everything from your schedule that is not bearing good fruit. Cut out unproductive committees, gossip, meaningless television, and complaining conversations. Invest that time in people who matter.

- **Problem:** Comparing yourself to others and trying to do activities you are not good at
  **Solution:** Stay in your comfort zone, respect your limits, and refuse to compare yourself with others. You were designed for a specific purpose, and trying to be like someone else will only create stress and dissatisfaction.

- **Problem:** Being easily offended and holding grudges
   **Solution:** Forgive quickly, even before the other person asks for it. Remember Jesus on the cross, and realize that holding offense is a choice that will keep you stressed and miserable. Being touchy is a behavior you can stop by deciding to let offenses go.

- **Problem:** Skipping exercise, rest, and relaxation because you are too busy
  **Solution:** Make exercise and relaxation non-negotiable priorities. Schedule mini breaks, take 10-minute vacations, and move your body daily. Your body and mind cannot sustain stress without proper care.

- **Problem:** Wasting time and energy on too many choices
  **Solution:** Be decisive. Simplify your life by reducing options, and trust that if you make a mistake, God will still lead you. Do not spend hours agonizing over decisions that can be made quickly.
