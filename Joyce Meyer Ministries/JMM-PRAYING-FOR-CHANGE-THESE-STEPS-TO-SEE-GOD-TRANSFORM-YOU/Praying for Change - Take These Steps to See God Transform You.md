# Joyce Meyer: Praying for Change? Take These Steps to See God Transform You

## Overview

This sermon by Joyce Meyer teaches believers that lasting personal transformation requires renewed thinking and intentional action rather than waiting passively for God to intervene. Drawing from her personal experience of surviving sexual abuse by her father, grandfather, and uncles, enduring fear, and rebuilding her life, she demonstrates that God established a specific plan for each person's life and has not changed that plan. The sermon teaches that to experience the abundant life Jesus provided through His death, you must renew your mind through Scripture study, reject jealousy and excuses, stop compromising your moral standards, and actively persevere in faith when faced with opposition.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel trapped by past trauma or abuse and need practical steps to break its emotional and behavioral hold on your life
- When you find yourself repeatedly comparing yourself to others and feeling bitter about what they have that you lack
- When you want to experience God's transforming work in your life but have not seen measurable change after praying
- When you need to replace recurring negative thought patterns with truth from Scripture
- When you are making choices that conflict with your stated values and need to stop compromising
- When you struggle with a complaining mindset and want to develop a habit of gratitude
- When you are facing discouragement, setbacks, or spiritual opposition and need encouragement to continue

## Steps

### Step 1: Ask God what He wants to do for you

Set aside 15 minutes of uninterrupted time and write down your answer to this specific question: "What do I want God to do for me in my life?" List each area where you desire change. Then, identify what action you are currently taking toward each item on that list. Read the following categories of what God has already made available to believers: salvation from sin, physical and emotional healing, freedom from fear and anxiety, inner peace, lasting joy, financial provision, wisdom for decisions, and the ability to love others well. Take the specific action God has already revealed through His Word, the Holy Spirit's conviction, or wise spiritual counsel. If you are unsure what step to take, ask God directly in prayer, search the Bible for a relevant promise or command, and consult a mature Christian leader who knows your situation. Understand that God can restore and heal damage caused by others' harmful actions, but your active participation in the healing process is required.

### Step 2: Renew your mind through the Word

Choose one Bible passage to study for the week. Read the full passage slowly, then read it again. Read it a third time, pausing after each verse to write down what it means in your own words. Look up any words you do not understand using a Bible dictionary or trusted commentary. Romans 12:2 states: "Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of the mind." After studying, spend five minutes asking God to reveal how this passage applies to your current circumstances. Write down any insights. Track your progress: the number of times you study the passage during the week is the amount of virtue and knowledge that will grow in your life as your thinking is renewed.

### Step 3: Stop looking at what you don't have

When you notice yourself wishing you had someone else's job, relationship, possessions, or opportunities, stop the thought and redirect your attention. Physically turn away from social media posts, conversations, or situations such as social media, workplace conversations, or family gatherings where you tend to compare your life to others. On a sheet of paper, write down three specific things you already have in that same area of life. For example, if you are comparing your career to a peer's promotion, write down three skills, experiences, or resources you currently possess. Thank God for each one out loud. Choose one of those assets and take a small action today to develop or use it. The goal is to shift from desiring what belongs to someone else to functioning in and enjoying what God has already given you.

### Step 4: Let go of past abuse and hurt

Acknowledge what happened to you by stating the facts out loud in one clear sentence. For example: "What happened to me was wrong, and it was not my fault." Do not minimize it, and do not exaggerate it. Write the sentence down. Then state your decision out loud: "What happened to me does not determine how I will behave for the rest of my life." This is your boundary. When you notice yourself using your past as justification for unkind words, unhealthy choices, or emotional withdrawal toward others, stop and choose a different response in that moment. Use your experience to help someone else by identifying one specific way you can support a person who is going through a similar situation, and take that action this week. This turns your pain into purpose rather than using it as an excuse.

### Step 5: Stop complaining and be thankful

Each morning before you get out of bed, name five specific things you are thankful for out loud. Make sure at least two of them are practical, tangible blessings: a roof over your head, food in your refrigerator, a bed to sleep in, clothing that fits, friends who care about you, or access to the Bible. Write them down in a notebook or on your phone. Throughout the day, when you feel yourself about to complain, pause and name three additional things you are thankful for before you speak. When you interact with people, intentionally look for something genuine to praise or encourage in them, and say it out loud before the conversation ends.

### Step 6: Put off the old nature and put on the new nature

Read Ephesians 4:22-24 out loud each morning. Identify one specific behavior or thought pattern from your old way of living that you need to stop today. Name it clearly: for example, lying when it is easier than telling the truth, losing your temper when you are frustrated, or ignoring people who are not useful to you. Then identify one specific new behavior that replaces it. For example, instead of lying, commit to telling the truth even when it costs you something. Instead of losing your temper, commit to walking away for ten minutes before responding. Each evening, evaluate honestly: did you put on the new nature today? If yes, note it. If no, confess it to God and to a trusted person, and recommit tomorrow.

### Step 7: Keep a good attitude in the midst of turmoil

Your attitude is your responsibility and no one else's. When a difficult situation occurs, do not allow yourself to react with anger, self-pity, or resentment. Instead, pause and ask God out loud for the grace to respond with a good attitude in that specific situation. Say out loud: "God, give me grace to have a good attitude right now." Choose one positive statement to speak over the situation. For example: "This is difficult, but I will get through it," or "God is with me in this." Your attitude during hardship affects your ability to persevere and to represent your faith well to others. Practice this daily so it becomes your default response when circumstances are painful or unfair.

### Step 8: Don't compromise

Define your non-negotiable standards in writing. List at least three behaviors or choices you will not engage in regardless of social pressure, such as lying, gossiping, watching content that violates your values, or making financial decisions that compromise your integrity. When you are faced with a situation where you are tempted to do slightly less than what you know is right, pause and ask yourself: "Am I doing this because it is right, or because it is easier?" Speak this statement out loud: "This is between me and God. I am not doing what everyone else is doing." Choose the action that aligns with your written standards, even if it means you stand alone.

### Step 9: Get your mind off yourself

Each morning, ask yourself two specific questions before you begin your day: "Who am I going to be with today?" and "What is one thing I can do to build that person up?" Write down your answers. Follow through by doing exactly what you identified. At the end of the day, evaluate whether you carried it out. Repeat this process daily. Practical examples include giving someone a specific compliment, helping someone with a task without being asked, listening to someone who needs to talk without interrupting, or sharing a resource that will help someone else. The goal is to shift your focus from what you need to what someone else needs.

### Step 10: Deal with jealousy and make peace

Identify one specific person toward whom you feel jealousy. Name the exact thing you want that they have: for example, their promotion, their marriage, their ministry position, or their financial situation. Write it down. Confess your jealousy out loud to God. Then, take the practical step of making peace with that person. If your jealousy has caused you to speak poorly about them, say something negative behind their back, or withdraw from them, go to that person directly and acknowledge what you did. Say out loud: "I have been jealous of you, and I was wrong to treat you that way." Ask for their forgiveness if your behavior toward them was unkind. Then, verbally declare out loud: "What God has for them is between them and God. What God has for me is between me and Him."

### Step 11: Fight the good fight of faith

Know that opposition is normal and expected when you make a decision to do something good. The devil's objective is to discourage you and make you quit. When you encounter resistance after deciding to follow God's direction, speak these words out loud: "Do your best. I am not giving up. I do not care how many times you knock me down. I will get up and I will do it again until I have the victory." Then, take the next physical action you can take in the right direction, even if it is very small. Do not wait for the opposition to stop. Keep moving forward one step at a time, and repeat this process every time you are knocked down.

## Examples

### Example 1: Joyce Meyer's personal testimony

Joyce Meyer was sexually abused by her father, grandfather, and uncles over a period of years. Her mother knew about the abuse and did not stop it. After the abuse ended, she married the first man who showed interest in her because she believed no one would ever truly want her, and that marriage brought five additional years of emotional pain. By her 20s, she could not remember a single day when she felt relaxed or at peace. For years, she repeated the excuse: "I was abused, I was abused, I was abused," using the abuse as an excuse to act out in anger, live in fear, and refuse to trust anyone. She then committed to studying the Bible systematically, spending thousands of hours over many years learning what it actually said. Her mind was gradually renewed. She stopped identifying herself as a victim and began using her experience to help other people who had been abused. She now teaches publicly that anyone can overcome what the devil has done to them by renewing their mind and doing their part.

### Example 2: Rachel and Leah

Jacob agreed to work for seven years for his uncle Laban in exchange for permission to marry Rachel, whom he loved because of her beauty. When the seven years passed, Laban deceived Jacob by giving him Leah, the older sister, instead. Jacob then worked another seven years to receive Rachel as his wife. The further complication was that Rachel could not conceive children, while Leah gave birth to son after son. Through this family situation, God taught Joyce Meyer the principle: "Nobody gets everything, but everybody gets something." Rachel had the love of her husband, and Leah had children. When you stop focusing on what you do not have and instead ask God to show you what you do have, you can begin to use and enjoy those blessings rather than resenting what someone else possesses.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Study the Bible by reading a passage multiple times, writing it in your own words, looking up unfamiliar words, and asking God how it applies to your life. Do not read it quickly without absorbing it.
- ✅ Spend time every day with God through prayer, Bible study, listening to a teaching, or listening to a sermon. Schedule a specific time so it does not get skipped.
- ✅ Choose to think the best of people rather than assuming the worst about them. When you catch yourself thinking negatively about someone, replace that thought with one positive thing you know to be true about them.
- ✅ When you feel like complaining, stop and name at least five specific things you are thankful for before you speak.
- ✅ Maintain a good attitude even when circumstances are painful, unfair, or difficult. Ask God for grace in that specific moment.
- ✅ Do not lower your moral standards because other people do. Remind yourself that your choices are between you and God.
- ✅ Get your mind off your own problems by identifying one person you can help today and doing it without being asked.
- ✅ Confess jealousy, resentment, or wrong behavior to a trusted spiritual mentor or to the person you have wronged so you can be healed and restored.
- ✅ When opposition comes after you decide to do something good, speak victory out loud and take the next right step, no matter how small.
- ✅ Renew your mind every day by filling it with Scripture. Proverbs 23:7 states: "For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he."

## Keep In Mind

- God has made salvation, healing, freedom from fear, peace, joy, provision, wisdom, and the ability to love others well available to you as a believer, but you must obey what God has revealed to you through Scripture, through the Holy Spirit's conviction, and through wise spiritual counsel in order to walk in those things.
- God established a specific plan for your life before you were born, and He has not changed His mind about that plan.
- You cannot live the Christian life by your own strength. Apart from Jesus, you can do nothing of eternal value.
- The amount of time and attention you give to studying and understanding the truth you hear determines how much of that truth is worked out in your life and character.
- Proverbs 23:7 states: "For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he." Your thoughts shape your life and your outcomes.
- What you believe determines what you settle for in life. You will not experience more than what you believe is possible, regardless of what Jesus accomplished for you.
- Scripture functions as a mirror: when you read it, you see both the nature of Jesus and the distance between your current character and His. Use it to measure your growth honestly.
- God sent Jesus to die so that you could experience full, abundant life now, not only in heaven after you die. John 10:10 states: "The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly."
- What God decides to do in another person's life is not your concern. Focus on obeying what God has called you to do, and trust Him with everyone else.
- You are not required to match anyone else's path or decisions. What God wants for your life is a personal matter between you and Him.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This sermon is intended for personal spiritual growth and private reflection
- Confession of faults or wrong attitudes should be done in a safe environment with Christians who demonstrate consistent faith, biblical knowledge, and a godly character over time, such as a pastor, small group leader, or spiritual mentor. Do not confess to someone who may use that information against you.
- If you have experienced abuse or trauma, seek additional support from a licensed counselor or pastor alongside your spiritual practice. Professional counseling and spiritual growth are not in conflict with each other.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Waiting for God to do everything while making no personal effort toward change
  **Solution:** Take specific action on what God has already told you to do: study the Bible using a structured method, renew your mind daily by replacing wrong thoughts with Scripture, apply what you learn to your daily choices, and take one step of faith even when you feel afraid or opposed.

- **Problem:** Using past abuse, trauma, or hurt as a permanent excuse for acting out in anger, living in fear, refusing to trust others, or using substances to cope
  **Solution:** Acknowledge the facts of what happened to you out loud, state clearly that those events do not authorize you to continue acting in harmful ways, and take one specific action today to respond differently than you did in the past. Turn your experience into help for someone else rather than using it as justification.

- **Problem:** Being jealous of what other people have and resenting them for it
  **Solution:** Write down three things you currently have in the same area where you feel lacking. Thank God for each one. Then take one action today to develop or use one of those assets. Remember that others' blessings are between them and God, and your blessings are between you and God.

- **Problem:** Giving up when opposition, difficulty, or discouragement comes after you decide to follow God
  **Solution:** Speak victory out loud using specific words. Take one small, physical step in the right direction. Repeat that process every time you are knocked down. Persistence in doing the right thing, not the absence of opposition, is what produces victory.

- **Problem:** Compromising your moral standards because other people are doing it or because it seems easier
  **Solution:** Review your written list of non-negotiable standards before you make a decision. Ask yourself whether you are choosing this because it is right or because it is easier. Speak out loud that your choices are between you and God, and choose the action that aligns with your written standards.

- **Problem:** Reading the Bible quickly without studying it and expecting life change to happen
  **Solution:** Read one passage three times, write it in your own words, look up words you do not understand, ask God how it applies to your specific situation, and write down your answer. Study produces transformation. Speed reading does not.
