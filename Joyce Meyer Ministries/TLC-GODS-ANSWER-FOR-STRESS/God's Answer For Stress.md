# God's Answer For Stress

## Overview

This sermon teaches that God's answer for stress is not simply advice, but a call to a different way of living. Joyce Meyer explains that while we live in a stressful world, God does not want us to live stressed out. Through biblical examples such as Moses in Exodus 17:12 needing Aaron and Hur to hold his hands up, and personal testimony from Joyce Meyer's 81 years of ministry, she shows how believers can find rest for their souls by keeping God first in daily time with Him, setting healthy boundaries with people, asking for help instead of isolating in pride, and embracing the yoke of Jesus described in Matthew 11:28-30. The message emphasizes that stress is not just a physical condition but a spiritual condition rooted in fear, self-centeredness, and striving apart from God, requiring intentional daily choices to rest in God's will rather than striving in our own strength. Self-centeredness, as Joyce Meyer teaches, is the root cause of many problems in our society because it tends toward greed, dishonesty, and a lack of care for others.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When your schedule, responsibilities, or relationships have created a consistent sense of anxiety or exhaustion that you cannot resolve on your own
- When you are burnt out from overcommitting and people-pleasing
- When you are weary and carrying burdens alone
- When you need to learn how to set healthy boundaries
- When you struggle with pride that keeps you from asking for help
- When your mind and emotions feel out of alignment with the peace that God promises in Philippians 4:6-7
- When you are working hard for God but forgetting to spend time with God
- When you need biblical perspective on rest and balance

## Steps

### Step 1: Acknowledge that stress is not God's will for your life

Recognize that while stress is common in this world, it is not the lifestyle God intends for you. Admit when you are carrying too much and agree that change is necessary. Evaluate whether your stress comes primarily from your own thoughts and choices (such as people-pleasing or overcommitting), from external pressures (such as a high-demand job or family crisis), or from a combination of both. Write down each stressor and ask: Is this something I can control? Is this something I am choosing? Is this something I need to accept for now? Be specific in your answers.

### Step 2: Admit that you cannot manage everything alone, stop justifying self-reliance, and be willing to be seen as someone who needs help

Moses could not hold his hands up alone and needed Aaron and Hur to support him in Exodus 17:12. If you are trying to carry every burden by yourself, you are not trusting God's design for community. Contact one or two specific people you trust—such as a spouse, close friend, small group leader, or pastor—and tell them you are carrying too much and need support. Ask those trusted people to pray for you specifically about the stress you are experiencing and the changes you need to make. Accept practical help when offered, such as having someone listen to your situation, review your calendar with you, or check in on you regularly. Pride will isolate you; humility will connect you to the help God has provided.

### Step 3: Set boundaries and stop people-pleasing

You do not have to say yes to every request, answer every call, or respond to every text immediately. Healthy boundaries protect your time, energy, and peace. Prepare a simple phrase such as 'I'm not able to take that on right now' and practice using it in low-stakes situations this week; remind yourself that declining a request is not a failure.

### Step 4: Keep God first in your life

Spend time with God before you work for God. Joyce Meyer learned this personally when God told her she was proud of working for Him but was not spending any time with Him. Begin each day with at least 15 to 30 minutes of prayer and Bible reading before checking your phone, email, or to-do list. Start with prayer, read one passage of Scripture, and worship through song or silence before you look at your tasks for the day.

### Step 5: Choose rest for your soul

God's answer for stress is rest. Rest is not simply sleeping more, though physical rest matters. Rest for your soul means entering the rest that Hebrews 4:10-11 describes, where the person who has entered God's rest has ceased from their own striving and human effort to find peace, just as God rested from His work of creation. Keep your emotions calm by identifying each emotion specifically—such as anxiety, frustration, or resentment—writing it down, and bringing it to God in prayer rather than suppressing it. When you feel stressed, pause and ask yourself what you are actually feeling, name it out loud or in writing, and then pray about that specific emotion rather than trying to ignore it. Maintain a positive mind by replacing anxious thoughts with specific verses such as Philippians 4:6-7, which says, "Do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God. And the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus." When an anxious thought comes, consciously replace it with this verse and repeat it until your mind shifts. Submit your daily choices, reactions, and timeline to what Scripture clearly teaches. Before making a decision or reacting to a situation, ask: Does this align with what the Bible says? If the answer is unclear, pray and wait rather than forcing a decision. Accept that rest and trust are part of God's will for you as described in Matthew 11:28-30 and Hebrews 4:10-11, even when circumstances feel urgent. God's timing is not always your timing, and His ways are higher than your ways as Isaiah 55:9 teaches. As Joyce Meyer teaches based on her own experience, a day of unresolved anger drains more energy than a full day of physical labor, and unforgiveness will suck all the energy out of you because it keeps your mind and emotions in a state of conflict. The people you are angry at are likely out living their lives unaffected, so your anger only continues to hurt you.

### Step 6: Embrace your current situation and learn from it

Every experience, including difficult ones, is an opportunity to build trust in God as described in Isaiah 40:28-31. Rather than rushing through difficulty or complaining about your circumstances, after reading Scripture and praying, ask yourself what character trait God is developing in you through this situation—such as patience, dependence, or humility—and write it down. Trust grows when you walk faithfully through what God has placed before you today, embracing the situation and saying, 'God, this is the way it is right now, and I want to learn what You are teaching me through it.'

### Step 7: Take Jesus' yoke upon you

Jesus said in Matthew 11:28-30, 'Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy and my burden is light.' A yoke is a wooden bar that joins two oxen so they pull the load together, as Vine's Expository Dictionary explains. When you take Jesus' yoke upon you, you are no longer pulling alone. He walks beside you, sharing the load. Stop striving as if everything depends on you.

### Step 8: Refuse new requests until you have reviewed your current calendar and removed at least one unnecessary commitment

Hurrying is a root cause of stress. When you take on more than your God-given responsibilities allow, you dishonor the wise limits on your time, energy, and commitments that God designed for your health and purpose. God has called you to be a good steward of the time, energy, and relationships He has given you, and overcommitting is poor stewardship. Your God-given responsibilities include your job to provide for your family, your role in your local church, and your personal relationship with Christ. Review your calendar for the next two weeks and cross out any event, meeting, or obligation that does not serve your God-given responsibilities. Ask: Does this honor God? Does it serve the family or work calling He has given me? Does it draw me closer to Him? If the answer is no to all three, cross it out. Slowness before God is not laziness; it is trust. If you are avoiding a responsibility that God has clearly called you to through Scripture or consistent godly counsel from mature believers, that is not trust—that is disobedience. The difference is whether your choice aligns with what God has already revealed in His Word rather than what you feel like doing. If you are waiting on God's timing rather than rushing ahead in fear or pride, that is trust.

### Step 9: Create balance between work, rest, play, and worship

A weekly rhythm of work, rest, recreation, and worship honors God and sustains your well-being, as 1 Peter 5:8 calls believers to be well-balanced. Block specific times on your calendar each week for rest, recreation, and worship—such as one full day of rest, two hours of enjoyable activity, and at least three worship or prayer sessions—treating these appointments as non-negotiable as you would a work meeting. 1 Peter 5:8 in the Amplified Bible calls believers to be well-balanced, warning that being out of balance opens a door for the enemy to enter your life. Neglecting any one of these areas will eventually produce stress in another. God designed rhythm into your life.

### Step 10: Stay in agreement with God's will daily

Each morning, write down your top three goals for the day, then pray, 'God, I surrender these to You. If any of them does not align with Your will as revealed in Scripture, redirect me today.' Be willing to change course when a door closes despite your best effort, when Scripture clearly contradicts your plan, or when godly counsel from mature believers consistently advises a different direction over time. Do not rely on feelings alone to determine God's guidance; test every impression against Scripture and wise advice. Choose one verse to memorize this week—such as Matthew 11:28-30 about Jesus' yoke—and write it on a card or in your phone notes. When you feel stressed, read that verse out loud or in your mind before reacting. Let the truth of that verse change your response by reminding you of who God is and what He has promised, rather than reacting out of emotion or habit. When you walk in agreement with God's Word, His peace will guard your heart and mind as Philippians 4:6-7 promises.

## Examples

### Example 1: Moses and the Battle

In Exodus, Moses stood on a hill holding his staff while the Israelites fought below. As long as his hands were raised, the Israelites were winning, but his arms grew heavy. Aaron and Hur came beside him, placed a stone under him, and each took hold of one of his hands so they remained steady until sunset. This illustrates that even great leaders need help, and God often provides that help through other people rather than expecting you to carry every burden alone.

### Example 2: Joyce Meyer's Dallas Trips

Joyce Meyer shared a personal example of how she used to travel to Dallas for meetings and feel intense pressure to perform perfectly in every situation. Through that season, she learned that striving to meet every expectation was exhausting and not God's best for her. She began to recognize the importance of setting limits and relying on God's grace rather than her own effort to impress people.

### Example 3: The Yoke of Jesus

A yoke is a wooden harness that connects two animals so they can pull a load together. When you take Jesus' yoke upon you, He walks beside you and helps carry what you cannot carry alone. Jesus said, "Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls." This is not a call to work harder, but to work differently—in partnership with Christ rather than in isolation.

### Example 4: The Stressed-Out Martha

In Luke 10, Martha was overwhelmed by all the work of hosting Jesus while her sister Mary sat at Jesus' feet listening. When Martha complained, Jesus gently told her that Mary had chosen what was better. This illustrates that preoccupation with tasks can keep us from the one thing that truly matters: sitting at Jesus' feet and listening to Him as Mary did in Luke 10:38-42, rather than being preoccupied with tasks like Martha was.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Write down each stressor you are carrying and rate its severity from 1 to 10, without criticizing yourself for feeling stressed
- ✅ Ask trusted people for help and accountability
- ✅ Decide your maximum number of weekly commitments and your latest acceptable work hour, communicate these limits to the people affected, and decline requests that exceed them
- ✅ Spend time with God before you serve others or work on your tasks
- ✅ Set aside 20 minutes for prayer, 10 minutes of worship music, and one moment of intentional trust where you verbally surrender a specific worry to God
- ✅ Accept that every experience can deepen your reliance on God
- ✅ Take Jesus' yoke and allow Him to share your burdens
- ✅ Slow down and evaluate whether your commitments align with God's will
- ✅ Keep a healthy balance of work, rest, play, and worship
- ✅ Stay in daily agreement with God's Word and His peace

## Keep In Mind

- God's answer for stress is rest, not more activity
- Rest is not just physical sleep; it is a settled state of trust in God that keeps your soul, emotions, and mind at peace regardless of your circumstances, as described in Hebrews 4:10-11. You experience this rest when you stop striving in your own strength and instead trust that God is in control.
- Asking for help is a sign of wisdom, not weakness
- Boundaries are not selfish; they are stewardship of the life God gave you
- You can serve God effectively without exhausting yourself
- Hurrying and overcommitting dishonor the wise limits on your time, energy, and commitments that God designed for your health and purpose
- A weekly rhythm of work, rest, recreation, and worship honors God and protects your well-being
- Experience in God's faithfulness builds trust that reduces anxiety
- Jesus wants to walk with you through every burden, not just fix your problems from a distance

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trying to carry every burden alone out of pride or fear of looking weak
  **Solution:** Reach out to trusted people and allow them to support you. Moses needed Aaron and Hur, and so do you.

- **Problem:** People-pleasing and saying yes to every request
  **Solution:** Practice setting boundaries. Your worth is not determined by how many tasks you complete for others.

- **Problem:** Working hard for God while neglecting time with God
  **Solution:** Prioritize your relationship with God before your to-do list. Ministry without intimacy leads to burnout.

- **Problem:** Trying to force your own will and timeline rather than trusting God
  **Solution:** Surrender your plans daily. Stress often comes when our will clashes with God's will. Rest in His timing.

- **Problem:** Overcommitting and filling every moment with activity
  **Solution:** Evaluate your calendar honestly and remove unnecessary commitments. Slowness before God is trust, not laziness.

- **Problem:** Carrying anxiety in your emotions and mind instead of resting in God's peace
  **Solution:** Choose to calm your emotions and renew your mind with God's Word. Rest for your soul is an intentional decision.
