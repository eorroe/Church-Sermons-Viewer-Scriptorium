# Stop Complaining

## Overview

This sermon teaches that complaining is not a harmless habit — it is a sin that insults God, opens the door for the enemy, and prevents the Holy Spirit from working in your life. Joyce Meyer explains that gratitude, not grumbling, is God's will for believers and the key to experiencing His power and deliverance. Through personal stories and scripture, this message challenges you to stop complaining and start living as a bright light in a crooked generation.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you find yourself frequently frustrated by minor inconveniences, abundance-induced confusion, or everyday problems
- When you pray about a situation but continue to grumble and feel stuck in it
- When you want to stand out as a believer in your workplace, family, or community by refusing to blend in with the world's complaining

## Steps

### Step 1: Catch Yourself Complaining and Treat It as Sin

The moment you hear yourself complain — about food, clothing, people, or circumstances — stop and recognize that complaining is sin. Repent and choose a different response. Do not let complaining become an automatic reaction to inconvenience or discomfort.

### Step 2: Replace Complaining with Prayer and Thanksgiving

Philippians 4:6 teaches: "Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving, not with complaining, let your request be made known unto God." When a problem arises, go to God in prayer instead of venting your frustration. Bring your request to Him with a heart of gratitude, not a spirit of complaint.

### Step 3: Stop Quenching the Spirit

1 Thessalonians 5:16-19 says to give thanks in all circumstances and not to quench the Spirit. Complaining actively stops the Holy Spirit from working in your life and situation. Choose thankfulness to keep the Spirit's power flowing freely.

### Step 4: Shift Your Focus to What You Have

The more you look at what you don't have, the more you miss what you do have. Make a deliberate practice of thanking God for the basics: hot water, clean water, the ability to walk and talk and see, your job, your family, your health. Gratitude rewires your perspective.

### Step 5: Simplify and Declutter

Abundance creates confusion and stress. Prune your possessions by giving away things you no longer use. A simpler life reduces opportunities to complain and helps you stay sane and grateful.

### Step 6: Make It a Game Against the Devil

The devil sets you up to get upset. Refuse to play his game. After reading this message, challenge yourself to see how long you can go without complaining, no matter what happens. When trouble comes, say: "Thank God I'm so blessed."

### Step 7: Stand Out by Being Thankful

In the workplace, at home, and in public, refuse to join in complaining conversations. When others grumble about working conditions, the boss, or their circumstances, say something like: "I know everything's not perfect, but I'm thankful I have a job." Be the light that shines in a crooked and twisted generation.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Missionary and the Cereal Aisle

A missionary who lived in Africa for years returned home to find a grocery store overflowing with cereal varieties. She went in to buy her favorite cereal and stood in the aisle so long, overwhelmed by choices, that she eventually gave up and left without buying anything. This story illustrates how abundance can create unnecessary frustration — and how complaining about having "too much" is a privileged problem.

### Example 2: Losing Pants at the Waldorf Astoria Spa

Joyce Meyer lost her pants at a luxury spa and had to walk through the lobby in her robe to get a replacement pair. Her immediate reaction was not gratitude — it was frustration. But she used the moment as a teaching opportunity, showing that even in absurd or embarrassing situations, we can choose to complain or we can choose to trust God. The pants were eventually found, but the real lesson was in the response.

### Example 3: 34 Things in 8 Weeks

Over an 8-week period, Joyce documented 34 frustrating events in her life — a back injury, a water-damaged table, lost luggage, nursing home calls about her mother, a broken window, a stained couch, lost glasses, no water for 24 hours, a stomach virus, and more. Despite the volume of inconveniences, she used them to illustrate that bad things happen to everyone, but complaining only makes them worse.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Do pray about problems instead of complaining about them
- ✅ Do give thanks in all circumstances — especially the difficult ones
- ✅ Do catch yourself in the act of complaining and immediately repent
- ✅ Do focus on what you have, not what you lack
- ✅ Do simplify your life by giving away things you no longer need or use
- ✅ Do stand out in complaining environments by speaking words of gratitude
- ❌ Don't complain about having too much or too many choices
- ❌ Don't pray about a problem and then turn around and complain about it
- ❌ Don't blame God, Moses, or others for your circumstances
- ❌ Don't quench the Holy Spirit by staying in a spirit of grumbling
- ❌ Don't blend in with the world's complaining culture

## Keep In Mind

- The Israelites wandered in the wilderness for 40 years because of complaining — a trip that should have taken only 11 days. Complaining can keep you out of your promised land far longer than necessary.
- "To complain means to remain." When you complain about a situation, you guarantee that you'll stay in it until you learn to be thankful.
- God says: "If you're going to complain about something, then don't bother praying about it." He does not answer complaints. He answers prayer.
- Jesus never complained, even when He had nowhere to lay His head and only fish to eat. Followers of Christ are called to the same standard.
- Thankfulness is not a feeling — it is a choice you make on purpose, even when you don't feel like it.

## Security & Safety Notes

This sermon is a spiritual and behavioral teaching, not a technical or security-related guide. There are no direct security concerns. However, if you apply these principles to digital or workplace settings, remember that speaking gratitude publicly does not require sharing personal information, details of private struggles, or sensitive data about family members, finances, or health. Maintain appropriate boundaries while being a positive influence.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Complaining feels like a natural response to frustration, so you don't notice you're doing it.
  **Solution:** Make it a habit to listen to your own words. When you catch yourself complaining, stop, repent, and replace the complaint with a thank-you prayer.

- **Problem:** You pray about a problem but continue to dwell on it with negative thoughts.
  **Solution:** After praying, consciously shift your focus. Speaking your request to God is enough — continuing to talk about the problem in complaint only undoes the good of your prayer.

- **Problem:** Gratitude feels impossible when you are in the middle of genuine hardship or pain.
  **Solution:** Thankfulness is not dependent on circumstances. Start small — thank God for breath, for sight, for one person who loves you. Thankfulness in small things builds the muscle for thankfulness in big things.

- **Problem:** You feel like your complaints are justified because your situation is genuinely difficult.
  **Solution:** Even in suffering, complaining does not solve the problem. It only adds a spiritual burden. Take your pain to God in prayer, and ask Him to give you a grateful heart in the midst of it.

- **Problem:** You don't want to stand out or seem ungrateful by not joining in workplace complaining.
  **Solution:** You can be honest without complaining. Saying "I'm thankful I have this job" does not mean you deny that things aren't perfect. It means you choose to focus on the good.
