# Joyce Meyer: Loving People Who Are Hard to Love

## Overview

This sermon teaches that love is not merely a feeling but a choice and action—specifically the Greek concept of *agape* (divine love), or unconditional love. Joyce Meyer explains that the people who need love most often act in ways that make them difficult to love because they are hurting, and it is the believer's responsibility to show them God's love regardless of personal comfort. The message challenges listeners to move beyond emotional preference and commit to loving others the way God loves them—unconditionally, intentionally, and through practical acts of kindness.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are struggling to love someone who has hurt you, offended you, or consistently behaves in a difficult manner
- When you feel justified in holding anger, resentment, or unforgiveness toward another person
- When you want to grow spiritually and develop the kind of love that reflects Christ to a hurting world

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand That Love Is Not a Feeling

Recognize that love is not based on how you feel about someone. Agape love is a decision you make with your will, not an emotion that happens to you. You do not have to feel right to do what is right. When you choose to love someone even when they are mean or difficult, you are operating in the highest form of love—the same love God has for us.

### Step 2: Look Beyond the Behavior to the Hurt

When someone acts in a hateful, offensive, or difficult way, understand that they are likely hurting deeply. The world is full of mean people because they are in pain, and what they really need is love. Shift your focus from their behavior to their condition, and ask God to give you His heart for them rather than your own frustration.

### Step 3: Choose to Act in Love Regardless of the Cost

Love requires action even when it is inconvenient, costly, or uncomfortable. This may mean doing something kind for someone who has mistreated you, visiting a difficult relative in a nursing home, or praying for your enemies. Do not wait until you feel like it—obedience to God's command to love is what produces growth and spiritual maturity.

### Step 4: Prepare Spiritually Each Morning

Begin each day by intentionally putting on the right spiritual disposition. Take time to pray, study God's Word, and ask the Holy Spirit to help you walk in love. Just as you choose your physical clothes each morning, you must also choose your spiritual attitude. Tell yourself that with God's help, you will behave rightly and love people well throughout the day.

### Step 5: Do Something Practical for the Person Who Is Hard to Love

When you are caught in a trap of negative feelings toward someone, break its power by doing something nice for them. Send a gift, offer to help with a need, or pray for their happiness. This is not about being a doormat or tolerating abuse—it is about releasing the grip of bitterness in your own heart and allowing God to work through you.

## Examples

### Example 1: Caring for Abusive Parents

Joyce Meyer shares the personal story of moving her abusive father and mother from their rural home to St. Louis, buying them a house, a car, and providing for their ongoing needs—even though her father had sexually abused her and her mother had failed to protect her. She visited them in the nursing home every other week for years, not because she felt like it, but because she knew it was the right thing to do. Eventually her father apologized, accepted Christ, and was saved. Joyce reflects that she thought she was buying a house, but in reality she bought a soul.

### Example 2: Your Neighbor with Stage Four Cancer

Meyer asks listeners to imagine a neighbor who has been difficult to deal with, only to discover he has stage four cancer and probably will not survive. Instead of thinking he deserves his fate, a person walking in love would mow his grass, shovel his snow, take him to doctor's appointments, and offer to get his groceries. This demonstrates that love is not about what people deserve—it is about reflecting the love of Christ, who died for us while we were still sinners.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Remember that love is a choice, not a feeling—you can decide to love even when you do not feel loving
- ✅ Look for good qualities in every person, no matter how much bad they have done
- ✅ Do something practical for someone who has mistreated you to break the power of bitterness
- ✅ Prepare spiritually each morning by praying and intentionally choosing to walk in love
- ✅ Forgive others quickly and keep a clean heart so nothing blocks your relationship with God

- ❌ Don't wait until you feel like it to do the right thing—feelings follow obedience, not the other way around
- ❌ Don't justify staying angry by pointing out how wrong the other person is— unforgiveness hurts you more than it hurts them
- ❌ Don't think loving someone means you must allow ongoing abuse or disrespect—some people must be loved from a distance
- ❌ Don't gossip or believe every negative thing you hear about others—let every accusation be established by two or three faithful witnesses

## Keep In Mind

- Love is the most important thing in the world—greater than faith or hope
- The kind of love God requires is *agape* love: a deliberate, willful choice to do what is right regardless of how you feel
- When you mistreat someone, you are damaging your own life more than you are damaging theirs—forgive others to set yourself free
- God will convict you and keep bringing a conviction to your mind until you obey it—when a thought won't go away, it is usually God speaking
- You cannot give away what you do not have—the love of God has been poured into your heart through the Holy Spirit, so you have divine love to give

## Security & Safety Notes

- Love does not mean tolerating abuse, danger, or ongoing harm. Some people, such as abusers or those who are dangerous, must be loved from a distance for your own safety and well-being
- Do not enable destructive behavior under the guise of love—set healthy boundaries while still choosing to wish the person well and pray for their redemption
- Protect your heart by not staying in relationships where you are being consistently mistreated, devalued, or harmed

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Waiting until you feel loving before you act in love. You will never feel like it, and feelings are not the standard for obedience.
  **Solution:** Choose to act in love by faith, knowing that obedience produces growth and God honors your decision to do what is right.

- **Problem:** Holding onto anger and unforgiveness because the other person "deserves" to be punished. You are actually the one suffering while they go on with their life.
  **Solution:** Forgive for your own freedom, not because the other person earned it. Forgiveness releases you from the prison of bitterness.

- **Problem:** Believing that loving someone means you have to be a doormat and let people walk all over you.
  **Solution:** You can love someone from a distance and still maintain healthy boundaries. God is not asking you to be abused—He is asking you to keep a clean heart and treat people rightly regardless of their behavior.

- **Problem:** Thinking you don't have anything good to offer because of your own past or current struggles.
   **Solution:** God has placed good in you, and He will give you the ability to love others through the Holy Spirit. Your past pain can actually qualify you to love hurting people with greater compassion.
