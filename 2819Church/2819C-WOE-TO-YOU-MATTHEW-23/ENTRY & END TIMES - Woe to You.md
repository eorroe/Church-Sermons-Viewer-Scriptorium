# ENTRY & END TIMES | Woe to You | Matthew 23:13-39 | Philip Anthony Mitchell

## Overview

This sermon is a direct exposition of Matthew 23:13-39, in which Jesus pronounces seven woes against the scribes and Pharisees. Philip Anthony Mitchell exposits the passage verse by verse, warning that God's judgment falls on religious leaders who shut the kingdom of heaven in people's faces, who travel across land and sea to win a single convert and then make him twice as much a son of hell as themselves, and who strain out a gnat but swallow a camel. The sermon contrasts these religious hypocrites with true righteousness that flows from the heart, calling every listener to repent of self-righteousness and to enter the kingdom through Christ alone.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When studying Matthew 23 and the seven woes Jesus pronounces against the scribes and Pharisees
- When seeking to understand the difference between external religiosity and genuine heart transformation
- When examining the dangers of legalism, moralism, and self-righteous religion
- When needing clarity on how God's moral law exposes sin and drives the sinner to Christ
- When considering the reality of God's judgment on false religious leaders
- When preparing a message on entering the kingdom of heaven and the cost of discipleship

## Steps

### Step 1: Hear Jesus' Woes Against the Religious Leaders (Matthew 23:13-36)

Jesus pronounces seven woes. The first woe (v. 13) declares that the scribes and Pharisees shut the kingdom of heaven in people's faces, entering it themselves while preventing others from entering. The second woe (v. 15) condemns their missionary zeal that produces converts who are twice as much sons of hell as they are. The third woe (v. 16-22) exposes their foolish reasoning about oaths, calling them blind guides who strain out a gnat while swallowing a camel. The fourth woe (v. 23-24) rebukes their tithing of mint, dill, and cumin while neglecting justice, mercy, and faithfulness. The fifth woe (v. 25-26) exposes their obsession with the outside of the cup while the inside is full of greed and self-indulgence. The sixth woe (v. 27-28) compares them to whitewashed tombs that look beautiful outwardly but are full of dead men's bones. The seventh woe (v. 29-36) condemns their claim to honor the prophets while actually filling up the measure of their fathers' sin by murdering and persecuting the messengers God sends.

### Step 2: Recognize How the Heart Produces Sin

Jesus teaches in Matthew 23:25-28 that outward religiosity cannot conceal an unclean heart. The scribes and Pharisees cleaned the outside of the cup but ignored the greed and self-indulgence within. Mitchell emphasizes that what ruins a person, topples ministries, destroys marriages, and destroys relationships all flow from the heart. The heart cannot be fixed by human effort — it must be transformed by salvation and the indwelling Holy Spirit who works sanctification.

### Step 3: Understand the Purpose of God's Moral Law

God's moral law, summarized in the Ten Commandments, was never given to save anyone. It was given to expose sin and drive the sinner to the Savior. When a person honestly measures himself against the commandments — "You shall have no other gods before Me," "You shall not murder," "You shall not commit adultery," "You shall not covet" — the law reveals that every person is guilty before God. Nobody passes the Ten Commandments. The law is a mirror that shows us our filth, not a ladder we climb to reach God.

### Step 4: Receive Christ's Perfect Righteousness

Jesus Christ is the only person who has ever kept the law perfectly in thought, word, and deed. He lived a sinless life and died a substitutionary death on the cross, exchanging His righteousness for the unrighteousness of guilty sinners. By faith alone in Christ alone, a sinner is declared righteous before God — not because of his own good works, church attendance, or moral behavior, but because Christ's perfect obedience is credited to his account. Repentance means turning from self-righteous religion and trusting Jesus as the only door into the kingdom.

### Step 5: Flee Self-Righteous Religion and Legalism

Verse 13 warns that every plant the heavenly Father has not planted will be rooted up. Coming to church, being raised in a religious home, or performing religious rituals does not secure salvation. True repentance requires coming out of false religion, moralism, and legalism, and trusting Jesus alone to save. Mitchell warns that many people sit in churches week after week thinking they are safe because of their religious activity, while their hearts remain far from God.

### Step 6: Flee Sexual Immorality as Sin Against Your Own Body

Out of the heart come evil thoughts, and sexual immorality is among the most destructive. Jesus taught that looking at a woman with lust commits adultery in the heart. Sexual immorality is not merely a social failing — it is an act of worship with another human being before God, replacing God's intended design for human intimacy with idolatrous gratification. God hates sexual sin with perfect holiness. The repentant believer must flee it, set holy boundaries, and honor God with his body as a temple of the Holy Spirit.

### Step 7: Transform Your Prayers to Seek God's Will

God created you to serve Him, not to be served by Him. When a believer truly grasps this truth, his prayers are no longer 45-minute monologues listing personal wants and needs. They become cries for God's glory and will: "Lord, what do You want me to do? Lord, transform my heart. Lord, open a door for me to share the gospel." The purpose of prayer is not to move God to your will, but to align your will with His.

### Step 8: Examine the Evidence of True Salvation

The sermon closes with a call to examine whether one's life evidences genuine regeneration. True salvation produces a changed heart, a love for Christ, a hunger for His Word, a desire to flee sin, and a growing capacity to love God and neighbor. External religion without this internal transformation is the very thing Jesus pronounced woe upon in Matthew 23.

## Examples

### Example 1: The First Commandment and the Rich Man

Jesus illustrated that the first commandment — "You shall have no other God before Me" — is enough to condemn every person. Take the richest man or the poorest man; measure him against this single command. Ninety-nine out of a hundred will fail. Anyone who has loved anything more than God — money, pleasure, approval, comfort — has broken the first commandment and stands guilty before a holy God. This is the purpose of the law: to shut every mouth and make the whole world accountable to God (Romans 3:19).

### Example 2: Personal Testimony of Repentance and Honor

After getting saved, the speaker and his wife lived in the same apartment but set holy boundaries. He slept on the couch for months, and they did not touch each other after the first night. They honored God in private, and when the time came, they were married. God moved on people's hearts to pay for everything. The point is clear: honoring God in private leads to His honoring you in public.

## Best Practices

- Center every sermon and every reading of Scripture on Christ, not on yourself
- Use God's moral law honestly to expose sin, not as a standard you can attain in your own strength
- Stand in Christ's righteousness alone for your justification before God
- Flee legalism, moralism, and every form of self-righteous religion
- Examine your heart honestly, because what ruins a person flows from within
- Flee sexual immorality and set holy boundaries in all relationships
- Transform your prayers from want-lists into petitions for God's will and glory
- Honor God in private so that He can honor you in public

## Keep In Mind

- God created you to serve Him, not the other way around
- The heart is the source of all sin and cannot be fixed by human effort — it requires regeneration and the Holy Spirit's sanctifying work
- Everything that ruins human beings — ministries, marriages, relationships — flows out of an unchanged heart
- Sexual immorality is sin against your own body and is an act of worship with another human being before God
- Without Christ, every person is under God's wrath because all have broken His commandments
- Every plant the Father has not planted will be rooted up — religious activity is not the same as true salvation
- The law condemns; Christ saves — know the difference and never confuse the two

## Security & Safety Notes

- False teachers make every sermon about the listener and put the listener at the center of every text
- If the blind lead the blind, both will fall into a pit — false teachers and their followers face eternal judgment in hell
- The moral law reveals that nobody is righteous before God apart from Christ
- Without Christ, every person stands condemned before God as a lawbreaker worthy of hell
- Whitewashed tombs — outwardly religious but inwardly dead — are precisely what Jesus pronounced woe upon in Matthew 23

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Listening to blind teachers who make the Bible about you instead of about Christ
  **Solution:** Test every sermon and teacher against Christ and the full counsel of Scripture, ensuring Christ is the center, not the listener
- **Problem:** Thinking church attendance, religious activity, or moral behavior makes you safe before God
  **Solution:** Examine your heart — true salvation is a new birth through Christ, not religious ritual. Come out of false religion and trust Jesus alone
- **Problem:** Treating prayer as a 45-minute monologue of personal petitions
  **Solution:** Transform your prayers into cries for God's will: "Lord, what do You want me to do? Transform my heart. Give me doors to share the gospel"
- **Problem:** Believing Christianity revolves around your comfort, happiness, or success
  **Solution:** Remember God created you to serve Him, not to be served by Him. Live for His glory, not your pleasure
- **Problem:** Underestimating the sinfulness of sexual immorality
  **Solution:** Honor God with your body as His temple. Set holy boundaries. Flee lust. Remember sexual sin is an act of worship with another person before God, replacing Christ with a created thing
