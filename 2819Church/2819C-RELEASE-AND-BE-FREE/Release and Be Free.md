# WISDOM & WONDER | Release and Be Free — Matthew 18:21-35

## Overview

This sermon, delivered by Philip Anthony Mitchell at 2819 Church, unpacks the parable of the unforgiving servant in Matthew 18:21-35, revealing that true freedom in Christ is found in releasing others as God has already released us. The core message is that forgiveness is not optional for the believer — it is a direct response to the incomprehensible mercy we have already received. When we hold onto offenses, we remain in spiritual bondage; when we forgive, we walk in the victory and authority Jesus already secured for us.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are struggling to forgive someone who has hurt or wronged you
- When you feel trapped by bitterness, resentment, or a grudge
- When you need to understand why forgiveness is essential for your own spiritual freedom
- When you want to walk in the victory and authority of Christ rather than remaining in emotional bondage
- When you need clarity on the difference between fighting *from* victory and fighting *for* victory

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize the Debt God Already Cancelled

Identify the ways you have been offended and acknowledge the weight of what you may be holding onto. Then, contrast that debt against the infinitely greater debt God cancelled in your life through Christ. Understand that your forgiveness of others is a direct overflow of the forgiveness you have already received. Ask the Holy Spirit to reveal any root of unforgiveness you have accepted as normal or justified.

### Step 2: Choose to Forgive in Faith

Make a deliberate, Spirit-led decision to release the person who wronged you — not because they deserve it, but because Christ in you does. Speak your forgiveness aloud, renounce the right to hold the offense, and cancel the debt in the same way your own debt was cancelled at the cross. This is not a feeling; it is an act of spiritual warfare fought from a place of victory.

### Step 3: Walk in the Freedom That Follows

After forgiving, guard your heart against re-entering that offense. Walk daily in the authority and peace that come from a released spirit. When the memory of the hurt returns, remind yourself that the debt has been cancelled and stand firm in that truth. Praise God for the freedom you now walk in, knowing that God's weapons of warfare are mighty for pulling down strongholds — including the stronghold of unforgiveness.

## Examples

### Example 1: Holding a Grudge After Betrayal

A believer feels deeply hurt by a close friend's betrayal. For months, the offense replayed in their mind, creating bitterness and stealing their joy. Applying this sermon, they sat with God and recognized how much He had already forgiven them. With tears, they verbally forgave their friend — not excusing the offense, but releasing the right to punish. Within days, the heavy burden lifted and they experienced the freedom of walking in Christ's victory rather than remaining in a spiritual basement of unforgiveness.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Compare your hurt against the cross — the debt God cancelled for you is infinitely greater than any debt owed to you
- ✅ Forgive quickly; do not allow a root of bitterness to grow (Hebrews 12:15)
- ✅ Speak your forgiveness aloud as a spiritual act of warfare, not just a private thought
- ✅ Fight from victory, not for victory — you are already victorious in Christ
- ✅ Surround yourself with a community of believers who will hold you accountable to walking in forgiveness
- ❌ Do not confuse forgiveness with trust — forgiveness releases the debt; trust must be rebuilt over time
- ❌ Do not wait for the other person to apologize — forgiveness is an act of your will, not their merit
- ❌ Do not minimize the hurt — acknowledge it, then release it to God
- ❌ Do not use forgiveness as a tool to manipulate or control the other person

## Keep In Mind

- Forgiveness is an act of your will empowered by the Holy Spirit, not a feeling that will arrive on its own
- The enemy has been dethroned — there is no equal power between God and the devil; the enemy's role is now limited to making noise for a season
- There is no dualism in the Christian life; we do not fight for a victory, we fight from a place of victory already secured by Christ
- Shedding tears and processing grief is not a sign of weakness — it is part of coming out of the basement of pain into the praise of freedom
- Forgiveness does not mean you must maintain a relationship with the person who hurt you; it means you release them from the debt they owe you

## Security & Safety Notes

- If you are in an abusive relationship, forgiveness does not mean you must remain in danger — seek safety first, then process forgiveness with God and a trusted pastor or counselor
- Do not suppress or ignore deep trauma; if the hurt is severe, walk through forgiveness with the guidance of a mature, trusted spiritual leader
- Unforgiveness opens the door to spiritual strongholds; protecting your heart through forgiveness is a form of spiritual self-defense

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing forgiveness means the offense didn't matter or wasn't real.
  **Solution:** Forgiveness acknowledges the real pain but chooses not to let it define you or hold you captive. Acknowledge the hurt fully, then release it.

- **Problem:** Waiting for the other person to deserve forgiveness before extending it.
  **Solution:** Forgiveness is not based on the other person's merit — it is based on the mercy you have already received from Christ. Forgive because you have been forgiven.

- **Problem:** Thinking forgiveness means you must stay in a harmful relationship.
  **Solution:** Forgiveness releases the debt and heals your heart; it does not obligate you to maintain a relationship with someone who continues to harm you.

- **Problem:** Forgiving once and expecting the memory and pain to never return.
  **Solution:** Forgiveness is a daily act. When the memory returns, re-affirm your decision to forgive and stand in the freedom Christ has already provided.
