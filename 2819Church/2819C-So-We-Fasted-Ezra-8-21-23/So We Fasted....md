# So We Fasted... | Ezra 8:21-23 | Philip Anthony Mitchell

## Overview

This sermon calls the church to return to the ancient biblical discipline of fasting and prayer as a means of humbling oneself before God, weakening the flesh, and drawing near to Christ. Through the examples of Ezra in Ezra 8:21-23, the church at Antioch in Acts 13, and the call to return in Joel 2, Pastor Philip Anthony Mitchell teaches that fasting is not optional for followers of Christ—it is a commanded practice that invites God to meet needs, provide guidance, bring revival, and transform lives. The sermon culminates in a call to seven days of consecration, urging the congregation to choose between a total fast (water only with no stimulation) or a partial fast (like Daniel's) as a means of denying the flesh and seeking God with their whole hearts.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are facing a dangerous or uncertain journey and need to humbly ask God for protection and guidance
- When you are struggling with the sinful nature of the flesh and need to weaken its hold over your life through intentional consecration
- When you need clarity, direction, or intervention from God on a significant decision, need, or spiritual battle

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand What Biblical Fasting Is

Biblical fasting is the voluntary abstinence from food for a set period of time for the purpose of consecrating the heart and drawing near to God through prayer. It is not a hunger strike. It is not simply giving up social media, music, or other minor comforts while still eating whatever you want. It is denying the flesh its deepest desire—food—and sometimes other pleasures, to declare to the flesh: "I have authority over you." True fasting is done privately, not for public applause, but to humble yourself before the one who sees in secret.

### Step 2: Examine Your Heart and Identify Your Need

Before you fast, take time to examine why you are doing it. Bring your sin, guilt, shame, brokenness, and needs before God honestly. Like the psalmists who poured out their hearts to God, pour out your heart to Him. The Lord is looking for those who will come to Him with sincerity, not those who perform religiosity for others to see. If you feel far from God, this is exactly the right time to fast—He calls you to return to Him with fasting and weeping (Joel 2:12).

### Step 3: Choose Your Fast Type

If you are pregnant, nursing, ill, or work a physically demanding manual labor job, choose the partial fast. If you are physically able and healthy, commit to a total fast for the full seven days. There are two biblical options described in this sermon:

- **Total Fast**: No food for the entire seven-day consecration period; liquids only (such as water). No added stimulation—no social media, television, radio, music, or anything the flesh uses for comfort and distraction. This mirrors Jesus' 40-day fast in the wilderness (Matthew 4:1-4) and the pattern of Daniel. Your food for the week becomes the Word of God. The first three days will be the most difficult physically; by the fourth day, your body adjusts and your spirit becomes more sensitive to the presence of God.
- **Partial Fast**: For those who cannot do a total fast due to physical limitations. This is modeled after Daniel's fast in Daniel 10, where he abstained from meat, delicacies, wine, and rich foods for 21 days while serving in a demanding government role. Eat only simple, plain foods—such as vegetables, fruits, and one simple meal per day. Deny yourself the things your flesh enjoys most: meat, rich foods, desserts, wine, and other pleasures. If it does not cost you something, it is not a fast.

### Step 4: Fast and Pray in Secret

Follow the instruction of Jesus in Matthew 6:5-18: when you fast, do not look gloomy or announce it to others to gain their approval. Anoint your head, wash your face, and go about your normal routine so that no one but your Father in heaven knows you are fasting. Do not use social media, text messages, or casual conversation to let others know you are on a fast. This is between you and God. The reward for secret fasting comes from the Father who sees in secret.

### Step 5: Structure Your Time During the Fast

Each day of the seven-day consecration, intentionally gorge yourself on the Word of God. Read Scripture, listen to worship, walk in quiet places, and spend extended time in prayer. Use the Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-13) as a model: begin by acknowledging who God is and worshipping Him; pray for His kingdom and His will to be done on earth; bring your genuine needs before Him; confess your sins honestly; ask for strength against temptation. The focus is not on what you cannot eat, but on who you are running after. Let every hunger pang remind you that your body is under authority and your spirit is being strengthened.

### Step 6: Engage in Community During the Fast

Even though you are fasting privately, do not isolate yourself from other believers. Throughout the week, encourage one another. Ask fellow believers what they are hearing from God, how they are holding up, and what they are reading in Scripture. This is a time for the church to seek the Lord together. Do not let offense or messiness in community keep you from leaning in—forgiveness, love, and fellowship are part of what it means to be the church. Use the fast as an opportunity to share all things in common as best you can, providing for one another practically.

### Step 7: Expect God to Move

Scripture is full of testimonies of those who fasted and prayed and saw God respond. In Ezra 8, God answered their fast with safe passage and provision on a dangerous journey. In Acts 13, God spoke and gave the church direction for ministry while they were fasting. In Joel 2, God promised that when His people return to Him with fasting and weeping, He is gracious, merciful, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love—He will relent from disaster and pour out His Spirit. Believe that God will meet your need, speak to you, give you guidance, and mightily intervene on your behalf.

### Step 8: Begin the Fast Together

Start the fast at the appointed time—on Monday morning, deny yourself your first meal and replace it with the Word of God and prayer. Your next meal will be the first communal meal after the seven days, ideally celebrated together with communion. Use the moment the fast begins to commit yourself fully: tell your flesh it has no authority over you, and tell Jesus that He is your priority for this season. Set the course for the year, and believe for miracles, healing, salvations, and revival.

## Examples

### Example 1: Ezra and the Journey to Jerusalem (Ezra 8:21-23)

Ezra was a priest and scribe leading more than a thousand Jews back to Jerusalem after exile in Babylon. The journey was dangerous—people were killed on that route. Ezra could have asked the Persian king for armed soldiers to protect them, but he was ashamed to do so because he had already bragged to the king about the power of their God. So he called the entire community to a corporate fast, to humble themselves before God and seek from Him a safe journey for themselves, their children, and all their goods. The people responded: "So we fasted and implored our God for this, and he listened to our entreaty." God met their need. Their fast was not about appearance or ritual—it was about placing their trust in God rather than in human protection.

### Example 2: The Church at Antioch Sends Out Barnabas and Saul (Acts 13:1-3)

While the church at Antioch was worshiping the Lord and fasting, the Holy Spirit spoke and said, "Set apart for me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them." After fasting and praying, the church laid hands on them and sent them off. This shows that fasting is not only for personal breakthrough—it is also for receiving divine guidance and direction. Many people today face important decisions about jobs, relationships, moves, and callings. This example teaches that when the church fasts together, God speaks. When you quiet the flesh through fasting, your spiritual ears open, and God can whisper direction that you would otherwise miss in the noise of your appetites.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Fast with sincerity, not to be seen or praised by others
- ✅ Fast in secret—anoint your head, wash your face, and go about your normal day
- ✅ Deny the flesh what it wants most: food, stimulation, comfort, and pleasure
- ✅ Spend the time you would normally spend eating reading the Word of God and praying
- ✅ Gorge yourself on Scripture—this is your food for the fast
- ✅ Encourage fellow believers throughout the week, asking how they are doing and what they are hearing from God
- ✅ Be committed to community—love, forgive, and lean in even when it is messy or painful
- ✅ Return to God honestly, even if you feel far from Him—He is gracious and merciful
- ✅ Expect God to move: to meet needs, give guidance, bring revival, and perform miracles

- ❌ Don't brag about your fast or let people know you are doing it for their approval
- ❌ Don't do a "punk American fast"—giving up coffee while still eating everything else is not fasting
- ❌ Don't focus all week on what you cannot eat—focus instead on running after Christ
- ❌ Don't pray with empty phrases or try to impress God with flowery religious language
- ❌ Don't let guilt, shame, or past sin keep you from returning to God in fasting
- ❌ Don't isolate yourself from community during the fast—lean in and encourage others

## Keep In Mind

- The Spirit of God is not handcuffed to one location or one building—He is with you wherever you are, even in the overflow room or online
- The Lord did not call you to merely play church or do church programs; He called you to be the church
- This ministry's foundation is being an Acts 2 church: deeply devoted to the Scriptures, prayer, community, and fellowship, sharing all things in common as far as possible
- Community will be messy, and sometimes it will hurt, and sometimes you will be offended—but this is where character is shaped; do not run from church to church when offended, but lean in and grow
- The greatest reward you will receive from fasting is not a miracle or a breakthrough—it is intimacy with Christ Himself
- Fasting weakens the flesh and makes the spirit grow strong; it creates spiritual antennas that allow you to hear God more clearly
- Your prayer life, Bible reading, and service may feel cold or dead right now—return to God with fasting and weeping, and He will revive you
- When you fast, God may give you a personal revival even before the nation experiences one—your breakthrough matters to Him

## Security & Safety Notes

- If you are pregnant or nursing, or if you work a physically demanding manual labor job, you must not attempt a total fast—choose the partial fast option instead
- Do not fast if you have a medical condition that requires regular food intake; consult a doctor before beginning any extended fast
- Fasting should be done with wisdom and care for your body—do not harm yourself in an attempt to be spiritual
- Keep your fast private: do not post about it on social media, do not announce it publicly, and do not perform your fasting for the approval of others

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Fasting to be seen by others, posting about it, or using it to gain spiritual status
  **Solution:** Fast in secret. Anoint your head, wash your face, and tell no one but God. The Father who sees in secret will reward you openly.

- **Problem:** Doing a watered-down or "punk American" fast—giving up one minor thing while still eating everything else and indulging the flesh
  **Solution:** Understand that true fasting denies the flesh what it wants most. If it does not cost you something, it is not a fast. Choose a total fast or a genuine partial fast.

- **Problem:** Focusing all week on what you cannot eat, complaining about hunger, or letting the fast become a point of contention
  **Solution:** Redirect your focus to what you are pursuing—Christ. Gorge yourself on the Word of God. Every hunger pang is an opportunity to declare your flesh is under authority.

- **Problem:** Feeling too guilty or too far from God to fast, believing God will not listen to you because of your sin
  **Solution:** Joel 2:12-13 is for you: "Return to me with all your heart, with fasting, with weeping, and with mourning; rend your hearts and not your garments." God is gracious, merciful, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love. He relents from disaster. Come to Him just as you are.

- **Problem:** Getting offended by the messiness of community and withdrawing from fellowship
  **Solution:** Character is shaped in community. Be committed to loving and forgiving each other quickly and easily. Lean into the mess rather than running from it—this is how you grow.

- **Problem:** Praying with empty phrases or trying to impress God with religious language
  **Solution:** Your Father already knows what you need before you ask. Be honest, simple, and direct in your prayers. The Lord's Prayer is a model, not a formula. Speak to God like you speak to your Father.
