# Sexual Immorality In The American Church

## Overview

This sermon confronts the American church's reluctance to address sexual immorality, exposing how false teaching centers people rather than God, and demonstrating through the Ten Commandments that all stand condemned apart from Christ. It calls believers to reject self-help narcissism, embrace the sufficiency of Christ's sacrificial death, and honor God with their bodies through holy living and authentic prayer.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When the church avoids addressing sexual sin publicly
- When teaching centers the listener rather than Christ and Scripture
- When believers need to understand the seriousness of sexual immorality and the sufficiency of Christ's righteousness

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize the problem

The American church often refuses to talk about sexual immorality, creating a culture of silence around this sin.

### Step 2: Identify false teaching

Blind teachers make every sermon about the listener, placing people at the center of Scripture instead of God.

### Step 3: Apply the moral test

Use the Ten Commandments to examine your life—everyone falls short and is Hellbound without Christ.

### Step 4: Embrace Christ's fulfillment

Jesus perfectly fulfilled the law in mind, heart, and action, then died sacrificially to exchange His righteousness for your unrighteousness.

### Step 5: Renounce self-centered Christianity

God did not create you to serve you; He created you to serve Him. Transform your prayers from self-serving begging to Kingdom-focused intercession.

### Step 6: Guard your heart

Understand that what defiles a person flows from the heart—evil thoughts, lust, and adultery in the heart all violate God's law.

### Step 7: Set holy boundaries

Honor God with your body by establishing practical boundaries, even when it means sacrifice, discomfort, or waiting for marriage.

### Step 8: Take sexual immorality seriously

Commit to either holy marriage or holy separation, rejecting the casual approach to sex in modern culture.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Rich Man and the Poor Man

Give both the rich man and the poor man the first commandment "you shall have no other God before me." 99% of people will fail because they have loved something more than God—money, status, relationships, or comfort. This shows that moral law condemnation is universal.

### Example 2: The Couple Who Honored God in Secret

The speaker and his wife lived together before marriage but, after being saved, chose to sleep separately for months to avoid sin. They married in private with only a pastor present because they wanted to honor God above public spectacle, and God provided for their wedding through others' generosity.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Let Scripture center God rather than yourself in teaching and interpretation
- ✅ Use the Ten Commandments as a mirror to reveal your need for Christ
- ✅ Set practical physical boundaries to honor God with your body
- ✅ Pray for God's will and kingdom rather than your own desires
- ❌ Don't avoid speaking about sexual immorality because it's uncomfortable
- ❌ Don't make sermons motivational self-help that puts the listener at the center
- ❌ Don't trust your own moral performance for righteousness
- ❌ Don't use your body for desires outside God's covenant of marriage

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon contains direct biblical condemnation of sexual immorality. Handle with pastoral sensitivity.
- The teaching on Hell and judgment is presented as biblical truth but should be shared with grace and clarity about the gospel.
- Personal testimonies about sexual sin require wisdom and discretion when shared in community settings.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** The church stays silent on sexual immorality to avoid offending people or appearing judgmental.
  **Solution:** Speak the truth in love, showing that God's design for sex is for our good and His glory.
- **Problem:** Listeners hear sermons as self-help rather than gospel-centered conviction.
  **Solution:** Always point people to Christ's fulfillment of the law and His righteousness imputed to believers, not their own behavior.
