# Marriage, Divorce, and Singleness: Wisdom from Matthew 19:1-12

## Overview
 
This sermon is an exposition of Matthew 19:1-12 delivered by Pastor Philip Anthony Mitchell of 2819 Church. It addresses the difficult and emotionally charged topics of marriage, divorce, and singleness with the stated aim of transformation rather than condemnation. The preacher traces Jesus' response to a trap set by the Pharisees, showing how God's original design for marriage — one man, one woman, one flesh, for life — stands in contrast to the cultural norms of both the first century and today. The message applies to married couples, divorced persons, and single believers alike, culminating in the gospel truth that Christ has forever committed Himself to His church and will never issue a certificate of divorce against His people.
 
## When to Follow This Sermon

- When wrestling with questions about marriage, divorce, or singleness from a biblical perspective
- When experiencing conflict or spiritual warfare in your marriage
- When feeling judged or condemned because of a past divorce
- When single and feeling pressure from culture or community to marry
- When engaged in theological debates about marriage, gender, or divorce and need discernment
- When tempted to let personal preferences override the clear teaching of Scripture
- When needing to understand that singleness is a legitimate, God-honored calling
 
## Steps
 
### Step 1: Recognize That Marriage Is God's Original Design

Return to Genesis and the beginning. God created humanity male and female (Genesis 1:27), instituted marriage between one man and one woman (Genesis 2:24), and designed sexual intimacy to be reserved exclusively for that covenant relationship. When the Pharisees came to Jesus with a politically charged trap — asking which side of the divorce debate He was on — Jesus refused to pick a side. Instead, He pointed them back to Genesis 1-2 and God's design at creation. Any cultural redefinition of marriage stands in direct contradiction to what God established at the beginning. When you face debates about marriage, your starting point must be the Word of God, not culture, not opinion, and not what pleases people.

### Step 2: Understand Marriage as a Covenant, Not a Contract

Marriage is a covenant relationship that reflects Christ and the church. It carries top priority over every other relationship — above friends, family, and in-laws. It calls for deep bonding through communication, sharing, and forgiveness. Sex is a powerful gift designed by God to bind a husband and wife together, and sexual activity outside of that covenant leads to emotional and spiritual disruption. Because marriage pictures Christ's relationship to the church, the adversary actively targets it — demonic influence can stir conflict, division, and strife between spouses. Fight for your marriage as a spiritual act, knowing that warfare is real and the enemy seeks to distort or destroy what God has joined together.

### Step 3: Understand the True Purpose of the Divorce Certificate in Deuteronomy 24

Moses' certificate of divorce (Deuteronomy 24:1-4) was not a license for frivolous divorce — it was a protective measure for women in a culture where they had no rights and men alone could dissolve a marriage. The phrase "she has found no favor in his eyes" opened the door to men divorced wives for burning a meal, for gaining weight, for finding someone more attractive. Moses permitted the certificate to give women at least some legal standing and protection. Jesus makes clear that this permission was given "because of the hardness of your heart" — it was a concession, not God's original intent. Divorce for trivial reasons is a violation of God's design, and it is the Word of God, not human opinion, that makes this clear.

### Step 4: Exercise Discernment When Engaging in Theological Debates

Not everyone who approaches you about theological matters has good intentions. The Pharisees came to Jesus publicly to trap Him, not to seek truth. In any conversation about marriage, divorce, gender, or doctrine, test the motives behind the question. Discernment is required. Not everyone trying to befriend you or engage you in debate has your best interest or God's glory at heart. Stand firm in the Word of God and do not allow yourself to be drawn into traps where any answer you give will be twisted against you or the truth.

### Step 5: Stop Giving Rebuttals Against God's Truth and Start Letting It Transform You

When the Word of God confronts your preferences, opinions, or lifestyle, the natural response of the sinful heart is to push back — just as the Pharisees did. They asked a question, received God's truth from Jesus, and then immediately offered a rebuttal. Do not be like them. When God's word speaks to your situation — whether you are married, divorced, or single — do not find reasons to argue with it. Instead, let it deal with your heart. Say to "This is what God said, and I must deal with this in my heart." Transformation comes not from debating the Word but from submitting to it.

### Step 6: For Married Couples — Fight for the Covenant

Your marriage is under spiritual attack. Satan hates biblical marriage because it is a picture of Christ and the church. Expect warfare, especially after major transitions such as marriage itself. Guard your heart against demonically inspired feelings toward your spouse that are not biblical. Practice deep bonding through communication, sharing, forgiveness, and holding fast to one another. Remember that God's design was for a man and woman to remain together until death — that is the standard to which you are called. If your marriage is in trouble, do not treat divorce as the automatic exit. Fight for the covenant, seek accountability, and lean into the body of Christ for support.

### Step 7: For Divorced Persons — Receive Grace, Not Condemnation

Jesus' teaching in Matthew 19 addresses a specific cultural context: men divorcing their wives for frivolous reasons. He does not address every possible scenario that leads to divorce — such as an unbelieving spouse abandoning the marriage or a person needing to divorce to escape abuse. To discern whether your situation falls into one of these exceptions, ask: Was the divorce the result of a pattern of frivolous, unrepentant rejection of the marriage covenant, or was it a response to abandonment by an unbelieving spouse or to physical danger that left no other option for safety? The sermon is clear that Jesus' words in Matthew 19:9 are aimed at the former context, not at those who have been abandoned or who have had to flee for their safety. If you have been divorced in such a circumstance, know that the Lord does not condemn you. The church must be a place of grace for those who have experienced the pain of divorce. Receive God's forgiveness, move forward, and do not let past wounds define your identity in Christ. God's heart for you is healing and restoration.

### Step 8: For Single Persons — Embrace Singleness as a Divine Calling

Singleness is not a curse, and marriage is not the goal of the Christian life. Jesus explicitly affirms singleness as a legitimate calling for some in Matthew 19:12, using the term "eunuchs" — a word his first-century hearers would have recognized as referring to men who were physically castrated. Jesus repurposes that term to describe three categories of those who do not marry: those born without the desire or capacity for marriage, those made single by circumstance beyond their control (such as widowhood or divorce with no prospect of remarriage), and those who voluntarily choose a life of celibacy for the sake of the kingdom of heaven. The single believer has the unique privilege of undivided devotion to the Lord. Stop letting cultural pressure, social media, or well-meaning people make you feel incomplete or cursed because you are single. See your singleness as a season — or a lifelong calling — that God is using for His purposes in you. Do not be so "thirsty" for marriage that you rush into it before you are spiritually mature enough for what God has called you to.

### Step 9: Remember That All Believers Are Married to Christ

Regardless of your marital status — married, divorced, or single — all of us have been unfaithful to Christ. We have broken our covenant with Him, yet He has never written us a certificate of divorce. He signed our marriage license in blood through His death on the cross, and He remains forever committed to His church. Every believer is the bride of Christ. This means that no matter what your earthly marriage has looked like, your identity and security are ultimately found in your unbreakable union with Jesus. Rest in that truth, and let it shape how you view all other relationships.

## Examples
 
### Example 1: The Man from New York

In the summer of 2024, by God's grace, 2819 Church baptized approximately one thousand brothers and sisters. Near the end of that baptism service, the preacher met a brother from New York who told him he had been on the verge of committing suicide until he found the ministry through social media. Through the proclamation of God's Word, this man was transferred from the domain of darkness into the kingdom of Christ. This testimony serves as a living reminder that the Word of God saves lives, that the church is called to reach the broken, and that no one is beyond the reach of God's grace — just as no one is beyond the reach of a Christ who will never issue a certificate of divorce against His own.

### Example 2: The Preacher's Own Marriage and the Dream

The preacher shared that he began dating Lena in October 2002, and they lived together and were intimate before marriage. After his conversion in November 2003 and their subsequent marriage in August 2004, warfare hit their home. A man from their past came to the preacher with a dream: he saw a man whisper into the preacher's ear and then into Lena's ear, and they immediately began fighting. As the preacher prayed, he received revelation that the man in the dream represented Satan, and that their marriage had become a target for the adversary the moment they entered into covenant. This personal testimony illustrates that marriage is not merely a natural relationship — it is a spiritual reality, a picture of Christ and the church, and the enemy of that picture will do everything he can to tear it down.

### Example 3: First-Century Jewish Divorce Culture

The Pharisees' question about divorce was rooted in a real first-century debate between Rabbi Shammai (conservative: divorce permitted only for sexual immorality or death) and Rabbi Hillel (liberal: divorce permitted for any cause). This mirrors modern culture wars between conservatives and liberals over marriage and divorce. Jesus' response shows that when the church is split along these lines, the answer is not to pick a side — it is to go back to the Word of God. In the Mishna, we read that men were divorcing their wives for burning meals, for gaining weight, for finding someone more attractive. This culture of casual, male-initiated divorce left women vulnerable and abused, which is why Moses permitted the certificate of divorce in the first place — not to endorse divorce, but to protect those who had no other legal standing.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Return to the Word of God as your first and final authority when faced with theological or cultural debates about marriage, divorce, or singleness
- ✅ Understand marriage as a covenant between one man and one woman that reflects Christ and the church
- ✅ Hold fast to your spouse through communication, forgiveness, and deep bonding
- ✅ Recognize that marriage makes you a spiritual target and respond with prayer and intentionality
- ✅ Exercise discernment in all conversations and relationships, testing the motives behind what people say
- ✅ Submit to the Word of God rather than offering rebuttals when it confronts your preferences
- ✅ Receive God's grace and forgiveness if you have been divorced — do not let past pain define you
- ✅ Embrace singleness as a divine calling that allows for undivided devotion to the Lord
- ✅ See singleness as a blessing, not a curse, and do not let cultural pressure push you into premature marriage
- ✅ Cling to the truth that Christ will never divorce you, regardless of your failures

## Keep In Mind

- The driving thrust of this passage is transformation, not condemnation. Do not use it to shame others — use it to allow God's Word to work in your own heart.
- Jesus was heading toward Jerusalem and His own death when He taught this passage. He would never return to Galilee again. Do not treat the weight of His words lightly.
- The crowds were following Jesus because they were tired of empty, cotton-candy doctrine. They wanted the meat of God's Word, even when it hurt. Be willing to receive hard truths.
- The Pharisees came publicly to trap Jesus — this was a staged theological ambush. Be aware that not every challenging question comes from a sincere desire to learn.
- Moses permitted divorce to protect women in a patriarchal culture with no legal rights — this was a concession, not an endorsement.
- God's original design was that no one would separate what He has joined together. Divorce was never the plan.
- Singleness is affirmed by Jesus as a calling for some, just as marriage is a calling for others. Both are good when lived in obedience to God.
- Regardless of your earthly marital status, all believers are spiritually married to Christ, who will never write you a certificate of divorce.

## Security & Safety Notes

- For those in abusive marriages: The sermon clarifies that Jesus is not addressing scenarios where a person must divorce to protect their life. His words in Matthew 19:9 are directed at the Pharisees' specific question about frivolous divorce, not at those fleeing abuse. If you are in danger, seek safety first and trust that God's grace covers you.
- For those struggling with same-sex attraction: The preacher explicitly states this is not an indictment on people who experience same-sex attraction. Do not condemn or shame others who struggle in this area.
- Do not allow anyone to use this sermon or this passage to manipulate, shame, or pressure you into a marriage or singleness that is not God's will for your life.
- Financial giving in response to the church's call: give faithfully and according to what God lays on your heart, not out of manipulation or coercion.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Using personal preferences or cultural opinion to override the clear teaching of Scripture on marriage, divorce, or singleness.
- **Solution:** Return to the Word of God. When Jesus was trapped between liberal and conservative sides, He answered with Scripture, not opinion. Do the same.
- **Problem:** Treating Deuteronomy 24 as a license for divorce rather than a protective concession for women in a patriarchal culture.
- **Solution:** Read the text in its full context. Moses permitted a certificate of divorce to protect women from abuse — he did not command or endorse divorce itself.
- **Problem:** Condemning people who have been divorced or using their past to define their identity in Christ.
- **Solution:** Remember that Jesus' teaching in Matthew 19 addresses a specific context of frivolous, male-initiated divorce. He did not address every possible scenario. Grace and healing are available to all who have been divorced.
- **Problem:** Pressuring single believers to marry or treating singleness as a deficiency.
- **Solution:** Jesus affirmed singleness as a legitimate calling. The single person has unique access to undivided devotion to the Lord. Stop treating marriage as the goal of the Christian life.
- **Problem:** Failing to recognize spiritual warfare in marriage and being unprepared for conflict.
- **Solution:** Understand that marriage is a target for the adversary because it pictures Christ and the church. Pray together, guard your hearts, and do not allow demonic influences to drive a wedge between you and your spouse.
- **Problem:** Engaging in theological debates with people who have impure motives, trying to trap you or provoke offense.
- **Solution:** Exercise discernment. Not everyone who engages you about doctrine has good intentions. Respond with the Word of God, not with emotional reactions.
