# Wisdom & Wonder | What Do I Still Lack? | Matthew 19:16-30 | Philip Anthony Mitchell

## Overview

This sermon confronts the deep human struggle of inadequacy, especially when it is entangled with religion and the futile attempt to earn salvation through good works. Using Matthew 19:16-30, Pastor Philip Anthony Mitchell shows how the rich young ruler's question "What do I still lack?" reveals the universal impossibility of moral perfection and the need for Christ alone. The message exposes the prosperity gospel, which teaches that God's love and favor depend on human performance, church attendance, tithes, and moral behavior, declares that heaven's standard is perfection, and points listeners to the only solution: being clothed in the righteousness of Christ through faith alone.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel inadequate and are tempted to earn God's approval through religious performance
- When you are struggling with the guilt of not being "good enough" spiritually
- When you need clarity on why good works cannot save you
- When you are tempted to compare your outward piety with others and assume you are righteous
- When you have sacrificed for Christ and wonder where your reward is
- When you need to be reminded that eternity with Christ is the greatest reward
- When you want to understand the true gospel versus the American prosperity gospel
- When you need courage to share the gospel confrontationally, pointing people to their sin and Christ's sufficiency

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize the Source of Your Inadequacy

Identify whether your feelings of inadequacy come from worldly voids (affirmation, loss, circumstances) or from religious striving. If your inadequacy is mingled with religion, understand that this is the most crippling form because no amount of human effort can satisfy God's standard. Admit that you cannot earn salvation through good deeds, church attendance, tithes, or moral behavior.

### Step 2: Understand the Standard for Heaven

Know that the only standard for entering eternal life is moral perfection as God Himself is perfect. Jesus said, "If you would enter life, keep the commandments." This is not optional. No human being can keep all of God's commandments perfectly. Recognize that even the visible commandments (do not murder, steal, commit adultery, lie, honor parents, love your neighbor) are kept imperfectly in the heart. The standard is perfection, and you fall short.

### Step 3: See That No One Is Good Except God

When someone asks "What good deed must I do?" understand that Jesus' first correction is to show there is no good in human beings apart from God. The Greek word for "good" means moral perfection and moral excellence. All have sinned and fallen short of God's glory. Do not trust in your own righteousness. It is impossible to be right with God on your own merit.

### Step 4: Allow the Law to Expose Your Sin

Let God's moral law serve as a schoolmaster to point you to Christ. The law is not for believers to earn salvation; it is for unbelievers to see their sin and need for a Savior. Jesus gave the rich young ruler six commandments — all visible ones that people can keep in public — to show that even external obedience is not enough. The commandment the young ruler violated was the invisible one: idolatry. His wealth was his god. Allow the Holy Spirit to convict you of the sin you cannot see in yourself.

### Step 5: Surrender Your Idols and Follow Christ

Jesus' invitation to the rich young ruler was not just to keep rules but to "come and follow me." The Lord requires you to surrender whatever is sitting on the throne of your heart — wealth, status, relationships, comfort — and follow Him alone. This is not a call to sell everything physically for every person, but it is a call to make Christ your all. When Christ calls you to follow, He already knows your past, your brokenness, and your failures. He still says, "Come."

### Step 6: Receive Christ as Your Righteousness

Since you cannot be morally perfect on your own, be clothed in the moral perfection of another — Jesus Christ. Your ticket to heaven is not your own goodness but faith in Christ alone. When the devil accuses you, remind him that you are clothed in Christ. Stop boasting in your talents, gifts, or religious performance. Your only boast is the cross and the work of Christ. He who began a good work in you will see it through.

### Step 7: Fix Your Eyes on Eternal Reward

If you have sacrificed for Christ — left relationships, jobs, comfort, or opportunities — do not grow weary. Do not compare your suffering with the prosperity of the wicked. Fix your eyes not on earthly rewards but on eternity. Jesus promised His disciples that those who leave everything for His name will receive a hundredfold and inherit eternal life. The greatest reward is not a house, spouse, or platform; it is Christ Himself and the new heaven and new earth where there will be no more death, mourning, crying, or pain.

### Step 8: Share the Gospel Urgently

Understand that the window for gospel proclamation is short. Every day, people around you — in nursing homes, workplaces, and neighborhoods — draw closer to death without Christ and need to hear the truth before it is too late. Be an agent on mission wherever you are. Confront people with their sin, tell them where they are going without Christ, and point them to the Savior who died for them. Do not give them false comfort or lies. Proclaim the biblical gospel with boldness, knowing that salvation is by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Rich Young Ruler

A wealthy, moral, religious man ran to Jesus and knelt before Him, asking what good deed he must do to inherit eternal life. He had kept the visible commandments outwardly since his youth. Yet Jesus exposed his idolatry — his wealth was his god. The man went away sorrowful because he could not surrender his possessions to follow Christ. This illustrates that moral religiosity without heart surrender to Christ is insufficient for salvation.

### Example 2: The Nursing Home Disciple

A member of 2819 Church serving as a volunteer in a nursing home noticed a resident's spiritual condition. She asked the elderly woman about her faith and learned that her son-in-law was Pastor Philip Anthony Mitchell. Through faithful witness, she led the elderly woman — Pastor Mitchell's mother-in-law — into a renewed, real relationship with Christ in the final weeks of her life. This demonstrates that God can use ordinary believers in ordinary places to accomplish eternal results.

### Example 3: Peter's Reward Question

Peter asked Jesus, "We have left everything and followed you. What then will we have?" This represents every believer who has sacrificed for Christ and wonders about their reward. Jesus pointed Peter and the disciples to the new heaven and new earth — the ultimate reward of living with God forever. This reframes earthly suffering as temporary and worthy of eternal glory.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Examine your heart regularly to identify hidden idols that compete with Christ
- ✅ Use the moral law to expose sin in yourself and others, not to earn salvation
- ✅ Clothe yourself in Christ's righteousness daily by faith, not by your own performance
- ✅ Fix your eyes on eternal rewards rather than temporal comfort, status, or possessions
- ✅ Proclaim the full gospel — including sin, repentance, and the need for Christ — without compromise
- ✅ Be sensitive to the Holy Spirit's promptings to witness wherever you are
- ✅ Rest in the truth that Christ knew what He was getting when He called you to follow Him
- ✅ Stop boasting in your gifts, talents, or religious achievements; boast only in the cross

## Keep In Mind

- Inadequacy mingled with religion is more crippling than worldly inadequacy because there is no bottom — you will never know when you have done enough to please God
- The American church often presents a false gospel that says God loves you and has a wonderful plan for your life without confronting sin
- Heaven is automatic for professing Christians in American culture, but Jesus said the path to eternal life is narrow and requires moral perfection
- Wealth and self-reliance are dangerous idols that keep people from seeing their need for Christ
- The first will be last and the last will be first — do not seek status in this life at the cost of eternal reward
- Eternity with Christ should be enough reward for any sacrifice made in this life

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not rely on your own moral performance for assurance of salvation; only Christ's imputed righteousness provides security
- False comfort given at funerals — telling unbelieving loved ones they are in a better place — is spiritually dangerous and eternally consequential
- The Lake of Fire is the eternal destiny for those whose names are not found in the Book of Life
- No one gets away with sin — all will be judged according to what they have done
- The gospel must include the bad news of sin and judgment, not just the good news of grace, or it is no gospel at all

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trying to earn salvation through church attendance, tithes, service, and good behavior.
  **Solution:** Understand that salvation is by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone. No human work can add to what Christ has already accomplished.

- **Problem:** Comparing your outward piety to others and assuming you are saved because you appear moral.
  **Solution:** Let the moral law expose the sin in your heart that no one else can see. Idolatry is often the commandment we violate while keeping the visible ones.

- **Problem:** Seeking personal glory, platforms, and status through Christianity.
  **Solution:** Repent of using Christ to position yourself for worldly success. The Christian life is about following Christ, not building your brand.

- **Problem:** Believing that everyone who dies automatically goes to heaven.
  **Solution:** Jesus said, "If you would enter life, keep the commandments." The standard is perfection. Without Christ, no one meets that standard.

- **Problem:** Despairing when you see the wicked prospering while you suffer for Christ.
  **Solution:** Fix your eyes on eternity. The sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory that shall be revealed. Your reward is coming.

- **Problem:** Avoiding evangelism because you do not want to offend people or make them uncomfortable.
  **Solution:** Be a faithful proclaimer of the biblical gospel. People need to be confronted about sin and pointed to the Savior. Do not give them pixie dust and lies.
