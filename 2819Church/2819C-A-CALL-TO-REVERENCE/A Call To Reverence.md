# A Call To Reverence ｜ Isaiah 6:1-8

## Overview

This sermon calls the church to a renewed reverence for the Lord and His house, centered on Isaiah's vision in Isaiah 6:1-8. The speaker, Philip Anthony Mitchell, confronts the desecration of sacred spaces by entertainment, secular music, and false teaching, urging listeners to return to a posture of holy fear and consecration. The message emphasizes that a prepared messenger is greater than a prepared message, and that through fasting, prayer, and surrender, believers can encounter the living God and respond with the readiness to say, "Here I am, send me."

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you feel far from God and need a tangible step toward His presence
- When you are preparing for a season of consecration, fasting, or spiritual renewal
- When you sense that your local church or the broader body of Christ has lost its sense of the sacred and holy
- When you are struggling with condemnation and need to remember your identity in Christ
- When you sense God calling you to boldness, even at the cost of persecution or unpopularity

## Steps

### Step 1: Draw Near to the Holy Place

Recognize that the altar is holy and approach it with reverent awe. Take a step of faith to draw near to God's presence — especially if you have been sitting in comfort or distance. Let the reality that tears are shed, prayers are lifted, and lives are changed at the altar move you to come before Him with the humility and holy fear that Isaiah displayed, rather than shrinking back in shame.

### Step 2: Consecrate Yourself Through Fasting and Prayer

Set apart a season — seven to eight days as described in this sermon — to fast and pray. Replace every meal with the Word of God. Choose either a total fast (water and Scripture only) or a partial fast (fruits, vegetables, and water). Use the time you would spend eating, watching TV, or scrolling to pray, read Scripture, and seek God's will above your own.

### Step 3: Kill the Flesh and Put Sin to Death

Use the consecration week to actively remove sin from your life. This includes sexual immorality, alcohol, drugs, secular music, pornography, social media that does not honor God, and any pleasure that competes with your love for Christ. Deny yourself so that your inner being — your heart and spirit — can be alive and sensitive to the Holy Spirit's leading. Remember: if your flesh does not feel the cost of this sacrifice, you have not truly fasted.

### Step 4: Pray in Secret and Out the Lord's Will

Pray throughout the day in quiet places, during prayer walks, at your job, in the gym, and wherever you are. Do not use prayer to beg God for your own desires; instead, pray "Your kingdom come, Your will be done." Pray for souls, for revival, for repentance, and for your own heart to be cleansed.

### Step 5: Encourage One Another in the Fast

Check in with fellow believers during the consecration week. Ask what God is saying to them, share what you are reading, and hold one another accountable. If you feel weak, call a sister or brother to pray with you. Let the fast be a shared journey of spiritual breakthrough.

### Step 6: Die to Self So Christ Can Be Seen More Clearly

Embrace the death of your own will, ambition, and unholiness so that Christ becomes more visible in and through you. As the speaker testified, he has worn only black since 2018 as a living funeral — a daily reminder that he is dead to culture and alive to Christ. Allow things that have been idols in your life to die so that the true King can be seen.

### Step 7: Respond to God's Call With "Here I Am, Send Me"

Like Isaiah, when you see the holiness of God revealed, respond with readiness to be sent. Do not hold back. Hurling yourself on the altar means saying yes to whatever assignment God has for you, regardless of cost. Be prepared to be used by God even when you feel unprepared, because a prepared messenger is greater than a prepared message.

## Examples

### Example 1: Isaiah's Vision and Cleansing

Isaiah saw the Lord high and lifted up in the year King Uzziah died. Recognizing the supreme holiness of God, he cried out, "Woe is me, for I am a man of unclean lips." A seraphim then took a burning coal from the altar and touched Isaiah's lips, declaring his guilt taken away and his sin atoned for. From that place of cleansing, Isaiah heard the voice of the Lord asking, "Who shall I send?" and he responded, "Here I am, send me." This illustrates the pattern of conviction, purification, and commission that the sermon calls every believer to enter.

### Example 2: The Fast That Shook the Church

The speaker shared from his own life that during a fast of consecration in the fall of 2022, the Lord told him to preach Matthew until he was done, to change the name of the church, and to begin sending him to preach across the nation. Everything the church had experienced in the prior year — rapid growth, a new song, expanded ministry — was the fruit of a fast. This demonstrates how consecration and surrender unlock revelation and direction that cannot be obtained in comfort.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Draw near to the altar physically and spiritually when you feel far from God
- ✅ Consecrate yourself with fasting and prayer before seeking God's direction for the year
- ✅ Replace every meal with the Word of God during a fast
- ✅ Use quiet, secret places for prayer so that only the Father who sees in secret rewards you
- ✅ Kill sin in your life during consecration — no compromise with worldly pleasures
- ✅ Encourage and check in with other believers during the fast
- ✅ Die to your own will and ambition so Christ can be more clearly seen in your life
- ✅ Respond to God's call with readiness, saying "Here I am, send me"
- ✅ Give cheerfully and sacrificially when called, without manipulation or obligation
- ❌ Do not desecrate the house of God with entertainment, secular music, or worldly practices
- ❌ Do not use prayer merely to manipulate God into blessing your personal plans
- ❌ Do not fast with a legalistic heart, fighting the Lord over food or rules
- ❌ Do not remain silent in the face of false prophets and the mockery of Christ's name
- ❌ Do not give out of guilt, manipulation, or fear — give only with a cheerful heart
- ❌ Do not rely on your own preparation or message more than your preparedness as a vessel

## Keep In Mind

- A prepared messenger is greater than a prepared message — your character and surrender matter more than your notes
- When King Uzziah, an idol, died, Isaiah saw the Lord more clearly; things must die in your life for Christ to be seen
- The very thing that made Isaiah feel most unworthy — his lips — was the very thing God used mightily for His glory
- God does not have to work around your sinfulness; He can use even your worst moments to display His grace
- You will never be sent back to what you were — God always takes you beyond what you could have imagined
- The resurrection of Jesus is proof that He has all authority, power, and glory — and that same power is available to you
- Condemnation is not from God; it is a lie from the evil one. You have been forgiven and declared righteous in Christ.
- The American pulpit is the greatest threat to the American church, not the devil
- False prophets and wolves in sheep's clothing will be exposed — God will topple their ministries
- The Kingdom of Heaven is at hand and time is running out — live with urgency

## Security & Safety Notes

- No condemnation belongs to those who are in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1) — do not allow guilt or shame to keep you from God's presence
- Do not bow to pressure from man-made rules or religious performance during fasting — if you need to eat, eat, and do not let legalism steal your reward
- You can never give your way into heaven, and you will not go to hell for not tithing — guard your heart against manipulation
- False prophets and false doctrine are real and dangerous — test everything against Scripture and hold leaders accountable
- Your past sins and failures do not disqualify you from God's call — He sees the perfect Son of God when He looks at you

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Feeling condemned and unworthy, which prevents you from drawing near to God
  **Solution:** Remember that Jesus already paid for your sin at the Cross. Condemnation is a lie from the enemy — run to God, not away from Him, and allow His grace to cleanse you.
- **Problem:** Fasting with a bitter or legalistic heart, fighting the Lord over food and rules
  **Solution:** If you find yourself angry or obsessed with what you cannot eat, you are operating in the flesh. Eat, and fast with the right heart. The Lord is not honored by legalism.
- **Problem:** Using prayer to beg God to bless your own plans instead of surrendering to His will
  **Solution:** Repent of self-centered prayer. Begin every prayer time with "Your kingdom come, Your will be done," and let that posture shape everything you ask for.
- **Problem:** Remaining silent when you see the name of Christ mocked and the church desecrated
  **Solution:** Cultivate a righteous indignation against the mockery of the King of Kings. Cry aloud, hold leaders accountable, and do not support ministries that defame Christ's name.
- **Problem:** Thinking your past disqualifies you from being used by God
  **Solution:** Isaiah's "unclean lips" became the very instrument God used for prophecy. Your worst moments can become your greatest testimony when surrendered to Christ.
- **Problem:** Giving out of manipulation, guilt, or obligation
  **Solution:** Give only with a cheerful heart. If you feel pressured or manipulated, do not give. God loves a cheerful giver, not a coerced one.
- **Problem:** Trying to fill sacred space with entertainment and secular culture
  **Solution:** Recognize that the house of God is holy. Remove every form of desecration — music, media, and practices that mock or diminish the presence of the living God.
