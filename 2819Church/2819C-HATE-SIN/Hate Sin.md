# Hate Sin

## Overview

This sermon teaches believers to develop a passionate hatred for sin as the root cause of all human suffering, brokenness, and separation from God. It explains that sin is not merely individual moral failure but the underlying source of trauma, relationship destruction, mental anguish, and societal decay. The message calls Christians to stop blaming people and circumstances and instead recognize sin as the true enemy, bringing it before God in repentance while pursuing deeper intimacy with Christ as the only lasting remedy.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you need to understand the root cause of pain and brokenness in your life
- When working through trauma, regret, or recurring patterns of destructive behavior
- When seeking to help others who are trapped by sin's consequences
- When you want to grow in holiness and stop treating sin as acceptable
- When you need to realign your perspective on societal problems and cultural decay

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize Sin as the Source of All Trouble

Identify the areas of pain, frustration, and sorrow in your life. Instead of blaming people, circumstances, or systems, recognize that sin is the underlying cause. Acknowledge that your insecurities, bad decisions, broken relationships, and mental anguish all trace back to sin's entrance into the world in Genesis 3.

### Step 2: Stop Blaming Everything Except Sin

When you feel hurt, frustrated, or betrayed, resist the urge to blame other people or situations. Say to yourself: "This is sin at work." Blame sin for the destruction it causes rather than making excuses or staying in self-pity. Shift your focus from people to the real enemy.

### Step 3: Hate Sin with Passion

Develop a holy hatred for sin. When you see sin in yourself, in others, in society, or on social media, let it grieve you. Do not become desensitized. When you notice sinful patterns rising in your heart, do not tolerate them — let them drive you to your knees in prayer.

### Step 4: Bring Sin to God in Prayer

When you sense sin working in your heart — pride, lust, bitterness, insecurity, unforgiveness — bring it immediately before God. Say: "God, work this out of me." Confess specific sins and ask Him to purify your heart. Do not keep sin on a leash or treat it like a pet you are willing to tolerate.

### Step 5: Keep Nailing Sin to the Cross

Make a daily practice of crucifying sinful desires and patterns. Every time you see or feel sin rising, reject it and submit yourself to Christ. Do not wait for sin to grow — deal with it at the first stirrings.

### Step 6: Ask God to Reveal Your Blind Spots

Pray like David: "Search my heart, O Lord, and see if there be any grievous way in me." Ask God to show you the areas where you are causing damage to yourself or others but cannot see it. Humility and self-examination are essential to avoid self-righteousness.

### Step 7: Pursue Intimacy with Christ as the Remedy

Understand that counseling, therapy, community, and self-help are helpful, but the only lasting remedy for the deepest issues of the human heart is deeper intimacy with Jesus. Invest in a close, personal, growing relationship with Christ. Let His presence transform you from the inside out.

## Examples

### Example 1: A Struggling Marriage

A husband and wife are constantly fighting. Instead of only blaming each other's words and actions, they recognize that pride, selfishness, and unforgiveness are sin at work. They stop pointing fingers and instead pray: "God, show us where sin is destroying our marriage. Work pride out of me. Work bitterness out of me." They pursue intimacy with Christ together, allowing Him to heal the root issues rather than just managing surface conflicts.

### Example 2: Social Media and Mental Anguish

A person feels depressed and anxious after spending time on social media, comparing their life to others. Instead of simply deleting the app or envying others, they recognize that comparison, discontentment, and idolatry of approval are sins at work. They hate those patterns, bring them before God, and choose to fill their mind with Scripture and worship. They also grieve when they see others posting content born out of sin and foolishness, praying for them rather than engaging in drama.

### Example 3: A Person Caught in Addiction or Secret Sin

Someone is trapped in a pattern of secret sin that is ruining their relationships, peace, and opportunities. They stop making excuses and stop blaming their past or other people. They confess: "Sin is killing me. I have been driven by my sin nature." They run to God in repentance, ask Him to search their heart and reveal blind spots, and commit to daily crucifixion of that sinful pattern through accountability and intimacy with Christ.

### Example 4: Observing Cultural and Societal Decay

A believer watches news of moral collapse — redefinitions of marriage, abortion, false teaching, and identity confusion. Instead of only getting angry at culture or politicians, they recognize these as symptoms of sin. They hate sin because they see it as the barrier between lost people and God. They pray for the lost, for their family members who do not know Jesus, and for the church to wake up and hate what God hates.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Identify the root of pain and brokenness as sin, not merely people or circumstances
- ✅ Blame sin for destruction rather than staying in self-pity or blame-shifting
- ✅ Hate sin when you see it in yourself, in others, and in society
- ✅ Bring sinful thoughts and patterns immediately to God in prayer
- ✅ Keep nailing sinful desires to the cross daily
- ✅ Ask God to search your heart and reveal blind spots
- ✅ Pursue deeper intimacy with Christ as the primary remedy for the heart's deepest issues
- ✅ Thank God for counselors and community, but do not rely on them as the final solution
- ✅ Grieve for those who are lost and disconnected from God because of sin
- ✅ Read Scripture in context and reject false teaching that minimizes sin

## Keep In Mind

- Sin is a source of much of humanity's pain and brokenness — not governments, not inequities, not other people. These are byproducts of sin.
- We live in a Genesis 3 world. Seeing life through the lens of Genesis 3 changes how you interpret pain, conflict, and brokenness.
- Many interpret the "thief" in John 10:10 as primarily bad teaching and lies sourced by sin — not only the devil. Proper hermeneutics matters.
- Self-righteousness often arises from blind spots where you cause damage but cannot see it. Humility and God's searchlight are essential.
- Even tears, trauma, and mental illness in this room have sin as part of their origin story.
- If you do not start hating sin, it will rob you of peace and damage your relationships.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use hatred of sin as an excuse to hate people. Sin is the target, not individuals. Hatred of sin should produce compassion for the broken, not cruelty toward the sinner.
- Avoid self-righteousness. Hating sin does not mean you are better than others — it means you see your own need for a Savior more clearly.
- Be careful with bold statements about cultural issues. Speak truth in love, with the goal of pointing people to Christ, not merely winning arguments or shaming others.
- Do not weaponize this teaching to condemn, judge, or control others. The sermon is first and foremost about your own heart before God.
- Remember that intimacy with Christ is the remedy — not performance, not religiosity, and not self-effort.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Staying in blame-shifting and self-pity rather than recognizing sin as the root cause.
  **Solution:** When you feel hurt or frustrated, pause and ask: "What sin is at the root of this?" Stop blaming people and start blaming sin for the destruction it causes.

- **Problem:** Treating sin like a pet or something to tolerate rather than hate.
  **Solution:** When you feel sinful patterns rising in your heart, bring them immediately to God in prayer and reject them. Do not negotiate with sin.

- **Problem:** Relying on counseling, therapy, or community as the final remedy instead of pursuing Christ.
  **Solution:** Thank God for every form of help, but remember that deeper intimacy with the Lord is the only lasting remedy for the deepest issues of your heart.

- **Problem:** Having blind spots where you cause damage to yourself or others without seeing it, leading to self-righteousness.
  **Solution:** Pray regularly: "God, search my heart. Show me the areas I cannot see. Reveal any grievous ways in me." Ask trusted believers to speak into your life as well.

- **Problem:** Misinterpreting Scripture to minimize sin or personalize the "thief" incorrectly.
  **Solution:** Study the Bible in its full context. Read chapters before and after any passage. Lies and bad teaching sourced by sin are the primary threat — not only external enemies.

- **Problem:** Becoming angry at culture, politicians, or other people but ignoring sin in your own heart.
  **Solution:** Hate sin first in yourself. Let personal repentance lead your response to cultural brokenness, not performative outrage.
