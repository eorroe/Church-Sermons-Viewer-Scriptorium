# How to Think About Money

## Overview

This sermon teaches that God uses finances as his favorite tool to test the human heart, revealing what you truly love, what you truly trust, and whether God can trust you with greater spiritual leadership and material responsibility. Pastor Rick explains that money is neither good nor evil—it is simply a tool—and that the Bible's central call is to love people and use money, not the other way around. By examining three foundational truths and the example of the dishonest manager in Luke 16, the sermon shows how to shift from hoarding earthly treasure to storing up treasure in heaven through wise planning, generous giving, and eternal investment, so that God can trust you with greater spiritual leadership and material responsibility.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you want to understand what your spending habits reveal about your true priorities
- When you are uncertain whether you are trusting God or your money for security, happiness, or fulfillment
- When you need to assess whether God can trust you with greater spiritual and material responsibility
- When you feel stuck in a "rat race" of chasing wealth and comparing yourself to others
- When you want to learn how to use money as a tool for God's purposes rather than pursuing it as an end in itself
- When you are ready to stop procrastinating and take concrete steps toward financial stewardship

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize That Money Tests What You Love Most

Understand that how you spend your time and money reveals your true priorities to God and everyone else. If you want to know what you love most, look at your calendar and your credit card statements, because your actions speak louder than your words. Matthew 6:21 states, "Where your treasure is, there your heart will be"—meaning that wherever you invest your money, your heart will follow. The moment you put money into something, you become interested in its success or failure. To store up treasure in heaven, intentionally direct your money toward eternal purposes rather than hoarding it on earth.

### Step 2: Identify What You Truly Trust Most

Examine whether you are trusting your money for security, happiness, or fulfillment, or whether you are trusting God for those things. Ask yourself: Am I trusting in my money for security, or am I trusting in God for my security? Am I trusting in my money for my happiness, or am I trusting in God for my happiness? Am I trusting in my money for my fulfillment, or am I trusting in God to make me feel good about me? Proverbs 11:28 says, "If you trust in your money, you will fall, but if you trust in God, you will flourish like a green tree." If your spiritual life feels stagnant or distant, Pastor Rick challenges you to check your checkbook—your spending will reveal what you truly trust.

### Step 3: Ask Whether God Can Trust You With Material Things

Recognize that how you handle worldly wealth determines whether God can trust you with true riches. Luke 16:10-12 teaches that whoever can be trusted with very little can be trusted with much, and whoever is dishonest with very little will also be dishonest with much. God is looking to see how well you manage material things before he gives you spiritual blessings. If you are spending more than you make, not saving for the future, not investing at all, or working for your money instead of letting your money work for you, you are not being trustworthy with worldly wealth. Unmanaged finances are a symptom of a life without God as Lord and manager, and you need God as the manager, lord, and CEO of your life.

### Step 4: Treat Money as a Tool, Not a Treasure

Reframe your relationship with money by understanding that it is a neutral tool that can be used for great good or great harm. Luke 16:9 says, "I tell you, use worldly wealth to gain friends for yourselves." Money is neither good nor bad—it can build great churches or finance drugs. The Bible's command is to love people and use money, but most people reverse this and end up loving money and using people, which God calls a "personal foul." Money should serve God's purposes, not become your master.

### Step 5: Look Ahead and Plan for the Future

Follow the example of the dishonest manager in Luke 16:1-9, who looked ahead to his future rather than living only for today. Proverbs 14:8 says, "The wise man looks ahead; the fool attempts to fool himself and won't face the facts." Face the financial facts in your life—what you earn, what you owe, what you own, and where your money is going. Most people never look ahead because our culture teaches you to have it now, spend it now, and forget about the future. Resist this mindset by taking the long view, considering not just retirement but eternity.

### Step 6: Make a Concrete Financial Plan

Create a written plan for your money in the form of a budget. Proverbs 16:9 says, "We should make plans, counting on God to direct us." A budget is simply planned spending—telling your dollars where you want them to go before the month begins rather than wondering where they went afterward. If you do not have a budget, you do not have a real financial plan. Include specific allocations for giving, savings, necessities, and discretionary spending. Without a plan, you will drift through life day by day, reacting to impulses rather than acting with purpose.

### Step 7: Act Quickly and Avoid Procrastination

Set your financial plan in motion without delay. The dishonest manager acted quickly instead of saying, "Someday I'll get my finances in order" or "One of these days I'll start saving." Proverbs 13:11 warns that money gathered little by little will grow, but only if you start now. "One of these days" is none of these days—do not let procrastination rob you of financial health and spiritual maturity. Think ahead, make a plan, and act on it immediately.

### Step 8: Use Money to Invest in Eternal Relationships

Use your worldly wealth to build spiritual friendships that will last into eternity. Luke 16:9 teaches that the best use of money is to gain friends for yourself so that when earthly wealth is gone, those friends will welcome you into your eternal dwelling. This does not mean you can buy your way into heaven—salvation is by grace through faith alone—but it means you should use a portion of your money to advance the gospel, reach people for Christ, and build eternal relationships. Store up treasure in heaven by investing in people, not just possessions.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Stock Investment Revelation

Most people could not care less how a company like Microsoft or Toyota performs as a business. But the moment they buy stock in that company, they become intensely interested in whether it succeeds or fails. This illustrates Jesus' point in Matthew 6:21—where you put your treasure, your heart will follow. The same is true for every purchase and investment you make. If you want your heart to be in heaven, put your money there through generous giving and eternal investment.

### Example 2: The Rat Race Illusion

Many people spend their lives trying to keep up with others, only to discover that their neighbors refinance their homes and file for bankruptcy. Pastor Rick says, "Even if you win the rat race, you're still a rat." This example shows the futility of pursuing wealth as an end in itself and the importance of breaking free from cultural pressure to hoard and compare.

### Example 3: The Trustworthiness Test

Imagine being the child of the world's wealthiest person, and your parent says they will give you everything—but not right now. Instead, they give you a tiny bit to see how well you handle it. If you manage it well, you will receive the full inheritance; if you blow it, you will lose the blessing. This is exactly what Jesus teaches in Luke 16:10-12. How you handle the little money God has given you now determines whether he can trust you with greater spiritual and material blessings.

### Example 4: The Spiritual Blahs

If you feel distant from God, spiritually stagnant, or lacking power in your life, Pastor Rick challenges you to check your checkbook. Your spending patterns will reveal whether you are trusting God or your money. If your financial life is unmanaged, your spiritual life will reflect that same lack of management. Turning your finances over to God and managing them wisely is often the key to renewed spiritual vitality.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Examine your calendar and credit card statements to discover what you truly love most
- ✅ Store up treasure in heaven by intentionally directing money toward God's kingdom purposes rather than hoarding on earth
- ✅ Ask hard questions about whether you trust money or God for security, happiness, and fulfillment
- ✅ Treat money as a tool to love people, not as a treasure to be loved and used to manipulate others
- ✅ Look ahead financially, face the facts, and plan for both the near future and eternity
- ✅ Create a written budget that allocates specific amounts for giving, savings, necessities, and discretionary spending
- ✅ Act quickly on your financial plan—do not procrastinate with "someday" thinking
- ✅ Use a portion of your money to advance the gospel and build eternal friendships
- ✅ Remember that unmanaged finances reveal a life without God as Lord and manager, and you need God as the CEO of your life
- ✅ Give generously to support ministry that reaches people for Christ

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace personal financial counsel, accountability, or expert review of your specific financial situation.
- Stop and ask for clarification if proper biblical context, personal discernment, or spiritual safety boundaries are missing from your study.
- Changing financial habits takes time and intentionality; do not expect overnight transformation.
- Money itself is not evil—the love of money is. Maintaining a healthy, neutral view of money as a tool is essential.
- Financial decisions carry real-world consequences; always apply biblical wisdom alongside prudent financial stewardship.
- No sermon alone replaces professional financial advice for complex situations such as debt, investments, or large purchases.
- Guard against dishonest gain in any form; God never blesses what is obtained through deception or unfairness.
- Be cautious of anyone who claims you can buy spiritual blessings or buy your way into heaven through financial giving.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Hoarding earthly treasure and neglecting eternal investment
  **Solution:** Redirect your spending toward God's kingdom purposes by giving generously and investing in people who will share eternity with you.
- **Problem:** Trusting money for security, happiness, or fulfillment instead of trusting God
  **Solution:** Examine your checkbook and calendar. If your spending does not reflect trust in God, adjust your habits to align with biblical priorities.
- **Problem:** Procrastinating financial decisions with "someday" thinking
  **Solution:** Create a written budget today and take one small action step immediately. "One of these days" is none of these days.
- **Problem:** Reversing the biblical command by loving money and using people
  **Solution:** Remind yourself daily that people are to be loved and money is to be used for God's kingdom purposes. Evaluate every financial decision through that lens.
- **Problem:** Failing to look ahead or make a financial plan
  **Solution:** Face your financial facts, write down what you earn, owe, own, and spend, and create a realistic plan for the future.
- **Problem:** Believing that spiritual stagnation is unrelated to financial stewardship
  **Solution:** Recognize the direct connection between how you handle money and your spiritual vitality. Unmanaged finances reveal a life without God as Lord and manager. Manage your finances well and watch your relationship with God deepen.
