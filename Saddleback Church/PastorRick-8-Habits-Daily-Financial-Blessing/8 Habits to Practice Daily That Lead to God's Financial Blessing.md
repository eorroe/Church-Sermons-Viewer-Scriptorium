# 8 Habits to Practice Daily That Lead to God's Financial Blessing

## Overview

This sermon teaches that God has made incredible promises of financial blessing, but they all carry premises—calling listeners to decide whether they will trust God or themselves with their finances. Pastor Rick emphasizes that Jesus spoke more about money than about heaven or hell, commending wise investors and warning against greed, because life is ultimately a test and a trust. The core message is that true blessing comes from doing finances God's way, not your own way, and that a person's life is not measured by the abundance of their possessions.

## When to Follow This Sermon

- When you are facing financial decisions and need to discern whether to trust God's way or your own way
- When you feel pressure to pursue wealth and need biblical perspective on greed and contentment
- When you want to understand what the Bible actually teaches about financial blessing
- When you are ready to examine your motives for wanting financial increase
- When you want to build daily financial habits that align with biblical principles

## Steps

### Step 1: Trust God as Your Source and Supply

Recognize that God—not your job, your savings, or anything else—is your true source of income and security. Your job is merely a channel; God is the supplier. If one channel closes, God can open another. Put your security in something that can never be taken from you: the unconditional love of God. If you find yourself worrying, that is a warning light that you are trusting something other than God for your security. Romans 11:36 reminds us that everything comes from God, exists by his power, and is intended for his glory. Proverbs 16:3 says to commit your work to the Lord, and then your plans will succeed.

### Step 2: Do Your Work as an Act of Worship

Whatever you do—whether sweeping streets or leading a company—do it with all your heart as if you are working for the Lord. If your heart is not in your work, consider changing jobs because life is too short to spend it doing work you hate, and the Bible says that is a sin. God blesses work that is done with five qualities: planning, initiative, integrity, focus, and persistence. Plan your work and work your plan (Proverbs 2:15). Take initiative instead of waiting for opportunity to arrive (Proverbs 10:4). Maintain honesty in every deal (Proverbs 16:11). Stay focused rather than chasing every new distraction (Proverbs 12:11). And persist through failures and criticism (Proverbs 13:11).

### Step 3: Keep Good Records

Write down what you earn, what you owe, what you own, and where your money is going. You cannot put aside money for offerings, savings, or anything else unless you know what you have and where it is going. Proverbs 27:23 says to know the state of your flocks and herds—today that means know your stocks and your spending. Easy credit plus ignorance equals disaster. If you ever say "I just don't know where it goes," you are already in trouble. Keeping good records reduces worry and gives you control over your finances.

### Step 4: Give the First 10% Back to God (Tithing)

Give a full tenth of what you earn back to God first—not the leftovers, but the first portion. Tithing is a statement of gratitude ("I wouldn't have anything if it weren't for you"), a statement of purpose ("You are first in my life"), and a statement of faith ("I believe you will keep your promises"). Malachi 3:10 challenges you to test God in this: "Bring the full tenth into the storehouse and see if I will not open the windows of heaven and pour out blessings for you." There are more promises in the Bible about tithing than about anything else.

### Step 5: Save and Invest for the Future

Put money aside every week so that your money can work for you instead of you working for money. The wise save for the future; the foolish spend whatever they get (Proverbs 21:20). Money saved little by little will grow (Proverbs 13:11). It is not how much you save that matters most, but the consistency of saving every single week. Proverbs 24:27 says to develop your business first before building your house—meaning reinvest in growing your wealth before spending on luxuries.

### Step 6: Set Up a Repayment Plan to Get Out of Debt

Create a concrete plan to pay off your debts and let no debt remain outstanding (Romans 13:8). If you are only paying the minimum on your cards, you are letting a debt remain outstanding, which means your money is working against you instead of for you. Follow the 10/10/80 plan: tithe 10% first, save 10% second, and live on 80%. If you cannot live on 80%, you are living beyond your means. Pay God first, pay yourself second, then set up a repayment plan for your debts. God will help you with these steps.

### Step 7: Budget Your Spending

A budget is simply a plan for your money—telling your dollars where you want them to go rather than wondering where they went. Proverbs 2:15 says to plan carefully and you will have plenty; if you act too quickly, you will never have enough. Acting too quickly means impulse buying—purchasing things you did not plan for based on emotion rather than reason. A budget puts you in control of your money instead of letting your money control you. Allocate specific amounts for giving, savings, necessities, and discretionary spending.

### Step 8: Enjoy What You Have and Give Generously to Those in Need

Do not let greed disguise itself as ambition or prudent planning. 1 Timothy 6:18 calls you to be rich in good works, to give generously to those in need, and to be always ready to share whatever God has given you. Enjoy what God has provided, but do not hoard it. The person who spends their life acquiring more and more without generosity has wasted their life. Use your money to do good, to reach people for Christ, and to build eternal friendships that will last beyond this life.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Faucet and the Supplier

Your job is like a faucet—it delivers water, but it is not the source of the water. God is the supplier. If one faucet gets turned off, God can turn another one on just as easily because He is the source. This perspective helps you stay calm during job loss or financial setbacks, knowing that God is still in control of your supply.

### Example 2: The 10/10/80 Plan

John D. Rockefeller, the wealthiest man of his day, attributed his financial success to giving the first 10% to God, saving the second 10%, and living on the remaining 80%. This plan ensures that you honor God, secure your future, and live within your means—before you spend a dime on anything else.

### Example 3: Easy Credit Plus Ignorance Equals Disaster

A person who does not keep good records and relies on easy credit is like someone driving with their eyes closed. Proverbs 27:23 warns that riches can disappear fast. The person who says "I just don't know where it goes" is already in financial trouble. Keeping detailed records and avoiding impulse buying are the only ways to prevent this.

### Example 4: Wealth Built Little by Little

A person who saves $50 every week and invests it consistently will build wealth over time through compound growth. Proverbs 13:11 says money gathered little by little will grow. This person is not chasing get-rich-quick schemes; they are being persistent and faithful with small amounts, which positions them for greater blessing later.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Decide upfront whether you will trust God or yourself with your finances
- ✅ Do your work with all your heart as an act of worship, regardless of the job
- ✅ Write down everything you earn, owe, own, and spend each month
- ✅ Give a full 10% of your income back to God before anything else
- ✅ Save and invest a consistent amount every week, no matter how small
- ✅ Set up a concrete repayment plan and pay more than the minimum on debts
- ✅ Create a written budget before the month begins and stick to it
- ✅ Give generously to those in need and use money to advance eternal purposes
- ✅ Remember that life is a test and a trust, and money is both a tool and a test
- ✅ Separate your net worth from your self-worth

## Keep In Mind

- This sermon does not replace personal financial counsel, accountability, or expert review of your specific financial situation.
- Stop and ask for clarification if required biblical context, permissions, or safety boundaries are missing from your study.
- These eight habits must be practiced together; doing only a few will not produce the full blessing God intends.
- Financial freedom is a process, not an event. Be patient and persistent.
 Financial decisions carry real-world consequences; always apply biblical wisdom alongside prudent financial stewardship.
- No sermon alone replaces professional financial advice for complex situations such as debt, investments, or large purchases.
- Guard against dishonest gain in any form; God never blesses what is obtained through deception or unfairness.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trusting your job, savings, or investments as your source of security instead of God
  **Solution:** Remind yourself daily that God is your true source and supplier, and worry is a warning light that you are trusting the wrong thing.
- **Problem:** Doing work half-heartedly or staying in a job that conflicts with your values
  **Solution:** Plan your work, take initiative, maintain integrity, stay focused, and persist—or prayerfully consider a change.
- **Problem:** Keeping no records and wondering where all the money went
  **Solution:** Track every dollar you earn, owe, own, and spend. Write it down and review it weekly.
- **Problem:** Tithing only the leftovers instead of the first 10%
  **Solution:** Give the first portion of your income to God before any bills or purchases, as an act of faith and gratitude.
- **Problem:** Waiting until you are out of debt to start saving and tithing
  **Solution:** Follow the 10/10/80 plan: tithe first, save second, then live on 80%. Debt repayment comes after honoring God and paying yourself.
- **Problem:** Impulse buying and failing to budget
  **Solution:** Create a written budget before the month begins and tell your money where to go instead of wondering where it went.
- **Problem:** Accumulating wealth without generosity
  **Solution:** Practice enjoying what you have while giving generously to those in need, using money to advance eternal purposes.
